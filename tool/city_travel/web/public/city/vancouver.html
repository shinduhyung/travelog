Getting around

SkyTrain for the airport and downtown; walk or bike the seawall.

  • ✓Metro ★SkyTrain Canada Line links YVR to downtown in about 25 minutes. Expo and Millennium lines plus SeaBus ferries cover the region.
  • ✓BusTransLink buses run frequent local routes; N10 runs overnight from the airport.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis accept credit cards; Uber and Lyft operate in Vancouver.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is easy outside rush hour; downtown parking is costly.
  • ✓Walking ★Walking and bike share (Mobi) suit the compact downtown and seawall.
  • ✓FerrySeaBus ferries are part of TransLink and cross to North Vancouver.

Climate

Mild coastal climate: wet, cool winters and warm, dry summers. Rain is common from October to March.

  • ☀️Best time: June to SeptemberWarm highs of 19–23°C and the fewest rain days. Ideal for parks, beaches and the seawall.
  • 🌧️Wet wintersNovember to January brings 19–20 rain days a month. Pack a waterproof jacket and shoes.
  • 🌡️Mild, not freezingWinter highs stay near 6°C and lows near 1–2°C. Snow is uncommon downtown.
  • 🏖️Short beach seasonJuly and August are the only reliably warm beach months, around 22–23°C.
  • 🌦️Spring is showeryMarch to May warms from 9°C to 16°C but rain days stay at 9–16. Bring layers.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a rain jacket and comfortable walking shoes year-round; sunscreen and a hat for summer.

Landmarks 27

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Stanley Park

    Vancouver's defining green space, a huge waterfront park with a famous seawall, beaches and old-growth forest. It is the first place most visitors head for. Tip: walk or cycle the seawall early in the morning to avoid crowds, and rent a bike near the entrance.

  2. 2

    Granville Island

    A lively urban hub with a public market, food stalls, galleries and waterfront views. It is a must-see for the atmosphere and local food. Tip: take the small False Creek ferry across for a scenic and cheap arrival.

  3. 3

    Canada Place

    The sail-roofed pier on the downtown waterfront, one of the city's most recognizable sights. It offers views over the harbour and mountains. Tip: go on a clear day for the best photos and combine it with a walk along the waterfront.

  4. 4

    Gastown Steam Clock

    A quirky steam-powered clock in the historic Gastown district, a classic photo stop. The surrounding cobbled streets are good for a wander. Tip: it whistles on the quarter hour, so time your visit to hear it.

  5. 5

    Capilano Suspension Bridge

    A dramatic suspension bridge over a forested canyon, a top attraction just outside downtown. It is popular for the treetop walkways and cliff walk. Tip: book tickets online and arrive early, as it gets busy midday.

  6. 6

    Grouse Mountain

    A mountain with a gondola ride and sweeping views over the city and coast. It is a signature Vancouver experience. Tip: check the weather before going, since views disappear in cloud, and dress warmly at the top.

  7. 7

    Vancouver Aquarium

    A well-known aquarium inside Stanley Park with marine exhibits and family-friendly displays. It pairs naturally with a park visit. Tip: buy tickets ahead and go on a weekday morning for fewer crowds.

  8. 8

    Lions Gate Bridge

    An iconic green suspension bridge linking downtown to the North Shore, with great views. It is a landmark worth seeing even briefly. Tip: walk or cycle across for the best experience, as parking nearby is limited.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Capilano Suspension Bridge or Grouse Mountain; check prices and shuttle options on the day.
    • Take the SeaBus to North Vancouver for the Lonsdale Quay market and skyline views.
    • Rent a bike (Mobi) and ride more of the seawall toward Kitsilano and the beaches.

    Getting around

    • SkyTrain Canada Line links YVR to downtown in about 25 minutes.
    • SkyTrain Expo and Millennium lines plus SeaBus ferries cover the region.
    • TransLink buses run frequent local routes; N10 runs overnight from the airport.
    • Taxis accept credit cards; Uber and Lyft operate in Vancouver.
    • Driving is easy outside rush hour; downtown parking is costly.
    • Walking and bike share (Mobi) suit the compact downtown and seawall.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown · ≈ CAD 200–450 a night
      Central for SkyTrain, shops and the seawall; walk to most sights.
    • West End · ≈ CAD 160–350 a night
      Quiet streets next to Stanley Park and English Bay beaches.
    • Yaletown · ≈ CAD 220–480 a night
      Lively restaurants and bars by the water; easy Canada Line access.
    • Gastown · ≈ CAD 180–400 a night
      Historic brick streets, cafés and nightlife; close to Waterfront station.

    Food & drink

    • Try Pacific salmon and spot prawns in season; ask what is local that day.
    • Granville Island Public Market: graze cheese, bread and fruit, then sit by the water.
    • Richmond has excellent Chinese food; the Canada Line gets you there in about 25 minutes.
    • Cheap eats: sushi, ramen and food trucks downtown, roughly CAD 10–20 a meal.
    • Book popular dinner spots ahead; weekend brunch queues are long.

    Tips

    1. Rent a bike and ride the Stanley Park Seawall, a stunning uninterrupted path around the peninsula
    2. Carry a high-quality rain jacket and waterproof shoes; umbrellas are often useless in the wind
    3. The sushi scene is widely considered some of the best and most affordable outside of Japan
    4. Compass Card or a contactless credit card can be tapped to use all public transit
    5. Downtown Eastside (DTES) struggles with severe visible poverty and drug use; tourists are often advised to bypass certain blocks
