Climate

Istanbul has a mild, wet climate: cool wet winters and warm, drier summers.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberWarm days from 21°C to 29°C and fewer rain days. Ideal for ferries and walking.
  • 🌧️Wettest in winterDecember and January have about 13–14 rain days. Bring a waterproof jacket and shoes.
  • 🥵Hot summer afternoonsJuly and August reach 29°C. Sightsee in the morning and rest in the shade at midday.
  • 🧥Cold winter nightsJanuary and February lows near 4°C. Pack warm layers for evenings.
  • 🌂Rain any monthEven summer has a few wet days. A small umbrella is always useful.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes and a hat for summer; a warm coat and waterproof shoes for winter; modest clothing for mosques.

Landmarks 33

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Hagia Sophia

    The defining monument of Istanbul, a vast former cathedral turned mosque whose dome and mosaics have awed visitors for nearly 1,500 years. It is the one sight no first-time visitor should skip. Go early in the morning to beat the crowds, and dress modestly since it is an active mosque.

  2. 2

    Topkapi Palace

    The opulent seat of the Ottoman sultans, with courtyards, harem rooms and treasury collections overlooking the water. Allow at least half a day to explore it properly. Buy tickets in advance and arrive at opening time to avoid queues.

  3. 3

    Blue Mosque

    A working mosque famous for its cascading domes and blue Iznik tiles, standing right across from Hagia Sophia. Visit outside prayer times, cover shoulders and knees, and carry a scarf for women.

  4. 4

    Basilica Cistern

    An eerie underground reservoir of hundreds of columns and dim reflected light, with the famous Medusa heads. It is a cool, quick stop on hot days. Tickets are timed, so book ahead and go early or late.

  5. 5

    Galata Tower

    A medieval stone tower giving the classic panorama over the Golden Horn and old city rooftops. The viewing balcony is small, so go at opening or near sunset. It sits at the end of Istiklal Avenue, easily reached on foot.

  6. 6

    Grand Bazaar of Istanbul

    One of the world's oldest covered markets, a maze of thousands of shops selling carpets, jewellery and spices. It is an experience as much as a shopping stop. Go on a weekday morning, and bargain politely.

  7. 7

    Dolmabahce Palace

    A lavish 19th-century waterfront palace blending European and Ottoman styles, with a spectacular ceremonial hall. Entry is by guided timed tour, so reserve a slot. It is a short walk or tram ride from the Bosphorus waterfront.

  8. 8

    Suleymaniye Mosque

    Mimar Sinan's masterpiece, perched on a hill with sweeping views over the Golden Horn. It is calmer than the Blue Mosque and free to enter. Visit outside prayer times and dress modestly.

  9. 9

    Maiden's Tower

    A tiny tower on an islet in the Bosphorus, one of the city's most photographed symbols. Boats shuttle to it from the Asian shore. Go in the late afternoon for soft light and shorter queues.

  10. 10

    Rumeli Fortress

    A massive 15th-century fortress on the Bosphorus built before the Ottoman conquest, with towers and walls to climb. The walkways are steep, so wear sturdy shoes. It is best reached by boat or bus along the European shore.

    Getting around

    • Metro and tram: M11 connects IST airport to city; trams serve old city; use Istanbulkart.
    • Buses: extensive but crowded; Havaist and Havabüs for airport transfers.
    • Taxis: yellow, blue, black; use apps like Uber and Bolt to avoid scams.
    • Ride apps: Uber and Bolt operate in Istanbul.
    • Driving: not recommended due to heavy traffic; parking is scarce.
    • Walking: best in Sultanahmet and Beyoğlu; ferries cross the Bosphorus for views.

    Where to stay

    • Sultanahmet · ≈ TRY 2,500–7,000 a night
      Walk to Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque and Topkapı. Quiet at night; tram nearby.
    • Beyoğlu · ≈ TRY 3,000–9,000 a night
      İstiklal Avenue, cafés and nightlife. Central for the new city and the Galata area.
    • Kadıköy · ≈ TRY 2,000–6,000 a night
      Asian side: food markets, bars and a local feel. Ferries to the old city.
    • Beşiktaş · ≈ TRY 2,800–8,000 a night
      Ferry and bus hub on the European shore; close to Dolmabahçe Palace and Ortaköy.

    Food & drink

    • Try Turkish classics: balık ekmek (fish sandwich) by the water, meze plates, and baklava with pistachio.
    • Kadıköy market streets are great for cheap eats: simit, döner and fresh produce. Expect roughly 50–200 TRY a snack.
    • Breakfast (kahvaltı) is a big spread of cheese, olives, eggs and tea. Many cafés serve it all morning.
    • Street food: simit rings, roasted corn and chestnuts in season. Check prices first in tourist areas.
    • Alcohol is widely sold in Beyoğlu and Kadıköy bars; it is limited near mosques in Sultanahmet.

    Tips

    1. Get an Istanbulkart — it is essential for metros, trams, buses, and ferries
    2. Taking a public ferry across the Bosphorus at sunset is the best cheap experience in the city
    3. Haggling is expected in the Grand Bazaar but not in standard shops or restaurants
    4. Dress modestly when visiting mosques (headscarves for women, covered knees for all)
    5. Street cats are beloved and protected by locals — treat them kindly
