Getting around

Walk in town; agree taxi fares first; rent a scooter for the beaches.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram on the island.
  • ✓BusLocal vans run fixed routes for RM1–3 but stop by 7PM. Not for evenings.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis have no meters, so agree the fare before boarding. Grab may work but coverage is patchy; check locally.
  • ✓Rental carScooters rent from about RM38 per 24 hours near the ferry terminal. Good for reaching beaches.
  • ✓Walking ★The town centre is small and walkable. Fine for markets, ferry and museums.
  • ✓FerryFerries link the island to Brunei, Menumbok, Sipitang, Lawas and Limbang. Check schedules on the day.

Climate

Tropical and hot all year, with rain in every month. February is the driest; October to December the wettest.

  • 🌡️Warm all yearDays sit around 29–30°C and nights around 25–26°C. No cool season.
  • 🌧️Rain every monthExpect 18–27 rain days a month. Showers are often short but heavy.
  • ☂️Best odds: FebruaryAbout 18 rain days, the lowest of the year. Still bring a rain jacket.
  • 🌊Wettest: October to DecemberAround 27 rain days each month. Sea trips may be cancelled.
  • 👕What to packLight, quick-dry clothes, sandals, a rain jacket or umbrella, and sunscreen.
  • ⛴️Ferry weatherHeavy rain and rough seas can delay boats. Check schedules on the day.

Landmarks 4

Must-see · 4

  1. 1

    Labuan War Cemetery

    The most moving sight in Labuan, a beautifully kept Commonwealth cemetery honouring thousands of soldiers who died in Borneo during World War II. It is a place for quiet reflection, so dress respectfully and go in the cooler morning hours; it is a short taxi ride from the town centre.

  2. 2

    Labuan Marine Park

    A group of protected islands with clear water, coral reefs and good snorkelling, the main reason many visitors come to Labuan. Boats leave from the town jetty and trips are best arranged in the dry season; bring sun protection and expect to pay for a boat charter or tour.

  3. 3

    Peace Park

    A calm memorial park built as a symbol of reconciliation, with a large monument and views over the water. It pairs naturally with the War Cemetery on a half-day history circuit; visit early or late in the day to avoid the heat.

  4. 4

    Labuan Museum

    A compact museum covering the island's history, culture and wartime past, useful for context before visiting the memorial sites. It is an easy stop in the town centre and a good option during rain or the midday heat.

    Swap in instead

    • Take a boat trip to one of the nearby small islands for snorkelling; check sea conditions and prices locally.
    • Visit the war memorial and museum sites if you like history; allow a half day.
    • Hop a ferry to Menumbok or Brunei for a change of scene; check schedules and passport rules first.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; the town centre is small and walkable.
    • Local buses (vans) run fixed routes, cost RM1-3, and stop by 7PM.
    • Taxis have no meters, so agree the fare before boarding.
    • Ride apps like Grab may work but coverage is patchy; check locally.
    • Scooters rent from about RM38 per 24 hours near the ferry terminal.
    • Ferries link the island to Brunei, Menumbok, Sipitang, Lawas and Limbang.

    Where to stay

    • Labuan Town · ≈ MYR 90–260 a night
      Walk to the ferry, markets, museums and cheap eats. Best base without a car.
    • Financial Park · ≈ MYR 150–380 a night
      Modern hotels and malls, a short walk from the town centre and waterfront.
    • Ferry Terminal area · ≈ MYR 80–220 a night
      Handy for early boats to Brunei and Sabah; simple guesthouses nearby.
    • Batu Manikar · ≈ MYR 180–450 a night
      Quieter beach side with resorts; you will want a car or taxi here.

    Food & drink

    • Eat seafood by the waterfront: grilled fish and prawns, prices vary by catch, so ask before ordering.
    • Try local noodles and rice dishes at coffee shops in town; a simple meal is often around RM8–15.
    • Pasar (market) stalls are good for cheap breakfast and fruit in the morning.
    • Night markets and food courts are the budget option; bring small cash notes.
    • Alcohol is limited and pricey on the island; many places are halal and dry. Check on the day.

    Tips

    1. Labuan is a duty-free zone, famous for cheap alcohol and chocolates
    2. The island is a relaxed financial and logistics hub
    3. Visit the WWII memorial for historical significance
    4. The island is small; you can tour most of it in a single day
    5. Use ferries for cheap and regular connections to the mainland
