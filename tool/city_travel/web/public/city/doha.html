Getting around

Doha Metro for the main sights, with taxis or ride apps for the last stretch.

  • ✓Metro ★Three lines (Red, Gold, Green); Msheireb is the main transfer station. About QR 2 per trip and QR 6 for a day pass; VIP cars cost more. Free metrolink buses connect stations to nearby areas.
  • ✓BusCheap but infrequent and often late; route maps are hard to read. Use the free metrolink feeder buses instead where you can.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Karwa and Al Million are the main taxis; Uber works well in Doha. Handy for the airport and late nights.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is on the right. Traffic jams and roadworks are common, so allow extra time.
  • ✗WalkingOnly pleasant November to March, and sights are spread out. Use the metro or a taxi in summer.
  • ✓TramA free tram runs in Lusail and Msheireb Downtown. Useful for short hops in those areas.

Climate

Hot desert climate: sunny nearly every day, very little rain, and a long, humid summer.

  • ☀️Best time: November to MarchDays around 23–30°C with cool evenings. Ideal for the souq, Corniche and desert trips.
  • 🥵Summer is extreme (June to September)Highs of 39–41°C. Plan indoor sights by day and go outside only at sunrise or after dark.
  • 💧Humid by the waterMuggy evenings from May to October, especially on the Corniche and at The Pearl.
  • 🌂Almost no rainOnly a few short showers between December and March. No umbrella needed most of the year.
  • 🌪️Occasional dustDusty winds can haze the sky and spoil desert views. Check the forecast before booking a dune trip.
  • 🧳What to packLight breathable clothes, a hat and strong sunscreen; a light layer for cold indoor AC and winter nights; modest clothing for mosques and souqs.

Landmarks 9

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Souq Waqif

    Doha's restored old market is the city's most atmospheric place to wander, with spice stalls, falcon shops and courtyard cafes. Come in the evening when it is liveliest and coolest, and dress modestly as it is a traditional area.

  2. 2

    Museum of Islamic Art, Doha

    A landmark building on its own island with a world-class collection of Islamic art and great views back to the skyline. Check opening days and ticket prices online before you go, and allow a couple of hours.

  3. 3

    The Pearl-Qatar

    A polished waterfront district of marinas, shops and restaurants built on reclaimed land. It is best in the cooler evening; taxis or a car are the easiest way to get there.

  4. 4

    National Museum of Qatar

    The striking desert-rose building tells the story of Qatar from its early history to the present. Book tickets ahead and visit in the morning to avoid the midday heat.

  5. 5

    Katara Cultural Village

    A cultural hub with galleries, an amphitheatre and a beach, good for an unhurried half-day. Go in the late afternoon or evening when it is cooler and more active.

  6. 6

    Doha Corniche

    The long waterfront promenade is the classic place to see the skyline and take a stroll. Walk it at sunset or after dark, when the heat drops and the city lights come on.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a desert day: dune drive and Inland Sea views south of the city. Book a tour; check prices on the day.
    • Swap a city afternoon for Katara Cultural Village: beach, galleries and the mosque with blue tiles.
    • Take the metro to Education City for the National Library and campus architecture.

    Getting around

    • Doha Metro has three lines (Red, Gold, Green); Msheireb is the main transfer station.
    • Metro fares are about QR 2 per trip and QR 6 for a day pass; VIP cars cost more.
    • Free metrolink feeder buses connect stations to nearby areas.
    • Karwa and Al Million are main taxis; Uber works well in Doha.
    • Buses are cheap but infrequent and often late; route maps are hard to read.
    • Driving is on the right; traffic jams and roadworks are common. A free tram runs in Lusail and Msheireb Downtown.

    Where to stay

    • West Bay · ≈ QAR 350–900 a night
      Modern towers and the Corniche on the doorstep; easy metro to the airport and sights.
    • Souq Waqif · ≈ QAR 250–700 a night
      Sleep in the old heart: market lanes, restaurants and the corniche walk at night.
    • Msheireb · ≈ QAR 400–950 a night
      Quiet new downtown next to Souq Waqif; main metro transfer at Msheireb station.
    • The Pearl · ≈ QAR 500–1,400 a night
      Marina living, cafés and a beach feel; good for longer stays and families.

    Food & drink

    • Try Qatari dishes: machboos (spiced rice with meat or fish), harees and luqaimat dumplings with date syrup.
    • Souq Waqif is the easy first stop: grilled fish, Lebanese mezze and shisha courtyards in the evening.
    • Cheap eats: shawarma, karak tea and Indian or Pakistani cafeterias, often around QR 10–30 a meal.
    • Book a hotel brunch or a waterfront dinner at The Pearl or Katara for a special night.
    • Alcohol is served only in licensed hotel bars and restaurants. Dress modestly in public places.

    Tips

    1. Dress modestly in public spaces like malls and markets (cover shoulders and knees)
    2. Alcohol is strictly regulated and only available in licensed international hotels and clubs
    3. Summertime heat is dangerous; locals spend the day indoors and socialize late at night
    4. Visit Souq Waqif in the evening for an authentic experience of local food, shisha, and shopping
    5. The weekends are Friday and Saturday; Friday mornings are very quiet due to prayers

    Souvenirs

    Bakhoor incense

    QAR 40–12030–50 g · estimate

    Bakhoor is a traditional Gulf incense made from wood chips and fragrant oils. It is burned at home and offered to guests, so it is a common sight in Qatari homes and shops.

    Where: Souq Waqif perfume and incense shops
    Ask to smell several blends before you buy. Buy a small amount, and skip the charcoal and lighters sold alongside it.

    Al Sadu woven pouch

    QAR 60–1801 small pouch · estimate

    Al Sadu is a traditional weaving style of Bedouin communities in Qatar and the wider Gulf. Its geometric patterns are rooted in desert life and local heritage.

    Where: Souq Waqif craft section
    Check that the piece is locally made before you pay. Ask the seller where it was woven.
