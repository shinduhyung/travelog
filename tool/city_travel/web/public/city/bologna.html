Getting around

Walk the centre; use TPER buses for longer hops.

  • ✗MetroNo metro yet; a tram network is under construction.
  • ✓Bus ★TPER buses cover the city. Single ticket €2.30, valid 75 min; €2.50 on board. Day pass about €9. Buy at tabacchi or newsstands.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered. Uber is limited; local apps work better.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the centre is restricted; cars are banned in parts during the day.
  • ✓Walking ★Walking is easy and pleasant. Bike hire is cheap, about €1–2 per 15 min.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cold, damp winters and hot, humid summers, with the best weather in spring and autumn.

  • 🌸Best time: April to June and September to OctoberMild days around 19–25°C, good for walking the porticoes.
  • 🥵July and August are hotHighs near 31–32°C with warm nights. Plan indoor sights at midday.
  • 🧥Winter is cold and dampJanuary highs near 8°C and lows around 1°C. Bring a warm coat.
  • 🌧️Rain in spring and autumnMay has the most rain days, around 13. Carry a small umbrella.
  • ☀️Summer is fairly dryJuly and August see around 7–8 rain days, often short storms.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable walking shoes, layers, and a light rain jacket in spring and autumn.

Landmarks 10

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Two Towers

    Bologna's medieval skyline is defined by these two leaning brick towers, the taller Asinelli and the leaning Garisenda, and they are the city's most recognizable icon. Climb the Asinelli for sweeping views, but book ahead and go early to avoid queues and heat.

  2. 2

    Piazza Maggiore

    This vast central square is the heart of Bologna, ringed by the Basilica of San Petronio and the Palazzo del Podestà. Visit in the morning for the best light and fewer crowds, and linger in the evening when locals gather.

  3. 3

    Archiginnasio of Bologna

    The former university building holds the stunning anatomical theatre, a wood-panelled room that is one of Bologna's great interiors. It is a short walk from Piazza Maggiore; check opening hours as the theatre can close for events.

  4. 4

    Porticoes of Bologna

    Bologna's miles of covered arcades are a UNESCO-listed feature of the city and make walking pleasant in any weather. Wander them on foot through the old town, and use them to reach San Luca on the long porticoed climb.

  5. 5

    San Luca Sanctuary

    This hilltop sanctuary sits at the end of the world's longest portico, a rewarding walk with views over the city. Take the walk or the San Luca Express from the centre, and allow a half day for the round trip.

  6. 6

    Basilica of San Petronio

    The unfinished facade and vast interior of Bologna's main church dominate Piazza Maggiore. Entry to the church is free but modest dress is required; a small ticket gives access to the terrace and museum.

  7. 7

    Santo Stefano

    This complex of seven interconnected churches is one of Bologna's most atmospheric religious sites. It is a quiet, free stop near the Two Towers, best visited on a weekday morning.

  8. 8

    University of Bologna

    Founded in 1088, it is the oldest university in continuous operation and shapes the city's lively student character. Walk the central streets around it and look for the historic seats and courtyards.

    Swap in instead

    • Day trip to Modena by train for balsamic and the food market; check times on the day.
    • Swap a museum morning for a walk up to San Luca under the long portico; check opening hours.
    • Take a train to Ferrara or Parma for a half-day of Renaissance streets and food.

    Getting around

    • No metro yet; a tram network is under construction.
    • TPER buses cover the city; single ticket €2.30, valid 75 min, €2.50 on board.
    • Day pass costs about €9; buy tickets at tabacchi or newsstands.
    • Taxis are metered; ride apps like Uber are limited, local apps work better.
    • Driving in the centre is restricted; cars are banned in parts during the day.
    • Walking is easy and pleasant; bike hire is cheap, about €1-2 per 15 min.

    Where to stay

    • Historical Centre · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      Walk to Piazza Maggiore, the porticoes and most trattorias. Best for first-timers.
    • Bologna Centrale · ≈ EUR 70–160 a night
      Next to the station for day trips to Florence or Modena; 15 minutes on foot to the centre.
    • University Quarter · ≈ EUR 65–150 a night
      Lively bars and cheap eats around Via Zamboni; good budget base, can be noisy at night.
    • Saragozza · ≈ EUR 70–170 a night
      Quieter residential area near the station and Porta Saragozza; good value, still walkable.

    Food & drink

    • Order tagliatelle al ragù, not spaghetti bolognese; locals eat tortellini in brodo too.
    • Try a plate of mortadella and crescentine (fried dough) at a casual osteria.
    • The Quadrilatero market streets near Piazza Maggiore are good for a cheap grazing lunch.
    • Book dinner ahead on weekends; popular trattorias fill up fast.
    • Aperitivo around 18:00–19:00 often comes with snacks; check the price before ordering.

    Tips

    1. Often called 'La Grassa' (The Fat One) because of its incredible food; authentic Tagliatelle al Ragù is mandatory
    2. Walk under the 40 kilometers of covered porticoes (a UNESCO World Heritage site)
    3. Climb the Asinelli Tower for incredible views of the red-roofed city
    4. Order 'Tortellini in Brodo' rather than 'Spaghetti Bolognese', which does not exist here
    5. A large student population keeps the nightlife vibrant and relatively affordable

      Souvenirs

      Traditional balsamic vinegar

      EUR 45–80100ml · shop prices

      A dark, syrupy vinegar made from cooked grape must and aged in wooden barrels. It is a specialty of the Emilia-Romagna region, where Bologna is the capital.

      Where: Tamburini, Via Caprarie
      Look for the Tradizionale DOP label, and choose the 12-year grade for the best quality.

      Parmigiano Reggiano

      EUR 10–22250-500g · estimate

      A hard, aged cheese with a protected DOP origin, made in Emilia-Romagna. Bologna's markets and delis are famous for selling it.

      Where: Tamburini or cheese shops in the Quadrilatero
      Check that it is vacuum-sealed and ask how to store it so it stays fresh on the trip home.
