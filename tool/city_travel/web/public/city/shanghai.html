Getting around

Metro for most trips: fast, cheap and English-signed.

  • ✓Metro ★Fast, cheap and English-signed; fares run about ¥3-9. Buy a Shanghai Public Transportation Card or use the metro app for tap-and-go.
  • ✓BusBuses cover gaps the metro misses; fares are about ¥2.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis are metered; expect ¥200-500 to Pudong Airport and about ¥60-100 to Hongqiao. DiDi works well; Uber does not operate in mainland China.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is not advised for visitors.
  • ✓WalkingWalking and shared bikes suit short trips.
  • ✓FerryFerries cross the Huangpu for about ¥2.
Recommended stay★ 2 nights (3 days)

Two nights suit you: Yuyuan, the Bund, Pudong, the former French Concession and major museums fill two sightseeing days, so a third night is optional.

Climate

Humid subtropical: hot, wet summers and cool, damp winters. Spring and autumn are the best times to walk.

  • 🌸Best time: April and OctoberHighs around 20–23°C with moderate rain. Ideal for the Bund and long walks.
  • 🥵Summer is hot and humidJuly and August hit 32°C with lows near 25–26°C. Plan indoor sights midday.
  • 🌧️Rain peaks in June to SeptemberUp to 18 rain days a month. Carry an umbrella and keep a backup indoor plan.
  • 🧥Winter is cold and dampJanuary highs near 9°C and lows near 2°C. A warm coat and layers help.
  • 💨Humidity year-roundSummers feel sticky; winters feel raw. Breathable clothes in summer, layers in winter.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable walking shoes, an umbrella, and layers. Add sunscreen and a hat for summer.

Landmarks 25

Must-see · 9

  1. 1

    The Bund

    Shanghai's iconic waterfront promenade, lined with historic colonial-era buildings facing the Pudong skyline across the Huangpu River. It is the single must-see for any first-time visitor. Go early in the morning or after dark when the skyline is lit; it is free and open all day, reachable by metro to East Nanjing Road.

  2. 2

    Yu Garden

    A classical Ming-dynasty garden with rockeries, pavilions and ponds in the old city. Pair it with the surrounding Yuyuan Bazaar for snacks and shopping. Arrive at opening to avoid crowds; small admission fee applies.

  3. 3

    Oriental Pearl Tower

    The futuristic tower that defined Pudong's skyline, with observation decks and a glass floor. Best visited on a clear day for views over the river and the Bund. Book tickets ahead and go near sunset to see both day and night views.

  4. 4

    Shanghai Tower

    China's tallest skyscraper, with a high-speed lift to an observation deck offering sweeping views of the city. Go on a clear day; tickets are sold on site and online, and late afternoon gives the best light.

  5. 5

    Nanjing Road

    Shanghai's famous pedestrian shopping street running from People's Square toward the Bund. It is a lively place to stroll, shop and people-watch. Visit in the evening when the neon lights are on; it is free and easy to reach by metro.

  6. 6

    People's Square

    The central civic square and transport hub, surrounded by the Shanghai Museum and Grand Theatre. A convenient starting point for exploring the city center. It is free and open; metro lines converge here.

  7. 7

    Shanghai Museum

    A leading museum of Chinese art and antiquities, including bronzes, ceramics and calligraphy. Admission is free but advance booking is often required. Allow a couple of hours; it sits right on People's Square.

  8. 8

    Xintiandi

    A restored shikumen lane district turned into a stylish pedestrian area of cafes, shops and restaurants. Good for an evening stroll and people-watching. It is free to wander and reachable by metro.

  9. 9

    Shanghai World Financial Center

    A distinctive bottle-opener-shaped skyscraper with an observation deck and a glass-floor walkway high above Pudong. Visit on a clear day; tickets are available on site and online.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a half-day in Suzhou by high-speed train for classical gardens and canals. Book tickets ahead; check times on the day.
    • Spend an evening on the Huangpu ferry (about ¥2) for a cheap river crossing and skyline views.
    • Visit Zhujiajiao water town for canals and old bridges. Go on a weekday if you can.

    Getting around

    • Metro is fast, cheap and English-signed; fares run about ¥3-9.
    • Buy a Shanghai Public Transportation Card or use the metro app for tap-and-go.
    • Buses cover gaps the metro misses; fares are about ¥2.
    • Taxis are metered; expect ¥200-500 to Pudong Airport and about ¥60-100 to Hongqiao.
    • DiDi works well for ride-hailing; Uber does not operate in mainland China.
    • Driving is not advised for visitors; walking and shared bikes suit short trips, and ferries cross the Huangpu for about ¥2.

    Where to stay

    • The Bund · ≈ CNY 600–1,800 a night
      Step out to the river skyline at night. Walk to Yu Garden and the old city. Metro lines nearby.
    • People's Square · ≈ CNY 400–1,100 a night
      Central and well connected. Walk to Nanjing Road shopping and the Shanghai Museum.
    • French Concession · ≈ CNY 450–1,300 a night
      Leafy streets, cafés and small shops. Good for slow evenings and food hunting.
    • Jing'an · ≈ CNY 500–1,400 a night
      Temple, malls and metro lines in one spot. Easy rides to most sights.

    Food & drink

    • Try xiaolongbao (soup dumplings) and shengjianbao (pan-fried pork buns). Popular spots get busy; go early or expect a queue.
    • Street snacks are cheap and good: scallion pancakes, jianbing and skewers. Prices vary by stall; check on the day.
    • Book a table for dinner in the French Concession or Jing'an. Many small restaurants fill up on weekends.
    • Breakfast locally: soy milk, youtiao (fried dough sticks) and congee from neighborhood shops.
    • Drink bottled or boiled water. Tap water is not for drinking.
