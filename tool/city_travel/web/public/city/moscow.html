Climate

Continental climate with cold winters and warm summers. Rain falls in every month, so pack a layer and an umbrella.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs of 17–24°C and long days. Ideal for walking, parks and the river.
  • 🥶Winter is long and coldDecember to February highs sit near -2 to -5°C. Wear a warm coat, hat, gloves and boots.
  • 🌧️Rain in every monthExpect 8–14 rain days monthly. July and December are wettest; August is driest.
  • 🧊Icy streets in winterPavements can be slippery from November to March. Choose shoes with grip.
  • 🌗Short winter daysDaylight is limited in December and January. Plan indoor sights and start early.
  • 🧳What to packSummer: light clothes and a rain jacket. Winter: heavy coat, thermals, hat, gloves and waterproof boots.

Landmarks 37

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Red Square

    The vast cobblestone heart of Moscow, ringed by the Kremlin, St. Basil's and GUM, and the first place every visitor heads. Come early morning or after dark to dodge the crowds and catch the buildings lit up. It's free and open around the clock; the nearest metro is Okhotny Ryad.

  2. 2

    Moscow Kremlin

    A walled city of cathedrals, palaces and armouries that has been the seat of Russian power for centuries. Buy tickets online and go on a weekday morning; the Armoury Chamber needs a separate timed ticket. Enter from the Alexander Garden side via metro Biblioteka imeni Lenina.

  3. 3

    St. Basil's Cathedral

    The candy-coloured onion domes on Red Square are Russia's most recognisable silhouette. Step inside to see the maze of chapels, and photograph it from the Zaryadye Park side at sunset. Tickets are sold at the door; modest dress is respectful.

  4. 4

    Lenin's Mausoleum

    A starkly modern tomb on Red Square holding the embalmed body of the Soviet founder. It's free but opens only a few short morning hours and closes on some weekdays, so check ahead; no bags, phones or photos inside, and expect a queue.

  5. 5

    Tretyakov Gallery

    The definitive collection of Russian painting, from medieval icons to the Blue Dancers and the great 19th-century masters. Allow at least half a day and book a timed ticket online to skip the line. Metro Tretyakovskaya is right outside.

  6. 6

    GUM

    The elegant 19th-century arcade facing the Kremlin, now a department store with a glass roof and historic cafés. It's a good rain shelter and a classic photo spot; go in the evening when the facade is illuminated. Metro Ploshchad Revolyutsii.

  7. 7

    Moscow Metro Stations

    Moscow's underground palaces — chandeliers, mosaics and marble — are a sight in themselves and the cheapest way to cross the city. Ride the circle line and stop at Komsomolskaya and Novoslobodskaya; avoid rush hour for photos. A single trip costs a few dollars.

  8. 8

    Bolshoi Theatre

    One of the world's great opera and ballet houses, with a gilded auditorium that alone justifies the visit. Book ballet or opera tickets well in advance online, or take a guided backstage tour if performances are sold out. Metro Teatralnaya.

  9. 9

    Cathedral of Christ the Saviour

    A colossal white-and-gold cathedral rebuilt in the 1990s, dominating the Moskva River bank near the Kremlin. Go up to the viewing gallery for river views and dress modestly; entry to the main church is free. Metro Kropotkinskaya.

  10. 10

    Novodevichy Convent

    A serene walled convent with red-and-white towers and a famous cemetery of Russian artists and leaders next door. It's a peaceful half-day away from the centre; combine it with a walk around the pond. Metro Sportivnaya.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for a country estate or monastery outside the centre; check opening days and transport first.
    • Art lovers can swap a museum for a gallery afternoon in the former factory districts.
    • In warm months, spend an evening on a river cruise or a walk in Gorky Park.

    Getting around

    • Metro is fast, cheap and runs about 05:30-01:00; stations are attractions in themselves.
    • Buses, trolleybuses and trams fill the gaps; a Troika card cuts fares.
    • Yandex Go is the main ride app; taxis are metered but agree the price first.
    • Driving in the centre is slow and parking is costly; use park-and-ride.
    • Central streets are walkable; bike lanes are limited and winter is icy.
    • No useful ferry service in the city.

    Where to stay

    • Tverskoy · ≈ RUB 6,000–18,000 a night
      Central and walkable. Close to Red Square, the Bolshoi and good restaurants. Metro everywhere.
    • Kitay-Gorod · ≈ RUB 5,500–15,000 a night
      Steps from Red Square and the Kremlin. Quiet lanes, easy metro links.
    • Arbat · ≈ RUB 5,000–13,000 a night
      Lively pedestrian street, cafés and shops. Walk to the Kremlin in about 20 minutes.
    • Khamovniki · ≈ RUB 5,500–16,000 a night
      Quieter, greener area near the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour and Gorky Park.

    Food & drink

    • Try Russian classics: borscht, pelmeni dumplings, blini and syrniki pancakes.
    • Look for stolovaya canteens for cheap, filling lunches; prices vary, check the day's menu.
    • Georgian food is everywhere and good: khachapuri bread and khinkali dumplings.
    • Coffee and pastry culture is strong in the centre; many cafés open early.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead for weekend evenings. Tipping of about 10% is common.

    Tips

    1. The Moscow Metro is not just a transit system but an underground museum — admire the station art
    2. Red Square and the Kremlin are essential, but book tickets for the Armory Chamber in advance
    3. Winters are extremely cold; ensure you have high-quality insulated clothing and boots
    4. The city is massive, so rely heavily on the Metro to bypass surface traffic
    5. English is not widely spoken, so having a translation app is highly recommended
