Getting around

Walk or take the free city tram in the centre; use the tram to Glenelg and trains for Port Adelaide.

  • ✓Metro ★Adelaide Metro runs trains, trams and buses with one tap-and-pay system. Trams and some city buses are free within the city centre. The tram runs to Glenelg.
  • ✓BusPart of the same Adelaide Metro tap-and-pay system. Some city buses are free in the centre.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis use the meter; airport pickups add a small surcharge. Uber and other ride apps operate across the city.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is easy but parking in the CBD costs money. Useful for the Barossa, McLaren Vale and the hills.
  • ✓Walking ★The city is flat and good for walking and cycling.
  • ✓TrainAdelaide Metro trains are part of the same tap-and-pay system. Useful for Port Adelaide and outer suburbs.

Climate

Mediterranean-style climate: hot, dry summers and cool, wetter winters. Rain is spread over the cooler months.

  • ☀️Best time: November to MarchWarm to hot days around 23–28°C and low rain. Ideal for beaches, the hills and outdoor dining.
  • 🌧️Wettest: June to AugustAbout 12–13 rain days a month and highs near 14–15°C. Pack a rain jacket and plan indoor stops.
  • 🌡️Cool wintersJuly lows drop to about 8°C. Mornings and evenings feel cold; bring layers.
  • 🏖️Beach weatherBest from December to March. By June to August the sea and air are cold for swimming.
  • 🍂Mild shoulder monthsApril, October and November are comfortable at about 21–23°C with moderate rain. Good for walking and wine regions.
  • 🧳What to packSummer: light clothes, hat and sunscreen. Winter: warm layers, a waterproof jacket and an umbrella.

Landmarks 10

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Glenelg Beach

    Adelaide's classic seaside escape, with a long sandy beach, a lively pier and a tram that runs straight from the city centre. It is the easiest place to combine a city break with a swim. Take the tram from Victoria Square; go on a warm afternoon and stay for sunset over the water.

  2. 2

    Adelaide Central Market

    A buzzing indoor market in the heart of the city, packed with fresh produce, cheese, coffee and small eateries. It is the best single place to taste local South Australian food. Visit on a weekday morning when it is busy but not crammed, and bring cash for small stalls.

  3. 3

    Adelaide Botanic Garden

    A large, free garden next to the city centre with glasshouses, a rose garden and shady paths. It is a peaceful must-see for anyone wanting a break from the streets. Enter from North Terrace and go early in the day to avoid the heat.

  4. 4

    Art Gallery of South Australia

    One of Australia's finest art museums, with strong Australian and Indigenous collections and a grand historic facade on North Terrace. Entry is free, making it an easy must-see. Check opening hours before you go, as it closes in the evening.

  5. 5

    Hahndorf

    A historic German-settled village in the Adelaide Hills, known for its main street of old buildings, bakeries and craft shops. It is a popular day trip from the city. It is easiest to reach by car or a organised tour; go on a weekday to avoid weekend crowds.

  6. 6

    National Wine Centre of Australia

    A modern wine museum and tasting venue at the edge of the Botanic Garden, introducing the wines of South Australia's regions. It is a good first stop for anyone planning to explore wine country. Book a tasting in advance and walk there from North Terrace.

  7. 7

    Coopers Brewery

    Australia's largest independent family-owned brewery, a landmark for beer lovers. Tours show the brewing process and end with tastings. Tours must be booked ahead and run on set days, so check times before travelling to the site.

  8. 8

    Adelaide Showgrounds

    A large events and amusement site best known for hosting the annual Royal Adelaide Show. It is worth checking what is on during your visit. It is a short tram or train ride from the city centre; buy tickets online to skip queues.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day in the Barossa Valley: cellar doors, big red wines and long lunches, about an hour's drive each way.
    • Take the ferry to Kangaroo Island for wildlife and coast, but it needs at least one overnight, not a day trip.
    • Swap a city afternoon for the Adelaide Hills: Hahndorf's German-style main street and lookout stops, about 30–40 minutes by car.

    Getting around

    • Adelaide Metro runs trains, trams and buses with one tap-and-pay system.
    • Trams and some city buses are free within the city centre.
    • Taxis use the meter; airport pickups add a small surcharge.
    • Uber and other ride apps operate across the city.
    • Driving is easy but parking in the CBD costs money.
    • The city is flat and good for walking and cycling.

    Where to stay

    • CBD · ≈ AUD 150–380 a night
      Walk to the Central Market, North Terrace museums and the free tram zone. Best base without a car.
    • North Adelaide · ≈ AUD 160–400 a night
      Quiet streets, pubs and cafés, a short walk or bus to the city and the Oval.
    • Glenelg · ≈ AUD 170–450 a night
      Beachfront stays with the tram running straight into the city.
    • Port Adelaide · ≈ AUD 120–300 a night
      Cheaper rooms near the docks, dolphin cruises and the railway museum; a train ride to the CBD.

    Food & drink

    • Start at Adelaide Central Market: graze on cheese, smallgoods, pastries and coffee. Many stalls close early afternoon, so go in the morning.
    • Try a pie floater or a bakery pie, and a Farmers Union iced coffee, a local habit.
    • The Barossa and McLaren Vale wine regions are day trips from the city. Book a tour or a driver if you plan to taste.
    • Laneways off Hindley Street and Peel Street have small bars and cheap eats. Book popular places ahead on weekends.
    • Seafood is best near the coast at Glenelg or Port Adelaide. Check the day's catch and prices on the board.

    Tips

    1. Often called the '20-minute city' because you can reach the beaches, the hills, or the airport in under 20 minutes from the CBD
    2. The Adelaide Central Market is one of the largest undercover fresh produce markets in the Southern Hemisphere
    3. Renting a car is highly recommended to explore the world-class Barossa Valley and McLaren Vale wine regions
    4. Take the tram to Glenelg for a beautiful beach sunset and relaxed dining
    5. The sun in summer is dangerously intense; wear a hat and apply high-SPF sunscreen rigorously

    Haigh's Chocolate Frogs

    AUD 18–35140–280g · estimate

    These chocolate frogs are made in South Australia, and Haigh's is one of Adelaide's best-known chocolate makers. They are a classic local treat to bring home.

    Where: Haigh's, Beehive Corner
    Ask for insulated packaging on hot days, because the chocolate can melt on the way home.

    South Australian Barossa Shiraz

    AUD 25–50750ml · estimate

    This red wine comes from the Barossa Valley, a famous wine region about an hour's drive from Adelaide. It is a good way to taste South Australia's wine culture.

    Where: National Wine Centre or East End Cellars
    Entry-level and everyday bottles are good value, so you can find a nice wine without a high budget.
