Climate

Tropical climate: hot most of the year, wetter in summer (Dec–Mar) and drier in winter (Jun–Aug).

  • ☀️Best months: May to SeptemberDrier and milder, with highs 25–27°C and fewer rain days. Good for both beach and city walks.
  • 🌧️Wet season: December to March15–16 rain days a month. Showers are often short and heavy; carry a small umbrella.
  • 🥵Hot summerHighs around 29–30°C with warm nights near 24°C. Pace yourself and drink water.
  • 🧥Cool evenings in winterJune to August lows drop to 18–19°C. A light jacket helps at night and on the water.
  • 🏖️Beach daysBeaches are busiest on hot weekends. Go early morning for space and softer sun.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, swimwear, sunscreen and a rain jacket. Add a light layer for winter evenings.

Landmarks 29

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Christ the Redeemer

    Rio's defining icon, a colossal Art Deco statue atop Corcovado with sweeping views over the city, bay and mountains. Go early in the morning for clearer skies and fewer crowds, and book official tickets online in advance; the usual access is by the Corcovado train or van from Cosme Velho.

  2. 2

    Sugarloaf Mountain

    The second postcard sight, reached by a two-stage cable car with panoramic views of Guanabara Bay and the beaches. Late afternoon is best so you catch sunset, and buying tickets online saves queueing time.

  3. 3

    Copacabana Beach

    The world-famous crescent of sand and wave-patterned promenade, essential for the classic Rio beach experience. Mornings are calmer for swimming and walking; keep valuables minimal and watch your belongings on busy days.

  4. 4

    Ipanema Beach

    Rio's stylish beach, backed by the Dois Irmãos peaks and known for its lively postos (lifeguard stations) that each draw a different crowd. Weekday mornings are quieter; bring cash for beach vendors and avoid bringing valuables.

  5. 5

    Maracanã

    One of the world's legendary football stadiums and a pilgrimage for fans. Check the schedule for a match or take a stadium tour; match days are electric but book tickets well ahead.

  6. 6

    Metropolitan Cathedral of Saint Sebastian

    A striking modern cathedral shaped like a Mayan pyramid, with soaring stained glass inside. It is in central Rio, easy to combine with Lapa and the Lapa Arches; entry is free and it is open daily.

  7. 7

    Escadaria Selarón

    The colorful tiled staircase in Lapa, a beloved photo spot and symbol of the city's bohemian spirit. Visit early to avoid crowds, and combine it with a walk through Lapa's nightlife streets.

    • Spend a morning in Tijuca forest or a half-day hike to Pedra Bonita for views.: Go early; weather can change fast.

    Getting around

    • Metro lines 1 and 2 are fast and safe; fares around R$4.70.
    • City buses are cheap (from R$4.70) but crowded; watch your belongings.
    • Taxis are metered; ride apps like Uber and 99 work well.
    • Driving is confusing with poor signage; parking is hard.
    • Walking is good in beach areas; bikes are popular along the coast.
    • Ferries (barcas) link Centro's Praça XV to Niterói.

    Where to stay

    • Copacabana · ≈ BRL 250–700 a night
      Classic beach base. Metro stations along the avenue, plenty of cheap eats and easy walks on the sand.
    • Ipanema · ≈ BRL 400–1,100 a night
      Nicer beach and better restaurants. Good for couples and first-time visitors who want to walk everywhere.
    • Botafogo · ≈ BRL 250–650 a night
      Central and well connected by metro. Close to Sugarloaf, Centro and nightlife in Lapa by taxi.
    • Centro · ≈ BRL 200–550 a night
      Cheapest and closest to museums and the ferry to Niterói. Quiet at night and weekends; check safety after dark.

    Food & drink

    • Try a feijoada lunch (black bean stew with pork) at a neighbourhood restaurant; portions are big, so share.: R$40–80
    • Eat at a kilo buffet (por quilo) for lunch: pay by weight and try many Brazilian dishes cheaply.: R$30–60
    • Grab a pão de queijo and a fresh juice from a corner padaria for breakfast.: R$10–20
    • Churrasco (grilled meat) rodízio restaurants are filling and popular; go hungry.: R$60–120
    • Beach vendors sell snacks and drinks. Carry small cash and keep your phone out of sight.: R$5–20

    Tips

    1. Do not bring valuables or flash your smartphone on the beach to avoid petty theft
    2. Use the Metro to easily reach Copacabana and Ipanema
    3. Take the bondinho (cable car) up Sugarloaf Mountain around sunset for the best views
    4. Entering favelas without a trusted local guide is highly discouraged due to safety risks
    5. Drink an ice-cold caipirinha or a mate (sweet iced tea) sold by beach vendors
