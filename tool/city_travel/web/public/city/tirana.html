Getting around

Walk the compact centre; use taxis or ride apps for longer hops and buses to save money.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Don't plan around rail inside the city.
  • ✓BusCity buses are the main public transport. A single ticket costs 40 lek, sold on board by a conductor.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Flag-fall 300 lek; a cross-town ride is 400–800 lek. Demand the meter. Apps like Speed Taxi, Patoko, Clust and VrapOn work like Uber.
  • ✓Rental carUseful for day trips outside the city. Traffic in the centre is busy; check parking at your hotel.
  • ✓Walking ★Best way to see the compact centre. Sidewalks can be uneven, so watch your step.
  • ✓BikeBike lanes exist on some main streets, but cycling can be risky due to traffic.

Climate

Mediterranean with hot, dry summers and cool, wet winters. Spring and autumn are the sweet spots.

  • ☀️Best time: May to June and September to OctoberHighs of 22–28°C and fewer rainy days. Ideal for walking the centre and day trips.
  • 🥵July and August are hotHighs around 32°C and lows near 20°C. Do sights in the morning, rest at midday, go out in the evening.
  • 🌧️Wettest in autumn and early springMay and November see about 11–14 rain days. Showers are often short; carry a small umbrella.
  • 🧥Cool wintersDecember to February highs of 11–14°C and lows near 2–4°C. Pack layers and a warm coat.
  • 🌂Rain is spread across the yearNo month is fully dry. Even summer has a few wet days, so a light rain jacket helps.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable shoes for uneven sidewalks, layers for cool evenings, sunscreen for summer, and an umbrella in spring and autumn.

Landmarks 10

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Skanderbeg Square

    Tirana's vast central plaza is the heart of the city and the natural starting point for any visit, ringed by the mosque, clock tower and national museum. Come early morning or after sunset when the lights come on and the square is at its most atmospheric; it is free and open at all hours.

  2. 2

    Enver Hoxha Pyramid

    This striking concrete pyramid, built as a monument to the communist dictator, is Tirana's most talked-about piece of brutalist heritage and a favorite spot for photos and views. It sits a short walk from Skanderbeg Square; go in the late afternoon for softer light and fewer people.

  3. 3

    Bunk'Art 1 Museum

    A sprawling Cold War bunker turned museum that gives a gripping look at Albania's communist past through its tunnels and rooms. It is a few kilometers from the center, so take a taxi or local bus; allow at least an hour and check opening hours before you go.

  4. 4

    National Historical Museum

    The country's main museum covers Albanian history from antiquity through the communist era, with the famous mosaic on its facade. It faces Skanderbeg Square, making it easy to combine with the square and mosque; a couple of hours is enough for the highlights.

  5. 5

    Ethem Bey Mosque

    A graceful 18th-century mosque right on Skanderbeg Square, known for its painted interior and peaceful courtyard. It is an active place of worship, so dress modestly and avoid visiting during prayer times; entry is free.

  6. 6

    Tirana Clock Tower

    The slender Ottoman-era clock tower next to the mosque is one of the city's best-loved symbols and offers a quick climb for a view over the square. It is steps from Skanderbeg Square; go on a clear day and watch your footing on the narrow stairs.

  7. 7

    Blloku district

    Once the sealed-off neighborhood for communist elites, Blloku is now Tirana's liveliest area for cafes, bars and evening strolls. It is a short walk south of the center; come in the evening when the streets fill up.

  8. 8

    Resurrection of Christ Orthodox Cathedral

    A modern Orthodox cathedral with a distinctive domed and arched design, worth a look for its frescoed interior. It is near the center and free to enter; dress respectfully and keep quiet during services.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Krujë: buses run from the north terminal, about an hour each way, for the castle and old bazaar. Check times on the day.
    • Add a day trip to Berat or Gjirokastër by bus or hired car; both are long day trips, so an overnight is better.
    • Ride the Dajti Ekspres cable car from the east edge of the city for views over Tirana; check opening hours on the day.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; city buses are the main public transport.
    • Single bus ticket costs 40 lek, sold on board by a conductor.
    • Taxis: flag-fall 300 lek, cross-town ride 400–800 lek; demand the meter.
    • Ride apps: Speed Taxi, Patoko, Clust, VrapOn Taxi Albania work like Uber.
    • Walking is best in the compact centre; sidewalks can be uneven.
    • Bike lanes exist on some main streets; cycling can be risky due to traffic.

    Where to stay

    • Skanderbeg Square · ≈ ALL 6,000–14,000 a night
      Walk to the main sights, museums and the airport bus; best for a first short stay.
    • Blloku · ≈ ALL 7,000–16,000 a night
      Bars, restaurants and cafés on your doorstep; lively at night, a short walk to the centre.
    • Rruga e Kavajës · ≈ ALL 5,000–11,000 a night
      Quieter street with mid-range hotels; 10–15 minutes on foot to the square.
    • Komuna e Parisit · ≈ ALL 4,500–10,000 a night
      Local, cheaper and near the big park and cable car; use buses or taxis to the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Start the day with byrek (flaky pastry with cheese or spinach) and a strong coffee, roughly 100–200 lek.
    • Try tavë kosi (baked lamb with yogurt) and fërgesë (peppers, tomato and cheese) at a traditional restaurant.
    • Blloku has the densest cluster of cafés and restaurants; walk a block off the main street for better prices.
    • Cheap eats: grilled meat, suflaqe (wraps) and bakery snacks for about 300–600 lek a meal.
    • Coffee culture is serious here. A macchiato at a local bar is often under 100 lek; sit and linger.

    Tips

    1. The city is famous for its vibrant, colorful painted buildings, a project to boost civic morale
    2. Visit Bunk'Art, a massive anti-nuclear bunker converted into an art and history museum
    3. Take the Dajti Ekspres cable car up the mountain for incredible city views
    4. Coffee culture is huge; spend hours at local cafes
    5. The city is very affordable compared to neighboring Western European capitals
