Getting around

Trains and subways with an IC card; taxis only for late nights.

  • ✓Metro ★JR, Tokyo Metro and Toei reach everywhere. Tap a Suica or Pasmo IC card, about ¥180–330 a ride.
  • ✓Train ★Same network as the metro for visitors; use it between neighbourhoods. Trains stop around midnight.
  • ✓BusBuses exist but trains are almost always faster for visitors.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsClean and metered, ¥500 start. Apps like GO and Uber work. Best after the trains stop.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in central Tokyo is slow and parking expensive; not recommended.
  • ✓WalkingEach neighbourhood is walkable, but you will take trains between them.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cold dry winters, a humid rainy season in June, and hot, sticky summers.

  • 🌸Best time: March to May and October to NovemberMild days around 17–23°C. Ideal for walking neighbourhoods and day trips.
  • 🥵Summer is hot and humid (July to September)Highs near 30–31°C with muggy nights. Plan indoor sights midday and go out early or late.
  • 🌧️Rainy season in JuneAbout 15 rain days and 26°C. Carry an umbrella and keep a museum or shopping backup.
  • ❄️Cold, dry wintersJanuary highs near 9°C and lows around 0°C. A warm coat and layers matter; rain is light.
  • 🌀Typhoon seasonLate summer and September can bring storms. Check forecasts and train status on the day.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable walking shoes, layers for cool evenings, an umbrella in June and September, and light breathable clothes in summer.

Landmarks 65

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Senso-ji

    Tokyo's oldest temple in Asakusa, entered through the Kaminarimon gate and the Nakamise shopping street. Free and open early; go before 9 am or after dark to avoid the crowds.

  2. 2

    Shibuya Crossing

    The world's busiest scramble crossing. Cross it yourself, then watch from Shibuya Sky or the Starbucks above; evenings are the most dramatic.

  3. 3

    Meiji Jingu

    A Shinto shrine in a 70-hectare forest right next to Harajuku. Free; mornings are calm, and you may see a traditional wedding procession on weekends.

  4. 4

    Tokyo Skytree

    At 634 m the tallest tower in Japan, with decks at 350 m and 450 m. On clear winter mornings you can see Mount Fuji; book a time slot online.

  5. 5

    Tokyo Tower

    The red-and-white 1958 tower, a retro icon. Best photographed from Shiba Park or Zojo-ji temple at dusk.

  6. 6

    Imperial Palace

    The Emperor's residence on the site of Edo Castle. The East Gardens are free to walk (closed Mon and Fri); the Nijubashi bridge is the classic photo.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day trip to Nikko or Kamakura by train; both are easy returns from central stations. Check times on the day.
    • Swap one city day for teamLab or a sumo morning session (tickets sell out; check the schedule).
    • Anime fans can swap a shopping afternoon for Nakano Broadway and a themed café; book popular cafés ahead.

    Getting around

    • Trains and subways (JR, Tokyo Metro, Toei) reach everywhere; tap a Suica or Pasmo IC card, about ¥180-330 a ride.
    • Buses exist but trains are almost always faster for visitors.
    • Taxis are clean and metered (¥500 start); apps like GO and Uber work.
    • Driving in central Tokyo is slow and parking expensive; not recommended.
    • Each neighbourhood is walkable, but you will take trains between them.
    • Trains stop around midnight; plan late nights or take a taxi.

    Where to stay

    • Shinjuku · ≈ JPY 9,000–30,000 a night
      Biggest train hub, endless food and nightlife, easy day trips west. Busy and bright at night.
    • Shibuya · ≈ JPY 10,000–35,000 a night
      Central for youth culture, shopping and nightlife. Good links to Shinjuku and the west side.
    • Ginza & Tokyo Station · ≈ JPY 14,000–45,000 a night
      Quiet, polished and walkable; best for Shinkansen day trips and first-time comfort.
    • Asakusa & Ueno · ≈ JPY 7,000–22,000 a night
      Cheapest rooms, old-town feel, Senso-ji on the doorstep and good airport links.

    Food & drink

    • Try sushi at a conveyor-belt shop for a cheap first taste; sit-down counters cost much more. Check prices before you sit.
    • Ramen and standing soba shops are the cheap lunch: expect roughly ¥800–1,300 a bowl.
    • Izakaya alleys (Omoide Yokocho in Shinjuku, Harmonica Yokocho in Kichijoji) are best for small plates and a beer.
    • Convenience stores (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) are good, cheap breakfasts and picnic food.
    • Many small restaurants are cash-only and some are ticket-machine only. Carry some yen and have a translation app ready.

    Tips

    1. IC cards (Suica/Pasmo) work on all trains, buses, and even at convenience stores.
    2. Convenience stores (7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson) serve genuinely good food — don't overlook them.
    3. Tipping is not practiced in Japan and can be considered rude.
    4. Coin lockers are widely available at major stations — great for storing luggage while exploring.
    5. Many restaurants have plastic food displays or vending machine ordering — easy for non-Japanese speakers.
