Getting around

Ride apps (PassApp, Grab, TADA) for fixed prices; city buses if you are on a tight budget.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Do not plan around rail in the city.
  • ✓Bus13 city bus lines run about 05:30–20:30. Fare is 1,500 riel; drivers give no change, so pay exact.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★PassApp, Grab and TADA show fixed prices, so no haggling. Tuk-tuks cost about US$2–3 in town and US$8 to the airport; agree the fare first if you hail one.
  • ✓Tuk-tuk ★The easy way around town: about US$2–3 a ride. Always agree the fare before you get in.
  • ✗Rental carNot recommended for visitors. Motorbikes rent for US$5–6 a day, but traffic is chaotic and theft is common; park in guarded areas.
  • ✓WalkingFine for short blocks near the riverside and palace, but heat and traffic make longer walks unpleasant. Bicycles and cyclos are slow but scenic; bike hire is US$1–3 a day.

Climate

Tropical and hot all year, with a dry season roughly November to April and a wet season May to October.

  • ☀️Best time: November to FebruaryHighs 31–33°C and few rain days. The most comfortable months for walking and temples.
  • 🥵Hottest in March and AprilHighs reach 34–35°C. Sightsee before 10am and rest in air-conditioning at midday.
  • 🌧️Wet season May to OctoberRain on 24–28 days a month, usually as heavy afternoon showers. Mornings are often clear.
  • 💧Humid year-roundLows stay 23–26°C, so nights are warm and sticky. Light, breathable clothes help.
  • 🌊Flooding in the peak rainsSeptember and October can flood streets. Wear sandals and allow extra time for tuk-tuks.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, a hat, sunscreen and insect repellent; a rain jacket or umbrella for May to October; modest dress for temples.

Landmarks 13

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Royal Palace of Cambodia

    The official residence of the King and the city's most iconic sight, with the Silver Pagoda inside its walls. Go early in the morning to avoid heat and crowds; tickets are around $10 and shoulders and knees must be covered.

  2. 2

    Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum

    A former school turned prison and interrogation center under the Khmer Rouge, now a sobering museum. Visit with a guide or audio tour for context; allow 1-2 hours and go early to avoid the heat.

  3. 3

    Killing Field

    The Choeung Ek memorial site, where thousands were executed, is a deeply moving place with a stupa of skulls. It is about 30 minutes by tuk-tuk from the center; combine with Tuol Sleng for a full understanding of Cambodia's recent history.

  4. 4

    Silver Pagoda

    Located within the Royal Palace grounds, this temple houses a floor of silver tiles and a revered emerald Buddha. Dress respectfully (no shorts or sleeveless tops) and visit in the morning when it is cooler.

  5. 5

    Wat Phnom

    The city's namesake hilltop temple, a peaceful spot with good views and a lively atmosphere with monkeys and locals. It is a short walk from the riverside; go early morning or late afternoon.

  6. 6

    National Museum of Cambodia

    Houses the world's finest collection of Khmer art, including sculptures from Angkor. The building itself is a beautiful example of Khmer architecture; a guide or audio tour is recommended.

  7. 7

    Mekong River

    The mighty Mekong is central to Phnom Penh's identity, with lively riverfront promenades and sunset cruises. Take a boat trip at sunset for great views of the city skyline.

  8. 8

    Independence Monument of Cambodia

    A striking monument in the heart of the city, especially beautiful when illuminated at night. It is a short walk from the Royal Palace and a good spot for photos.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a half-day to the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum and the Killing Fields of Choeung Ek; go early, it is emotional and hot.
    • Take a slow boat or bus to Siem Reap for Angkor Wat if you have two spare days; check times and tickets on the day.
    • Cross the river to Areyksat for a quiet half-day of village lanes and river views, a short ferry ride from the city.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; the city bus network has 13 lines running about 05:30-20:30.
    • City bus fare is 1,500 riel per ride; drivers give no change, so pay exact.
    • Tuk-tuks cost about US$2-3 in town and US$8 to the airport; agree the fare first.
    • Ride apps PassApp, Grab and TADA work well and show fixed prices, so no haggling.
    • Motorbikes rent for US$5-6 a day; traffic is chaotic and theft is common, so park in guarded areas.
    • Cyclos and bicycles are slow but scenic; bike hire is US$1-3 a day.

    Where to stay

    • Riverside (Sisowath Quay) · ≈ KHR 60,000–350,000 a night
      Walk to the Royal Palace, night market and riverside bars; most tuk-tuks and restaurants are on your doorstep.
    • Royal Palace area · ≈ KHR 80,000–400,000 a night
      Quiet streets right by the main sights and the National Museum; good for early starts before the heat.
    • BKK1 · ≈ KHR 100,000–500,000 a night
      Modern cafés, embassies and mid-range hotels; a short ride from the sights and the best food street in town.
    • Tonle Bassac · ≈ KHR 120,000–600,000 a night
      Newer high-rises and malls near the Independence Monument; calm nights and easy ride-app pickups.

    Food & drink

    • Start the day with kuy teav (rice-noodle soup) or bai sach chrouk (grilled pork and rice) at a street stall; expect a few thousand riel.
    • Try fish amok (coconut curry steamed in a banana leaf) and lok lak (stir-fried beef with lime-pepper dip) at a mid-range Khmer restaurant.
    • Eat at the evening food stalls and night market near the riverside; point at what looks busy and pay in small riel notes.
    • Drink only bottled or filtered water, and be careful with ice from street carts; hotel and restaurant ice is usually fine.
    • Vegetarian and halal options are easy to find around BKK1 and the riverside; ask before ordering fish sauce or prahok.

    Tips

    1. PassApp is the most popular, transparent, and cheap way to hire tuk-tuks
    2. Visit the S-21 Genocide Museum and the Killing Fields for essential, sobering history
    3. Dress modestly when visiting the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda
    4. The riverside area (Sisowath Quay) is the center for dining, bars, and tourism
    5. Carry small denominations of US dollars, which are widely accepted and used alongside the Riel
