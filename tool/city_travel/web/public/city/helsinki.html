Getting around

Trams and walking in the centre; one HSL ticket covers metro, buses, trains and the Suomenlinna ferry.

  • ✓MetroRuns on the HSL ticket system; useful for east-west trips and to the main station.
  • ✓Tram ★Trams cover the centre well on the same HSL ticket. Buy by contactless card, HSL app or R kiosk; cash is not accepted on board.
  • ✓BusBuses run on the HSL ticket system and reach areas trams don't. Same ticket rules apply.
  • ✓TrainLocal trains are part of HSL. Airport trains need an ABC ticket; city centre travel needs AB.
  • ✓FerryThe Suomenlinna ferry is included in the HSL ticket system.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis wait at ranks and the airport; Bolt and Uber operate in Helsinki.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is easy but parking is costly. Walking and trams cover the centre well, so a car is rarely needed.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is compact and walkable. Bikes are popular in summer, with city bike stations widespread.

Climate

Northern city with cold winters and mild, bright summers. Rain falls year-round, so pack layers.

  • ☀️Best time: June to AugustHighs around 19–21°C with long daylight. Ideal for ferries, islands and walking.
  • 🥶Winter is cold (Dec–Feb)Highs near -1 to 1°C and lows down to -6°C. Snow and ice are possible; wear warm, grippy shoes.
  • 🌧️Rain in every monthExpect roughly 9–14 rain days a month. A light waterproof layer is worth packing all year.
  • 🌱Spring is slowMarch stays near freezing; April warms to about 7°C. Bring a jacket even on sunny days.
  • 🍂Autumn cools fastSeptember around 15°C, October about 9°C. Layers and waterproof shoes help.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a rain jacket and comfortable walking shoes. Add a warm hat and gloves from November to March.

Landmarks 15

Must-see · 4

  1. 1

    Helsinki Cathedral

    The white neoclassical cathedral dominates Senate Square and is the city's most recognizable symbol. Climb the steps for views over the harbor; entry is free, but dress modestly and avoid visiting during services.

  2. 2

    Suomenlinna

    This UNESCO-listed sea fortress on a cluster of islands is a must-see for its history, walls, and coastal walks. Take the 15-minute public ferry from Market Square (HSL ticket valid); go on a weekday morning to avoid crowds.

  3. 3

    Temppeliaukio Church

    A unique church carved into solid rock with a copper dome, famous for its acoustics and architecture. There is a small entry fee; visit early or late to dodge tour groups, and check for concert schedules.

  4. 4

    Uspenski Cathedral

    The red-brick Eastern Orthodox cathedral offers a striking contrast to its Lutheran counterpart and fine harbor views. Entry is free; dress respectfully (shoulders and knees covered) and note it may close for services.

    • In summer, hop a ferry to an island like Pihlajasaari for a swim and a walk.

    Getting around

    • HSL runs metro, trams, buses, local trains and the Suomenlinna ferry on one ticket system.
    • Buy tickets by contactless card, HSL app or R kiosk; cash is not accepted on board.
    • Airport trains need an ABC ticket; city centre travel needs AB.
    • Taxis wait at ranks and the airport; ride apps like Bolt and Uber operate in Helsinki.
    • Driving is easy but parking is costly; walking and trams cover the centre well.
    • Bikes are popular in summer; city bike stations are widespread.

    Where to stay

    • Kluuvi · ≈ EUR 120–260 a night
      Central and walkable: main station, trams and the big sights are minutes away.
    • Kruununhaka · ≈ EUR 130–280 a night
      Quiet old streets near the cathedral and Market Square; good for first-time sightseeing.
    • Kallio · ≈ EUR 90–200 a night
      Livelier and cheaper, with bars and cafés; a short tram or metro ride to the centre.
    • Kamppi · ≈ EUR 120–270 a night
      Bus and metro hub under one roof; handy for day trips and late arrivals.

    Food & drink

    • Try Finnish basics: salmon soup (lohikeitto), rye bread and Karelian pasties.
    • Market Square (Kauppatori) stalls sell snacks and berries in season; check opening hours on the day.
    • Lunch is often cheaper than dinner; look for weekday lunch offers in the centre.
    • Cafés serve cinnamon buns (korvapuusti) and strong filter coffee; Finns drink a lot of it.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead on weekends; many kitchens close earlier than in bigger cities.

    Tips

    1. Going to a public sauna (like Löyly) and jumping into the freezing Baltic Sea is a quintessential Finnish experience
    2. The city is incredibly safe, clean, and runs with high efficiency
    3. English is spoken fluently by almost everyone
    4. Winters are extremely dark with only a few hours of daylight, while midsummer offers nearly 24 hours of light
    5. Tap water is among the cleanest and best-tasting in the world
