Climate

Hot desert climate: sunny most days, almost no rain, and a very hot summer from June to September.

  • ☀️Best time: November to MarchDays around 19–27°C and cool nights. Ideal for the Pyramids and long walks.
  • 🥵Summer is extreme (June to September)Highs of 36–38°C. Do outdoor sights at sunrise; stay indoors at midday.
  • 🌂Almost no rainOnly about 1–2 rain days a month in winter. No umbrella needed most of the year.
  • 🌪️Dust and hazeSpring can bring dusty winds that blur the skyline. Check the air before desert trips.
  • 🌙Cool evenings in winterNights drop near 9–11°C from December to February. A jacket helps after dark.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, a hat and sunscreen; a warm layer for winter nights; modest dress for mosques.

Landmarks 45

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Pyramids of Giza

    The last surviving wonder of the ancient world and the reason many people come to Egypt. Go early in the morning to beat the heat and crowds, and wear comfortable shoes for the sand and stone.

  2. 2

    Great Sphinx of Giza

    The iconic limestone statue with a human head and lion body, guarding the Giza plateau. It is included with the Giza site ticket; visit at opening time for the best light and fewer people.

  3. 3

    Egyptian Museum

    Home to a vast collection of ancient Egyptian artifacts, including royal mummies and treasures. Allow at least two hours; tickets are sold at the entrance and photography may require an extra fee.

  4. 4

    Cairo Citadel

    A medieval Islamic fortification offering panoramic views over Cairo. Wear modest clothing as it contains active mosques, and go in the late afternoon for cooler weather.

  5. 5

    Mosque of Muhammad Ali

    The striking alabaster mosque inside the Citadel, a symbol of Ottoman Cairo. Remove your shoes before entering and dress conservatively; it is busiest around midday prayer.

  6. 6

    Khan el-Khalili

    A historic bazaar full of shops, cafés and narrow alleys, perfect for souvenirs and people-watching. Visit in the evening when it is liveliest, and be ready to bargain.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for Islamic Cairo: the Citadel, Sultan Hassan Mosque and Khan el-Khalili bazaar.
    • Swap a city day for Saqqara and Dahshur to see older pyramids with fewer crowds.
    • Take a short Nile dinner cruise or a felucca ride at sunset instead of an evening indoors.

    Getting around

    • Metro: three lines, clean and cheap; tickets EGP8-20 by distance.
    • Buses: red-white-blue city buses EGP4-6; air-conditioned EGP7-12; minibuses EGP5-15.
    • Taxis: white cabs; meters often unused, agree or pay the fair sum on exit.
    • Ride apps: Uber and Careem work well in Cairo; often easier than street taxis.
    • Driving: not advised for visitors; traffic is heavy and rules are loose.
    • Walking: fine in some districts but crossings and pavements are poor; little cycling.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown · ≈ EGP 900–3,500 a night
      Central for the Egyptian Museum, metro and cheap eats. Lively and noisy; good value.
    • Zamalek · ≈ EGP 2,000–7,000 a night
      Quiet island district with cafés and embassies. Calmer evenings, easy taxis to most sights.
    • Giza · ≈ EGP 1,200–6,000 a night
      Closest to the Pyramids and the Grand Egyptian Museum. Far from Downtown sights.
    • Heliopolis · ≈ EGP 1,000–4,000 a night
      Near the airport and quiet streets. Best for early flights or a last night.

    Food & drink

    • Try koshari: rice, lentils and pasta with tomato sauce. Cheap and filling, often under EGP 100.
    • Street snacks: ta'ameya (herb falafel), ful medames and fresh baladi bread. Look for busy stalls.
    • Grilled meats and pigeon are Cairo classics. Ask for the price before ordering at tourist-area restaurants.
    • Sweet tooth? Try basbousa and kunafa from a local sweet shop, sold by weight.
    • Tap water is not for visitors; drink bottled water and be careful with raw salads at cheap places.

    Tips

    1. Uber or Careem are highly recommended over hailing street taxis to avoid haggling
    2. Dress modestly, especially when visiting mosques and older neighborhoods
    3. Crossing the street is an art; walk at a steady pace and let cars flow around you
    4. Haggling in the Khan el-Khalili bazaar is expected and part of the cultural experience
    5. Avoid the extreme summer heat by scheduling outdoor sightseeing for early mornings
