Getting around

Tuk-tuks and ride apps for most trips; buses are cheap but slow.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Don't plan around rail inside the city.
  • ✓BusBuses are the main public transport. Fares start at Rs 30, pay the conductor onboard.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★PickMe and Uber both work well. Tuk-tuks are everywhere; agree a price or use a meter taxi.
  • ✗Rental carNot covered in our checked facts. Most visitors use tuk-tuks, apps and buses instead.
  • ✓WalkingWorks in Fort and Pettah, but pavements are uneven. Use a tuk-tuk for longer hops.
  • ✓TrainCommuter trains run on four lines, including the coastal line to Mount Lavinia.
  • ✓Tuk-tuk ★Everywhere and easy to flag. Agree the price first or ask for the meter.

Climate

Tropical and hot all year, with a long wet season from April to October and drier, sunnier months from December to February.

  • ☀️Best time: December to FebruaryWarm days around 30–31°C and the fewest rain days, about 11 a month. Best for walking and the beach.
  • 🌧️Wet season: April to OctoberRain on 25–30 days a month, heaviest in May. Expect short heavy downpours, often in the afternoon.
  • 🌡️Hot and humid year-roundHighs stay near 28–31°C and lows near 23–25°C. Humidity makes it feel hotter.
  • 🌂Always carry rain coverEven in dry months a shower is possible. A small umbrella or light rain jacket helps.
  • 👕What to packLight breathable clothes, sandals, sunscreen and a hat. A light layer for air-conditioned places.
  • 🕌Dress for templesCover shoulders and knees at religious sites. Remove shoes where asked.

Landmarks 12

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Gangaramaya Temple

    Colombo's best-known Buddhist temple, a mix of worship hall, museum and library packed with statues, relics and gifts from around the world. It sits by Beira Lake, so you can combine both in one walk. Dress modestly with shoulders and knees covered, and go in the morning to avoid the heat.

  2. 2

    Lotus Tower

    A striking lotus-shaped tower and the city's most visible modern landmark, with an observation deck giving wide views over Colombo and the coast. A good first stop to get your bearings. Go near sunset for the best light, and check opening hours before you set out.

  3. 3

    Galle Face Green

    The city's seafront promenade, where locals walk, fly kites and eat snacks by the Indian Ocean at dusk. It is free and open to all, and the best time is late afternoon into sunset. It is a short walk from the Fort and the Old Parliament Building.

  4. 4

    Pettah Market

    A dense, busy bazaar district that shows Colombo at its most energetic, with spices, textiles and everyday goods spilling into the streets. Come with patience and keep valuables close. Mornings are calmer, and the Red Mosque is nearby.

  5. 5

    Red Mosque

    A vividly painted mosque in the Pettah area, famous for its striking red-and-white facade. It is an active place of worship, so dress modestly and check whether visitors are allowed at the time. It pairs naturally with a walk through Pettah Market.

  6. 6

    Independence Memorial Hall

    A memorial hall marking Sri Lanka's independence, set in a calm park with a distinctive Kandyan-style roof. It is a quick, worthwhile stop for modern history and photos. Visit in the cooler morning or late afternoon.

  7. 7

    National Museum of Colombo

    The country's main museum, holding art, artefacts and royal regalia that give context to Sri Lankan history. Allow an hour or two. It is close to Viharamahadevi Park, so the two make an easy half-day.

  8. 8

    Viharamahadevi Park

    Colombo's large central park, with shade, lawns and a relaxed local atmosphere. A good place to rest between sights. It is next to the National Museum and the Town Hall area.

    ★★★★★ · Full day · 114 km from Colombo

    Take a direct train from Colombo Fort (about 3 hours through the hill country) and then a tuk-tuk into the centre, which is a few minutes from Kandy station.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for a train ride south to Mount Lavinia for the beach and a seafood lunch.
    • Add a half day in Pettah: the Red Mosque, the markets and the old streets.
    • Take a longer day trip to Galle or Kandy by train; book seats early and check times on the day.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; buses are the main public transport.
    • Bus fares start at Rs 30, pay the conductor onboard.
    • Tuk-tuks are everywhere; agree a price or use a meter taxi.
    • PickMe and Uber both work well in Colombo.
    • Commuter trains run on four lines, including the coastal line to Mount Lavinia.
    • Walking works in Fort and Pettah but pavements are uneven.

    Where to stay

    • Fort · ≈ LKR 12,000–35,000 a night
      Walk to the old colonial buildings, the lighthouse and the station. Handy for the airport road and trains.
    • Pettah · ≈ LKR 6,000–18,000 a night
      Cheapest base and right in the bazaar chaos. Basic rooms; noisy and busy streets.
    • Kollupitiya · ≈ LKR 15,000–45,000 a night
      Central and modern: shops, restaurants and the Galle Face seafront walk nearby.
    • Mount Lavinia · ≈ LKR 10,000–30,000 a night
      Beach suburb south of the city. Quieter, with a train ride into Fort.

    Food & drink

    • Try rice and curry, egg hoppers, string hoppers and kottu roti. Small local cafés are cheapest.
    • Eat short eats: fish cutlets, rolls and samosas from bakeries, about Rs 50–150 each.
    • Pettah and Fort have cheap Indian and Sri Lankan eateries; check hygiene and go where locals queue.
    • Seafood is good along the coast; ask the price per kilo before ordering.
    • Tea is everywhere. Order a plain milk tea and expect it very sweet unless you say otherwise.

    Tips

    1. Use apps like PickMe or Uber for tuk-tuks to avoid haggling and overcharging
    2. Enjoy the sunset and local street food like Isso Vade (prawn fritters) at Galle Face Green
    3. Traffic can be heavy and chaotic; crossing the street requires confidence
    4. Dress modestly when visiting Buddhist or Hindu temples (cover shoulders and knees, remove shoes)
    5. Eat Kottu Roti, a famous local street food dish made with chopped flatbread and spices

      Souvenirs

      Ceylon Tea

      LKR 2000–5000100–200g tin · estimate

      Ceylon tea is grown in Sri Lanka's hill country and is famous worldwide. Colombo is the island's main trading hub, so it is an easy place to buy good tea.

      Where: Dilmah t-Lounge or Mlesna official shops
      Check that the label says Sri Lanka as the origin and shows the grade.

      Cinnamon

      LKR 700–180050–100g · estimate

      Sri Lanka is known for true cinnamon, which is milder and more delicate than the cassia sold in many other countries. It is a traditional spice of the island.

      Where: Keells or Cargills Food City in the city
      Look for the name Cinnamomum verum on the package to confirm you are buying Ceylon cinnamon.

      Batik Scarf

      LKR 2500–60001 piece · estimate

      Laksala is the government-run craft shop, selling local handicrafts with fixed prices. Batik is a traditional hand-dyed fabric that is widely made in Sri Lanka.

      Where: Laksala on York Street or in Thummulla
      Compare silk and cotton fabrics, since the price and feel differ a lot.
