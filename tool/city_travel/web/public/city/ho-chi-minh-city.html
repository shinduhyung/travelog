Getting around

Ride apps for most trips; Metro Line 1 for Ben Thanh to Suoi Tien.

  • ✓Metro ★Metro Line 1 links Ben Thanh Market to Suoi Tien and the Eastern Bus Terminus. Fares 7,000-20,000 VND, trains every 8-12 min, 5AM-10PM.
  • ✓BusGreen and blue buses cover 170+ routes. Fares 5,000-7,000 VND local, 10,000-50,000 VND intercity. Tap a contactless Visa or Mastercard.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Grab, Xanh SM and beTaxi work well; fares are fixed and often cheaper than street taxis. Use Mai Linh or Vinasun metered taxis and check the meter is on.
  • ✓Motorbike taxiXe om are everywhere. Agree a price first or book via app; short hops about 20,000 VND.
  • ✓Ferry / waterbusSaigon Waterbus runs on the Saigon River from District 1 to Thu Duc. A cheap river ride.
  • ✗Rental carNot practical for visitors; traffic is heavy and parking is hard. Use apps and the metro instead.
  • ✗WalkingFine for short blocks, but long walks are impractical in the heat. Use a ride for anything far.

Climate

Tropical city: hot all year, with a dry season roughly December to April and a very wet season from May to October.

  • ☀️Best time: December to MarchHighs 31-34°C, lows 23-25°C and only 3-10 rain days a month. Best for walking and markets.
  • 🌧️Wet season: May to October25-29 rain days a month. Showers are often heavy but short; streets can flood.
  • 🥵Hot year-roundHighs stay 30-35°C. March and April are hottest at 35°C. Plan outdoor sights for early morning.
  • 🌂Pack rain gear in the wet monthsA light rain jacket or umbrella helps from May to October. Wear quick-dry shoes.
  • 💧Humid most daysLows rarely drop below 23°C. Light, breathable clothes work all year.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, sunscreen, a hat and a refillable water bottle; add rain gear for May-October.

Landmarks 20

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    War Remnants Museum

    A powerful museum documenting the Vietnam War through photographs, military equipment and personal accounts. It is one of the city's most visited sights and offers a sobering perspective on modern history. Tip: visit early in the morning to avoid crowds and heat; tickets are inexpensive.

  2. 2

    Cu Chi Tunnels

    An extensive network of underground tunnels used during the war, now a popular historical site where you can crawl through sections and learn about guerrilla tactics. It's a must-see for history buffs. Tip: go early to beat the heat and crowds; wear comfortable shoes and bring water.

  3. 3

    Notre Dame Cathedral of Saigon

    A beautiful French colonial cathedral in the heart of the city, though currently under renovation. Its distinctive red bricks and twin towers make it a iconic landmark. Tip: check if interior visits are allowed; exterior viewing is still worthwhile, and it's free.

  4. 4

    Independence Palace

    The former presidential palace, famous for the tank crashing through its gates in 1975, marking the end of the war. The preserved rooms and bunkers offer a fascinating glimpse into history. Tip: rent an audio guide for context; go early to avoid tour groups.

  5. 5

    Saigon Central Post Office

    A stunning example of French colonial architecture, still functioning as a post office. Its vaulted ceilings and old maps make it a photographer's delight. Tip: visit during opening hours and send a postcard for a unique souvenir.

  6. 6

    Ben Thanh Market

    The city's most famous market, offering everything from souvenirs to local snacks. It's a great place to experience the bustling atmosphere and practice bargaining. Tip: visit early morning for fewer crowds and fresher food; bargain hard.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for the Cu Chi tunnels; book a tour or go by bus, and check the day's schedule.
    • Take the Saigon Waterbus to Thu Duc for a cheap river ride and a different view of the city.
    • Add a Mekong Delta day trip to My Tho or Ben Tre; long bus rides, so start early.

    Getting around

    • Metro Line 1 links Ben Thanh Market to Suoi Tien and the Eastern Bus Terminus; fares 7,000-20,000 VND, trains every 8-12 min, 5AM-10PM.
    • Green and blue city buses cover 170+ routes; fares 5,000-7,000 VND local, 10,000-50,000 VND intercity; tap a contactless Visa or Mastercard.
    • Grab, Xanh SM and beTaxi apps work well for cars and motorbike taxis; fares are fixed and often cheaper than street taxis.
    • Use Mai Linh or Vinasun metered taxis and check the meter is on; other firms may overcharge.
    • Motorbike taxis (xe om) are everywhere; agree a price first or book via app; short hops about 20,000 VND.
    • Saigon Waterbus runs on the Saigon River from District 1 to Thu Duc; walking long distances is impractical in the heat.

    Where to stay

    • District 1 · ≈ VND 900,000–3,500,000 a night
      Walk to Ben Thanh Market, the river and the main sights; Metro Line 1 starts here.
    • Pham Ngu Lao · ≈ VND 400,000–1,200,000 a night
      Backpacker strip with the cheapest beds, tour desks and late-night food.
    • District 3 · ≈ VND 700,000–2,500,000 a night
      Quieter streets, local cafés and a short ride to District 1 sights.
    • District 7 · ≈ VND 1,200,000–4,000,000 a night
      Modern riverside area with malls and family hotels; far from the old city, so plan on taxis.

    Food & drink

    • Start with pho for breakfast; a bowl at a local stall is often 40,000-70,000 VND.
    • Try banh mi, com tam (broken rice) and bun bo Hue; street stalls are cheap, roughly 30,000-60,000 VND a dish.
    • Vietnamese coffee is strong: ca phe sua da (iced with condensed milk) costs about 20,000-45,000 VND.
    • Eat at busy stalls with high turnover. Drink bottled or filtered water; skip ice if you have a sensitive stomach.
    • Book a street-food walk or a cooking class early in the trip; check prices and reviews before you book.

    Tips

    1. When crossing the street, walk slowly and predictably; motorbikes will flow around you
    2. Download Grab for safe, cheap, and meter-free rides around the city
    3. Vietnamese iced coffee (Cà phê sữa đá) is incredibly strong and sweet — a must-try
    4. Be highly vigilant against bag-snatching by passing motorbikes, especially in District 1
    5. Street food is generally safe and delicious; look for stalls with high turnover of locals
