Getting around

Metrô for long hops; Uber or 99 for late nights and hilly areas.

  • ✓Metro ★Modern, safe and the fastest way around. Lines 1–5 and 9 suit tourists.
  • ✓BusCovers the whole city. Fares are R$5.30; Bilhete Único gives free transfers within 3 hours.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis are metered; Guarucoop has a monopoly at GRU and fares are posted. Uber and 99 are widely used and usually cheaper.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is stressful. Traffic peaks 06:00–09:00 and 16:00–19:00, so avoid renting a car.
  • ✓WalkingFine in central and Jardins areas. Bikes are popular too, with many cycleways.
  • ✓TrainCPTM trains are modern and safe; Line 9 is useful for tourists.
Recommended stay★ 2 nights (3 days)

Two nights suit you: Paulista Avenue, MASP, Centro and Pinacoteca or Liberdade fill two sightseeing days, while long meals and nightlife need not add a third night.

Climate

Subtropical and mild year-round, with a dry, cool winter (Jun–Aug) and a wet, warm summer (Dec–Mar).

  • ☀️Best time: April to SeptemberMilder days around 22–26°C and fewer rain days. July is the driest, with about 3 rain days.
  • 🌧️Summer is wet (Dec–Mar)About 19–24 rain days a month. Storms are often short and heavy; keep indoor plans ready.
  • 🧥Winter nights are coolJune to August lows sit around 12–13°C. Days stay mild near 22°C. Bring a jacket.
  • 🌂Carry an umbrella in summerDownpours come fast from December to March. A small umbrella beats waiting it out.
  • 🌡️No extreme heatHighs stay around 22–27°C all year. It is rarely very hot or very cold.
  • 🧳What to packLight layers, a rain jacket or umbrella, comfy walking shoes and a warm layer for winter evenings.

Landmarks 22

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Ibirapuera Park

    São Paulo's most famous park and a must-see for its green space, museums and lakes. Go early on a weekday to avoid crowds; it's free and easily reached by metro and bus.

  2. 2

    São Paulo Museum of Art

    Home to one of Latin America's finest art collections and an iconic modernist building on Paulista Avenue. Closed on Mondays; check ticket prices online and arrive early on weekends.

  3. 3

    Paulista Avenue

    The city's main artery, packed with museums, shops and cultural institutions. Visit on a Sunday when the avenue is closed to cars and becomes a lively open street.

  4. 4

    Mercado Municipal de Sao Paulo

    A historic market famous for its stained-glass windows and local foods like mortadella sandwiches and pastel. Go on a weekday morning for fewer crowds; bring cash for snacks.

  5. 5

    Sao Paulo Cathedral

    One of the world's largest neo-Gothic cathedrals, right in the historic center. Visit during daylight hours and keep an eye on your belongings in the surrounding square.

  6. 6

    Theatro Municipal

    A beautifully restored early 20th-century opera house modeled on Parisian theaters. Guided tours are available; check the schedule for performances and book in advance.

  7. 7

    Liberdade

    The heart of São Paulo's Japanese community, with street markets and Asian restaurants. Go on a weekend for the lively street fair; it's easily reached by metro.

  8. 8

    Batman Alley

    A colorful open-air street-art gallery in Vila Madalena, perfect for photos. Visit during the day and combine with a stroll through the surrounding Vila Madalena district.

  9. 9

    Museum of the Portuguese Language

    An engaging museum housed in a historic building near the cathedral, dedicated to the Portuguese language. Check opening hours; it's closed on Mondays.

  10. 10

    Pinacoteca do Estado

    A leading art museum in a striking historic building with a strong collection of Brazilian art. Closed on Mondays; tickets are affordable and the surrounding area is worth exploring.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for Ibirapuera Park and the museums around it; it is a big park and easy to spend half a day.
    • Swap one city day for a trip to the coast or the hills; check bus times and prices on the day.
    • Art lovers can swap a shopping afternoon for the Pinacoteca and the Luz area near the Centro.

    Getting around

    • Metrô and CPTM trains are modern, safe and the fastest way around; Lines 1-5 and 9 suit tourists.
    • Buses cover the whole city; fares are R$5.30 and Bilhete Único gives free transfers within 3 hours.
    • Taxis are metered; Guarucoop has a monopoly at GRU and fares are posted on a tabela.
    • Uber and 99 operate widely and are usually cheaper than taxis.
    • Driving is stressful; traffic peaks 06:00-09:00 and 16:00-19:00, so avoid renting a car.
    • Bikes are popular with many cycleways; walking is fine in central and Jardins areas.

    Where to stay

    • Jardins · ≈ BRL 450–1,100 a night
      Leafy, safe and central. Walk to Avenida Paulista and the MASP; good restaurants nearby.
    • Paulista · ≈ BRL 300–750 a night
      Right on the main avenue and metro line. Best base for first-time sightseeing.
    • Vila Madalena · ≈ BRL 280–700 a night
      Street art, bars and nightlife. Livelier and a bit rougher; use ride apps at night.
    • Centro · ≈ BRL 180–500 a night
      Cheapest and closest to historic sights and markets. Busy by day, quiet after dark; check the block.

    Food & drink

    • Try a Brazilian lunch: feijoada (bean and pork stew) or a prato feito (rice, beans, meat and salad), often R$30–60.
    • Mercado Municipal downtown: pastel de bacalhau and huge mortadella sandwiches. Go before noon to beat the crowds.
    • Padarias (bakeries) serve cheap breakfasts and coffee all day; a pão na chapa and coffee is about R$10–15.
    • São Paulo has strong Japanese, Italian and Arab food. Look for restaurants in Liberdade and Bixiga.
    • Churrascarias and kilo buffets (comida por kilo) are good value; pay by weight, usually R$60–100 per kilo.

    Tips

    1. The culinary scene is world-class, especially for pizza and Japanese food (Liberdade district)
    2. Traffic jams can be paralyzing; rely on the Metro during rush hours
    3. Be mindful of your surroundings; do not flash expensive phones or jewelry on the street
    4. A Bilhete Único card integrates fares across buses, Metro, and CPTM trains
    5. Sundays on Paulista Avenue are fantastic — the street is closed to cars and filled with music and markets
