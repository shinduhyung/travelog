Uber, InDrive or a taxi with the fare agreed first; walk only in Zona Rosa and San Benito.

Climate

Tropical highland climate: warm all year, with a sharp dry season from November to March and a long, wet season from May to October.

  • ☀️Best time: November to MarchHighs around 29–31°C, lows near 19°C, and few rain days. Ideal for walking the centre.
  • 🌧️Wet season: May to OctoberRain on 24–29 days a month. Showers often hit in the afternoon; do outdoor sights early.
  • 🌡️Warm year-roundHighs stay between 27 and 31°C every month. Evenings are mild, around 19–21°C.
  • 🌂Pack rain gear in the wet monthsA light raincoat or umbrella and quick-dry shoes help from May to October.
  • ⛰️Cooler, greener hillsThe wet months turn the hills around the city lush; views are best on clear mornings.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, sunscreen and a hat; a light layer for evenings; rain protection if you visit May to October.

Landmarks 8

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    San Salvador Cathedral

    The city's central church and the burial site of Archbishop Óscar Romero, it anchors the historic heart around Plaza Barrios. Visit in the morning on a weekday for quieter access, and dress modestly since it is an active place of worship.

  2. 2

    Monument to the Divine Savior of the World

    This tall statue of Christ is the city's best-known symbol and a natural photo stop. Come in the late afternoon for softer light, and note it sits along a busy boulevard so arrange a taxi or rideshare rather than walking far.

  3. 3

    Iglesia El Rosario

    A striking modern church famous for its curved concrete shell and rainbow-colored stained glass. Go on a sunny morning when the light through the glass is at its best, and keep quiet as services may be underway.

  4. 4

    Joya de Ceren

    A UNESCO-listed archaeological site often called the Pompeii of the Americas, where a buried farming village is preserved. It lies outside the city, so plan a half-day trip with transport arranged in advance and bring sun protection.

  5. 5

    National Theater of San Salvador

    A restored historic theater that is a highlight of the downtown architecture. Check the schedule for performances or guided visits, and go during daylight hours when the surrounding streets are busiest.

  6. 6

    Plaza Libertad

    A central historical square surrounded by landmarks and good for a short orientation walk. Stop by in the morning, and keep valuables close in this busy downtown area.

  7. 7

    Parque Cuscatlán

    The city's main urban park, with green space and memorials, good for a relaxed break. Visit on weekends when locals gather, and avoid the park after dark.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day trip to the Joya de Cerén ruins, a UNESCO site often called the Pompeii of the Americas; check opening days before you go.
    • Swap an afternoon for the Museo de Arte de El Salvador (MARTE) in San Benito.
    • Climb to a viewpoint above the city, such as the Devil's Door area, with a guide and in daylight.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; the city relies on buses.
    • City buses are cheap but crowded; keep valuables close.
    • Taxis are common; agree the fare before you get in.
    • Uber and InDrive work in the city and to the airport.
    • Driving is possible but traffic and aggressive driving make it stressful.
    • Walking suits Zona Rosa and San Benito; other areas are spread out.

    Where to stay

    • Zona Rosa · ≈ USD 60–160 a night
      Hotels, restaurants and bars within walking distance; a good base for first-timers.
    • Colonia Escalón · ≈ USD 70–200 a night
      Quieter, greener streets with malls and cafés; short taxi rides to the centre.
    • Colonia San Benito · ≈ USD 80–220 a night
      Upscale and walkable, next to Zona Rosa and the museums; calm at night.
    • Centro Histórico · ≈ USD 30–90 a night
      Cheapest and closest to the cathedral and plazas; busier and less polished at night.

    Food & drink

    • Try pupusas, the national dish: thick corn tortillas stuffed with cheese, beans or pork, usually USD 1–2 each.
    • Eat at a pupusería for lunch or dinner; revueltas (mixed) and queso con loroco are safe first choices.
    • Breakfast: fried plantains, beans and cream, or a tamal wrapped in banana leaf.
    • Zona Rosa and Escalón have the widest choice of restaurants, from local to international.
    • Street food is cheap and tasty; pick busy stalls and drink bottled water.

    Tips

    1. Use Uber for safe, transparent, and reliable transportation
    2. Visit the nearby Boquerón National Park for stunning views from the volcano rim
    3. Stick to safer commercial zones like Zona Rosa for dining and nightlife
    4. Eat pupusas, the national dish of stuffed corn tortillas
    5. Carry a light jacket as evenings in the mountain valley can be crisp
