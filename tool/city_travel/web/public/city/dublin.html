Getting around

Walk the compact centre; use the Luas tram or buses for longer hops.

  • ✓Tram ★Luas has two lines, Red and Green, across the centre. Flat €2 adult fare with a Leap Card.
  • ✓Bus ★Dublin Bus and Go-Ahead cover most of the city. Pay exact coins or use a Leap Card (€2 flat fare, daily caps apply).
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsMetered taxis; Free Now and Uber work in Dublin. Handy for late nights and the airport.
  • ✓Rental carHeavy traffic and limited parking. Use Park & Ride at Luas or DART stops instead of driving in.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is compact and best on foot. Dublinbikes stations are widely spread for longer rides.

Climate

Mild, changeable and often wet: rain on roughly 12–15 days every month, and rarely very hot or very cold.

  • 🌧️Rain any monthExpect rain on about 12–15 days each month. Showers are often short; carry a compact waterproof.
  • 🧥Pack layersHighs run from about 8°C in winter to 19°C in summer. A warm mid-layer and a rain jacket cover most trips.
  • ☀️Best months: May to SeptemberWarmest and brightest, with highs of 15–19°C. Still bring rain gear — wet days happen.
  • 🥶Winter is cool, not freezingJanuary and February sit around 8–9°C by day and 4°C at night. Wind off the river feels colder.
  • 🕶️Long summer eveningsJune and July give the longest daylight, good for late walks and outdoor pints.
  • 👟Wear comfortable shoesYou will walk a lot on cobbles and wet pavements. Waterproof shoes beat an umbrella in wind.

Landmarks 26

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Guinness Storehouse

    Ireland's most visited attraction, this seven-story brewery experience tells the story of Guinness and ends with a pint in the Gravity Bar with panoramic city views. Book tickets online in advance to skip the queue, and go early morning or late afternoon for fewer crowds.

  2. 2

    Trinity College Library

    Home to the Book of Kells and the stunning Long Room, this library is a must-see for its history and architecture. Tickets are timed and sell out quickly, so reserve online ahead; visit right at opening for a quieter experience.

  3. 3

    Temple Bar

    Dublin's cultural quarter is famous for its lively pubs, street performers, and vibrant nightlife. It's best explored in the evening when the atmosphere is buzzing, but expect higher prices; for a quieter pint, venture to nearby streets.

  4. 4

    Dublin Castle

    A historic castle that served as the seat of British rule in Ireland, now a government complex with opulent state apartments. Guided tours are the only way to see the interiors, so check tour times and arrive early to secure a spot.

  5. 5

    St Patrick's Cathedral

    Ireland's largest cathedral, founded in 1220, offers stunning Gothic architecture and a rich history. Visit on a weekday morning to avoid crowds, and consider attending a choral evensong for a memorable experience.

  6. 6

    Kilmainham Gaol

    This former prison is a powerful symbol of Ireland's struggle for independence, with guided tours telling poignant stories. Tours often sell out, so book tickets online well in advance; it's a short tram ride from the city center.

  7. 7

    Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin

    Dublin's oldest cathedral, dating back to the 11th century, features a beautiful crypt and stunning medieval architecture. Combine your visit with Dublin Castle nearby, and check for evensong times to hear the choir.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for Howth: DART train to the harbour, cliff walk and seafood lunch.

    • Add a distillery or brewery tour (Jameson, Guinness Storehouse) — book a slot online to skip queues.

    Getting around

    • Trams: Luas has two lines, Red and Green, across the centre.
    • Buses: Dublin Bus and Go-Ahead cover most of the city; pay exact coins or use a Leap Card.
    • Leap Card: €5 deposit; flat €2 adult fare on buses and trams; daily caps apply.
    • Taxis: metered; apps like Free Now and Uber work in Dublin.
    • Driving: heavy traffic and limited parking; use Park & Ride at Luas or DART stops.
    • Walking and bikes: the centre is compact; Dublinbikes stations are widely spread.

    Where to stay

    • Temple Bar · ≈ EUR 140–260 a night
      Central and walkable to Trinity, the Castle and the river. Noisy at night and the priciest per square metre.
    • Georgian Quarter · ≈ EUR 120–230 a night
      Quiet Georgian streets near Merrion Square and the National Gallery; good for couples and longer stays.
    • Docklands · ≈ EUR 110–210 a night
      Modern hotels, often better value, near the Luas Red line and the 3Arena; a 15–20 minute walk to the centre.
    • Rathmines · ≈ EUR 90–170 a night
      Local neighbourhood with cafés and pubs; cheaper rooms, but you bus or Luas into the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Try a full Irish breakfast, then coddle or Irish stew in a traditional pub; lunch mains often €12–18.
    • Seafood chowder and fish and chips are the safe bets; check the catch of the day on the board.
    • Book dinner ahead for Friday and Saturday; popular places in the centre fill up.
    • Cheap eats: chip shops, bakeries and market stalls around €6–12 a meal.
    • Pints cost roughly €6–8 in the centre, more in Temple Bar. Check for a live-music cover charge.

    Tips

    1. A Leap Card is the most cost-effective and easiest way to use all public transit
    2. Thank the bus driver when exiting — it is a strict and polite local custom
    3. Temple Bar is famous but highly expensive; seek out pubs in other neighborhoods for a more local vibe
    4. Weather changes constantly; always dress in layers and carry a raincoat
    5. Guinness genuinely tastes better in Dublin; visit the Storehouse for the full experience
