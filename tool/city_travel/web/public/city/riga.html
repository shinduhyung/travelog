Getting around

Walk the Old Town and centre; use trams and buses for longer hops, and ride apps at night.

  • ✗MetroNo metro in Riga. Don't plan around rail inside the city.
  • ✓Bus ★Trams, buses and trolleybuses by Rigas Satiksme: single fare €1.50 valid 90 minutes with transfers. Buy via Mobilly, Narvesen or ticket machines and scan the vehicle QR code. 24-hour pass €5, 3-day €8, 5-day €10; free on some holidays.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsBolt, Panda Taxi and Red Cab work well. Avoid hailing random street taxis.
  • ✗Rental carNot advised: traffic and parking fees up to €8/hour in the Old Town.
  • ✓Walking ★Best way to see the Old Town, but cobblestones are rough. Bikes and e-scooters are common April to September.
  • ✓Ferry / boatSeasonal boat to Jūrmala runs May to September, €15–20, about 2.5 hours.

Climate

Baltic climate: cold winters, mild summers and rain in every month. Summer days are long; winter days are short.

  • ☀️Best time: June to AugustHighs around 22°C and long daylight. The busiest and priciest season; book ahead.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearExpect 9–15 rain days every month. July and December are wettest; May is driest.
  • 🧥Winter is coldJanuary highs near 0°C and lows around -4°C. Snow is possible; wear a warm coat and boots.
  • 🌗Short winter daysNovember to February has little daylight. Plan indoor sights and start early.
  • 🍂Shoulder seasons are quietApril–May and September–October are cheaper with fewer crowds, but pack rain gear.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes for cobblestones. Add a warm hat and gloves from November to March.

Landmarks 13

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Riga Old Town

    A UNESCO-listed maze of cobbled lanes, medieval churches and merchant houses that is the heart of any first visit. Go early in the morning to enjoy the squares before the crowds arrive.

  2. 2

    House of the Blackheads

    This ornate 14th-century guild hall, rebuilt after WWII, is one of Riga's most photographed buildings. Buy a ticket to see the lavish interior and climb to the small museum upstairs.

  3. 3

    Freedom Monument

    A towering symbol of Latvian independence guarded by soldiers, located between Old Town and the Art Nouveau District. Visit at dusk when it is softly lit, and watch the changing of the guard on the hour.

  4. 4

    Art Nouveau District

    Several streets are lined with spectacular early-20th-century facades, many designed by Mikhail Eisenstein. Take a self-guided walk along Alberta iela; the Riga Art Nouveau Museum is a worthwhile stop.

  5. 5

    Riga Central Market

    Housed in converted Zeppelin hangars, this is one of Europe's largest markets and a great place to taste local produce. Go hungry and try grey peas with bacon at one of the food stalls.

  6. 6

    Riga Cathedral

    The city's largest medieval church, famous for its massive organ and cloister. Climb the tower for a panoramic view of Old Town; entry to the tower is ticketed separately.

  7. 7

    St. Peter's Church

    Its slender spire dominates the skyline and the viewing platform offers the best panorama of Riga. Take the lift up (small fee) on a clear day for photos.

  8. 8

    Latvian National Opera

    A grand 19th-century theatre with affordable tickets and excellent productions. Check the schedule for opera or ballet; the building itself is worth a look even without a show.

  9. 9

    Daugava River

    The wide river divides Old Town from the newer districts and offers pleasant promenades. Walk along the embankment at sunset for views of the skyline and bridges.

  10. 10

    Jūrmala Resort

    A short train ride from Riga, this seaside town has a long sandy beach and wooden villas. It is a perfect half-day escape in summer; trains run frequently from Riga Central Station.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap an afternoon for Jūrmala: seasonal boat May to September, €15–20, about 2.5 hours, or a short train ride.
    • Add the Art Nouveau district walk on Alberta iela and the nearby museum if you like architecture.
    • History fans can add a half-day at the occupation museum or the Soviet-era sights outside the centre.

    Getting around

    • Trams, buses, and trolleybuses run by Rigas Satiksme; single fare €1.50 valid 90 minutes with transfers.
    • Buy tickets via Mobilly app, Narvesen shops, or ticket machines; activate by scanning the vehicle QR code.
    • 24-hour pass costs €5, 3-day €8, 5-day €10; public transport is free on some holidays.
    • Ride apps Bolt, Panda Taxi, and Red Cab work well; avoid hailing random street taxis.
    • Driving is not advised due to traffic and parking fees up to €8/hour in the old town.
    • Old Town is best on foot; cobblestones are rough. Bikes and e-scooters are common April to September.
    • Seasonal boat to Jūrmala runs May to September, €15-20, about 2.5 hours.

    Where to stay

    • Vecrīga (Old Town) · ≈ EUR 60–160 a night
      Walk to the cathedral, squares and museums. Noisy at night and cobblestones are rough; ask for a quiet room.
    • Centrs · ≈ EUR 50–130 a night
      Art Nouveau district and good cafés; 10–15 minutes on foot to the Old Town. Good transport links.
    • Āgenskalns · ≈ EUR 40–100 a night
      Quiet wooden-house streets across the river, near the market and parks. Cheaper; cross the bridge to the centre.
    • Maskačka · ≈ EUR 35–90 a night
      Local, budget-friendly neighbourhood with the central market nearby. Simple tram or bus ride to the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Try Latvian dishes: grey peas with bacon, rye bread, and smoked fish. Lunch menus are the best value.
    • Riga Central Market: old zeppelin hangars with cheese, berries, smoked meat and cheap snacks.
    • Book ahead for popular Old Town restaurants in summer; walk-ins fill up after 19:00.
    • Lido is a good budget canteen chain for hearty local food; pay by weight.
    • Black balsam is the local herbal liqueur. Sip it slowly or try it in a cocktail.

    Tips

    1. The city boasts one of the highest concentrations of Art Nouveau architecture in the world
    2. The Old Town is entirely pedestrianized and beautiful to explore on foot
    3. Try local favorites like black bread and Riga Black Balsam (an herbal liqueur)
    4. English is widely spoken, but a few words of Latvian are appreciated
    5. Winters are dark and extremely cold; plan accordingly

      EUR 30–701 pair · estimate
