Climate

High-altitude city with mild days all year and a wet summer. Rain falls mostly in afternoon storms from June to September.

  • ☀️Best time: November to AprilDry, sunny days around 22–27°C. Ideal for walking, ruins and markets.
  • 🌧️Wet season June to SeptemberUp to 26–27 rain days a month. Storms usually hit in the afternoon; keep mornings for outdoor plans.
  • 🧥Cool nights all yearLows of 7–13°C. Evenings need a light jacket, especially in December and January.
  • 🌡️Mild, not hotHighs stay around 22–27°C. The altitude can make sun feel stronger; use sunscreen.
  • ⛈️Afternoon downpoursSummer storms are short but heavy. Carry a compact rain jacket and waterproof shoes.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a light rain jacket in summer, comfortable walking shoes and a hat.

Landmarks 33

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Zocalo

    The vast main square is the heart of Mexico City and the natural starting point for any first visit, ringed by the National Palace and the cathedral. Come early in the morning for cooler air and fewer crowds, and note that the flag ceremony happens here daily.

  2. 2

    Metropolitan Cathedral of Mexico City

    This enormous colonial cathedral anchors the Zocalo and is one of the most important churches in the Americas. It is free to enter but modest dress is expected, and it sits right beside the metro and the square.

  3. 3

    National Palace

    The seat of the federal government faces the Zocalo and is famous for the murals inside. Bring photo ID for entry, check opening hours in advance, and go on a weekday morning to avoid queues.

  4. 4

    Chapultepec Castle

    Perched on the hill in Chapultepec Park, this castle offers city views and grand rooms that make it a top stop. Arrive when it opens to beat the line, and be ready for a steep walk up or a short park train ride.

  5. 5

    National Museum of Anthropology

    One of the world's great museums, with the Aztec Sun Stone and vast halls on Mexico's ancient cultures. Allow at least half a day, and note that it is usually closed on Mondays.

  6. 6

    Frida Kahlo Museum

    The Blue House in Coyoacan draws visitors from around the world to see where Kahlo lived and worked. Book timed tickets online well ahead, as walk-up entry is often sold out.

  7. 7

    Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe

    The most visited Catholic shrine in the Americas, home to the image of the Virgin of Guadalupe. Go on a weekday morning for a calmer visit, and dress respectfully; it is reachable by metro plus a short walk.

  8. 8

    Templo Mayor

    The excavated Aztec main temple sits right beside the Zocalo, giving a striking sense of the city's layered past. Combine it with the cathedral next door and go early to avoid midday heat.

  9. 9

    Palacio de Bellas Artes

    This marble palace is the city's cultural showpiece, with murals and a grand theater hall. Check the schedule for performances, or simply admire the exterior and the neighboring Alameda park.

  10. 10

    Angel of Independence

    The golden angel on Paseo de la Reforma is the city's most recognizable monument and a lively gathering point. Visit at dusk when it is lit, and use the nearby metro or a short walk along the avenue.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Teotihuacán: go early by bus or tour to climb the pyramids before the heat and crowds.
    • Add a day in Coyoacán for the Frida Kahlo museum (book tickets online) and the markets.
    • Take a boat (trajinera) at Xochimilco on a weekend; agree the price per boat before boarding.

    Getting around

    • Metro: 12 lines, M$5-6 per ride, 05:00-00:00 weekdays; very crowded at rush hour.
    • Metrobús BRT and trolleybuses cover many main avenues; pay with a rechargeable card.
    • Taxis are white and magenta; agree the fare or use the meter, and prefer sitio stands at night.
    • Uber, DiDi and inDrive work widely and are cheap; check the plate before getting in.
    • Driving is slow and parking is hard; walking is best in Centro, Roma and Condesa.
    • Bikes: EcoBici bike-share has stations in central areas; helmets are not common.

    Where to stay

    • Centro Histórico · ≈ MXN 900–2,800 a night
      Walk to the Zócalo, Templo Mayor and Bellas Artes. Busy by day, quieter at night; use sitio taxis after dark.
    • Roma-Condesa · ≈ MXN 1,400–4,200 a night
      Leafy streets, cafés and bars; good for food and nightlife. Easy walking, short ride apps to the center.
    • Polanco · ≈ MXN 2,500–7,500 a night
      Upscale hotels near Chapultepec Park and the anthropology museum. Calm and walkable, pricier restaurants.
    • Coyoacán · ≈ MXN 1,100–3,200 a night
      Colonial lanes, the Frida Kahlo house and markets. Slower pace; longer rides to the center.

    Food & drink

    • Eat tacos al pastor from busy stalls; a few tacos cost M$60–120. Pick places with a crowd and turnover.
    • Markets are cheap and good: Mercado de San Juan and La Merced for produce, cheese and cooked food.
    • Try pozole, tamales, chilaquiles and tlayudas. Breakfast at a fonda is often M$80–150.
    • Drink only bottled or filtered water, and skip ice from street carts if your stomach is sensitive.
    • Book ahead for top restaurants in Roma and Polanco; walk-ins are hard on weekend nights.

    Tips

    1. The high altitude can cause fatigue or mild sickness on your first day — stay hydrated
    2. Do not drink the tap water; always rely on bottled or heavily filtered water
    3. Street food (tacos, tamales) is incredible, but choose stalls with a crowd of locals
    4. Traffic is notoriously dense; plan extra time for car travel and use the Metro or Metrobús
    5. Many world-class museums are closed on Mondays
