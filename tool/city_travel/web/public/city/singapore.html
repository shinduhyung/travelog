Getting around

MRT for most trips; ride apps or metered taxis for late nights and door-to-door.

  • ✓Metro ★MRT trains are fast and cheap. Tap a contactless credit card or buy an EZ-link card.
  • ✓BusBuses cover gaps the MRT misses. Fares are distance-based and low.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis meter fares. Grab and Gojek ride apps are widely used.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is possible but costly: ERP tolls, high parking and a car-ownership tax regime.
  • ✓WalkingWalking is pleasant in the core. Covered walkways help in rain and heat.
  • ✓FerryFerries run from HarbourFront and Tanah Merah to the Riau Islands and Johor.
Recommended stay★ 2 nights (3 days)

Two nights suit you: Marina Bay, Gardens by the Bay, Chinatown, Little India and Kampong Glam fit two days, without needing a third night for Sentosa or the zoo.

Climate

Tropical and hot all year: highs near 29–30°C, lows near 24–25°C, and rain on most months.

  • 🌡️Warm every monthHighs stay around 29–30°C and lows around 24–25°C. No cool season.
  • 🌧️Rain is commonExpect 18–28 rain days a month. February is the driest; May, November and December are the wettest.
  • ☔Short heavy showersDownpours often pass quickly. Carry a small umbrella and use covered walkways.
  • 💦High humidityIt feels hotter than the numbers. Drink water and take breaks in air-conditioning.
  • 🌤️Best outdoor monthsFebruary and July–September have fewer rain days. Still plan around afternoon storms.
  • 🧳What to packLight breathable clothes, sandals or quick-dry shoes, an umbrella and sunscreen. A light layer for cold indoor AC.

Landmarks 48

Must-see · 9

  1. 1

    Marina Bay Sands

    Singapore's most recognisable modern icon, with its ship-shaped SkyPark and sweeping views over the bay. Come around sunset for the best light and to catch the evening light show; book SkyPark tickets online in advance.

  2. 2

    Gardens by the Bay

    A futuristic garden with the Supertree Grove and giant glass conservatories that is a must-see for first-timers. Visit in the late afternoon and stay for the free evening Supertree light show; buy conservatory tickets ahead.

  3. 3

    Merlion Park

    The half-lion, half-fish Merlion is the city's emblem and the classic photo spot facing Marina Bay. Go early morning or evening to avoid the heat and crowds; it is free and an easy walk from Raffles Place MRT.

  4. 4

    Jewel Changi Airport

    Home to the Rain Vortex, the world's tallest indoor waterfall, set in a lush indoor garden. It is free to enter the mall; arrive outside peak hours for clearer views and photos.

  5. 5

    Chinatown Heritage District

    Historic shophouse streets packed with temples, markets and food, ideal for a first taste of old Singapore. Wander early in the day, dress respectfully at temples, and reach it easily by Chinatown MRT.

  6. 6

    Buddha Tooth Relic Temple

    A striking Buddhist temple in the heart of Chinatown housing a revered relic and peaceful halls. Entry is free; wear modest clothing covering shoulders and knees, and avoid peak prayer times.

  7. 7

    Kampong Glam & Haji Lane

    Colourful heritage streets around the Sultan Mosque, with boutiques and cafes along Haji Lane. Visit in the morning for cooler weather and quieter lanes; the area is a short walk from Bugis MRT.

  8. 8

    Little India

    A vibrant neighbourhood of temples, markets and Indian food that feels like another world. Go on a weekday and dress modestly at Sri Mariamman Temple; reach it via Little India MRT.

  9. 9

    Universal Studios Singapore

    The island's flagship theme park with rides and shows for all ages. Buy tickets online, arrive at opening, and take the Sentosa Express to reach it.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a mall afternoon for a walk in the Botanic Gardens, free and open early.
    • Take a short ferry to Pulau Ubin or the Southern Islands for a slower day; check timings on the day.
    • Add a half-day in Kampong Glam and Little India for temples, murals and cheap eats.

    Getting around

    • MRT trains are fast and cheap; tap a contactless credit card or buy an EZ-link card.
    • Buses cover gaps the MRT misses; fares are distance-based and low.
    • Taxis meter fares; Grab and Gojek ride apps are widely used.
    • Driving is possible but costly: ERP tolls, high parking and a car-ownership tax regime.
    • Walking is pleasant in the core; covered walkways help in rain and heat.
    • Ferries run from HarbourFront and Tanah Merah to the Riau Islands and Johor.

    Where to stay

    • Marina Bay · ≈ SGD 250–700 a night
      Walk to Gardens by the Bay, the Merlion and the bay lights. Best for first-timers who want the skyline at night.
    • Orchard Road · ≈ SGD 200–550 a night
      Malls on your doorstep and a central MRT line. Good for shoppers and easy taxi rides anywhere.
    • Chinatown · ≈ SGD 120–350 a night
      Hawker food, temples and shophouse hotels. Cheaper than the bay and close to the river.
    • Bugis · ≈ SGD 130–380 a night
      Lively street market, good MRT links and short walks to Kampong Glam and Little India.

    Food & drink

    • Eat at hawker centres: Maxwell, Lau Pa Sat and Old Airport Road. A meal is often SGD 4–8.
    • Start the day with kaya toast, soft-boiled eggs and kopi at a kopitiam.
    • Try chilli crab, laksa, chicken rice and char kway teow. Prices vary widely by stall.
    • Many stalls are cash-only or use local payment apps. Carry small notes.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead. Hawker queues are shortest before noon and after 2pm.

    Tips

    1. EZ-Link card covers MRT and buses — the easiest way to get around.
    2. Hawker centres serve excellent meals from S$3–8; must-try: chicken rice, laksa, char kway teow.
    3. Air conditioning is everywhere — bring a light layer for malls and indoor spaces.
    4. Chewing gum is banned for sale; fines apply for littering and jaywalking.
    5. Grab (ride-hailing) is widely used and more predictable than taxis.

    Souvenirs

    Ya Kun Kaya Jam

    SGD 7–12About 220-250 g, 1 jar · estimate

    A sweet coconut and egg jam spread, and a classic Singapore breakfast staple. It is sold at the same cafes that serve the kaya toast locals eat every morning.

    Where: Official Ya Kun Kaya Toast outlets
    Ask the outlet to pack the jar for travel, since they can wrap it for the flight.

    TWG Blend Tea

    SGD 25–4550-100 g · estimate

    TWG is a tea brand founded in Singapore, known for its luxury blends and elegant tins. It makes a practical gift that reflects the city's polished tea culture.

    Where: TWG Tea stores at ION Orchard or Marina Bay Sands
    Check the blend name and the origin listed on the tin before you pay, since blends vary widely.

    Peranakan Ceramic Small Plate

    SGD 20–601 piece · estimate

    These plates carry the bright floral and bird motifs of Peranakan culture, which grew out of Chinese and Malay roots in Singapore's shophouse neighborhoods. They are a distinctly local keepsake.

    Where: Peranakan Museum shop or Supermama
    Hand-painted pieces cost more than printed designs, so ask the seller which method was used.
