Getting around

Walk downtown and ride the free T there; use buses, the T or ride apps for hills and river crossings.

  • ✓MetroLight rail ('The T') serves downtown, the North Shore and the south. Free downtown between Allegheny and First Avenue stations.
  • ✓Bus ★Buses reach most neighbourhoods. Fare is $2.75 with ConnectCard or app; cash needs exact change. A $7 day pass gives unlimited rides, and tickets allow transfers for three hours.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and Lyft are widely used and often cheaper than taxis. Street hailing is hard, so book in the app.
  • ✓Rental carHandy for day trips, but hills and rivers make driving confusing. A GPS helps.
  • ✓WalkingDowntown is walkable, but expect hills and steps. The two inclines are a paid ride, not a walk.
  • ✓InclineTwo historic inclines climb the hills for views. Fare is $2.75 with ConnectCard or app; cash exact fare.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cold, grey winters and warm, humid summers, with rain spread through the year.

  • ☀️Best time: May to OctoberHighs run about 18–29°C. May, September and October are the most comfortable for walking.
  • 🥶Winter is cold (December to February)Highs near 3–7°C and lows down to about -5°C. Snow and ice are possible; wear warm, grippy shoes.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 10–16 wet days every month, peaking in spring and early summer. Pack a light rain jacket.
  • 🥵Humid summersJuly and August highs near 28–29°C with muggy air. Do hilltop views and trails early in the day.
  • 🍂Crisp autumnOctober highs near 18°C and cool nights. Good light and fall colours on the hills.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable walking shoes for hills and steps; a warm coat and hat in winter.

Landmarks 11

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    Duquesne Incline

    A historic cable car climbing Mount Washington for one of the best skyline views in the United States, where the three rivers meet downtown. Go near sunset for the best light, and bring exact change or a card for the small fare; it is a short ride from downtown across the river.

  2. 2

    Point State Park

    The green tip of downtown where the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers form the Ohio, with fountains and riverfront paths. It is free and open daily; visit in the morning or late afternoon for cooler walking and fewer crowds.

  3. 3

    Andy Warhol Museum

    The largest museum devoted to a single artist in North America, celebrating Pittsburgh-born Andy Warhol across seven floors. Check opening days in advance and allow at least two hours; it sits on the North Shore, walkable from downtown.

  4. 4

    Phipps Conservatory and Botanical Gardens

    A Victorian glasshouse in Schenley Park with lush seasonal displays and tropical rooms. Book timed tickets online, especially on weekends, and go early to avoid the busiest hours.

  5. 5

    Carnegie Museum of Natural History

    Home to famous dinosaur skeletons and strong natural science collections in Oakland. Pair it with the adjacent art museum and check hours ahead; it is a short bus ride from downtown.

    Swap in instead

    • Ride both inclines: Duquesne for the classic view, Monongahela for a quieter one. Fare is $2.75 each way.
    • Add a half-day at the Carnegie Museum of Natural History and the Andy Warhol Museum in Oakland and the North Shore.
    • Sports fans: check the Pirates, Steelers or Penguins schedule; tickets vary, so check on the day.

    Getting around

    • Pittsburgh Regional Transit runs buses, light rail ('The T') and two inclines.
    • Bus and incline fare is $2.75 with ConnectCard or mobile app; cash exact fare, no change.
    • Day pass costs $7; tickets allow unlimited transfers for three hours.
    • The T is free within downtown between Allegheny and First Avenue stations.
    • Uber and Lyft are widely used and often cheaper than taxis; street hailing is hard.
    • Downtown is walkable, but hills and rivers make driving confusing; a GPS helps.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown · ≈ USD 140–320 a night
      Walk to Point State Park, the inclines and the ballparks. Free T rides inside downtown.
    • Strip District · ≈ USD 130–300 a night
      Food markets, cafés and the riverfront trail on the doorstep; short walk or bus to downtown.
    • South Side · ≈ USD 110–260 a night
      Bars and restaurants on East Carson Street; walk over the bridge to downtown sights.
    • Oakland · ≈ USD 120–280 a night
      Next to the universities, museums and Phipps Conservatory; quieter evenings.

    Food & drink

    • Order a Primanti Bros. sandwich: meat, fries and coleslaw stacked inside the bread.
    • Try pierogies and Polish plates in the Strip District and Lawrenceville.
    • Strip District on a Saturday morning: coffee, bakeries and food stalls; go early, it gets busy.
    • Cheap eats: a slice of pizza or a hoagie for roughly $8–15; sit-down dinners run about $20–40 a head.
    • Book ahead for popular Lawrenceville and Downtown restaurants on weekend evenings.

    Tips

    1. Take the historic funicular inclines up Mt Washington for the best views of the city skyline
    2. Explore the diverse cultural districts and the many parks
    3. Pittsburgh has more bridges than any other city in the world
    4. The city is home to major research universities, driving its modern tech economy
    5. Traffic can be confusing due to the unique river and bridge geography
