Getting around

Metro and S-trains for most trips; rent a bike for short hops in good weather.

  • ✓Metro ★Runs 24/7. M1/M2 link the airport to the centre in about 14 min.
  • ✓Train ★S-trains and buses cover the city; a 3-zone ticket costs about DKK 30.
  • ✓BusBuses fill the gaps the trains miss. Same ticket zones apply.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered and pricey. Uber and Bolt operate in the city.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the centre is slow and parking is costly. Park outside and take transit.
  • ✓BikeThe city is flat and bike-friendly; rental bikes are everywhere.
  • ✓FerryHarbour buses and small ferries cross the canals cheaply.
  • ✓WalkingThe old centre is compact and easy on foot; cobblestones need comfy shoes.

Climate

Cool coastal climate: mild summers, cold winters, and rain in every month. Pack layers and a rain jacket.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs of 15–21°C and long days. Best for walking, bikes and the harbour.
  • 🌧️Rain all year9–13 rain days every month. Showers pass quickly; a light waterproof beats an umbrella in wind.
  • 🧥Winter is cold and darkDecember to February highs of 4–6°C, lows near 1°C. Daylight is short; plan indoor sights.
  • 🌬️Windy by the waterThe harbour and canals feel colder than the forecast. A windproof layer helps.
  • 🌡️Summers are mild, not hotJuly and August peak around 20–21°C. Rarely need air-conditioning.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes for cobblestones. Add a warm hat and gloves in winter.

Landmarks 20

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Nyhavn

    The 17th-century harbor with colorful gabled townhouses and wooden ships is Copenhagen's most photographed spot. Visit early morning to avoid crowds, or take a canal boat tour from the quay.

  2. 2

    Tivoli Gardens

    One of the world's oldest amusement parks, offering charming gardens, rides, and evening illuminations. Book tickets online and go after dark for the best atmosphere.

  3. 3

    The Little Mermaid

    The iconic bronze statue inspired by Hans Christian Andersen's fairy tale sits on a rock by the harbor. It's a short walk from Nyhavn; go early to avoid tour groups.

  4. 4

    Amalienborg

    The royal family's winter residence, featuring four identical palaces around an octagonal square. Watch the changing of the guard at noon (daily) and visit the museum.

  5. 5

    Rosenborg Castle

    A Renaissance castle housing the Danish crown jewels and royal regalia. Buy a combined ticket with the Round Tower and visit on a weekday to avoid queues.

  6. 6

    Christiansborg Palace

    Home to the Danish Parliament and royal reception rooms, with ruins of the original castle in the basement. Climb the tower for free panoramic views of the city.

  7. 7

    The Round Tower

    A 17th-century astronomical observatory with a spiral ramp leading to a rooftop platform. Go at sunset for great photo opportunities; entry is inexpensive.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Roskilde: the Viking Ship Museum and the cathedral, about 25 min by S-train.
    • Take the train to Louisiana Museum of Modern Art north of the city; check opening days on the day.
    • Cross to Malmö, Sweden, by train over the Øresund bridge for a half-day change of scene.

    Getting around

    • Metro runs 24/7; M1/M2 link the airport to the centre in about 14 min.
    • S-trains and buses cover the city; a 3-zone ticket costs about DKK30.
    • Taxis are metered and pricey; apps like Uber and Bolt operate in the city.
    • Driving in the centre is slow and parking is costly; park outside and take transit.
    • The city is flat and bike-friendly; rental bikes are everywhere.
    • Harbour buses and small ferries cross the canals cheaply.

    Where to stay

    • Indre By (centre) · ≈ DKK 900–1,800 a night
      Walk to Nyhavn, Tivoli, the palaces and the Round Tower. Best for a first short visit.
    • Vesterbro · ≈ DKK 700–1,400 a night
      Next to the central station and Tivoli; lively food and bar streets. Easy airport trains.
    • Nørrebro · ≈ DKK 600–1,200 a night
      Cheaper, young and multicultural. Good cafés and street food; 10–15 min by bike or metro to the centre.
    • Østerbro · ≈ DKK 750–1,500 a night
      Quiet, leafy and close to the Little Mermaid, the lakes and the park. Good for families.

    Food & drink

    • Start with smørrebrød: rye bread with herring, egg and shrimp. Lunch portions are bigger than they look.
    • Try a bakery 'kanelsnegl' (cinnamon roll) and Danish pastries; they are cheapest to take away.
    • Rejsekort or the app for cheap eats: hot dogs from a pølsevogn stand cost about DKK 30–45.
    • Nørrebro and Vesterbro have the best value: falafel, shawarma and street food halls, about DKK 60–100 a meal.
    • Book dinner ahead on weekends. A sit-down dinner with drinks often runs DKK 250–450 per person.

    Tips

    1. Cycling is the absolute best way to get around; rent a bike but obey the strict cycling rules
    2. Do not walk in the bike lanes under any circumstances
    3. Public transit runs on a zone system; the City Pass is a great option for tourists
    4. Experience 'Hygge' by enjoying coffee and pastries in a cozy, candlelit cafe during the dark winter
    5. Tap water is excellent, free, and strictly regulated; never buy bottled water
