Getting around

Use the RapidKL metro for most sights; Grab for late nights and places off the rail grid.

  • ✓Metro ★RapidKL lines 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11 and 12 cover most sights. Trains every 2–7 minutes. Buy a Touch 'n Go card (RM10 plus top-up).
  • ✓TrainKTM lines 1, 2 and 10 and ERL lines 6 and 7 link KL Sentral to suburbs and the airport. Contactless cards work on KTM and ERL only.
  • ✓BusCheap but slow. Use buses for short hops off the rail grid.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Grab is the main ride app. Metered taxis exist but agree the fare first.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is stressful in jams. Only worth it for trips out of the city.
  • ✓WalkingFine in the centre, but pavements are patchy. Watch your step and cross with care.

Climate

Tropical and hot all year: highs around 30–32°C, lows around 23–24°C, and rain on most months. It is wettest from March to May and October to December.

  • 🌡️Hot every monthHighs stay around 30–32°C and lows around 23–24°C all year. There is no cool season.
  • 🌧️Rain is normalExpect 17–29 rain days a month. February is driest; November is wettest.
  • ⛈️Afternoon stormsDownpours are often short and heavy, usually later in the day. Plan outdoor sights for the morning.
  • 💦High humidityIt feels muggy most days. Take breaks in air-conditioned malls and cafés.
  • ☂️Pack rain gearA small umbrella or light rain jacket helps. Pavements can flood briefly after storms.
  • 👕What to wearLight, breathable clothes and comfortable shoes. A light layer for strong indoor AC. Cover shoulders and knees at mosques and temples.

Landmarks 27

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Petronas Towers

    The twin towers are Kuala Lumpur's defining icon and a symbol of the city's modern skyline. Book the skybridge and observation deck tickets in advance, and go at dusk for day and night views. The towers are easy to reach by LRT at KLCC station.

  2. 2

    Batu Caves

    A massive Hindu shrine set in limestone caves, reached by a steep flight of colorful steps guarded by the giant Lord Murugan Statue. Go early morning to avoid heat and crowds. Dress respectfully, covering shoulders and knees, and take the KTM Komuter train to Batu Caves station.

  3. 3

    Merdeka Square

    This historic square is where Malaysia's independence was declared, surrounded by colonial-era buildings and the tall flagpole. Visit in the cooler early morning and combine it with nearby Masjid Jamek and Central Market on foot.

  4. 4

    KL Tower

    A landmark telecommunications tower with an observation deck offering sweeping views over the city and the Petronas Towers. Come around sunset for the best light. Tickets are sold at the base, and it is a short walk from Bukit Bintang.

  5. 5

    Thean Hou Temple

    One of the largest Chinese temples in Southeast Asia, with ornate roofs and city views from its hillside setting. It is free to enter; dress modestly and visit in the morning for quieter grounds. A taxi or ride-hailing car is the easiest way up.

  6. 6

    Islamic Arts Museum Malaysia

    A world-class museum with beautiful collections of Islamic art, textiles and architecture models, plus a good cafe. It is a calm, air-conditioned break from the heat. Check opening hours, as it closes on some public holidays, and it sits near the National Mosque of Malaysia.

  7. 7

    Merdeka 118

    One of the world's tallest skyscrapers, this new landmark dominates the skyline near Merdeka Square. The observation deck requires timed tickets booked ahead. It is best combined with a walk through the historic Merdeka Square area.

  8. 8

    National Mosque of Malaysia

    A striking modern mosque with a distinctive folded-roof design, open to visitors outside prayer times. Entry is free; dress code requires covered shoulders and legs, with robes often provided. It is a short walk from the Islamic Arts Museum Malaysia.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for the Batu Caves in the morning, then the Forest Research Institute trails in the afternoon.
    • Take a day trip to Melaka or the Genting Highlands; buses and trains run from KL Sentral — check times on the day.
    • Add a night in the Cameron Highlands for cool air and tea plantations if you want a break from the heat.

    Getting around

    • RapidKL metro lines 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11, 12 cover most sights; trains every 2-7 min.
    • KTM lines 1, 2, 10 and ERL lines 6, 7 link KL Sentral to suburbs and the airport.
    • Touch 'n Go card costs RM10 plus top-up; contactless cards work on KTM and ERL only.
    • Buses are cheap but slow; use them for short hops off the rail grid.
    • Grab is the main ride app; taxis are metered but agree the fare first.
    • Driving is stressful in jams; walking is fine in the centre but pavements are patchy.

    Where to stay

    • KLCC · ≈ MYR 250–700 a night
      Walk to the Petronas Towers and the park. Best metro links and easy airport train from KL Sentral.
    • Bukit Bintang · ≈ MYR 180–550 a night
      Malls, street food and nightlife on your doorstep. Central for walking to many sights.
    • Chinatown · ≈ MYR 90–300 a night
      Cheapest base for food and markets. Close to Merdeka Square and the old town.
    • Brickfields · ≈ MYR 120–350 a night
      Little India near KL Sentral, so airport trains and metro are minutes away. Good value.

    Food & drink

    • Eat at hawker centres: Jalan Alor for grilled skewers and seafood, and Lot 10 Hutong for classic stalls in air-conditioning.
    • Try nasi lemak, char kway teow, laksa and roti canai. A hawker meal is often around RM 8–20.
    • Mamak restaurants (Indian-Muslim) are open late and cheap. Good for supper after a night out.
    • Chinatown and Petaling Street for noodles, dumplings and fresh juice; check prices before ordering seafood.
    • Alcohol is pricey and limited outside bars and hotels. Many places are halal; look for the sign.

    Tips

    1. Touch 'n Go cards are widely used for public transit and toll payments
    2. Weather can change quickly; always carry an umbrella for sudden afternoon downpours
    3. Street food in Jalan Alor is vibrant but expect it to be slightly tourist-priced
    4. Dress modestly when visiting mosques or temples
    5. Use ride-hailing apps like Grab instead of hailing taxis from the street to avoid overcharging
