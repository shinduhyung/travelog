Getting around

Walk in the centre and use the metro for longer hops; use Uber or Bolt for the airport.

  • ✓Metro ★Line E links the airport to the centre. Validate your Andante card before boarding.
  • ✓BusSTCP buses cover the city. Night bus 3M runs to the airport hourly.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and Bolt work well. Airport rides cost about €10–25.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is chaotic: narrow hilly streets and hard parking. Best avoided.
  • ✓Walking ★The best way around the centre, though it is hilly.
  • ✓BoatDouro river boats and the Leixões harbour serve cruise and tour trips.

Climate

Mild Atlantic climate: wet from October to March, warm and dry in July and August.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs of 20–25°C and few rain days, especially July and August.
  • 🌧️Wettest months: October to MarchAround 12–14 rain days a month. Bring a rain jacket and waterproof shoes.
  • 🌡️Mild wintersJanuary highs near 13°C and lows near 7°C. Rarely freezing, but damp and chilly.
  • 🥵Warm summers, not extremeJuly and August peak around 24–25°C. Comfortable for walking, but the hills are steep.
  • 🌂Rain can come any monthEven summer has a few wet days. A small umbrella is worth packing.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a rain jacket and comfortable shoes for cobbled hills. Add sunglasses and a hat for summer.

Landmarks 18

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Ribeira District

    Porto's riverside old town is a UNESCO-listed maze of narrow lanes, tiled facades and cafes spilling toward the Douro. It is the heart of the city and the best place to start a first visit. Go early in the morning to enjoy it before the crowds, and wear comfortable shoes for the cobbles and hills.

  2. 2

    Dom Luís I Bridge

    This iron arch bridge is Porto's icon, linking the city with Vila Nova de Gaia across the Douro. Walk the upper deck for sweeping views, especially at sunset. The lower deck connects to Ribeira, and the upper level is reached by metro or a steep walk.

  3. 3

    Livraria Lello

    One of the world's most beautiful bookshops, with a sweeping red staircase and ornate woodwork that reportedly inspired Harry Potter. Book a timed ticket online in advance, as queues are long and entry is ticketed. It is a short walk from Clerigos Tower.

  4. 4

    Clerigos Tower

    This baroque tower is the city's tallest landmark and offers a panoramic view over the rooftops after a climb of around 240 steps. Climb in the morning for clearer light and shorter lines. It sits in the center, near Livraria Lello and São Bento station.

  5. 5

    Palácio da Bolsa

    The former stock exchange is famous for its lavish Arabian Hall and grand interiors. Guided visits are the only way in, so check times and book ahead. It is a few minutes' walk from Ribeira and São Francisco Church.

  6. 6

    Porto Cathedral

    The city's Romanesque cathedral sits on a hilltop with a terrace overlooking the old town and river. It is a calm, historic stop and a good starting point for walking down to Ribeira. Dress respectfully and go early to avoid tour groups.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for the Douro valley by train from São Bento; check times on the day.
    • Take a short coastal train or bus to Matosinhos for seafood and the beach.
    • Add a day trip to Guimarães or Braga by train for history and old streets.

    Getting around

    • Metro line E links the airport to the centre; validate your Andante card before boarding.
    • STCP buses cover the city; night bus 3M runs to the airport hourly.
    • Uber and Bolt work well; airport rides cost about €10-25.
    • Driving is chaotic; narrow hilly streets and hard parking make it best avoided.
    • Walking is the best way around the centre, though it is hilly.
    • Douro river boats and the Leixões harbour serve cruise and tour trips.

    Where to stay

    • Ribeira · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      On the river under the old houses. Walk to the bridge, boats and Port cellars. Noisy and hilly at night.
    • Baixa · ≈ EUR 80–200 a night
      Flat, central and close to São Bento station, Bolhão market and metro. Best base for a first visit.
    • Cedofeita · ≈ EUR 60–150 a night
      Quieter streets, galleries and cafés. Good value and a short walk or metro ride to the centre.
    • Boavista · ≈ EUR 90–230 a night
      Modern hotels, the Casa da Música and Serralves nearby. Further out; use the metro or buses.

    Food & drink

    • Try a francesinha (thick sandwich with sauce) at a local tasca. Portions are big; share if unsure.
    • Bacalhau (salt cod) comes many ways. Ask what is fresh that day.
    • Cross to Vila Nova de Gaia for Port cellar tours and tastings; book ahead in summer.
    • Bolhão market in Baixa is good for cheese, ham and a cheap lunch.
    • Sit down for grilled fish near the river, but check prices first. Expect around €12–25 a main.

    Tips

    1. Try the Francesinha, a massive, meat-heavy local sandwich smothered in melted cheese and a tomato-beer sauce
    2. Cross the iconic Dom Luís I Bridge on foot for spectacular views of the river and city
    3. Visit the Port wine cellars located just across the river in Vila Nova de Gaia
    4. Wear very comfortable shoes with good grip for the steep, slippery cobblestone streets
    5. Buy an Andante card for seamless use of the metro and buses
