Getting around

CTA 'L' trains and buses for most trips; walk along the lakefront.

  • ✓Metro ★CTA 'L' trains run 24 hours on some lines; fares about $2.50, $5 from O'Hare.
  • ✓Bus ★CTA buses cover the city; pay with Ventra card or contactless.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are plentiful but pricey in rush hour; Uber and Lyft both work.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is slow and parking is costly; skip a car downtown.
  • ✓WalkingWalking and Divvy bike-share are great along the lakefront.
  • ✗FerryNo ferries for visitors; the lakefront path is the best route.

Climate

Continental climate: cold winters, warm summers, and rain spread through the year.

  • ❄️Cold winters (Dec–Feb)Highs near 0–4°C and lows down to -7°C. Wind off the lake makes it feel colder.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 8–14 rain days each month; April is wettest. Carry a light rain jacket.
  • ☀️Best months: May–SeptemberHighs 19–27°C. Ideal for the lakefront, walking and outdoor dining.
  • 🥵Summer heatJuly and August peak near 27°C with humid days. Plan indoor breaks at midday.
  • 🍂Crisp fallSeptember and October cool to 17–24°C with fewer rain days. Good for parks.
  • 🧥What to packLayers year-round; a warm coat, hat and gloves for winter; a rain jacket and comfortable shoes for walking.

Landmarks 32

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Cloud Gate

    This mirrored sculpture, known as 'The Bean', is Chicago's most iconic photo spot and a must-see for its playful reflection of the skyline. Visit early in the morning to avoid crowds and get a clean shot.

  2. 2

    Art Institute of Chicago

    World-class museum housing masterpieces like 'A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte' and 'American Gothic'. Book tickets online and arrive at opening to see the highlights before it gets busy.

  3. 3

    Willis Tower

    Formerly the tallest building in the world, its Skydeck offers thrilling views from glass boxes. Go at sunset for the best light, but buy tickets in advance to skip lines.

  4. 4

    Magnificent Mile

    A premier shopping and dining avenue with stunning architecture. Stroll it during the day to enjoy the bustling atmosphere and window shopping.

  5. 5

    Navy Pier

    A lively harbor with rides, restaurants, and lake cruises. Visit on a weekday to avoid crowds and catch the evening fireworks in summer.

  6. 6

    Chicago Architecture Tour

    A boat cruise along the Chicago River showcasing architectural gems. Book a daytime tour for clear views and bring a jacket for the breeze.

  7. 7

    Millennium Park

    A vibrant park featuring Cloud Gate and beautiful gardens. It's free to enter; go on a sunny day for a picnic or a leisurely walk.

  8. 8

    Shedd Aquarium

    One of the first inland aquariums, home to belugas and penguins. Purchase tickets online and visit on a weekday morning for fewer crowds.

    Michigan City · 1 h 22 by train

    ★★★☆☆ · Full day · 95 km from Chicago

    Best way: The South Shore Line runs direct from downtown Chicago in about 1h 22m, but you will need a taxi or rideshare to reach the dunes and trails.

    Take the South Shore Line commuter train from Millennium Station in Chicago directly to Michigan City, about 1.5 hours, then use a taxi or rideshare to reach the Indiana Dunes beaches and trails.

    • Train ★1 h 22 · from $11-$13
    • Car1 h 20 · taxi ≈ $240

    What to see

    • Indiana Dunes National Park beaches
    • Dune trails such as Mount Baldy
    • Washington Park Beach and Michigan City lakefront
    • Old Michigan City lighthouse area

    Good to know

    • Weekend South Shore service can be less frequent than weekday service, so check the timetable.
    • The dune sights are spread across several areas, so a taxi or rideshare will make the most of your time there.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a downtown day for the Museum of Science and Industry or a lakefront walk in Hyde Park.
    • Take an architecture boat tour on the river if the weather is dry.
    • Spend an afternoon in Pilsen for murals and Mexican food.

    Getting around

    • CTA 'L' trains run 24 hours on some lines; fares about $2.50, $5 from O'Hare.
    • CTA buses cover the city; pay with Ventra card or contactless.
    • Taxis are plentiful but pricey in rush hour; Uber and Lyft both work.
    • Driving is slow and parking is costly; skip a car downtown.
    • Walking and Divvy bike-share are great along the lakefront.
    • No ferries for visitors; the lakefront path is the best route.

    Where to stay

    • The Loop · ≈ USD 150–380 a night
      Central for museums, the river walk and transit; easy walk to the lakefront.
    • River North · ≈ USD 170–420 a night
      Restaurants and galleries close by; short walk to the Magnificent Mile.
    • Magnificent Mile · ≈ USD 180–450 a night
      Shopping and lakefront access; good for first-time visitors.
    • Lincoln Park · ≈ USD 130–320 a night
      Quieter, near the zoo and park; cheaper than downtown.

    Food & drink

    • Try deep-dish pizza once; expect a 30–45 minute bake time, so order when you sit down.
    • Chicago-style hot dogs: no ketchup. Cheap lunch at many stands.
    • Italian beef sandwiches are a local staple; ask for it 'dipped'.
    • Explore neighborhood restaurants in Wicker Park, Logan Square and Pilsen for better value than downtown.
    • Book popular dinner spots ahead; weekends fill up fast.

    Tips

    1. The Ventra card covers all CTA trains (the 'L') and buses
    2. Deep-dish pizza is a sit-down meal, not a quick slice — expect 45+ minutes wait time
    3. Winter winds off Lake Michigan make it significantly colder than the thermometer shows — dress in layers
    4. The Magnificent Mile on Michigan Avenue is the main shopping strip
    5. Architecture boat tours on the Chicago River are one of the best ways to see the city
