Getting around

Trams and S-Bahn trains for almost everything; walking in the centre.

  • ✗MetroNo metro. Use trams and S-Bahn trains instead.
  • ✓BusTrams and buses cover the centre. Buy and validate tickets before boarding. Single zone 110 tickets cost about CHF 4-5; 24-hour cards about CHF 9-10.
  • ✓Train ★S-Bahn suburban trains link the centre with suburbs and the airport. Fast and frequent.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsUber and Bolt operate; local taxi apps also work. Useful late at night.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is congested and parking is expensive. Public transport is usually faster.
  • ✓Walking ★Walking is easy in the centre. Lake boats run from Bürkliplatz in summer.
  • ✓FerryLake boats from Bürkliplatz run in summer; a pleasant way to see the lake.

Climate

Temperate climate with four clear seasons. Summers are warm, winters are cold, and rain is possible in any month.

  • ☀️Best time: June to SeptemberHighs around 20–25°C and lows around 11–15°C. Best for the lake, boats and hill walks.
  • ❄️Winter is cold (December to February)Highs around 4–7°C and lows near -2 to 0°C. Pack a warm coat, hat and gloves.
  • 🌧️Rain in every monthAbout 12–18 rain days a month. May is the wettest. Always carry a small umbrella.
  • 🌂Plan indoor backupsMuseums and cafés are good rainy-day options. Check the forecast each morning.
  • 🌡️Big swings between seasonsFrom about 4°C in January to 25°C in July. Layers work best in spring and autumn.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable walking shoes, a waterproof layer and a warm jacket for winter. Light clothes for summer.

Landmarks 11

Must-see · 3

  1. 1

    Grossmünster

    Zurich's iconic twin-towered Romanesque church, the legendary birthplace of the Reformation in German-speaking Switzerland. Climb the tower for sweeping views over the old town and lake. Go early morning to avoid crowds; modest dress recommended.

  2. 2

    Fraumünster Church

    Famous for its stunning stained-glass windows by Marc Chagall and Augusto Giacometti, set in a serene Romanesque church. A must for art and architecture lovers. Visit on a weekday morning for quieter viewing; small entry fee for the church.

  3. 3

    Lindenhof

    A tranquil hilltop square with Roman ruins and a lime tree, offering the best free panorama of the Limmat River and old town. Perfect for a picnic or sunset stroll. Reach it via short stairs from the river; go at dusk for romantic views.

    Swap in instead

    • Take a lake boat from Bürkliplatz in summer and stop at a lakeside village for a walk.
    • Ride the train to Uetliberg hill for a short hike and city views; check the weather first.
    • Swap a city afternoon for a day trip to Lucerne or the Rhine Falls by train.

    Getting around

    • Trams and buses cover the centre; buy and validate tickets before boarding.
    • S-Bahn suburban trains link the centre with suburbs and the airport.
    • Single zone 110 tickets cost about CHF 4-5; 24-hour cards cost about CHF 9-10.
    • Ride apps like Uber and Bolt operate; local taxi apps also work.
    • Driving is congested and parking is expensive; public transport is faster.
    • Walking is easy in the centre; lake boats run from Bürkliplatz in summer.

    Where to stay

    • Altstadt · ≈ CHF 180–420 a night
      Right in the old town by the river and lake; walk to most sights. Quiet at night.
    • Bahnhofstrasse · ≈ CHF 220–550 a night
      Central and handy for the main station and trams; the shopping street on your doorstep.
    • Niederdorf · ≈ CHF 150–350 a night
      Lively old-town quarter with bars and cheap eats; good for nightlife and budget stays.
    • Enge · ≈ CHF 170–400 a night
      Quiet, leafy area near the lake and museums; a short tram ride to the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Try cheese fondue or raclette in an old-town restaurant; portions are big, so share.
    • Zurich-style sliced veal in cream sauce (Zürcher Geschnetzeltes) is the local classic.
    • Markthalle im Viadukt under the railway arches has many small food stalls; good for lunch.
    • Chocolate shops and cafés are everywhere; a hot chocolate is a cheap treat.
    • Supermarket sandwiches and takeaway are the budget option; restaurant meals cost much more.

    Tips

    1. Public transport is expensive but runs with absolute Swiss precision; always buy a ticket as spot checks are common
    2. Drink water from any of the hundreds of public fountains — it is fresh, highly regulated spring water
    3. Almost all shops are closed on Sundays, with the exception of those inside the main train station (Hauptbahnhof)
    4. English is widely spoken, but learning a quick 'Grüezi' (hello) in Swiss German is highly appreciated
    5. Swimming in Lake Zurich or the Limmat River is a major summer activity; look for the local 'Badis' (bathhouses)
