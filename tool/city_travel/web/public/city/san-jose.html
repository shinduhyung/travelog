Climate

Mediterranean-style: mild, wet winters and warm, almost rain-free summers.

  • ☀️Best time: May to OctoberWarm and mostly dry, highs 23–29°C. Ideal for parks, trails and outdoor dining.
  • 🌧️Wet wintersDecember to March brings about 9–10 rain days a month. Pack a rain jacket and plan indoor backups.
  • 🥵Hot summer afternoonsJuly and August reach about 29°C. Do hikes and walks in the morning; stay in the shade at midday.
  • 🌡️Cool evenings year-roundLows drop to 7–9°C in winter and about 15°C in summer. Bring a light layer for evenings.
  • 🍂Pleasant shoulder monthsApril, May and October are mild and mostly dry — good all-round months to visit.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a rain jacket for winter, comfortable shoes for walking, and sunscreen and a hat for summer.

Landmarks 16

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Winchester Mystery House

    A sprawling Victorian mansion famous for its maze-like layout, staircases to nowhere and doors that open onto walls. It is San Jose's most distinctive attraction and a must for first-time visitors. Book tickets online in advance and go early in the day to avoid the biggest crowds.

  2. 2

    Tech Interactive

    An interactive science and technology museum in downtown San Jose with hands-on exhibits that suit both kids and adults. It is a good rainy-day or family stop. Check opening days before you go, as hours vary by season.

  3. 3

    Rosicrucian Egyptian Museum

    Houses one of the larger collections of Egyptian artifacts on the US West Coast, set in a distinctive Egyptian-style building. It is a quieter, focused museum that rewards a couple of hours. Allow extra time to walk the surrounding park.

  4. 4

    San Jose Municipal Rose Garden

    A free, well-kept rose garden that is especially pretty in spring and early summer when the blooms peak. It is an easy, relaxing stop. Visit in the morning for softer light and fewer people.

  5. 5

    Plaza de Cesar Chavez

    The central downtown square, close to museums and the Tech Interactive, and a natural starting point for a walking day. It often hosts events. Combine it with nearby downtown sights rather than making a special trip.

  6. 6

    San Pedro Square Market

    A lively downtown market hall with many food stalls and a sociable atmosphere, good for a casual meal. It is a practical lunch or dinner stop near the other downtown sights. Go slightly off peak hours to find seating.

  7. 7

    History Park

    An outdoor history park with historic buildings and exhibits that give a sense of the region's past. It is a pleasant, low-key stop, especially for families. Check the schedule, as some buildings open only at certain times.

  8. 8

    San Jose Museum of Art

    A downtown art museum with a focus on modern and contemporary work. It pairs well with the Tech Interactive and the plaza for a compact downtown day. Confirm current exhibitions and hours before visiting.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for the Rosicrucian Egyptian Museum and the rose garden next door; a calm, cheap half-day.
    • Nature lovers: swap an afternoon for a hike at Alum Rock Park in the eastern hills; go early in summer.
    • Have a car? Drive to the coast (Santa Cruz) or the hills for a half-day; check traffic before you go.

    Getting around

    • VTA light rail links downtown, the airport area, and suburbs.
    • VTA buses cover the city; Clipper card or contactless pay works.
    • Taxis are metered; Uber and Lyft operate widely.
    • Driving is easy but parking downtown costs money; rush hour is slow.
    • Downtown is walkable; bikes and scooters are common.
    • No ferry service in San Jose.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown · ≈ USD 130–320 a night
      Walk to museums, the convention center and the San Pedro Square food spots. Best base without a car.
    • SoFA District · ≈ USD 120–300 a night
      Gallery and nightlife quarter south of downtown; close to the convention center and the arts scene.
    • Santana Row · ≈ USD 220–480 a night
      Shops, restaurants and a lively evening scene; good for couples and shoppers, but you'll want a car or ride app.
    • Japantown · ≈ USD 110–260 a night
      Quiet, walkable and full of Japanese restaurants and markets; a short ride from downtown.

    Food & drink

    • Try a Mission-style burrito or a plate of tacos at a taqueria; many are cheap and filling, roughly $10–15.
    • San Pedro Square Market downtown: many small stalls under one roof, good for groups and picky eaters.
    • Japantown for ramen, sushi and Japanese bakeries; walk the few blocks and pick what looks busy.
    • The area grows much of the region's produce; look for farm-to-table menus and weekend farmers' markets.
    • Book popular dinner spots ahead for Friday and Saturday; walk-ins can mean a long wait.

    Tips

    1. Renting a car is necessary for getting around the sprawling Silicon Valley
    2. Visit the Tech Interactive museum and the quirky Winchester Mystery House
    3. The cost of living, dining, and housing is among the highest in the US
    4. Traffic on Highway 101 and I-280 is notoriously severe
    5. Santana Row is a popular outdoor center for high-end shopping and dining
