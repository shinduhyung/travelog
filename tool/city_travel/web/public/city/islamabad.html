Getting around

Ride apps or taxis for most trips; the Metro Bus is cheap for the Islamabad–Rawalpindi run.

  • ✓Metro ★Metro Bus red line links Islamabad with Rawalpindi Saddar; a single ticket is about Rs 30. The Orange line shuttle runs from the airport parking lot (row 22) to the Metro network for about Rs 90.
  • ✓BusBlue and green bus lines use a separate ticketing system and run on regular roads.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis are plentiful; agree the fare first, roughly Rs 200 per sector. Careem and InDrive are the local favourite; Careem Bike is cheapest.
  • ✓Rental carCar hire with driver is easy. Useful for Taxila, Murree and hill trips.
  • ✓WalkingPleasant in the planned sectors, but distances are long. Use a ride for anything far.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cool dry winters, a hot pre-monsoon spring, a wet humid monsoon in July–August, and a dry pleasant autumn.

  • ☀️Best time: October to AprilMild to cool days and low rain. Ideal for Faisal Mosque, Taxila and the Margalla trails.
  • 🥵Hot before the monsoon (May–June)Highs of 34–36°C. Sightsee early morning and rest at midday.
  • 🌧️Monsoon in July–AugustAbout 19–20 rain days a month and humid nights. Short heavy showers; keep plans flexible.
  • 🌦️Spring showersMarch has about 11 rain days. A light rain jacket helps on hill walks.
  • 🧥Cold winter nightsJanuary lows near 6°C and December near 7°C. Bring a warm jacket for evenings.
  • 🧳What to packModest, breathable clothing; sturdy shoes for trails; a warm layer in winter; sun protection year-round.

Landmarks 10

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    Faisal Mosque

    One of the largest mosques in the world, its striking modern design and hilltop setting make it Islamabad's most iconic sight. Visit outside prayer times, dress modestly, and remove shoes; it is a short taxi ride from the city centre.

  2. 2

    Pakistan Monument

    A granite monument shaped like a blooming flower, with panoramic views over the city and a museum inside. Go late afternoon for sunset and city lights; tickets are inexpensive and it is easily reached by taxi.

  3. 3

    Margalla Hills

    Forested hills offering hiking trails and viewpoints right on the city's edge. Best visited early morning or late afternoon; bring water and wear sturdy shoes, and take a taxi to the trailheads.

  4. 4

    Saidpur Village

    A restored traditional village with cobbled lanes, cafes and craft shops at the foot of the Margallas. A pleasant stop for a meal or stroll; go on a weekday to avoid crowds and take a taxi.

  5. 5

    Rawal Lake

    A scenic lake with a park and walking paths, popular for picnics and boating. Visit in the cooler hours; entry to the park is cheap and taxis are readily available.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a half-day to Taxila's UNESCO ruins and museum, roughly an hour northwest by car.
    • Cross into old Rawalpindi for Raja Bazaar and the bazaars around Murree Road; go with a local or a guide.
    • In summer, head up to Murree or the Galyat hills for cooler air; check road conditions on the day.

    Getting around

    • Metro Bus red line links Islamabad with Rawalpindi Saddar; single ticket about Rs 30.
    • Orange line shuttle runs from the airport parking lot (row 22) to the Metro network for about Rs 90.
    • Blue and green bus lines use a separate ticketing system and run on regular roads.
    • Taxis are plentiful; agree the fare first, roughly Rs 200 per sector.
    • Careem and InDrive ride apps are the local favourite; Careem Bike is cheapest.
    • Car hire with driver is easy; walking is pleasant in the planned sectors but distances are long.

    Where to stay

    • Blue Area · ≈ PKR 8,000–22,000 a night
      Central business strip; easy taxi and Metro Bus access to most sights.
    • F-6 / F-7 · ≈ PKR 12,000–35,000 a night
      Leafy, walkable sectors with cafés, restaurants and upscale hotels near the Margalla foothills.
    • G-6 / G-8 · ≈ PKR 6,000–15,000 a night
      Good-value guesthouses and small hotels; close to Blue Area and the Metro Bus line.
    • E-7 / F-5 · ≈ PKR 15,000–40,000 a night
      Quiet, green and close to Faisal Mosque and the Margalla road; best for hill views.

    Food & drink

    • Try Pakistani classics: chicken karahi, seekh kebabs, haleem and biryani. Portions are big; share.
    • Look for a local 'food street' area for grilled meat and parathas in the evening; ask your hotel which is closest.
    • Melt-in-mouth nihari and halwa puri are traditional breakfasts; go early before they sell out.
    • Vegetarians are well served: daal, chana, saag and paneer dishes are on most menus.
    • Tap water is not safe to drink. Stick to bottled or filtered water, and skip ice if unsure.

    Tips

    1. Hike the well-maintained trails of the Margalla Hills for excellent views over the city
    2. Visit the Faisal Mosque, a masterpiece of modern Islamic architecture
    3. The city is divided into neat sectors (e.g., F-6, F-7) which have their own commercial centers (Markaz)
    4. It is noticeably quieter, cleaner, and more orderly than other major Pakistani cities
    5. Dress conservatively and respect local Islamic customs
