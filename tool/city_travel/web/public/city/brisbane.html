Getting around

Walk the CBD and South Bank, then use the 50c Translink fares and CityCat ferries for longer trips.

  • ✓MetroBrisbane Metro M1 and M2 buses run every 5–15 minutes along the busway. Fast and frequent.
  • ✓BusTranslink buses cover the city. A 50c flat fare covers most trips with up to 3 transfers.
  • ✓TrainTranslink trains link the CBD with the suburbs and airport line. Same 50c flat fare on most trips.
  • ✓Ferry ★CityCat ferries are a scenic and practical way to cross the river. A cheap sightseeing ride too.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsUber and DiDi operate; taxis are plentiful and take cards.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is easy outside peak hours, but parking in the CBD is costly.
  • ✓Walking ★The CBD is walkable, with riverside paths good for walking and cycling.

Climate

Subtropical: warm to hot most of the year, with a wet summer and a mild, dry winter.

  • ☀️Best time: April to SeptemberMild highs of 21–26°C and few rain days. Ideal for walking, ferries and day trips.
  • 🌧️Wet summer (December to March)About 12–16 rain days a month. Showers can be heavy but often short. Carry a rain jacket.
  • 🥵Hot and humid in summerHighs near 28–29°C with muggy air. Plan outdoor sights for early morning.
  • 🧥Cool winter nightsJune to August lows drop to 10–11°C. Bring a jacket for evenings by the river.
  • 🌂Rain is unevenAugust is the driest month with about 5 rain days. January and March are the wettest.
  • 🧳What to packLight layers, a rain jacket, sunscreen and a hat. Add a warm layer for winter evenings.

Landmarks 13

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    South Bank Parklands

    Brisbane's signature waterfront precinct, with a man-made beach, riverside walks and gardens, and it is the city's most famous sight. Go early in the morning or on a weekday to avoid the crowds, and bring swimwear if you want to use the free lagoon.

  2. 2

    Story Bridge

    An iconic steel bridge over the Brisbane River and one of the city's defining landmarks. Walk or cycle across for free river views, or view it lit up at night from the nearby riverside paths.

  3. 3

    Brisbane City Hall

    A grand heritage building in the heart of the city and a must-see for its architecture and central location. It is free to enter the public areas; check opening hours before you go and dress comfortably for the climb to the clock tower if available.

  4. 4

    Mount Coot-tha Lookout

    The classic spot for panoramic views over Brisbane and beyond. Go around sunset for the best light, and note that public transport is limited, so a car or rideshare is the easiest way up.

  5. 5

    Lone Pine Koala Sanctuary

    A world-famous wildlife sanctuary and the top pick for seeing Australian animals up close. Book tickets online in advance and arrive early, as it is a short drive from the city centre.

  6. 6

    Queensland Art Gallery

    A major free cultural attraction with strong Australian and international collections. Entry to the general collection is free; go on a weekday for a quieter visit.

  7. 7

    City Botanic Gardens

    A peaceful riverside garden right by the city centre, ideal for an easy stroll. Visit in the cooler morning hours and combine it with a walk along the Brisbane River.

  8. 8

    Queen Street Mall

    The city's central shopping and pedestrian hub, good for a quick taste of everyday Brisbane. It is busiest on weekends, so go on a weekday if you prefer space.

  9. 9

    Brisbane River

    The river defines the city and links many of its best sights. A river walk or ferry ride is a cheap, scenic way to get around; go at dusk for the best views.

  10. 10

    Howard Smith Wharves

    A revived riverside precinct under the Story Bridge, popular for walks and river views. It is liveliest in the evening; go earlier if you want a quieter stroll.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for a day trip to the koala sanctuary at Lone Pine. Check transport and entry prices on the day.
    • Take a CityCat ferry up the river for a cheap scenic ride instead of a paid cruise.
    • Add a day at Moreton Island or Stradbroke Island if you have a spare day. Book ferries ahead.

    Getting around

    • Translink runs trains, buses and ferries; a 50c flat fare covers most trips with up to 3 transfers.
    • Brisbane Metro M1 and M2 buses run every 5-15 minutes along the busway.
    • CityCat ferries are a scenic and practical way to cross the river.
    • Uber and DiDi operate; taxis are plentiful and take cards.
    • Driving is easy outside peak hours; parking in the CBD is costly.
    • The CBD is walkable, with riverside paths good for walking and cycling.

    Where to stay

    • CBD · ≈ AUD 180–420 a night
      Walk to the river, Queen Street Mall and the CityCat ferries. Best base for first-timers.
    • Fortitude Valley · ≈ AUD 150–350 a night
      Nightlife, live music and cheap eats. A short walk or one train stop from the CBD.
    • South Bank · ≈ AUD 200–480 a night
      Riverside parklands, the lagoon beach and museums. Family-friendly and walkable.
    • West End · ≈ AUD 140–320 a night
      Relaxed cafés and markets, close to South Bank. Good value for longer stays.

    Food & drink

    • Try a classic café breakfast: eggs, sourdough and good coffee. Brisbane takes coffee seriously.
    • Eat at the markets: weekend farmers' markets and the West End markets have cheap, fresh food.
    • Seafood by the river: fish and chips at South Bank or along the waterfront.
    • Fortitude Valley and West End have the widest range of Asian and Middle Eastern cheap eats.
    • Book ahead for riverside dinners on Friday and Saturday nights. Check opening hours on the day.

    Tips

    1. Get a Translink Go Card for all public transport including the CityCat ferries
    2. Take advantage of the free CityHopper ferry that loops around the inner city
    3. Visit South Bank for its man-made beach and extensive parklands
    4. Sun protection is absolutely critical year-round due to the intense Australian sun
    5. Be aware of swooping magpies during the spring breeding season
