Getting around

Use Uber, Bolt or SafeBoda for most trips; matatus are cheap but crowded.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Don't plan around rail in the city.
  • ✓Matatu (minibus)Fixed routes from Old Taxi Park and other stages; fares roughly UGX 1,000–3,000. Cheap but crowded and slow in traffic.
  • ✓Boda-bodaFast but risky. Agree the fare first, usually UGX 3,000–20,000 in town. App bodas like SafeBoda include helmets.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber, Bolt, Faras and SafeBoda work in Kampala. Special-hire taxis rarely use meters; agree a fare, often UGX 15,000–20,000 for central trips.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is possible but hectic. Traffic is heavy and rain slows it further.
  • ✓WalkingFine by day in central areas. Use rides at night and in heavy rain.

Climate

Kampala sits near the equator: warm days, mild nights and rain in every month, heaviest from March to May and October to November.

  • 🌡️Warm days, mild nightsHighs stay around 25–28°C all year and lows around 17–19°C. Light layers are enough.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearEvery month has rain days, from about 13 in January and July to 26 in April.
  • ☔Wettest monthsApril, May, October and November are the rainiest. Expect short, heavy afternoon showers.
  • 🌤️Drier windowsJanuary, February and July have the fewest rain days. Still carry a rain jacket.
  • 🛣️Rain and trafficHeavy rain slows traffic and floods some roads. Build extra time into rides in wet months.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, a rain jacket or umbrella, quick-dry shoes and mosquito repellent for evenings.

Landmarks 7

Must-see · 4

  1. 1

    Kasubi Tombs

    A UNESCO World Heritage site and the royal burial ground of the Buganda kings, this thatched-domed complex is the most important cultural site in Uganda. It is a must-see for understanding Buganda history and traditions. Practical tip: visit in the morning to avoid crowds; entry is by guided tour, and modest dress is appreciated.

  2. 2

    Gaddafi National Mosque

    One of Africa's largest mosques, it offers a striking blend of Islamic architecture and panoramic views from its minaret. The climb to the top is worth it for a 360-degree look over Kampala. Practical tip: dress conservatively (women should cover hair and shoulders), and avoid prayer times; a small entry fee applies.

  3. 3

    Namugongo Martyrs Shrine

    A major pilgrimage site commemorating the 22 Ugandan martyrs, with a moving history and impressive modern basilica. It is especially vibrant during the June 3 pilgrimage. Practical tip: go on a weekday for a quieter visit; the site is about 12 km northeast of the centre and reachable by taxi.

  4. 4

    Baha'i House of Worship

    The only Baha'i temple in Africa, set in peaceful gardens on a hill with views over the city. Its distinctive nine-sided dome and tranquil atmosphere make it a refreshing stop. Practical tip: open daily, free entry, and easy to combine with a visit to the nearby Uganda National Museum.

    • Add a day for the Uganda Museum and the Kasubi Tombs area (check opening hours on the day).
    • Take a boat trip on Lake Victoria or visit the Uganda Wildlife Education Centre in Entebbe.
    • Shop for crafts and fabrics at the National Theatre craft market and Owino market with a local guide.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; public transport is matatu minibuses, boda-bodas and special-hire taxis.
    • Matatus run fixed routes from the Old Taxi Park and other stages; fares are roughly UGX 1,000-3,000.
    • Boda-bodas are fast but risky; agree the fare first, usually UGX 3,000-20,000 in town.
    • Uber, Bolt, Faras and SafeBoda apps work in Kampala; app bodas include helmets.
    • Special-hire taxis rarely use meters; agree a fare, often UGX 15,000-20,000 for central trips.
    • Driving is possible but hectic; walking is fine by day in central areas, and there is no ferry service.

    Where to stay

    • Kololo · ≈ UGX 250,000–700,000 a night
      Quiet, leafy hill close to the centre; many embassies, cafés and mid-range to upscale hotels.
    • Nakasero · ≈ UGX 150,000–450,000 a night
      Central and walkable to the market, shops and offices; good mix of budget and business hotels.
    • Bugolobi · ≈ UGX 200,000–550,000 a night
      Relaxed suburb with a popular mall, restaurants and easy rides to the centre.
    • Munyonyo · ≈ UGX 300,000–900,000 a night
      On Lake Victoria; resorts and lake views, but 20–40 minutes by car from the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Start with a rolex: a chapati rolled with eggs and vegetables, a cheap street breakfast or lunch.
    • Try luwombo (meat or chicken steamed in banana leaves) and matoke at a local restaurant.
    • Eat grilled tilapia by Lake Victoria in Munyonyo or at lakeside spots; check the price per fish first.
    • For cheap eats, look for local cafeterias serving beans, rice and posho; a meal is often a few thousand UGX.
    • Drink bottled or filtered water, and be cautious with raw salads and street food if your stomach is sensitive.

    Tips

    1. Use the SafeBoda app to hire motorcycle taxis whose riders provide helmets and obey traffic laws
    2. Eat a 'Rolex', the legendary Ugandan street food consisting of an omelette rolled inside a chapati
    3. Traffic is incredibly slow, so factor in significant buffer times for any car journey
    4. Visit the Kasubi Tombs (if open) and the massive Gaddafi National Mosque
    5. English and Luganda are the primary languages spoken
