Getting around

Metro and trams for the centre; walk between the Duomo, Brera and Navigli.

  • ✓Metro ★Lines M1–M5 cover the centre. Tickets cost €2.20 for 90 minutes.
  • ✓BusTrams and buses run frequently. Buy tickets at newsagents or by app.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered; queues are long at peak hours and during Fashion Week. Uber works in Milan and ride apps are common for airport runs.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the centre is restricted (Area C). Use park-and-ride at metro termini.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is flat and walkable. Bike sharing is easy to find.

Climate

Northern Italian climate: cold, damp winters and warm to hot summers, with rain spread across the year.

  • ☀️Best time: April to June, September to OctoberMild days around 18–27°C. Comfortable for walking and sightseeing.
  • 🥵Summer is hot (July–August)Highs near 30°C and humid. Plan indoor sights at midday and go out in the evening.
  • 🌧️Rain in every monthMay is wettest, with rain on about 16 days. Carry a small umbrella year-round.
  • ❄️Cold winters (December–February)Highs around 8°C and lows near 0°C. A warm coat and layers are essential.
  • ⛈️Summer thunderstormsShort, heavy storms are common in July and August. They pass quickly.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes for cobbles; a hat and sunscreen for summer.

Landmarks 18

Must-see · 4

  1. 1

    Duomo di Milano

    Milan's cathedral is the city's defining icon, with a vast marble facade and a rooftop terrace of spires and statues. Book rooftop tickets online in advance and go early morning to beat the crowds; dress modestly as it is a working church.

  2. 2

    Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II

    This grand 19th-century glass-roofed arcade links the Duomo to Teatro alla Scala and is a must-see for its mosaics and architecture. It is free to walk through; visit early or late to avoid the busiest crowds.

  3. 3

    Sforza Castle

    The former ducal fortress houses major museums and courtyards in the heart of the city. The courtyards are free to enter, and it is an easy walk from the Duomo; check museum hours before going.

  4. 4

    Piazza del Duomo

    The central square is the natural starting point for any Milan visit, framed by the cathedral and the Galleria. It is open at all hours and free; arrive early for the calmest photos.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for a Lake Como day trip by train from Milano Centrale; check times and fares on the day.
    • Design fans can add a half-day at the Triennale design museum and the Porta Nuova towers.
    • Football lovers: check whether AC Milan or Inter play at San Siro during your dates and book early.

    Getting around

    • Metro lines M1-M5 cover the centre; tickets cost €2.20 for 90 minutes.
    • Trams and buses run frequently; buy tickets at newsagents or by app.
    • Taxis are metered; queues are long at peak hours and during Fashion Week.
    • Uber works in Milan; ride apps are common for airport runs.
    • Driving in the centre is restricted (Area C); use park-and-ride at metro termini.
    • The centre is flat and walkable; bike sharing is easy to find.

    Where to stay

    • Duomo · ≈ EUR 150–400 a night
      Steps from the cathedral, metro hub and shopping. Noisiest and priciest, but you walk to everything.
    • Brera · ≈ EUR 140–350 a night
      Quiet cobbled lanes, the Pinacoteca and good restaurants. Central and walkable.
    • Navigli · ≈ EUR 110–280 a night
      Canals, aperitivo bars and nightlife. Livelier and cheaper; 20–25 minutes' walk to the Duomo.
    • Porta Nuova · ≈ EUR 130–320 a night
      Modern towers, Bosco Verticale and easy metro links. Good value near the station.

    Food & drink

    • Try risotto alla milanese (saffron rice) and cotoletta, a breaded veal cutlet.
    • Aperitivo is a Milan ritual: a drink around €8–12 often comes with a buffet of snacks, roughly 6–9pm.
    • Panzerotti and pizza slices are cheap eats, about €3–6. Busy bakeries mean fresh food.
    • Book the Last Supper and popular trattorias ahead; many good spots fill up days in advance.
    • Coffee standing at the bar costs less than sitting at a table. Check the price list.

    Tips

    1. Aperitivo hour (usually 6-9 PM) is a quintessential Milanese experience; buy a drink, get a buffet of food
    2. Tickets for Da Vinci’s 'The Last Supper' sell out months in advance — book early
    3. Dress code tends to be more polished and formal than in other Italian cities
    4. Contactless payment works directly on metro turnstiles and trams
    5. August is traditionally a holiday month; many smaller restaurants and shops may close
