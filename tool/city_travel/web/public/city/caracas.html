Getting around

Metro for most cross-city trips; agree taxi prices first or use a ride app.

  • ✓Metro ★Clean, cheap and fast. Buy a single, round trip or 10-journey multi abono.
  • ✓BusMetrobuses link metro stations to areas off the underground, but run roughly every 20 minutes. Por puesto minibuses are cheap but slow, crowded and often in poor condition.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis have no meters; agree the price first and expect quotes near double the fair fare. Uber and inDrive work but coverage varies; confirm before relying on them.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is hectic. Only worth it for trips outside the city.
  • ✓WalkingFine by day in safe districts, but avoid quiet streets after dark.

Climate

Mild all year at this altitude: highs around 26–28°C, cool nights, and rain in every month. The driest months are January and February.

  • 🌤️Mild all yearHighs stay around 26–28°C and lows around 17–20°C. No extreme heat or cold.
  • 🌧️Rain in every monthExpect 11–25 rain days a month. Showers are often short; a light rain jacket helps.
  • ☔Wettest: July and AugustAbout 24–25 rain days. Keep indoor options ready and check mountain trips on the day.
  • ☀️Driest: January and FebruaryAround 11–12 rain days. The best window for walking and outdoor plans.
  • 🌙Cool eveningsNights drop to 17–20°C. Bring a light layer for evenings out.
  • 🧳What to packLight layers, a rain jacket or compact umbrella, and comfortable shoes for hilly streets.

Landmarks 8

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    National Pantheon of Venezuela

    The country's most important memorial, where Simon Bolivar is buried, and the top landmark for first-time visitors. Go in the morning on a weekday to avoid crowds, and dress modestly since it is a working national shrine.

  2. 2

    Birthplace of Simon Bolivar

    A restored colonial house museum in the old center that shows where the liberator was born. It is a short walk from Plaza Bolivar, so pair the two in one outing; check opening hours before you go as they can be limited.

  3. 3

    Plaza Bolivar

    The historic heart of the old town, ringed by colonial buildings and close to the cathedral. Visit in daylight hours and keep valuables out of sight; it is an easy stop on foot from the center's main streets.

  4. 4

    University City of Caracas

    A UNESCO-listed modern architecture campus with striking murals and buildings by Carlos Raul Villanueva. Access to some areas is restricted, so go with a guide or on a weekday when the campus is active.

  5. 5

    Museo de Arte Contemporaneo

    One of the region's strongest modern art collections, worth a couple of hours for art lovers. Confirm opening days in advance and allow time to wander the surrounding park area.

    Swap in instead

    • Ride the cable car up El Ávila (Waraira Repano) for city views and cool air; check it is running on the day.
    • Add a day for the colonial centre and the birthplace of Simón Bolívar, if it is open to visitors.
    • Food lovers can swap one museum morning for a market visit and a long lunch in Las Mercedes.

    Getting around

    • Metro is clean, cheap and fast; buy a single, round trip or 10-journey multi abono.
    • Metrobuses link metro stations to areas off the underground, but run roughly every 20 minutes.
    • Por puesto minibuses are cheap but slow, crowded and often in poor condition.
    • Taxis have no meters; agree the price first and expect quotes near double the fair fare.
    • Ride apps like Uber and inDrive work in Caracas but coverage varies; confirm before relying on them.
    • Driving is hectic; walking is fine by day in safe districts, but avoid quiet streets after dark.

    Where to stay

    • Altamira · ≈ VES 40–140 a night
      Central, walkable and close to the metro; good base for museums and plazas. Check the exact rate on the day.
    • Las Mercedes · ≈ VES 50–180 a night
      Best for food and nightlife; many restaurants and bars within walking distance.
    • Bellas Artes · ≈ VES 30–110 a night
      Cheapest of the four and near the main museums and parks; busier and less polished at night.
    • La Castellana · ≈ VES 45–160 a night
      Quiet, leafy streets with cafés and offices; a calm base between Altamira and Las Mercedes.

    Food & drink

    • Try an arepa: grilled corn bread filled with cheese, shredded beef or black beans. Cheap and everywhere.
    • Pabellón criollo is the national plate: shredded beef, rice, black beans and fried plantain.
    • Las Mercedes has the widest choice of restaurants and cafés; book ahead on weekends.
    • Street grills and bakeries are the cheapest meals. Prices in VES move fast, so ask before you order.
    • Coffee is strong and local. Order a cortadito at a café counter for a quick, cheap stop.

    Tips

    1. Safety is a major concern; avoid walking at night, displaying valuables, or visiting unfamiliar neighborhoods
    2. Take the cable car (Teleférico) up El Ávila for stunning views of the valley and the sea
    3. Arepas and cachapas are essential, delicious local staples
    4. The US Dollar is widely used in daily transactions alongside the Bolivar
    5. Use trusted, pre-arranged transport rather than hailing street taxis

      Souvenirs

      Venezuelan single-origin chocolate

      USD 10–2060-100 g, 2-3 bars · estimate

      Venezuela is known for fine cacao, and single-origin bars show the bean's origin and cocoa percentage on the label. Buying them in Caracas means you are getting chocolate from the country that grows the beans.

      Where: KaKao or Cacao de Origen, both in Caracas. KaKao is at C.C. Paseo Las Mercedes; Cacao de Origen is at Hacienda La Trinidad.
      Check the cocoa percentage and origin on the wrapper. Budget in US dollars, since prices are often quoted that way.

      Aged Venezuelan rum

      USD 20–45700-750 ml bottle · estimate

      Venezuelan aged rum is a well-known local spirit, and Ron Santa Teresa is one of the country's most recognized brands. It makes a practical gift that connects you to the city's drinking culture.

      Where: Specialty liquor shops that carry Ron Santa Teresa.
      Make sure the seal is intact and the bottle shows Venezuelan origin before you pay.
