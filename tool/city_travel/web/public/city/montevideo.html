Getting around

Walk in the centre and use ride apps or buses for longer trips.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Buses are the only mass transit.
  • ✓Bus ★Get a free STM card at Abitab; cash fare is about UYU 36, card fares cheaper. STM gives 2 buses in 1 hr for about UYU 29, or unlimited buses in 2 hr for about UYU 44.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Ride apps like Uber work and are often cheaper and safer than street taxis. Taxis are metered; a 10-minute ride costs about UYU 100.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is possible but parking is scarce and expensive. Walking is easier in the centre.
  • ✓WalkingEasy in the centre and along the Rambla. Use buses or apps for longer hops.
  • ✓FerryBuquebus ferries link Ciudad Vieja with Buenos Aires and Colonia.

Climate

Mild four-season climate with warm summers and cool winters. Rain falls all year, so keep plans flexible.

  • ☀️Best time: December to MarchWarm days around 24–26°C and mild nights. Best for beaches and the Rambla.
  • 🧥Cool winters (June to August)Highs near 14–15°C and lows near 9–10°C. Bring a warm jacket and layers.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 7–10 rain days every month. October is the wettest. A small umbrella helps.
  • 🌬️Breezy coastThe Rambla is windy; a light windproof layer is useful even in summer.
  • 🌡️No extreme heatSummers are warm, not scorching. Evenings stay comfortable for outdoor dinners.
  • 🧳What to packSummer: light clothes, sunscreen and a hat. Winter: warm layers, a coat and closed shoes.

Landmarks 13

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Ciudad Vieja

    Montevideo's historic old town is the heart of the city, packed with colonial-era streets, plazas and cafés. Wander it on foot; mornings are quieter and safer for strolling, and it sits right next to the port.

  2. 2

    Palacio Salvo

    This landmark palace towers over Plaza Independencia and is Montevideo's most recognizable silhouette. Check opening hours before going up for views, and pair it with a walk around the plaza.

  3. 3

    Plaza Independencia

    The main square links the old town with the modern city and is the natural starting point for sightseeing. Visit in the morning or early evening; it is an easy walk from Ciudad Vieja.

  4. 4

    Teatro Solis

    One of South America's grand old theaters, worth seeing for its architecture and performances. Book tickets or a guided visit in advance, and dress smartly for evening shows.

  5. 5

    Rambla de Montevideo

    The long waterfront promenade is where locals walk, run and watch the sunset over the river. Go late afternoon for the best light; it is free and easy to reach from most of the city.

  6. 6

    Mercado del Puerto

    The port market is the classic spot for grilled meat and local atmosphere. Arrive early for lunch to beat the crowds, and expect a lively, casual setting.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; buses are the only mass transit.
    • Get a free STM card at Abitab; cash fare is about UYU 36, card fares cheaper.
    • STM gives 2 buses in 1 hr for about UYU 29, or unlimited buses in 2 hr for about UYU 44.
    • Ride apps like Uber work and are often cheaper and safer than street taxis.
    • Taxis are metered; a 10-minute ride costs about UYU 100.
    • Driving is possible but parking is scarce and expensive; walking is easy in the centre.
    • Buquebus ferries link Ciudad Vieja with Buenos Aires and Colonia.

    Where to stay

    • Ciudad Vieja · ≈ UYU 1,800–4,500 a night
      Old-town squares, Mercado del Puerto and the Buquebus ferry terminal on foot. Lively by day, quiet at night.
    • El Centro · ≈ UYU 1,500–3,500 a night
      Central and good value. Walk to Plaza Independencia and the Rambla; many buses pass through.
    • Pocitos · ≈ UYU 2,200–5,500 a night
      Beach neighbourhood on the Rambla. Cafés, safe evening walks and quick rides to the centre.
    • Carrasco · ≈ UYU 3,500–8,000 a night
      Leafy, calm and near the airport. Best if you want quiet nights and a garden hotel.

    Food & drink

    • Try Uruguayan staples: asado (grilled beef), chivito (a loaded steak sandwich) and milanesa.
    • Mercado del Puerto near the port is the classic stop for grilled meat; go at lunch, prices are mid to high.
    • A mate gourd and thermos is the local habit. Buy a cheap set at a market and join in.
    • Bakeries sell bizcochos and medialunas for a cheap breakfast, about UYU 100–200.
    • Dinner starts late, often after 21:00. Many restaurants close on Sunday night; check on the day.

    Tips

    1. Stroll or rent a bike to travel along the Rambla, the massive coastal promenade
    2. Locals carry a thermos and mate gourd everywhere; it is a vital cultural ritual
    3. Try a Chivito, a legendary and massive Uruguayan steak sandwich
    4. The city is widely considered one of the safest and most relaxed capitals in Latin America
    5. Visit the Mercado del Puerto for an incredible, meat-heavy traditional Asado lunch
