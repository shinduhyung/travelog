Getting around

Grab for cars and motorbike taxis; walk in the Old Quarter.

  • ✓MetroLine 2A runs Cat Linh to Yen Nghia; Line 3 runs Cau Giay to Nhon. Fares are distance-based, about 8,000–15,000 VND; tap contactless cards at gates.
  • ✓BusCity buses are cheap and air-conditioned, about 7,000–12,000 VND per ride.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Grab works well for cars and motorbike taxis; fares are fixed in the app. Metered taxis: Mai Linh and Taxi Group are reliable; flag-fall about 20,000 VND.
  • ✗Rental carNot practical for most visitors: chaotic traffic and hard parking. Use Grab or taxis instead.
  • ✓Walking ★Fine in the Old Quarter, but crossing takes nerve. Traffic is chaotic; take your time at crossings.

Climate

Subtropical Hanoi: mild, drier winters and hot, very wet summers. Rain peaks from July to September.

  • 🌤️Best time: October to AprilMilder days around 21–29°C and fewer rain days. Good for walking the Old Quarter.
  • 🥵Hot, humid summer (May to September)Highs of 31–33°C with many rain days. Sightsee early morning and after showers.
  • 🌧️Wettest months: July to SeptemberAround 21–24 rain days a month. Showers can be heavy; keep a raincoat handy.
  • 🧥Cool winter (December to February)Highs near 21–22°C and lows around 14–15°C. Bring a jacket for evenings.
  • ☂️Rain is often shortEven in wet months, showers pass. Plan indoor stops like museums and cafés as backup.
  • 🧳What to packLight layers, a raincoat or umbrella, comfortable shoes for uneven streets, and a warm layer for winter.

Landmarks 18

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum

    The embalmed body of Vietnam's founding father lies in a vast granite mausoleum on Ba Dinh Square, a pilgrimage site for locals and a sober must-see for visitors. Go early morning (closed Mondays and Fridays, and for several weeks each autumn); dress modestly with shoulders and knees covered, and leave cameras and bags at the entrance.

  2. 2

    Hanoi Old Quarter

    A dense maze of 36 historic guild streets where motorbikes, street vendors and colonial shophouses collide—the best place to feel Hanoi's energy. Wander on foot in the late afternoon or early evening when the heat eases and the beer corners fill up.

  3. 3

    Temple of Literature

    Vietnam's first university, founded in 1070, with tranquil courtyards, stone stelae and Confucian temples—one of the city's most beautiful and historic sites. Visit early to beat the tour groups; tickets are cheap and the grounds are shady.

  4. 4

    Imperial Citadel of Thang Long

    A UNESCO-listed complex of royal palaces, gates and bunkers that layers a thousand years of Vietnamese history. The site is large and exposed, so go in the cooler morning hours and allow at least two hours.

  5. 5

    One Pillar Pagoda

    A tiny wooden pagoda perched on a single stone pillar, rebuilt after French destruction and now an icon of Hanoi. It sits near the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum, so combine the two in one early-morning outing.

  6. 6

    St. Joseph's Cathedral

    A neo-Gothic cathedral that looks straight out of Europe, surrounded by cafés and a lively square in the Old Quarter. Open outside mass times; dress respectfully and climb the bell tower if you can.

  7. 7

    Hoan Kiem Lake

    The heart of Hanoi, where locals jog at dawn and the red Huc Bridge leads to Ngoc Son Temple on a tiny island. Come at sunrise for tai chi and calm, or at night when the lake is lit up.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Ha Long Bay or Lan Ha Bay: a long day trip or an overnight on a boat.
    • Take a half-day to the Temple of Literature and the Imperial Citadel if you like history.
    • Spend an afternoon in Tay Ho for lakeside cafés and a quieter side of Hanoi.

    Getting around

    • Metro Line 2A runs Cat Linh to Yen Nghia; Line 3 runs Cau Giay to Nhon.
    • Metro fares are distance-based, about 8,000-15,000 VND; tap contactless cards at gates.
    • City buses are cheap and air-conditioned, about 7,000-12,000 VND per ride.
    • Grab works well for cars and motorbike taxis; fares are fixed in the app.
    • Metered taxis: Mai Linh and Taxi Group are reliable; flag-fall about 20,000 VND.
    • Traffic is chaotic; walking is fine in the Old Quarter but crossing takes nerve.

    Where to stay

    • Old Quarter · ≈ VND 350,000–1,500,000 a night
      Walk to Hoan Kiem Lake, street food and markets. Lively and loud; ask for a quiet room.
    • French Quarter · ≈ VND 900,000–3,500,000 a night
      Wide tree-lined streets, the Opera House and quieter nights, a short walk from the lake.
    • Hoan Kiem · ≈ VND 600,000–2,500,000 a night
      Central and handy for the lake, the Old Quarter edge and evening walks.
    • Tay Ho · ≈ VND 700,000–3,000,000 a night
      Lakeside expat area with cafés and quieter streets; 15–25 minutes by taxi to the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Start with pho for breakfast, bun cha at lunch and a bia hoi (fresh beer) corner in the evening.
    • Eat where locals sit: small stalls and family restaurants in the Old Quarter are often the best value.
    • Try egg coffee (ca phe trung) and strong drip coffee at a café near Hoan Kiem Lake.
    • Street food is cheap: expect roughly 30,000–80,000 VND for a bowl or plate; check on the day.
    • Drink bottled water and eat busy, freshly cooked stalls. Carry small notes for street vendors.

    Tips

    1. Grab is the best way to get around; haggling with street taxis or cyclos can lead to scams
    2. Cross the street by walking slowly and steadily; the sea of motorbikes will naturally avoid you
    3. Sit on tiny plastic stools in the Old Quarter to eat Pho or drink Bia Hoi (fresh draft beer)
    4. Winters (Jan-Feb) can be surprisingly cold and damp, so bring a jacket
    5. Traffic is chaotic and pedestrian sidewalks are mostly used for parking motorbikes and seating
