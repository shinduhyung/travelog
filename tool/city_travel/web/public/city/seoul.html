Getting around

Get a T-money card and use the metro for most trips; buses beat traffic on short hops.

  • ✓Metro ★9 numbered lines plus suburban lines. Base fare ₩1,550 with card, ₩1,650 single ticket. Stops late at night.
  • ✓Bus ★Buses have their own lanes and beat traffic. Use a T-money card for both bus and metro.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsMetered and plentiful. Kakao T is the main ride-hailing app; Uber works too.
  • ✓Rental carPossible but traffic jams are common and parking is costly. Not needed for most trips.
  • ✓WalkingGood in Myeongdong, Jongno and Hongdae. Use metro for longer hops between districts.
  • ✓Climate CardUnlimited 1–7 day transit passes from ₩5,000 to ₩20,000. Good value if you ride often.
  • ✓Night busSubway stops late at night; night buses run on major routes.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cold, dry winters and hot, rainy summers. Spring and autumn are the best times to visit.

  • 🌸Best time: April to May and September to OctoberMild highs of 18–25°C with fewer rainy days. Ideal for palaces, walking and markets.
  • 🥶Winter is cold and dryJanuary highs near 2°C and lows around -7°C, with only about 3 rain days. Bring a heavy coat.
  • 🌧️Rainy season in July and AugustAbout 18 and 16 rain days, with highs near 29°C. Expect heavy downpours; carry an umbrella.
  • 🥵Hot, humid summerJune to August feels muggy. Plan outdoor sights for morning and rest indoors in the afternoon.
  • 🍂Crisp autumnSeptember and October cool to 19–25°C with fewer wet days. The most comfortable months.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for spring and autumn, a warm coat for winter, and a rain jacket or umbrella for summer.

Landmarks 53

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Gyeongbokgung Palace

    The grandest of Seoul's royal palaces and the top sight for any first-time visitor, with ceremonial gates and mountain-backed courtyards. Go early morning to beat the crowds, and note the changing of the guard ceremony adds atmosphere.

  2. 2

    N Seoul Tower

    The city's most recognizable landmark on Namsan, offering sweeping views over the skyline. Ride the Namsan Cable Car up and go near sunset for day-to-night views; tickets are needed for the observation deck.

  3. 3

    Bukchon Hanok Village

    A hillside neighborhood of traditional hanok houses that makes for the classic Seoul photo walk. Visit on a weekday morning and keep quiet, as many buildings are private homes.

  4. 4

    Changdeokgung Palace and Huwon

    A UNESCO-listed palace whose Secret Garden is the highlight of many Seoul trips. The Huwon garden is visited on timed guided tours, so book ahead, especially in autumn.

  5. 5

    Dongdaemun Design Plaza

    A striking piece of modern architecture by Zaha Hadid that anchors the city's design district. It is free to walk around outside and easy to reach by subway; evenings light it up nicely.

  6. 6

    Gwanghwamun Square

    The ceremonial heart of Seoul in front of Gyeongbokgung, lined with statues and broad open space. It is a natural starting point for a palace day and is free and open at all hours.

  7. 7

    Myeongdong Cathedral

    A landmark Gothic-style church set among the busy shopping streets of Myeongdong. Dress modestly and visit outside mass times for a quiet look inside.

  8. 8

    Jongmyo Shrine

    A serene UNESCO royal ancestral shrine with a long, solemn hall. Visits are by guided tour only on most days, so check the schedule before you go.

  9. 9

    Hongdae Street

    The youthful, energetic district around the university, known for street performances and nightlife. Come in the evening when the streets are liveliest.

  10. 10

    Insadong Cultural Street

    A traditional street of tea houses, galleries and craft shops, good for souvenirs. It pairs easily with nearby palaces and is best explored on foot in the afternoon.

    • Add a day trip to Suwon Hwaseong Fortress or the DMZ tour; both need a full day, book ahead.

    Getting around

    • Metro: 9 numbered lines plus suburban lines; base fare ₩1,550 with card, ₩1,650 single ticket.
    • Buses have own lanes and beat traffic; use a T-money card for both bus and metro.
    • Taxis are metered and plentiful; Kakao T is the main ride-hailing app, Uber works too.
    • Climate Card gives 1-7 day unlimited passes from ₩5,000 to ₩20,000.
    • Driving is possible but traffic jams are common; parking is costly.
    • Subway stops late at night; night buses run on major routes.

    Where to stay

    • Myeongdong · ≈ KRW 90,000–220,000 a night
      Walk to the shopping streets, Namsan and the subway hub at Myeongdong station. Easiest base for first-timers.
    • Jongno · ≈ KRW 80,000–200,000 a night
      Next to Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon hanok lanes and Insadong. Quiet at night, central for sightseeing.
    • Gangnam · ≈ KRW 100,000–280,000 a night
      Modern hotels, big malls and nightlife. Good for business trips; longer rides to the old palaces.
    • Hongdae · ≈ KRW 70,000–170,000 a night
      Cheapest of the four, young and lively, close to the airport rail link. Noisy on weekend nights.

    Food & drink

    • Eat at Gwangjang Market: bindaetteok (mung-bean pancakes), mayak gimbap and knife-cut noodles. Expect roughly ₩5,000–12,000 a plate.
    • Korean BBQ is a group meal. Order pork belly or beef, wrap it in lettuce with garlic; expect ₩15,000–30,000 per person.
    • Street snacks in Myeongdong and Hongdae: tteokbokki, hotteok and fried chicken. Most items cost ₩3,000–8,000.
    • Cheap and filling: gimbap shops and university-area restaurants serve a full meal for about ₩6,000–10,000.
    • Many restaurants are open late. Look for 24-hour spots around markets and Hongdae; check on the day for closing times.

    Tips

    1. Purchase a T-money card from any convenience store; it is essential for all public transit
    2. Google Maps is heavily restricted for walking/driving directions — download Naver Map or KakaoMap
    3. Tipping is strictly not practiced in restaurants, taxis, or hotels
    4. Many restaurants and cafes, especially in areas like Hongdae and Gangnam, stay open late into the night
    5. Public restrooms in subway stations are widespread, free, and generally very clean
