Getting around

Metro for longer hops, walking in the centre, and Bolt or Uber at night.

  • ✓Metro ★Four lines, frequent and cheap. Use a contactless card or an Activ card.
  • ✓BusTrams and buses are extensive but crowded. Validate your card on boarding.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Use kiosks at the airport for taxis. Uber and Bolt work well in the city.
  • ✓Rental carTraffic is heavy at rush hour and parking is scarce in the centre. Skip it for city days.
  • ✓WalkingThe centre is walkable. Bikes are available but bike lanes are limited.
  • ✓TrainGara de Nord is the main station for day trips; check timetables on the day.
  • ✗FerryNo ferry services in Bucharest.

Climate

Continental climate: hot summers, cold winters, and rain spread through the year.

  • ☀️Best time: May, June and SeptemberWarm days around 22–27°C and long evenings. Ideal for walking the old town and parks.
  • 🥵Summer can be hotJuly and August reach about 30°C. Sightsee in the morning and evening; rest indoors at midday.
  • 🥶Winters are coldJanuary highs near 4°C and lows around -4°C. Bring a proper coat, hat and warm shoes.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearExpect roughly 5–12 rain days each month. June is wettest; September is driest. A small umbrella helps.
  • 🍂Spring and autumn are mildApril, May and October are comfortable for walking, with highs of 18–22°C.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes for cobblestones; a warm coat for December to February.

Landmarks 18

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Palace of the Parliament

    The world's heaviest building and a monumental symbol of the communist era, with over 1,000 rooms and opulent interiors. Book a guided tour in advance (passport required) and go early to avoid crowds.

  2. 2

    Old Town, Bucharest

    The historic heart of the city, packed with cobbled streets, 19th-century buildings, and lively cafes and bars. Best explored on foot in the late afternoon or evening when the area comes alive.

  3. 3

    Romanian Athenaeum

    A stunning neoclassical concert hall with a famous domed ceiling and frescoes, home to the George Enescu Philharmonic. Check the schedule for concerts; otherwise visit during the day for a peek at the lobby.

  4. 4

    Stavropoleos Monastery

    A tiny, beautifully carved Orthodox church and monastery tucked away in the Old Town, known for its exquisite frescoes and peaceful courtyard. Dress modestly and visit early morning for quiet reflection.

  5. 5

    Herastrau Park

    Bucharest's largest park, with a lake, gardens, and the open-air Village Museum inside. Rent a boat or bike, and combine with a visit to the museum; go on a weekday for fewer crowds.

  6. 6

    Village Museum

    An open-air museum in Herastrau Park showcasing traditional Romanian rural architecture with original houses and churches. Allow at least two hours; wear comfortable shoes as paths are uneven.

  7. 7

    Cărturești Carusel

    A spectacular bookstore in a restored 19th-century building, often called the most beautiful in Europe. It's free to enter; visit on a weekday morning to avoid the Instagram crowds.

  8. 8

    Revolution Square

    The site of the 1989 revolution that ended communism, surrounded by important buildings like the former Royal Palace. Combine with a visit to the National Museum of Art nearby.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for a train to Brașov or Sinaia; check timetables and buy tickets at Gara de Nord.
    • Add a half-day at the open-air Village Museum (Muzeul Satului) in Herăstrău park.
    • Slow down in Cișmigiu or Herăstrău park with a coffee and a walk instead of another museum.

    Getting around

    • Metro: four lines, frequent and cheap; use contactless card or Activ card.
    • Trams and buses: extensive but crowded; validate card on boarding.
    • Taxis: use kiosks at airport; apps like Uber and Bolt work well.
    • Driving: traffic heavy at rush hour; parking scarce in centre.
    • Walking: centre is walkable; bikes available but bike lanes limited.
    • No ferry services in Bucharest.

    Where to stay

    • Lipscani · ≈ RON 250–650 a night
      The old town: bars, cafés and sights on your doorstep. Noisy at weekends, so ask for a back room.
    • Centru Civic · ≈ RON 220–550 a night
      Next to the Palace of Parliament and the big boulevards. Quieter at night, good metro links.
    • Piața Victoriei · ≈ RON 280–700 a night
      Central and calm, near the museums and parks. Easy metro and airport bus connections.
    • Gara de Nord · ≈ RON 180–450 a night
      Cheapest beds and the main train station. Handy for day trips, but a 25–30 minute walk to the old town.

    Food & drink

    • Try the classics: sarmale (stuffed cabbage rolls), mici (grilled sausages) and ciorbă (sour soup).
    • Look for a 'bistro' or 'cramă' lunch menu; a two-course set lunch is often good value.
    • Obor market is the place for cheese, produce and cheap local snacks; go in the morning.
    • Vegetarian and vegan spots are growing fast in the centre; check menus before you go.
    • Prices vary widely. Check the bill and confirm service charges in touristy old-town places.

    Tips

    1. You must book a guided tour in advance to enter the Palace of the Parliament
    2. The Old Town (Centrul Vechi) is almost entirely dedicated to energetic bars, clubs, and restaurants
    3. Use ride-hailing apps instead of street taxis to avoid being overcharged
    4. Internet speeds in cafes and hotels are famously among the fastest in Europe
    5. Tap water is technically safe, but locals generally prefer to drink bottled water

    Souvenirs

    Horezu pottery bowl

    RON 50–1501 small bowl · estimate

    This is traditional Romanian folk pottery from the town of Horezu. Its hand-painted designs are a well-known part of Romania's craft heritage.

    Where: My Romanian Store, central Bucharest
    Check that the piece is signed by the artist.

    Traditional Romanian embroidered blouse (ie)

    RON 250–7001 cotton blouse · estimate

    The ie is the classic embroidered blouse worn in Romanian folk dress. Its patterned embroidery varies by region and is a common keepsake from Bucharest.

    Where: My Romanian Store
    Hand embroidery costs much more than machine embroidery, so ask which one you are buying.
