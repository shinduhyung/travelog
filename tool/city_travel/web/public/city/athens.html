Getting around

Metro for the centre and airport; walk in Plaka and Monastiraki.

  • ✓Metro ★3 lines, running about 05:30–00:30, later on Friday and Saturday nights. One OASA ticket costs €1.20 and covers metro, tram and bus with transfers for 90 minutes.
  • ✓BusBuses and the tram use the same €1.20 OASA ticket. The airport express bus X93/X95 costs €5.50.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are yellow; insist on the meter and expect shared rides at busy times. Uber and Bolt work in Athens and help avoid fare haggling.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the centre is hard: narrow streets, little parking and heavy traffic. Skip it for city days.
  • ✓Walking ★Best way around Plaka, Monastiraki and Syntagma. Wear shoes with grip for marble and stone paths.
  • ✓FerryPiraeus port is about 10 km from the centre, with ferries to the islands. Reach it by metro.

Climate

Mediterranean climate: hot, dry summers and mild, wetter winters. Spring and autumn are the sweet spots.

  • ☀️Best time: April to June and September to OctoberDays around 20–29°C with little rain. Ideal for ruins, walking and the coast.
  • 🥵Summer is very hot (July–August)Highs near 33°C and few rain days. Sightseeing is hard at midday; go early or late.
  • 🌧️Wettest in winterDecember to February have about 7–8 rain days a month. Showers are usually short.
  • 🧥Cool wintersJanuary highs near 12°C and lows around 5°C. Bring a warm coat for evenings.
  • 🌊Sea seasonBeach weather runs roughly June to September, warmest in July and August.
  • 🧳What to packSun hat and sunscreen for summer; a light rain jacket and warm layer for winter; comfortable shoes for marble and stone paths.

Landmarks 19

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Parthenon

    The iconic temple on the Acropolis is the symbol of ancient Greece and a must-see for any first-time visitor. Go early in the morning to avoid crowds and heat; buy a combined ticket that includes other sites.

  2. 2

    Acropolis Museum

    This modern museum houses the treasures of the Acropolis, including the Parthenon sculptures, with stunning views of the hill. Visit in the late afternoon for cooler temperatures and fewer crowds; tickets can be bought online.

  3. 3

    Plaka

    The charming old town at the foot of the Acropolis is perfect for strolling through narrow streets lined with shops and tavernas. Wear comfortable shoes and explore early evening when the area comes alive.

  4. 4

    Ancient Agora of Athens

    This ancient marketplace was the heart of classical Athens and offers a peaceful escape from the city bustle. The Temple of Hephaestus here is one of the best-preserved Greek temples; go early to avoid the heat.

  5. 5

    Temple of Olympian Zeus

    These massive columns are a testament to ancient ambition and are especially magical at sunset. Combine your visit with Hadrian's Arch next door; the site is compact and quick to see.

  6. 6

    Syntagma Square

    The central square is home to the Greek Parliament and the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, guarded by evzones. Catch the changing of the guard every hour; it's free and a great photo opportunity.

  7. 7

    Panathenaic Stadium

    The all-marble stadium hosted the first modern Olympics in 1896 and offers a unique glimpse into sports history. Climb to the top for panoramic views; audio guides are available and worth it.

    Swap in instead

    • Take a day trip to Cape Sounion for the Temple of Poseidon and a sunset over the sea. Check bus times on the day.
    • Ferries from Piraeus reach the islands; a day trip to Aegina is the easiest. Book ahead in summer.
    • Add a half-day at the National Archaeological Museum, then wander the Exarchia streets nearby.

    Getting around

    • Metro has 3 lines; trains run about 05:30-00:30, later on Friday and Saturday nights.
    • One OASA ticket costs €1.20 and covers metro, tram and bus with transfers for 90 minutes.
    • Airport metro or bus costs about €9 each way; express bus X93/X95 costs €5.50.
    • Taxis are yellow; insist on the meter and expect shared rides at busy times.
    • Ride apps such as Uber and Bolt work in Athens; use them to avoid fare haggling.
    • Driving in the centre is hard: narrow streets, little parking and heavy traffic.
    • Piraeus port is about 10 km from the centre, with ferries to the islands.

    Where to stay

    • Plaka · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      Old-town lanes right under the Acropolis; walk to the Ancient Agora and most central sights.
    • Syntagma · ≈ EUR 110–280 a night
      Central and well connected: metro to the airport and Piraeus, walk to the National Garden and Plaka.
    • Monastiraki · ≈ EUR 80–200 a night
      Flea market, street food and the metro hub; lively at night and close to the Ancient Agora.
    • Koukaki · ≈ EUR 70–180 a night
      Quiet residential streets near the Acropolis Museum with good local tavernas; often better value.

    Food & drink

    • Try souvlaki and gyros from a grill house: cheap, fast and everywhere. Expect about €3–5 for a wrap.
    • Order meze to share: tzatziki, fava, grilled octopus and small fried fish. Two or three plates per person is plenty.
    • Eat dinner late like locals, roughly 21:00–23:00. Many tavernas are quiet before 20:00.
    • Look for a taverna with a handwritten menu and local diners rather than a photo menu on the street.
    • In summer, eat lunch by the sea on the coast and keep the centre for dinner. Book popular places ahead on weekends.

    Tips

    1. Wear shoes with good grip when visiting the Acropolis, as the ancient marble steps are very slippery
    2. Locals eat dinner very late, often starting around 9 PM or 10 PM
    3. Buy a combined archaeological ticket to access the Acropolis and several other ancient sites at a discount
    4. Avoid the midday heat in summer by visiting ruins early in the morning and taking a long afternoon break
    5. The Plaka neighborhood is picturesque but touristy; head to Psyrri or Exarcheia for a more local, edgy vibe

    Souvenirs

    Greek worry beads (komboloi)

    EUR 15–45small string, 1 strand · estimate

    Komboloi are strings of beads that Greeks handle to pass the time and calm their hands. They are an everyday part of Greek culture found in cafes and homes across Athens.

    Where: Kombologadiko, Amerikis 9, Kolonaki
    Check the material: resin, amber, and stone beads feel and sound different, so handle a few before you choose.

    Olive oil and soap

    EUR 8–20500 ml of oil or 1 bar of soap · estimate

    Greece is one of the world's largest olive oil producers, and Athens markets sell local oil and traditional olive oil soap. Buying them here lets you take home a true taste of Greek everyday life.

    Where: Monastiraki, Varvakios Central Market
    Look for "extra virgin" on the label and check the acidity level, which should be low, as a sign of quality.
