Getting around

Ride apps (Uber, Bolt) for most trips; BRT buses and the Blue Line rail if you're saving money.

  • ✓MetroBlue Line rail runs Marina to Mile 2; fare about ₦375, Cowry Card only.
  • ✓Bus ★BRT buses use dedicated lanes; cash not accepted, buy a Cowry Card (₦500–1,000).
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and Bolt work widely; taxis are metered or negotiated. Agree the price first.
  • ✗Rental carNot recommended for first-time visitors; traffic is heavy and driving rules are loosely followed.
  • ✗WalkingOnly in a few central areas. The city is spread out and pavements are uneven.
  • ✓Danfo minibusYellow minibuses cover most routes; fares start around ₦50 and rise with distance. Cash only.
  • ✓Okada (motorbike taxi)Quick but risky, and banned in many districts and after dark. Avoid unless you know the area.
  • ✓FerryFerries link Lagos Island, Banana Island, Mile 2 and the mainland. A fast way to skip traffic.

Climate

Tropical climate: hot all year, with a long wet season from roughly April to October and a drier stretch from November to March.

  • ☀️Best time: December to FebruaryWarm days around 32°C and the fewest rain days (5–8). Ideal for beaches, markets and walking.
  • 🌧️Wet season: April to OctoberRain days climb to 22–28 a month. Downpours are often short but heavy; carry a rain jacket.
  • 🌡️Hot and humid all yearHighs stay between 27 and 32°C, lows near 24–26°C. Humidity makes it feel hotter.
  • 🚗Flooding slows trafficIn the wettest months (June, September, October) roads can flood. Allow extra time and avoid low-lying routes.
  • 🌊Beach days need timingBeaches are best in the dry months. In the wet season, go early and check the sky.
  • 🧳What to packLight breathable clothes, sandals, sunscreen, a rain jacket and insect repellent. A light layer for air-conditioned rooms.

Landmarks 13

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    National Theatre of Nigeria

    A striking modernist landmark and the city's most famous sight, worth seeing for its bold architecture and cultural role. Go in the morning on a weekday to avoid crowds, and check ahead for any scheduled performances or events.

  2. 2

    Lekki Conservation Centre

    A peaceful nature reserve with a famous canopy walkway, offering a break from the city's bustle. Visit early in the day for cooler weather and better wildlife spotting; wear comfortable shoes.

  3. 3

    Freedom Park Lagos

    A former colonial prison turned memorial and arts venue, rich in history and local culture. It's best visited in the late afternoon when events often take place; entry is usually free or low-cost.

  4. 4

    Nigerian National Museum

    Home to important archaeological and ethnographic collections, including the famous Nok terracotta pieces. Go on a weekday morning for a quieter experience; photography may require permission.

  5. 5

    Third Mainland Bridge

    One of Africa's longest bridges, offering sweeping views of the lagoon and city skyline. Cross it during off-peak hours to avoid traffic; it's best experienced as part of a drive towards the island.

  6. 6

    Lagos Island waterfront

    The historic heart of Lagos with colonial-era buildings and lively markets nearby. Visit in the morning to see the port in action, and be mindful of your belongings in crowded areas.

  7. 7

    Tafawa Balewa Square

    A vast historical square where Nigeria's independence was celebrated, surrounded by iconic monuments. It's an easy stop while exploring the island; go early to avoid the heat.

  8. 8

    Nike Art Gallery

    A vibrant gallery showcasing a huge collection of Nigerian art and textiles. It's free to enter and a great place to buy authentic souvenirs; open daily, but check hours before you go.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for Badagry: old slave-trade sites and the coast, a long drive west of the city.
    • Take a short boat trip to Tarkwa Bay or a Lekki beach for a slower day by the Atlantic.
    • Music fans can spend an evening at a live afrobeats or highlife venue; ask your hotel for a safe, current recommendation.

    Getting around

    • Blue Line rail runs Marina to Mile 2; fare about ₦375, Cowry Card only.
    • BRT buses use dedicated lanes; cash not accepted, buy a Cowry Card (₦500-1,000).
    • Yellow danfo minibuses cover most routes; fares start around ₦50 and rise with distance.
    • Ride apps like Uber and Bolt work widely; taxis are metered or negotiated.
    • Okadas (motorbike taxis) are quick but risky and banned in many districts and after dark.
    • Ferries link Lagos Island, Banana Island, Mile 2 and the mainland; walking is best in a few central areas only.

    Where to stay

    • Victoria Island · ≈ NGN 70,000–200,000 a night
      Central for business, restaurants and the Landmark beach; easy to reach Ikoyi and Lagos Island.
    • Ikoyi · ≈ NGN 90,000–280,000 a night
      Quiet, leafy and upscale; close to the museums and good restaurants. Good for couples and business.
    • Lekki · ≈ NGN 45,000–180,000 a night
      Beach clubs, nightlife and many mid-range stays. Traffic can be heavy; pick a place near your plans.
    • Ikeja GRA · ≈ NGN 35,000–120,000 a night
      Near the airport and quieter at night. Cheaper, but far from the beaches and island sights.

    Food & drink

    • Try Nigerian classics: jollof rice, egusi soup with pounded yam, and pepper soup. Portions are big.
    • Suya (grilled spiced beef skewers) is a street favourite; buy from busy stalls in the evening.
    • Lagos does great seafood: grilled fish, prawns and catfish pepper soup near the water.
    • Cheap eats: buka (local canteens) serve rice and stew for a few hundred naira; check prices on the day.
    • Street food and tap water are risky for some stomachs. Drink bottled water and eat where it is busy and freshly cooked.

    Tips

    1. Traffic jams (known locally as 'go-slows') are notorious; plan heavy buffer times for travel
    2. Use Uber or Bolt for transit rather than navigating the Danfo buses or hailing street taxis
    3. The city has an incredible music and nightlife scene, especially on Victoria Island and Lekki
    4. Be cautious of your surroundings and avoid walking at night in unfamiliar areas
    5. Power outages can occur, so ensure your accommodation has a reliable backup generator

      Souvenirs

      Adire fabric scarf

      NGN 8000–250001 cotton scarf · estimate

      Adire is a traditional Yoruba resist-dyeing method, and Lagos is one of its best-known centers. Each scarf shows patterns made by tying, folding, or stitching the cloth before dyeing.

      Where: Lekki Arts & Crafts Market or PR Fabrics, Lekki Phase 1
      Check that the colors do not run by rubbing a damp corner of the cloth on white paper.

      Aso oke accessory

      NGN 10000–300001 small pouch or stole · estimate

      Aso oke is a narrow-strip woven cloth from the Yoruba people, often worn at celebrations. Lagos is a major place to see and buy it, and small pieces make easy gifts.

      Where: Nike Art Gallery, Lekki
      Ask whether the cloth is hand-woven and locally made, since machine-made copies are common.
