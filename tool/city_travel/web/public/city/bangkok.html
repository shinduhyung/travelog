Getting around

BTS Skytrain and MRT for the centre, river boats for temples, and Grab or Bolt for the rest.

  • ✓Metro ★BTS Skytrain and MRT subway cover the centre; fares about ฿16–65, contactless cards accepted on most lines.
  • ✓BusCity buses are very cheap but hard to use for visitors.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Metered taxis start at ฿35; insist on the meter. Grab and Bolt apps are often easier.
  • ✗Rental carNot recommended for visitors: heavy traffic and hard parking. Use trains, boats and apps instead.
  • ✓WalkingWalking is hot and pavements are uneven; combine short walks with the Skytrain.
  • ✓FerryChao Phraya Express boats link the riverside temples, Chinatown and ICONSIAM.
  • ✓Tuk-tukTuk-tuks are fun for short rides; agree the price first.

Climate

Tropical city: hot all year, with a dry, cooler season from November to February and a long wet season from May to October.

  • ☀️Best time: November to FebruaryHighs around 31–32°C, lows 22–23°C and only 4–5 rain days. Best for temples and walking.
  • 🥵Hot season: March to MayHighs of 33–34°C. Sightsee early morning and late afternoon; rest in air-conditioning at midday.
  • 🌧️Wet season: May to October20–26 rain days a month, often short heavy afternoon showers. Carry a light rain jacket or umbrella.
  • 💧Humid all yearExpect sticky air most days. Light, breathable clothes help; so does a spare shirt.
  • 🌂Plan around the rainKeep indoor backups like malls, food courts and museums. Storms usually pass within an hour or two.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, sandals, sunscreen and a hat. Cover shoulders and knees for temples. A rain jacket for May–October.

Landmarks 31

Must-see · 3

  1. 1

    The Grand Palace

    The former royal palace and home of the Emerald Buddha (Wat Phra Kaew). Strict dress code (covered shoulders and legs); go at 8:30 opening to beat heat and crowds, and ignore anyone saying it is closed.

  2. 2

    Wat Pho

    Temple of the 46 m Reclining Buddha, a short walk from the Grand Palace, and the birthplace of Thai massage, which you can get on site.

  3. 3

    Wat Arun

    The Temple of Dawn across the river, covered in porcelain mosaic. Take the 5-baht cross-river ferry from Tha Tien pier; best photographed from the east bank at sunset.

    Phra Nakhon Si Ayutthaya (Ayutthaya) · 45 min by train

    ★★★★★ · Full day · 78 km from Bangkok

    Best way: The train is the fastest option at 45 minutes and leaves you close to the historical park, where a tuk-tuk or bicycle covers the ruins.

    Take a direct train from Bangkok's Hua Lamphong or Bang Sue station (about 1.5 hours), or a minivan from Victory Monument, then use a tuk-tuk or bicycle to reach the historical park.

    • Train ★45 min · from $1-$26
    • Bus1 h 30 · from $3-$60
    • Car1 h 01 · taxi ≈ 580 THB

    What to see

    • Wat Mahathat (Buddha head in tree roots)
    • Wat Phra Si Sanphet
    • Wat Chai Watthanaram
    • Ayutthaya Historical Park bicycle loop

    Good to know

    • Check the timetable for train times, since frequency isn't listed here.
    • The historical park is best explored by bicycle or tuk-tuk, as the sites are spread out.
    • Visiting in the cooler morning hours makes walking between the ruins more comfortable.

    Kanchanaburi · 1 h 35 by road

    ★★★★☆ · Full day · 128 km from Bangkok

    Best way: The bus takes about 3 hours each way and still needs a songthaew or tuk-tuk into town, so a tour avoids the long, fiddly last-mile connection.

    Take a bus from Bangkok's Sai Tai Mai (Southern) bus terminal (about 2 to 2.5 hours), then a songthaew or tuk-tuk into town for the bridge and river.

    • Train3 h 43 · from $1-$27
    • Bus3 h 00 · from $4-$13

    What to see

    • Death Railway section at Tham Krasae
    • Erawan Falls (by local transport)

    • Many visitors pair the trip with the WWII history museums in town.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a temple day for a day trip to Ayutthaya by train or minivan; ruins and history, check times on the day.
    • Add a floating market or canal trip outside the centre; go early morning to beat the heat and crowds.
    • Spend a night in Chinatown for food and markets, or take a cooking class to learn the dishes you liked.

    Getting around

    • The BTS Skytrain and MRT subway cover the centre; fares about ฿16-65, contactless cards accepted on most lines.
    • City buses are very cheap but hard to use for visitors.
    • Metered taxis start at ฿35; insist on the meter. Grab and Bolt apps are often easier.
    • Tuk-tuks are fun for short rides; agree the price first.
    • Chao Phraya Express boats link the riverside temples, Chinatown and ICONSIAM.
    • Walking is hot and pavements are uneven; combine short walks with the Skytrain.

    Where to stay

    • Sukhumvit · ≈ THB 1,200–4,500 a night
      On the BTS Skytrain and MRT, endless food and nightlife, easy airport link. Good first-time base.
    • Siam · ≈ THB 1,500–5,000 a night
      Central shopping malls and the Skytrain interchange; short rides to most sights.
    • Riverside · ≈ THB 2,500–9,000 a night
      River views, express boats to the temples and ICONSIAM; quieter evenings.
    • Old Town · ≈ THB 700–3,000 a night
      Walk to the Grand Palace and Wat Pho; cheapest beds and the backpacker strip.

    Food & drink

    • Eat street food where locals queue: noodles, grilled chicken and som tam for roughly ฿50–120 a plate.
    • Yaowarat (Chinatown) after dark is the classic food crawl; go hungry and share dishes.
    • Try the big local dishes: boat noodles, pad thai, khao soi and mango sticky rice.
    • Food courts in malls are cheap, clean and air-conditioned; a good rainy-day option.
    • Drink bottled or filtered water; ice in busy cafés and stalls is usually fine, but use your judgement.

    Tips

    1. Use the BTS Skytrain or MRT to avoid notoriously severe street traffic
    2. Street food is safe and exceptional — look for stalls with high local turnover
    3. Always agree on a fare or insist on the meter before getting into a taxi or tuk-tuk
    4. Dress modestly when visiting temples (cover shoulders and knees)
    5. Carry small cash notes, as many street vendors do not accept cards
