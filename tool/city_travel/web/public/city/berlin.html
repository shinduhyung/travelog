Climate

Temperate climate with cold winters and mild summers. Rain is spread through the year, so pack layers.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs around 19–25°C and lows of 9–16°C. Best for walking, parks and outdoor sights.
  • 🥶Winter is cold (December to February)Highs of 4–6°C and lows near or below freezing. Bring a warm coat, hat and gloves.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 8–14 rain days a month. No dry season; carry a small umbrella or rain jacket.
  • 🌂Wettest monthsJanuary and December have the most rain days, around 12–14. Plan indoor backups.
  • 🍂Spring and autumn are mildApril and October sit around 14–15°C highs. Good for sightseeing with fewer crowds.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes. Add a warm coat and gloves for November to March.

Landmarks 45

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Brandenburg Gate

    Berlin's defining monument and the symbol of the city's reunification, standing where the Wall once divided East and West. It is free to visit and open around the clock, so come early in the morning for photos without crowds. It sits at the west end of Unter den Linden and is easily reached by S-Bahn to Brandenburger Tor.

  2. 2

    Reichstag Building

    The German parliament's glass-domed home offers free rooftop views over the city and a close look at its modern architecture. Entry to the dome is free but requires advance online registration with your passport details. Book a slot at least a few days ahead, especially in summer.

  3. 3

    Berlin Wall Memorial

    This open-air site on Bernauer Strasse preserves a stretch of the Wall, the death strip and a viewing tower, making the division of the city tangible. It is free and best visited in the morning before tour groups arrive. Take the U8 or S-Bahn to Bernauer Strasse.

  4. 4

    Checkpoint Charlie

    The famous Cold War crossing point is a quick but essential stop for understanding Berlin's divided past. The outdoor site is free and always accessible, though the nearby museum charges admission. Visit early or late in the day, as the corner gets very crowded midday.

  5. 5

    Pergamon Museum

    Home to monumental reconstructions such as the Pergamon Altar and the Ishtar Gate, this is one of the world's great archaeology museums. Tickets are timed and sell out, so book online in advance. Note that parts of the museum may be closed for long-term renovation, so check before you go.

  6. 6

    Berlin Cathedral

    This grand Protestant church on Museum Island impresses with its dome and ornate interior, and the climb to the dome gallery rewards you with rooftop views. There is an admission fee for the interior and dome. Dress modestly and go on a weekday morning to avoid queues.

  7. 7

    East Side Gallery

    The longest surviving stretch of the Berlin Wall, now an open-air gallery of murals along the Spree River. It is free and open at all hours; walk it in the morning light. Reach it via Ostbahnhof or Warschauer Strasse stations.

  8. 8

    Jewish Museum Berlin

    Daniel Libeskind's striking building and its exhibits trace German-Jewish history and the Holocaust with unusual emotional power. Tickets are timed and best booked online. Allow at least two hours and take the U-Bahn to Hallesches Tor.

  9. 9

    Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe

    The field of concrete steles near the Brandenburg Gate is a solemn, powerful memorial, with an underground information centre beneath it. Entry is free, and the centre is closed on Mondays. Visit at dusk for a quieter, more reflective experience.

  10. 10

    Sanssouci Palace

    Frederick the Great's rococo summer palace in Potsdam, surrounded by terraced vineyards and parkland, is a rewarding half-day trip from Berlin. Palace entry is timed and limited, so reserve tickets online. Take the regional train to Potsdam and allow time to wander the gardens.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a museum afternoon for Potsdam: take the S-Bahn out and walk the parks and palaces.
    • Add a Spree river cruise for a different view of the centre.
    • Spend a half day at a former airport park or a lake beach in summer; check transport on the day.

    Getting around

    • U-Bahn and S-Bahn cover most of the city; buy tickets from machines before boarding.
    • Buses and trams fill the gaps; night buses run when trains stop.
    • Taxis are metered; apps like Uber and Bolt operate, but local apps like Free Now are common.
    • Driving is stressful: heavy traffic, low-emission zone, and limited parking.
    • Walking and cycling are great; watch for bike lanes.
    • No ferries in the city centre, but river cruises run on the Spree.

    Where to stay

    • Mitte · ≈ EUR 110–260 a night
      Walk to Museum Island, the Reichstag and Unter den Linden. Best first-time base; central transport.
    • Kreuzberg · ≈ EUR 80–190 a night
      Cheaper, lively and full of food. Close to the East Side Gallery and nightlife; grittier streets.
    • Prenzlauer Berg · ≈ EUR 90–210 a night
      Quiet, leafy and family-friendly. Cafés and parks; a short tram or U-Bahn ride to the centre.
    • Charlottenburg · ≈ EUR 100–240 a night
      Classic west Berlin near the palace, Kurfürstendamm shopping and the zoo. Calmer evenings.

    Food & drink

    • Try Berlin classics: currywurst, döner kebab and a hearty lunch at a traditional Kneipe.
    • Street food markets are a cheap, fun dinner. Check days and locations online before you go.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead, especially at weekends. Many kitchens close by about 22:00.
    • Vegetarian and vegan options are widespread and good value.
    • Tap water is safe to drink. A sit-down lunch is often cheaper than dinner.

    Tips

    1. Validate paper tickets before boarding trains, or buy digital tickets via the BVG app
    2. Carry cash as many bars, cafes, and smaller restaurants still do not accept cards
    3. Nightlife and techno clubs (like Berghain) have strict door policies and casual dress codes
    4. Sundays are very quiet with most retail shops and supermarkets strictly closed
    5. Try a Currywurst or a Döner Kebab — both are iconic Berlin street foods
