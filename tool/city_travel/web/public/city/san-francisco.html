Getting around

Walk neighbourhoods and use Muni or BART for longer hops; skip a rental car in town.

  • ✓Metro ★BART is the fast regional metro; Muni Metro covers the city. Buy a Clipper card to ride both.
  • ✓BusMuni buses fill the gaps; use the same Clipper card. Slow at peak times.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered; Uber and Lyft are widely used and handy for late nights.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is slow and parking is costly; skip a rental car in town.
  • ✓Walking ★The best way to see neighbourhoods, but hills are steep. Wear good shoes.
  • ✓FerryFerries from the Ferry Building reach Sausalito, Tiburon and Alcatraz. Pay with Clipper.
  • ✓TrainCaltrain runs regional services; use the Clipper card.

Climate

Mild and Mediterranean-style: cool, wet winters and dry, foggy summers. Rarely hot or freezing.

  • ☀️Best time: September to OctoberWarmest and driest months, highs near 21°C. Ideal for walking and the coast.
  • 🌧️Wet winters (Dec–Mar)Around 8–10 rain days a month and highs of 13–15°C. Bring a waterproof jacket.
  • 🌫️Summer fogJune to August is dry but cool and foggy, highs 19–20°C. The coast is chillier than inland.
  • 🧥Layer upMornings and evenings are cool year-round. A light jacket helps every month.
  • 💨Wind on the bridge and coastThe Golden Gate and beaches are windy and colder than downtown. Wear a windproof layer.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable walking shoes for hills, layers, a rain jacket in winter, and sunglasses for fog-free afternoons.

Landmarks 17

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Golden Gate Bridge

    The world's most photographed bridge, an engineering marvel and symbol of San Francisco. Walk or bike across for free; go early morning to avoid fog and crowds, or late afternoon for golden light.

  2. 2

    Alcatraz

    The infamous former federal prison on an island in the bay, now a national park with a gripping audio tour. Book tickets well in advance (often weeks ahead) and take the ferry from Pier 33; dress in layers as it's windy and cool.

  3. 3

    Fisherman's Wharf

    A lively waterfront hub with sea lions at Pier 39, clam chowder in sourdough bowls, and views of Alcatraz. Visit on a weekday morning to avoid crowds; it's easily reached by cable car or F-line streetcar.

  4. 4

    Cable Cars

    The world's last manually operated cable car system, a moving National Historic Landmark. Ride the Powell-Hyde line from Union Square to Fisherman's Wharf; board at the turnaround for a seat and hold on tight on the hills.

  5. 5

    Painted Ladies

    The iconic row of Victorian houses on Alamo Square, best known from the 'Full House' opening credits. Combine with a picnic in Alamo Square Park; go on a clear day for skyline views behind the houses.

  6. 6

    Palace of Fine Arts

    A stunning Beaux-Arts rotunda and lagoon built for the 1915 Panama-Pacific Exposition. It's free to visit; go at sunset for romantic photos, and walk over from Crissy Field or the Marina.

  7. 7

    Golden Gate Park

    A vast urban park larger than Central Park, home to gardens, museums, and bison. Rent a bike or take a picnic; don't miss the Japanese Tea Garden and the de Young Museum.

  8. 8

    Twin Peaks

    Two hills offering the best panoramic views of the city and bay. Drive or take a rideshare up; go at sunset or night for sparkling city lights, but bring a jacket as it's windy and cold.

  9. 9

    Lombard Street

    The famous 'crookedest street' with eight hairpin turns on a steep block. Walk down the stairs on either side for free; driving it is fun but expect queues, so go early morning.

  10. 10

    Coit Tower

    An Art Deco tower on Telegraph Hill with 360-degree views and WPA-era murals inside. Take the elevator to the top for a small fee; walk up the Filbert Steps for a scenic approach.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Muir Woods redwoods or Sausalito by ferry; check tour and shuttle options on the day.
    • Add a Golden Gate Park half-day: gardens, museums and Ocean Beach at the end.
    • Take a ferry to Alcatraz; tickets sell out, so book well ahead.

    Getting around

    • BART is the fast regional metro; Muni Metro and buses cover the city.
    • Buy a Clipper card for BART, Muni, Caltrain, and ferries.
    • Taxis are metered; Uber and Lyft are widely used.
    • Driving is slow and parking is costly; skip a rental car in town.
    • Walking is the best way to see neighbourhoods; hills are steep.
    • Ferries from the Ferry Building reach Sausalito, Tiburon, and Alcatraz.

    Where to stay

    • Union Square · ≈ USD 180–420 a night
      Central for shopping, cable cars and transit; easy walk to Chinatown and the waterfront.
    • Fisherman's Wharf · ≈ USD 200–480 a night
      Steps from Alcatraz ferries, Pier 39 and the cable car; family-friendly but touristy.
    • SoMa · ≈ USD 170–400 a night
      Close to Oracle Park, museums and BART; good value and quick airport links.
    • Mission District · ≈ USD 150–350 a night
      Best food and nightlife; sunny, walkable streets and BART nearby.

    Food & drink

    • Get a Mission-style burrito in the Mission District; portions are huge, so share or go hungry.
    • Ferry Building Marketplace: sample local cheese, bread and coffee; check opening days on the day.
    • Chinatown dim sum is cheap and filling; go before noon for the freshest trays.
    • Cioppino (seafood stew) and sourdough bread bowls are the classic wharf dishes; expect tourist prices.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead; walk-ins are hard at dinner on weekends.

    Tips

    1. Dress in layers as microclimates mean the weather can change drastically block by block
    2. Public transit is generally better than driving due to extreme hills, one-way streets, and expensive parking
    3. Don't call it 'San Fran' — locals prefer 'SF' or 'The City'
    4. Watch your step and belongings in certain downtown areas like the Tenderloin
    5. Visit Golden Gate Park, which is actually larger than New York's Central Park
