Getting around

Walk the historic centre, then use buses with a Macau Pass card or taxis to cover longer distances.

  • ✗MetroMacau has no metro system. Use the Light Rail or buses instead.
  • ✓Light Rail (LRT)The Taipa Line links Taipa, Cotai and the Barra area. Check locally for current routes.
  • ✓Bus ★Buses cover the peninsula and islands. Pay with a Macau Pass card or exact cash coins. Check locally for fares.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are plentiful and cheap. Ask for the meter. Ride-hailing apps have limited availability, so check locally.
  • ✗Rental carDriving is not recommended. Roads are congested and parking is scarce and costly.
  • ✓Walking ★The historic centre is best explored on foot. Taipa and Coloane suit cycling and shuttle buses.
  • ✓FerryFerries run from Outer Harbour and Taipa to Hong Kong, Shenzhen and Zhuhai, so you can plan day trips by boat.

Climate

Subtropical climate: hot, humid summers with heavy rain, and mild, drier winters.

  • ☀️Best time: October to DecemberHighs of about 23–27°C, with fewer rain days. Good for walking the historic centre.
  • 🥵Summer is hot and wet (June to September)Highs of about 27–31°C, with 21–26 rain days a month. Plan indoor visits for the afternoon.
  • 💧Humid most of the yearHumidity is high from May to September. Carry water and dress in light fabrics.
  • 🌂Rain is common in summerSummer brings frequent showers. Bring a compact umbrella or light rain jacket.
  • 🌀Typhoon seasonTyphoons are possible from about July to September. Check weather warnings and ferry status before travel.
  • 🧳What to packLight breathable clothes, sunscreen and a rain layer. Bring a warm layer for winter evenings.

Landmarks 19

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Ruins of St. Paul's

    The facade of this 17th-century Jesuit church is the most photographed image in Macau and a symbol of the city's Portuguese heritage. It stands at the top of a steep granite stairway, with a museum of Christian art beneath it. Visit in the morning or late afternoon to avoid the largest crowds, and wear comfortable shoes for the climb.

  2. 2

    Senado Square

    This paved square in the Historic Centre is lined with pastel colonial buildings and mosaic pavements. It is the easiest place to start a walk through the old town and to see the local mix of Portuguese and Chinese architecture. Go in the evening, when the buildings are lit and the square is lively, and reach it on foot from Ruins of St. Paul's.

  3. 3

    Guia Fortress

    This 16th-century fort sits on Guia Hill and offers the best panoramic views over the old town and the peninsula. The chapel inside is one of the oldest surviving structures of its kind in the city. Take a bus or taxi up, or walk the hill path, and go before sunset for the views.

  4. 4

    A-Ma Temple

    Dedicated to A-Ma, the sea goddess who is honoured as the protector of fishermen and sailors, this temple is said to give the city its name. Its granite courtyards and incense-filled halls are a good contrast to the colonial sights nearby. Go early on a weekday, dress modestly, and bring small change for the incense and offerings.

  5. 5

    Macau Tower

    At 338 metres, this tower offers observation decks and a revolving restaurant with sweeping views over the city and the Pearl River Delta. The Sky Walk and bungy jumping are popular for thrill-seekers. Book observation tickets online ahead of time, especially for evening slots and weekends.

  6. 6

    Taipa Houses

    These restored Portuguese-era houses on Taipa Island are now a small museum and cultural space that shows how the island's residents lived. They pair well with a stroll through nearby Taipa Village and its street market. Take a short taxi or bus ride from the Historic Centre and allow an hour or two.

    Swap in instead

    • Take a ferry from Outer Harbour for a day trip to Hong Kong. Check schedules and fares on the day.
    • Spend a half day in Coloane Village and the quiet beaches on the south side of the island.
    • Cross to Zhuhai for a day trip by ferry or land border. Check entry rules and visa needs before you go.

    Getting around

    • Light Rail (LRT) Taipa Line links Taipa, Cotai, and the Barra area. Check locally for current routes.
    • Buses cover the peninsula and islands. Pay with a Macau Pass card or exact cash coins. Check locally for fares.
    • Taxis are plentiful and cheap. Ask for the meter. Ride-hailing apps have limited availability, so check locally.
    • Driving is not recommended. Roads are congested and parking is scarce and costly.
    • The historic centre is best explored on foot. Taipa and Coloane suit cycling and shuttle buses.
    • Ferries run from Outer Harbour and Taipa to Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and Zhuhai, so you can plan day trips by boat.

    Where to stay

    • Senado Square · ≈ MOP 600–1,500 a night
      Best base for the historic centre and Macanese restaurants. Most sights are a short walk away, and the buses are close by.
    • Outer Harbour · ≈ MOP 500–1,300 a night
      Near the ferry terminal for day trips to Hong Kong, Shenzhen and Zhuhai. Quieter, with a view over the water.
    • Taipa Village · ≈ MOP 500–1,400 a night
      Old Portuguese streets, cafés and a relaxed village feel. Light rail and buses link it to the peninsula.
    • Cotai Strip · ≈ MOP 900–2,500 a night
      Large casino resorts with pools, shows and shops. Good for family stays and nightlife, but far from the old town on foot.

    Food & drink

    • Try Macanese classics: African chicken, pork chop bun, minchi and Portuguese egg tarts.
    • Eat in old family-run restaurants in the historic centre and Taipa Village. Dishes are often shared.
    • Pork chop buns and egg tarts are cheap snacks. Buy them from bakeries and small cafés.
    • Casino resorts have many restaurants, from buffets to fine dining. Check opening hours before you go.
    • Booking ahead is wise for weekend dinners and popular Macanese restaurants. Check on the day.
