Getting around

Uber, DiDi or a taxi for most trips; cheap city buses if you're saving money.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Don't plan around rail.
  • ✓BusCity buses cost about ₡500 per ride, no transfers or day passes. Use the Moovit app for routes and schedules.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis should use a meter but often don't; agree the price first. Uber and DiDi work well, though Uber is technically illegal.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is slow and confusing. Only worth it for out-of-town trips.
  • ✓Walking ★Fine downtown by day. Bike lanes are limited.

Climate

Tropical highland city: mild all year, with a dry season roughly December to March and heavy afternoon rain from May to October.

  • ☀️Best time: December to MarchHighs 24–27°C, lows near 16°C and few rain days. Best for walking and day trips.
  • 🌧️Wet season May to October24–29 rain days a month. Rain usually comes in the afternoon; do outdoor plans in the morning.
  • 🌡️Mild temperatures all yearHighs stay 24–27°C and lows 16–17°C. Bring a light layer for evenings.
  • ☂️Pack rain gearA compact umbrella and a light waterproof jacket help from May to November.
  • 🌫️Cloud and mist on hillsVolcano and cloud-forest trips can be cloudy or closed; check on the day.
  • 🧴Sun at altitudeThe city sits high; sun is strong even on cloudy days. Use sunscreen.

Landmarks 12

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    National Theatre of Costa Rica

    The country's grandest cultural building, an ornate 1897 theater with a gilded interior and marble lobby that is a symbol of San José's coffee-era wealth. It anchors the Plaza de la Cultura, so you can combine it with the museums nearby. Go on a weekday morning for guided tours and fewer crowds.

  2. 2

    Pre-Columbian Gold Museum

    Houses a stunning collection of gold artifacts from Costa Rica's indigenous cultures, displayed in underground galleries beneath the Plaza de la Cultura. It is compact and easy to pair with the National Theatre. Buy tickets at the entrance and allow about an hour.

  3. 3

    National Museum of Costa Rica

    Set in a former fortress with a butterfly garden and a bullet-marked wall, it tells the country's history from pre-Columbian times to the 1948 civil war. It is a short walk from the city center. Visit in the morning to avoid afternoon rain.

  4. 4

    Jade Museum

    A well-organized museum with the largest collection of American jade, plus other pre-Columbian artifacts, in a modern building. It is a good rainy-day stop and close to the National Museum. Check opening hours, as it closes some days.

  5. 5

    Metallic Building

    A striking prefabricated iron building from the late 19th century, shipped from Europe and now a cultural landmark in the city center. It is best seen from the outside and is a quick photo stop. Combine it with a walk through the nearby Mercado Central.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day trip to a coffee farm or Poás or Irazú volcano; tours leave early, check on the day for closures.
    • Swap a city afternoon for the Museo del Oro and Museo Nacional, both downtown.
    • Take a bus to Cartago or the Orosi Valley for colonial history and cooler air.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; buses cover the city and country from several downtown terminals.
    • City buses are cheap, about ₡500 per ride, with no transfers or day passes.
    • Use the Moovit app for bus routes and schedules.
    • Taxis should use a meter but often don't; agree the price first.
    • Uber and DiDi work well in the city, though Uber is technically illegal.
    • Driving is slow and confusing; walking is fine downtown but bike lanes are limited.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown (Avenida Central) · ≈ CRC 25,000–70,000 a night
      Walk to the National Theatre, museums and markets. Busy and basic by night; keep valuables close.
    • Barrio Amón · ≈ CRC 35,000–90,000 a night
      Quiet old quarter of colonial houses and small hotels, a short walk from downtown sights.
    • Barrio Escalante · ≈ CRC 45,000–120,000 a night
      Best food and nightlife strip; leafy streets and boutique hotels. Good for evenings.
    • San Pedro · ≈ CRC 30,000–80,000 a night
      University district near the museums and San Pedro Mall; cheaper and local, a short bus or taxi from the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Eat at a soda (small local diner): casado (rice, beans, plantain, meat) is a filling lunch, often ₡3,000–6,000.
    • Mercado Central downtown: fruit, coffee and cheap stalls; go before midday.
    • Barrio Escalante has the widest restaurant choice; book on weekend evenings.
    • Try gallo pinto at breakfast and fresh tropical fruit; coffee is a local point of pride.
    • Tap water is generally safe in the city; check on the day outside it.

    Tips

    1. Use Uber for safe, reliable transportation within the city
    2. Visit the National Theater and the Gold Museum
    3. The city is a perfect hub for booking tours to volcanoes, cloud forests, and waterfalls
    4. The 'Pura Vida' lifestyle is the national mantra—expect a relaxed pace
    5. Always drink bottled water and bring a jacket for cool evenings
