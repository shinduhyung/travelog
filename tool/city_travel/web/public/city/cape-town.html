Getting around

Uber or Bolt for most trips; drive yourself for the peninsula and Winelands.

  • ✗MetroNo metro. Don't plan around rail inside the city.
  • ✓TrainMetroRail links the centre to the Southern Suburbs and Simon's Town, but there is no Sunday service. Safe by day; avoid night travel.
  • ✓BusBuses cover main routes and show on Google Maps. Useful but slower than ride apps.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and Bolt are the easiest way around; airport to centre costs about R150. Ignore unofficial 'airport taxi' touts.
  • ✓Rental car ★Easy driving on good roads. Keep doors locked and avoid unknown areas after dark.
  • ✓WalkingFine in the City Bowl, Waterfront and Sea Point by day. Use rides at night or for longer trips.

Climate

Mediterranean climate: warm, dry summers and cool, wet winters. Wind and cloud can change mountain plans fast.

  • ☀️Best time: November to MarchWarm, mostly dry days around 22–26°C. Ideal for beaches, Table Mountain and the Winelands.
  • 🌧️Wettest: June to AugustAround 10–11 rain days a month and highs near 16–17°C. Keep indoor plans ready.
  • 💨Windy daysThe Cape is windy, especially in summer. Check the cableway status before heading up Table Mountain.
  • 🌊Cold Atlantic waterThe sea stays cold even in summer. Bring a wetsuit or swim briefly at Camps Bay.
  • 🧥Cool eveningsNights drop to 10–18°C. A light jacket helps year-round.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, sunscreen, a hat, a rain jacket for winter, and sturdy shoes for hiking.

Landmarks 27

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Table Mountain Aerial Cableway

    The cable car up Table Mountain is Cape Town's defining experience, with rotating cars and sweeping views over the city and both oceans. Go early in the morning to beat the crowds and wind, and check the weather before you buy tickets since the cableway closes in high winds.

  2. 2

    V&A Waterfront

    This working harbor is the city's liveliest gathering place, packed with restaurants, shops and views of Table Mountain across the water. It is an easy walk from the city center or a short taxi ride, and evenings are best for dinner with the mountain lit up behind you.

  3. 3

    Robben Island Prison

    The ferry to Robben Island and the guided tour of Nelson Mandela's former prison cell is a sobering must-do for understanding South Africa's history. Book tickets well in advance, especially in summer, as ferries sell out and crossings depend on calm seas.

  4. 4

    Boulders Beach

    A sheltered cove where you can watch African penguins up close from boardwalks and sometimes share the sand with them. Go early or late to avoid the midday crowds, and bring a windbreaker since the Cape wind can be strong.

  5. 5

    Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden

    This vast garden at the foot of Table Mountain showcases South Africa's unique flora, with a famous treetop walkway and seasonal blooms. Allow at least half a day, wear comfortable shoes, and consider a picnic on the lawns.

  6. 6

    Bo-Kaap

    The brightly painted houses of this historic Cape Malay quarter make it one of the city's most photographed neighborhoods. Walk the steep streets respectfully since people live here, and stop for a koeksister or bobotie at a local cafe.

    Hermanus · 1 h 35 by road

    ★★★★★ · Full day · 118 km from Cape Town

    Best way: A direct long-distance coach takes you along a scenic coastal drive to a stop within walking distance of the harbour.

    Take a long-distance coach from Cape Town to Hermanus (about 1.5 to 2 hours, with a scenic coastal drive); the town centre and harbour are a short walk or taxi ride from the bus stop.

    • Car1 h 35 · taxi ≈ 1,450 ZAR

    What to see

    • Cliff path along the coast
    • Old Harbour and fish market
    • Whale-watching viewpoints (seasonal, roughly June to November)
    • Stony Point penguin colony

    Good to know

    • Whale-watching season is roughly June to November, the best time for this trip.
    • Check coach timetables in advance, as service frequency may be limited.

    Stellenbosch · 44 min by road

    ★★★★☆ · Full day · 55 km from Cape Town

    Best way: The long-distance coach is more dependable than the often-disrupted local rail and drops you near the town centre.

    Take a Metrorail train or a long-distance coach from Cape Town to Stellenbosch (about 1 hour), then walk or take a short local taxi into the town centre; check current timetables, as local rail service is often disrupted.

    • Car44 min · taxi ≈ 690 ZAR

    What to see

    • Stellenbosch Village Museum
    • Dorp Street historic buildings
    • Oak-lined town centre and university campus
    • Local wine estates on the Helshoogte and Simonsberg routes

    Good to know

    • Local rail service is often disrupted, so check current timetables before you go.
    • Many wine estates are spread around the surrounding area, so a short taxi ride between them helps.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for the Cape Winelands: a full day of tastings in Stellenbosch or Franschhoek.
    • Take the ferry to Robben Island for the history tour; book ahead and check weather.
    • Drive the Cape Peninsula loop to Cape Point and Boulders Beach penguins in one long day.

    Getting around

    • MetroRail commuter trains link the centre to the Southern Suburbs and Simon's Town; no Sunday service.
    • Trains are safe by day; avoid night travel or ride in a crowded car with a companion.
    • Most ticket offices take cash only; routes show on Google Maps.
    • Uber and Bolt are the easiest way around; airport to centre costs about R150.
    • Ignore unofficial 'airport taxi' touts; they are unmetered and overpriced.
    • Driving is easy on good roads; keep doors locked and avoid unknown areas after dark.

    Where to stay

    • City Bowl · ≈ ZAR 900–2,500 a night
      Central base under Table Mountain. Walk to restaurants and museums; short rides to most sights.
    • V&A Waterfront · ≈ ZAR 1,800–5,000 a night
      Safe, walkable and full of shops and food. Easy start for Robben Island ferries.
    • Sea Point · ≈ ZAR 1,000–2,800 a night
      Promenade walks and ocean views. Good value and quick rides to the centre and beaches.
    • Camps Bay · ≈ ZAR 1,500–4,500 a night
      Beach on your doorstep and famous sunsets. Quieter at night; you'll need rides to town.

    Food & drink

    • Try a braai (South African barbecue) and a Cape Malay curry in the Bo-Kaap.
    • Seafood at the V&A Waterfront or Kalk Bay harbour; ask the price before you order.
    • Book a Winelands lunch in Stellenbosch or Franschhoek; many estates need a booking.
    • Cheap eats: biltong, koeksisters and food-market stalls around the City Bowl.
    • Tipping is normal, around 10–15% in restaurants. Check if a service charge is already added.

    Tips

    1. Uber is the safest and most reliable way to get around the city and out to the wine regions
    2. Never hike Table Mountain or Lion's Head alone, and pack for sudden, extreme weather changes
    3. Visit Boulders Beach to see the famous colony of wild African penguins
    4. Be aware of 'load shedding', which are scheduled national power outages
    5. Do not leave valuables visible in your car or unattended on the beach

    Souvenirs

    Rooibos tea

    ZAR 60–150100-200g · estimate

    A caffeine-free herbal tea made from South African rooibos plant leaves. It is grown only in the Cederberg region, so it is a strong local keepsake from Cape Town.

    Where: Tea shops near Company's Garden or Woolworths
    Buy pure rooibos. Check that the packet has no plant seeds mixed in.

    Cape Malay masala

    ZAR 40–120100-200g · estimate

    A spice blend used in Cape Malay cooking, which grew out of the city's mix of cultures. It is closely tied to the colorful Bo-Kaap neighborhood.

    Where: Atlas Trading Co., Bo-Kaap
    Check the grind date on the package so the spices still smell strong.
