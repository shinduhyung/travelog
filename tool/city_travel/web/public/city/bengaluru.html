Getting around

Use Namma Metro for long hops and ride apps or autos for short trips.

  • ✓Metro ★Namma Metro has Green, Purple and Yellow lines; fares ₹10–90, smart card ₹50. Use it to dodge traffic.
  • ✓BusBMTC buses cover the city but are slow and crowded at peak times.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber, Ola and Rapido work well; auto-rickshaws are cheaper for short hops.
  • ✓Rental carDriving yourself is stressful; hire a car with a driver instead.
  • ✓WalkingWalking is fine in small pockets, but pavements are patchy and traffic heavy.

Climate

Tropical highland city: mild all year, with a long monsoon from June to October.

  • ☀️Best time: November to FebruaryDry, mild days around 26–30°C and cool nights. Ideal for parks, markets and walking.
  • 🥵Hot before the rains (March to April)Highs reach 33–34°C. Sightsee in the morning and stay shaded at midday.
  • 🌧️Long monsoon (June to October)Rain on 16–24 days a month. Expect short heavy showers; carry an umbrella and plan indoor backups.
  • 🌂May is the changeoverAbout 16 rain days as pre-monsoon showers arrive. Weather can flip within an hour.
  • 🌡️Cool nights all yearLows stay around 16–21°C. A light layer helps in the evening and in air-conditioned places.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, a rain jacket or umbrella in monsoon months, comfortable shoes for patchy pavements, and sunscreen.

Landmarks 6

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Lalbagh Botanical Garden

    Bengaluru's most beloved green space, home to a famous glasshouse and centuries-old trees. Go early morning for cool air and fewer crowds; the garden is closed on some public holidays, so check ahead.

  2. 2

    Bangalore Palace

    A Tudor-style royal residence with carved wood interiors and a trove of royal memorabilia. It sits in the north of the city; a taxi or auto is the easiest way to reach it, and an entry ticket is required.

  3. 3

    Cubbon Park

    A vast central park of shaded avenues right in the heart of the city, ideal for a morning walk. It is free to enter and easily reached by metro; weekends are liveliest.

  4. 4

    ISKCON Temple Bangalore

    A large hilltop temple complex that is one of the city's most visited religious sites. Dress modestly, expect security checks, and visit in the evening when the temple is lit up.

  5. 5

    Vidhana Soudha

    The imposing seat of the state legislature, a landmark of neo-Dravidian architecture. It is best viewed from outside; the illuminated facade after dark is the highlight, and photography inside is not allowed.

  6. 6

    Wonderla (Bangalore)

    A big amusement and water park on the city's outskirts, popular with families. Plan a full day, book tickets online in advance, and arrange transport since it is well outside the centre.

    Swap in instead

    • Day trip to Mysuru: trains take about 2–3 hours each way. See the palace and Devaraja Market; check times on the day.
    • Sunrise at Nandi Hills: hire a car with driver, leave very early, and check the weather before you go.

    Getting around

    • Namma Metro has Green, Purple and Yellow lines; fares ₹10-90, smart card ₹50.
    • BMTC buses cover the city but are slow and crowded at peak times.
    • Use the metro to dodge traffic; Google Maps shows metro and bus schedules.
    • Uber, Ola and Rapido work well; auto-rickshaws are cheaper for short hops.
    • Driving yourself is stressful; hire a car with a driver instead.
    • Walking is fine in small pockets, but pavements are patchy and traffic heavy.

    Where to stay

    • MG Road · ≈ INR 2,500–9,000 a night
      Central and on the metro. Walk to Cubbon Park and the shops; easy rides to most sights.
    • Indiranagar · ≈ INR 3,000–10,000 a night
      Best for food and nightlife. Many cafés, bars and restaurants within walking distance.
    • Koramangala · ≈ INR 2,500–9,000 a night
      Young, lively area with cafés and startups. Good value stays; traffic can be heavy.
    • Jayanagar · ≈ INR 2,000–7,000 a night
      Quiet, local south Bengaluru. Close to Lalbagh and traditional food spots; metro nearby.

    Food & drink

    • Start the day with a masala dosa and filter coffee at a stand-up 'darshini' — usually ₹60–150.
    • Try a South Indian thali (meals) at lunch: rice, sambar, rasam and vegetables, often ₹150–350.
    • Bengaluru is famous for biryani; ask your hotel for a well-known local spot and go early.
    • Indiranagar and Koramangala have craft breweries and cafés; expect ₹300–600 for a beer.
    • Street food like chaat and idli is cheap and tasty, but choose busy stalls and drink bottled water.

    Tips

    1. Traffic jams are legendary; always leave plenty of buffer time for road travel
    2. Enjoy traditional South Indian breakfast (dosa, idli) and filter coffee at local 'Darshinis'
    3. The high altitude provides much cooler, pleasant weather compared to the rest of India
    4. The city has a massive craft beer culture with dozens of microbreweries
    5. Carry bottled water and avoid unpeeled street fruits to prevent stomach upsets
