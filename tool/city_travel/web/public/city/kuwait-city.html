Getting around

Taxis or ride apps for most trips; buses if you are saving money.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Don't plan around rail.
  • ✓BusKPTC and CityBus share routes: about 300 fils in town, 350 fils longer. Bus 13 links the airport to Mirqab in about 1 hour.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis ignore meters, so agree the fare first; orange taxis start near KD 1. Careem and Uber operate; check coverage locally.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is common but traffic is heavy. Check licence rules before booking.
  • ✗WalkingFine only in cooler months, and the city is spread out. Use taxis in summer.

Climate

Hot desert climate: long, very hot summers, mild winters and almost no rain.

  • ☀️Best time: November to MarchHighs of 19–26°C and cool evenings. Ideal for souks, the corniche and walking.
  • 🥵Summer is extreme (June to September)Highs of 41–45°C. Plan indoor sights by day and go out only early or late.
  • 🌂Very little rainA few rain days between November and March. No umbrella needed most of the year.
  • 🌬️Dusty winds possibleDust can haze the sky in spring and summer. Check conditions before desert trips.
  • 🧴Strong sunSun is intense even in winter. Use sunscreen, a hat and water.
  • 🧳What to packLight breathable clothes, a light layer for winter nights and cool indoor AC, and modest dress for mosques.

Landmarks 17

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Kuwait Towers & Corniche

    The city's most recognizable icon, a trio of blue-tiled towers on the waterfront with a viewing deck and a revolving restaurant. The surrounding Corniche is perfect for an evening stroll. Tip: visit around sunset for cooler weather and great photos; modest dress is recommended.

  2. 2

    Liberation Tower

    One of the world's tallest telecommunications towers, offering sweeping views over Kuwait City. It's a must-see for its futuristic design and central location. Tip: check opening hours in advance as access can be limited; go on a clear day for the best views.

  3. 3

    Souk Al-Mubarakiya

    A bustling traditional market where you can experience old Kuwaiti commerce, spices, and local food. It's the heart of the city's heritage and a great place to wander. Tip: visit in the late afternoon or evening when it's liveliest; bargain politely and try the local snacks.

  4. 4

    Grand Mosque of Kuwait

    The country's largest mosque, an architectural masterpiece with beautiful Islamic design. It's a significant religious and cultural site. Tip: non-Muslims can join guided tours (check times); dress modestly and remove shoes before entering.

  5. 5

    Al Hamra Tower

    A striking modern skyscraper and one of the tallest buildings in the world, known for its unique twisting shape. It's a symbol of Kuwait's contemporary architecture. Tip: while the tower itself is mostly offices, the surrounding area has upscale shops and cafes; best viewed from a distance at dusk.

  6. 6

    Seif Palace

    The official palace of the Emir, featuring traditional Islamic architecture and beautiful gardens. It's a landmark of political and historical importance. Tip: it's not open to the public, but you can admire the exterior and take photos from outside; best visited during daylight hours.

  7. 7

    National Museum of Kuwait

    Home to artifacts from Kuwait's history, including the famous Al-Sabah collection of Islamic art. It's an essential stop for understanding the country's heritage. Tip: check for special exhibitions; the museum is air-conditioned, making it a good midday activity.

  8. 8

    Tareq Rajab Museum

    A private museum with an impressive collection of Islamic art, ceramics, and manuscripts. It's a hidden gem for culture lovers. Tip: it's located in the Jabriya district; arrange a taxi and confirm opening hours before you go.

    • Swap a city afternoon for the Kuwait Towers and the corniche walk at sunset.
    • Add a half-day to the Grand Mosque and the Tareq Rajab Museum of Islamic art.
    • Families can swap a souk visit for the Scientific Center aquarium and its dhow harbour.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; buses and taxis do the work.
    • KPTC and CityBus share routes: about 300 fils in town, 350 fils longer.
    • Bus 13 links the airport to Mirqab in about 1 hour.
    • Taxis ignore meters, so agree the fare first; orange taxis start near KD 1.
    • Ride apps like Careem and Uber operate; check locally for coverage.
    • Driving is common but traffic is heavy; walking is fine only in cooler months.

    Where to stay

    • Mirqab · ≈ KWD 25–70 a night
      Central and close to the souks and corniche; bus 13 from the airport stops here.
    • Salmiya · ≈ KWD 30–90 a night
      Lively shopping and café district; good for families and evening walks.
    • Sharq · ≈ KWD 35–110 a night
      Business district with the towers and waterfront; handy for meetings.
    • Bneid Al Gar · ≈ KWD 25–75 a night
      Quiet residential spot between the centre and Salmiya; good value.

    Food & drink

    • Try Kuwaiti machboos (spiced rice with meat or fish) and mutabbaq samak, a rice-and-fish dish.
    • Souq Sharq and the old Mubarakiya market are good for dates, spices and cheap local snacks.
    • Fish is a local staple; look for grilled hammour or zubaidi at seafood restaurants.
    • Cheap eats: shawarma and Indian or Pakistani cafeterias, often a few KWD a meal.
    • Alcohol is not sold in Kuwait. Expect juice bars and strong Arabic coffee instead.

    Tips

    1. Summer temperatures can exceed 50°C (122°F); avoid outdoor activities during the day
    2. Alcohol is strictly prohibited nationwide
    3. Dress modestly in public spaces
    4. Visit Souq Al Mubarakiya for a traditional Kuwaiti market experience
    5. Weekend falls on Friday and Saturday
