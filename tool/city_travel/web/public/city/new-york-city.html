Getting around

The subway for most trips; walk Manhattan's grid for short hops.

  • ✓Metro ★The subway runs 24 hours. Tap OMNY (contactless) for $3.00 a ride, with a weekly fare cap.
  • ✓BusBuses use the same fare. The M-routes cover Manhattan avenues.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsYellow cabs are metered (plus congestion surcharges). Uber and Lyft work everywhere.
  • ✓Rental carDriving and parking in Manhattan are slow and expensive. Skip the car.
  • ✓Walking ★Manhattan's grid is easy to walk. 20 blocks north-south is about a mile.
  • ✓FerryThe free Staten Island Ferry passes the Statue of Liberty.

Climate

Four clear seasons. Hot, humid summers and cold winters. Spring and autumn are the best times to walk the city.

  • 🌸Best time: April to June and September to OctoberHighs of 16–26°C. Comfortable for walking and parks. These are the busiest months.
  • 🥵Summer is hot and humid (July to August)Highs near 28–30°C with muggy nights. Plan indoor sights at midday and walk in the morning or evening.
  • 🧥Winter is cold (December to February)Highs of 4–7°C and lows near or below 0°C. Bring a warm coat, hat and gloves.
  • 🌧️Rain is spread through the yearAbout 8–13 rain days a month. April and July are the wettest. Carry a small umbrella.
  • 🍂Autumn is crisp and clearOctober highs near 19°C and fewer rain days. Great for parks and skyline views.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable walking shoes, layers, a rain jacket and sunscreen for summer. A warm coat for winter.

Landmarks 75

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Statue of Liberty

    The symbol of America on Liberty Island. Only Statue City Cruises from Battery Park lands there; book pedestal or crown tickets weeks ahead. The free Staten Island Ferry passes it without landing.

  2. 2

    Times Square

    Neon heart of Midtown and the Broadway theatre district. Go after dark for the full effect; the TKTS booth sells same-day discount show tickets.

  3. 3

    Central Park

    843 acres of lawns, lakes and bridges in the middle of Manhattan. Walk from the Plaza to Bethesda Terrace and Bow Bridge, or rent a bike.

  4. 4

    Empire State Building

    The 1931 Art Deco skyscraper with an open-air deck on the 86th floor. Open until late; go at sunset for day and night views in one visit.

  5. 5

    Brooklyn Bridge

    The 1883 stone-and-steel bridge. Walk it from Manhattan to DUMBO (about 30 minutes) for skyline views; early morning avoids the crowds.

  6. 6

    Metropolitan Museum of Art

    One of the world's greatest museums, from Egyptian temples to Van Gogh. Allow half a day; the rooftop garden is open in warmer months.

  7. 7

    9/11 Memorial

    Two reflecting pools in the footprints of the Twin Towers, free and open daily. The museum below needs a timed ticket and about two hours.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a Midtown day for Brooklyn: walk the Brooklyn Bridge, then DUMBO and Brooklyn Bridge Park.
    • Add a half-day at the American Museum of Natural History or the Met. Both need 3–4 hours.
    • Take the free Staten Island Ferry for skyline and Statue of Liberty views, then ride back.

    Getting around

    • The subway runs 24 hours; tap OMNY (contactless) for $3.00 a ride, with a weekly fare cap.
    • Buses use the same fare; the M-routes cover Manhattan avenues.
    • Yellow cabs are metered (plus congestion surcharges); Uber and Lyft work everywhere.
    • Driving and parking in Manhattan are slow and expensive; skip the car.
    • Manhattan's grid is easy to walk; 20 blocks north-south is about a mile.
    • The free Staten Island Ferry passes the Statue of Liberty.

    Where to stay

    • Midtown · ≈ USD 180–550 a night
      Walk to Times Square, Central Park and Grand Central. Best for first-timers and short trips. Noisy and busy.
    • Lower Manhattan · ≈ USD 170–500 a night
      Near the Statue of Liberty ferry, Wall Street and the Brooklyn Bridge. Quiet at night on weekends.
    • Upper West Side · ≈ USD 160–450 a night
      Next to Central Park and the Natural History Museum. Calmer, family-friendly, good subway links.
    • Brooklyn · ≈ USD 140–420 a night
      Brooklyn Bridge Park, DUMBO and cheaper rooms. 20–30 minutes to Midtown by subway.

    Food & drink

    • Start with a New York breakfast: a bagel with cream cheese and lox, or a bodega egg sandwich, about $8–15.
    • Grab a pizza slice for $4–6. Sit-down dinners run $30–60 a person before drinks.
    • Chinatown and Flushing have the best cheap eats: dumplings, noodles and roast meats for $10–20.
    • Food halls and markets (Chelsea Market, Essex Market) let a group split up and eat cheaply.
    • Book popular dinner spots a few days ahead. Many places add tax and tip on top of menu prices.

    Tips

    1. MetroCard or OMNY tap-to-pay works on all subway and buses — buy a 7-day unlimited pass for extended stays.
    2. Many museums offer free or pay-what-you-wish hours (MoMA on Fridays, Met suggested admission).
    3. Tip 18–22% at restaurants and for taxis — it's standard, not optional.
    4. Times Square and Midtown are tourist hubs — explore neighborhoods like Brooklyn, Harlem, or the West Village for a local feel.
    5. Bagels and dollar slices are iconic — don't leave without trying both.
