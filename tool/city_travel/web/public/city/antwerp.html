Getting around

Walk the compact centre; use trams for longer hops.

  • ✓MetroPre-metro trams run underground in the centre. Buy a ticket before you ride.
  • ✓BusDe Lijn buses and trams share tickets. A 10-fare card costs about €21.
  • ✓Tram ★Trams reach most sights. Single fares last one hour; pay contactless, cash is not accepted.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsUber and Bolt work in Antwerp. Taxis wait at the station and main squares.
  • ✓Rental carNarrow streets and a low-emission zone make driving awkward. Register foreign cars before entering.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is compact and walkable. Many streets are narrow and slow for cars.
  • ✓BikeBikes are popular. Cycle superhighways F1, F4 and F14 reach the city.

Climate

Temperate maritime climate: mild summers, cool winters, and rain in every month.

  • 🌤️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs of 19–23°C and long days. Good for walking the centre and the docks.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 11–14 rain days every month. Carry a small umbrella or rain jacket.
  • 🧥Cool wintersDecember to February highs of 7–9°C and lows near 2°C. Dress in layers.
  • ☀️Mild, not hot, summersJuly and August peak near 23°C. Rarely need air-conditioning.
  • 🌬️Windy and changeableWeather can shift within a day. Check the forecast each morning.
  • 🧳What to packWaterproof shoes, a rain layer, and a warm jumper for evenings, even in summer.

Landmarks 11

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Antwerp Central Station

    Widely ranked among the world's most beautiful railway stations, with a soaring stone concourse and layered platforms that make arriving in Antwerp an event in itself. It is still a working station, so visit outside the morning and evening rush for the best photos, and note that it sits at the end of the main shopping street, a short walk from the old town.

  2. 2

    Cathedral of Our Lady, Antwerp

    The city's tallest church and its defining spire, home to major Rubens paintings and centuries of history. Go early in the day to avoid queues, and check opening hours before you travel as they vary by season.

  3. 3

    Grote Markt

    The historic square at the heart of the old town, ringed by ornate guild houses and the city hall, and the natural starting point for a first walk. It is free and open at all hours; come in the morning for quieter photos or in the evening for the lit-up facades.

  4. 4

    Het Steen

    Antwerp's medieval castle on the Scheldt, the oldest surviving building in the city and a compact, atmospheric stop. It is close to the Grote Markt, so pair the two on foot, and check current opening times as the site has been redeveloped for visitors.

  5. 5

    Museum aan de Stroom

    A striking modern museum whose rooftop panorama over the port and city is a highlight in its own right. The rooftop is reachable separately from the exhibitions; go late afternoon for the best light and fewer crowds.

  6. 6

    Rubens House

    The preserved home and studio of the painter Peter Paul Rubens, one of the city's essential art stops. Book ahead in peak season, as entry is timed and capacity is limited.

  7. 7

    Plantin-Moretus Museum

    A UNESCO-listed printing house and family home that tells the story of early modern publishing in Antwerp. It is a quieter alternative to the big art museums and rewards a slow visit; check opening days, as many Antwerp museums close on Mondays.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for a short train ride to Mechelen or Ghent; check times on the day.
    • Add a half-day at the Plantin-Moretus Museum, a UNESCO-listed printing house.
    • Take a walk or bike ride along the Scheldt river and the old docks in Eilandje.

    Getting around

    • De Lijn runs trams, buses, and pre-metro trams; a 10-fare card costs about €21.
    • Single fares last one hour; pay contactless on board, cash is not accepted.
    • Ride apps like Uber and Bolt work in Antwerp; taxis wait at the station and main squares.
    • The centre is compact and walkable; many streets are narrow and slow for cars.
    • Bikes are popular; cycle superhighways F1, F4, and F14 reach the city.
    • The city centre is a low-emission zone; register foreign cars before entering.

    Where to stay

    • Old Town (Centrum) · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      Walk to the cathedral, Grote Markt and museums. Best for first-timers.
    • Het Zuid · ≈ EUR 85–200 a night
      Quiet streets with galleries, bars and the Museum of Fine Arts nearby.
    • Station District · ≈ EUR 70–170 a night
      Next to the grand Antwerp Central station; easy train links and good value.
    • Eilandje · ≈ EUR 95–230 a night
      Old docks, the MAS museum and modern waterfront hotels.

    Food & drink

    • Try Flemish classics: stoofvlees (beer-braised beef), vol-au-vent and waterzooi.
    • Belgian beer is the local drink. Ask for a small tasting glass and try a few.
    • Waffles and chocolate shops are everywhere in the centre; prices rise near the cathedral.
    • Look for lunch deals (dagschotel) at cafés away from the Grote Markt for better value.
    • Book dinner on weekends; popular places in Het Zuid fill up.

    Tips

    1. Roughly 80% of the world's rough diamonds pass through Antwerp's Diamond District
    2. Renting a bike is the easiest and most local way to navigate the flat city
    3. Visit the spectacular Centraal Station even if you are not catching a train
    4. Antwerp is the fashion capital of Belgium; explore the boutique shops in the Nationalestraat
    5. Try local Belgian frites with mayonnaise and a traditional Bolleke beer
