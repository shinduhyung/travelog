Getting around

Metro for cheap cross-city hops; taxis or ride apps for the Old City and late nights.

  • ✓Metro ★Runs 06:00–24:00. Fare 0.60 AZN with a Bakı Metropoliten Kart (3 AZN refundable deposit).
  • ✓BusBuses cost 0.60 AZN. Newer red buses need a metro card to pay.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Agree on the fare beforehand; a typical city trip is 4–10 AZN. Ride apps like Bolt work.
  • ✓Rental carSpeed cameras are common and fines arrive by SMS. Drive carefully.
  • ✓WalkingPedestrian zones around Fountain Square and the Old City. Crossings can be risky at night.
  • ✓BikeThe city is fairly flat and bike-friendly, but there are no dedicated lanes.

Climate

Baku has cold, damp winters and hot, dry summers. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable for walking.

  • 🌸Best time: April to May and September to OctoberHighs around 17–26°C with moderate rain. Good for the Old City and the boulevard.
  • 🥵Summer is hot and dryJuly and August reach about 31°C with lows near 23°C. Go out early or after sunset.
  • 🧥Winter is cold and dampJanuary and February sit near 8–9°C highs and 3°C lows, with around 5–7 rain days.
  • 🌧️Wettest in autumn and early winterNovember has about 9 rain days and October about 8. Bring a light rain jacket.
  • 💨Windy cityBaku is known as the City of Winds. The seafront can feel cooler and breezier than inland streets.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for spring and autumn, a warm coat for winter, and light breathable clothes plus sunscreen for summer.

Landmarks 15

Must-see · 9

  1. 1

    Icherisheher

    The walled Old Town is Baku's heart and a UNESCO-listed maze of lanes, caravanserais and courtyards. It is the best place to start a first visit. Go early morning to avoid crowds and heat; it is walkable from the city centre and free to enter.

  2. 2

    Maiden Tower of Baku

    This mysterious cylindrical tower inside Icherisheher is the city's symbol and offers views over the Old Town and bay. Climb it for the panorama and the small museum. Buy a ticket on site and go on a weekday morning to skip queues.

  3. 3

    Palace of the Shirvanshahs

    A 15th-century royal complex with a courtyard, mausoleum and palace rooms, right inside the Old Town. It pairs naturally with the Maiden Tower on a walking day. Check opening hours before you go and allow about an hour.

  4. 4

    Flame Towers

    The three flame-shaped skyscrapers are Baku's modern icon and glow at night. Best seen from Baku Boulevard after dark. You cannot usually go up, so plan a walk along the seafront for the view.

  5. 5

    Heydar Aliyev Center

    Zaha Hadid's flowing white building is a must for architecture fans and houses exhibitions inside. The exterior is photogenic from every angle. Take a taxi or metro and check exhibition tickets in advance.

  6. 6

    Baku Boulevard

    A long Caspian seafront promenade for strolling, with the Flame Towers and carpet museum nearby. It is ideal at sunset. It is free and easy to reach from the centre on foot.

  7. 7

    Azerbaijan Carpet Museum

    The building shaped like a rolled carpet sits on the boulevard and holds a rich collection of Azerbaijani carpets. It is a good indoor stop on hot or rainy days. Combine it with a boulevard walk.

  8. 8

    Ateshgah of Baku

    The fire temple at Surakhany is a historic site of fire worship on the Absheron Peninsula. It is a short trip outside the centre. Go with a taxi or tour and allow half a day with Yanar Dag.

  9. 9

    Yanar Dag

    A hillside where natural gas keeps a fire burning, a striking sight tied to Azerbaijan's 'land of fire' identity. It is outside the city, so pair it with Ateshgah. Visit in the evening for the best effect.

    Getting around

    • Metro runs 06:00–24:00; fare 0.60 AZN with a Bakı Metropoliten Kart (3 AZN refundable deposit).
    • Buses cost 0.60 AZN; newer red buses require a metro card for payment.
    • Taxis: agree on fare beforehand; typical city trip 4–10 AZN. Ride apps like Bolt work.
    • Driving: speed cameras are common; fines sent by SMS.
    • Walking: pedestrian zones around Fountain Square and the old town; crossings can be risky at night.
    • Bikes: city is fairly flat and bike-friendly, but no dedicated lanes.

    Where to stay

    • Old City · ≈ AZN 90–320 a night
      Inside the walls, walk to Maiden Tower and the Palace of the Shirvanshahs. Quiet lanes, but narrow streets and few cars.
    • Fountain Square · ≈ AZN 110–380 a night
      The pedestrian heart of the city: cafés, shops and easy walks to the Old City and the boulevard.
    • Seafront Boulevard · ≈ AZN 130–450 a night
      Water views and a long promenade; good for evening walks. A bit further from the Old City lanes.
    • Nizami Street · ≈ AZN 100–350 a night
      Central shopping street with many mid-range hotels and metro access nearby.

    Food & drink

    • Try plov (rice with meat or saffron), dushbara dumplings in broth, and kebab with sumac onions.
    • Tea is a ritual: order çay with jam, walnuts and sweets at a çay evi (tea house).
    • Qutab is a thin stuffed flatbread, cheap and filling; good for a quick lunch.
    • Look for pomegranate dishes and fresh herbs in season; the fruit is a local symbol.
    • Prices vary; a simple local meal is often well under 20 AZN. Check the bill and confirm prices in tourist areas.

    Tips

    1. Wander through the walled 'Icherisheher' (Old City) for ancient architecture and hidden cafes
    2. Check out the Flame Towers, especially at night when they are illuminated
    3. It is nicknamed the 'City of Winds' for a reason—it is consistently very breezy
    4. Bolt is the most convenient way to get around
    5. Enjoy the Baku Boulevard for a long, scenic walk along the Caspian Sea
