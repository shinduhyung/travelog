Getting around

Uber or a taxi with the price agreed first; buses only if you're saving money.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Don't plan around rail in the city.
  • ✓BusCity buses run fixed routes but stops are unlabeled; look for crowds on the sidewalk. Colectivos follow set routes and often have long lines.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis are everywhere; agree the price before you get in and walk away from high offers. Uber works and is often safer and about half the taxi price; drivers may ignore the app map.
  • ✓Rental carAn option for hill-town day trips. Traffic is busy and streets are steep; check your licence needs first.
  • ✓WalkingFine in the centre by day. Avoid strolling Comayagüela after dark.

Climate

Mild highland climate: warm days, cool nights, and a long rainy season from May to October.

  • ☀️Best time: December to MarchDays around 25–30°C and nights near 16–17°C, with the fewest rain days. Ideal for walking the centre.
  • 🌧️Rainy season May to OctoberRain days climb to 19–26 a month. Showers often come in the afternoon; plan indoor sights then.
  • 🌡️Mild, not tropical-hotHighs stay between 25 and 31°C all year thanks to the altitude. Nights are cool, 16–19°C.
  • 🧥Pack a light layerEvenings and early mornings are cool. Bring a light jacket and an umbrella in the wet months.
  • 🥾Wet-season shoesStreets and hills can be slick from May to October. Waterproof shoes help.
  • 📅Shoulder monthsNovember and April are in-between: some rain, comfortable temperatures, fewer crowds.

Landmarks 6

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    Christ of El Picacho

    The towering statue of Christ overlooking Tegucigalpa is the city's most recognizable sight and the top landmark for first-time visitors. Go early in the morning or late afternoon for clearer views and cooler air, and bring a light layer since the hilltop can be breezy.

  2. 2

    Basílica de Suyapa

    This basilica is the country's most important religious site and draws pilgrims year-round, making it a cultural must-see. Dress modestly out of respect, and visit on a weekday morning to avoid the largest crowds.

  3. 3

    Parque Nacional La Tigra

    A cloud forest park just outside the city offers hiking and a cool escape from the urban heat. Arrive early, wear sturdy shoes, and consider going with a guide since trails can be muddy.

  4. 4

    Museum of National Identity (MIN)

    This museum gives a compact overview of Honduran history and culture, useful for context before exploring the city. Check opening hours in advance, as schedules can vary.

  5. 5

    Valle de Ángeles

    A traditional village in the hills near Tegucigalpa known for crafts and a relaxed atmosphere. It makes an easy half-day trip; go on a weekend for the liveliest market scene.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day trip to Valle de Ángeles or Santa Lucía, small colonial towns in the hills. Check transport on the day.
    • Swap a city afternoon for a museum or a viewpoint; ask your hotel which are open that day.
    • With more time, consider a longer trip toward Copán Ruinas. It is far, so plan an overnight.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; getting around relies on buses, taxis and ride apps.
    • City buses run fixed routes but stops are unlabeled; look for crowds on the sidewalk.
    • Colectivos follow set routes and often form long lines of waiting passengers.
    • Taxis are everywhere; agree the price before you get in and walk away from high offers.
    • Uber works and is often safer and about half the taxi price; drivers may ignore the app map.
    • Walking is fine in the centre by day; avoid strolling Comayagüela after dark.

    Where to stay

    • Centro · ≈ HNL 500–1,400 a night
      Walk to the cathedral, plazas and main museums. Busiest by day; quiet and empty after dark.
    • Comayagüela · ≈ HNL 350–900 a night
      Cheapest beds and local markets across the river. Avoid walking here after dark.
    • Boulevard Morazán · ≈ HNL 900–2,200 a night
      Modern hotels, restaurants and malls. Easy taxi and ride-app pickups.
    • Lomas del Guijarro · ≈ HNL 1,200–3,000 a night
      Quiet, hilly, upscale area with the safest-feeling streets and good views.

    Food & drink

    • Try baleadas: thick flour tortillas with beans, cheese or egg. Cheap and everywhere.
    • Look for Honduran plates: grilled meat with rice, beans and fried plantain.
    • Sopa de caracol (conch soup) and seafood are worth ordering at coastal-style places.
    • Markets in Comayagüela are good for cheap local snacks by day; go with a local or by taxi.
    • Coffee is a local pride: ask for Honduran beans at cafés on Boulevard Morazán.

    Tips

    1. Safety is a concern; stick to designated tourist areas and use registered transport
    2. The city is quite hilly, so wear comfortable shoes
    3. Visit El Picacho for the best views of the valley and the giant Christ statue
    4. Try baleadas, a classic Honduran dish of flour tortillas with beans and cheese
    5. The new airport is about an hour outside the city; plan transit accordingly
