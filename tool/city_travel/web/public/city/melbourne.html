Getting around

Trams in the CBD and trains for longer hops; walk the grid for short trips.

  • ✓MetroTrains run every 10–20 minutes and reach the suburbs. A Night Network runs after midnight on Fri–Sat.
  • ✓Tram ★Trams are free inside the CBD Free Tram Zone. Touch on with myki only if you travel beyond it.
  • ✓BusBuses fill the gaps where trains and trams don't go. Pay with myki.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered; Uber, DiDi and Ola operate and often cost less. Handy for late nights.
  • ✓Rental carEasy on freeways but CBD parking is costly. Skip the car in town; rent only for day trips.
  • ✓Walking ★The CBD grid is walkable. Shared bikes and scooters cover short hops.

Climate

Temperate and changeable: warm dry summers, cool wet winters, and rain possible in any month.

  • ☀️Best time: December to MarchWarm days around 24–26°C and fewer rain days. Ideal for the beach, gardens and outdoor dining.
  • 🥶Winter is cool and damp (June to August)Highs only 13–14°C with 10–13 rain days a month. Bring a warm coat and plan indoor sights.
  • 🌦️Rain in every monthExpect 6–13 rain days whatever the season. Carry a small umbrella and a light layer.
  • 🌡️Weather changes fastA warm morning can turn cool and showery by afternoon. Dress in layers you can add or shed.
  • 🏖️Beach days are summer-onlySt Kilda and the bay are best December to March. Water stays cool even then.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes for walking. Add a hat and sunscreen for summer.

Landmarks 23

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Royal Exhibition Building

    A UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the world's oldest surviving exhibition halls, it is the grand centrepiece of Carlton Gardens. Visit on a weekday morning for quieter gardens, and check ahead whether interior tours are running since access is often limited to guided visits.

  2. 2

    Flinders Street Station

    The city's most photographed facade, a yellow Edwardian landmark sitting right on the Yarra at the main rail hub. It is free to admire from the corner of Swanston and Flinders Streets; come at dusk when the clocks and dome light up.

  3. 3

    Shrine of Remembrance

    Victoria's solemn war memorial, with a crypt, gallery and a rooftop balcony giving sweeping views over the city. Entry is free; it is a short walk from the Royal Botanic Gardens and best visited in the cooler morning hours.

  4. 4

    Federation Square

    The city's central urban hub, home to major museums and galleries and a natural meeting point by the river. Drop by for the architecture and events, then walk across to the Yarra for the riverside promenade.

  5. 5

    National Gallery of Victoria

    Australia's oldest and most visited public gallery, with a strong international and Australian collection and a famous stained-glass ceiling. General entry is free; allow a few hours and check opening days before you go.

  6. 6

    Queen Victoria Market

    A sprawling historic market for produce, deli goods and souvenirs, and a Melbourne institution. Go early in the morning for the freshest food and fewer crowds; it is a short tram ride from the central grid.

  7. 7

    Melbourne Cricket Ground

    One of the world's great sports stadiums and the spiritual home of Australian cricket and football. Book a stadium tour or catch a match; the surrounding parkland is an easy walk from the city.

  8. 8

    Royal Botanic Gardens Victoria

    Lush, free gardens along the river with lakes, lawns and themed plant collections. Enter from the Shrine side and bring water and sun protection; it is ideal for a relaxed half-day.

  9. 9

    Eureka Tower

    A soaring skyscraper whose Skydeck offers the best high views over the city and bay. Go near sunset for day-to-night views, and book tickets online to skip queues.

  10. 10

    St Kilda Beach

    The city's classic seaside escape, with a pier, promenade and nearby penguin viewing at dusk. Take a tram from the centre and allow time to wander the foreshore.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for the Great Ocean Road: a long day tour, or drive yourself and stay a night in Apollo Bay.
    • Take a train to the Yarra Valley or Mornington Peninsula for wineries; check timetables and book tastings.
    • Phillip Island for penguins at dusk: tours run from the city, or drive about 2 hours. Book ahead.

    Getting around

    • Trains, trams and buses run on myki; a 2-hour fare is about $5.70 and a weekday daily cap about $11.40.
    • Trams are free inside the CBD Free Tram Zone; touch on only if you travel beyond it.
    • Buses fill the gaps; trains run every 10-20 min and a Night Network runs after midnight on Fri-Sat.
    • Taxis and Uber, DiDi and Ola all operate; taxis are metered and apps often cost less.
    • Driving is easy on freeways but parking in the CBD is costly; skip the car in town.
    • The CBD grid is walkable; shared bikes and scooters cover short hops.

    Where to stay

    • CBD · ≈ AUD 140–380 a night
      Walk to the laneways, Queen Victoria Market and the free tram zone. Best base for a first visit.
    • Southbank · ≈ AUD 160–420 a night
      River views and a short walk over the bridge to the CBD. Good for evenings out.
    • St Kilda · ≈ AUD 120–320 a night
      Beach, pier and cake shops. A tram ride from the centre; quieter at night.
    • Fitzroy · ≈ AUD 130–350 a night
      Bars, galleries and cafés on Brunswick Street. Walkable and lively.

    Food & drink

    • Start with coffee: Melbourne cafés take it seriously. A flat white is the local order, about $4–6.
    • Queen Victoria Market: graze the deli hall and hot-doughnut stall. Cheapest on a weekday morning.
    • Laneway bars and small restaurants fill the CBD blocks off Flinders Lane. Book dinner on weekends.
    • Try a pub counter meal (parma or pie) for a filling lunch, roughly $20–30.
    • Asian food is strong and cheap: dumplings and noodles in Chinatown and Richmond, about $12–20 a plate.

    Tips

    1. Trams traveling within the designated CBD Free Tram Zone are entirely free
    2. The coffee is world-class — order a 'flat white' or 'magic' at a hidden laneway cafe
    3. Always carry an umbrella or a jacket, as the weather can change drastically within hours
    4. Explore the narrow, graffiti-covered laneways like Hosier Lane for street art and boutique dining
    5. Myki card is mandatory for public transport outside the free tram zone; tap on and off
