Getting around

Use Uber, Careem or taxis for most trips; the BRT bus is the cheap backbone.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram in Amman. Don't plan around rail.
  • ✓Bus ★The BRT bus network is the fast backbone, about 0.610 JOD a ride. City buses are cheap but often Arabic-only; pay exact fare or swipe a Visa or MasterCard.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Yellow and grey taxis use meters; most trips in town stay under 2–5 JOD. Uber and Careem work well, with core rides about 1–4 JOD.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is common but traffic is heavy. Rental desks are at the airport and in town.
  • ✗WalkingWalking is patchy: steep hills, missing sidewalks and few crossings. Plan some rides.

Climate

Mediterranean-style climate: hot, dry summers and cool, wet winters. Spring and autumn are the best times to visit.

  • 🌸Best time: April to May and OctoberWarm days around 23–28°C with little rain. Ideal for the Citadel and walking downtown.
  • 🥵Hot, dry summer (June to September)Highs of 31–33°C and almost no rain. Sightsee early morning and evening; rest indoors at midday.
  • 🌧️Wet winter (December to February)Highs of 12–15°C with 6–9 rainy days a month. Bring a warm layer and a rain jacket.
  • 🌂Rain is seasonalMost rain falls from November to March. Summer months are almost completely dry.
  • 🌡️Cool nights year-roundEvenings drop fast, especially in winter. A light jacket helps in spring and autumn.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes and sunscreen for summer; warm layers and a rain jacket for winter. Comfortable shoes for steep, hilly streets.

Landmarks 6

Must-see · 2

  1. 1

    Amman Citadel

    The hilltop site where Amman's history layers from Roman to Umayyad times, with the Temple of Hercules columns and the Umayyad palace remains. It is the city's defining sight and gives wide views over downtown. Go early morning or late afternoon for cooler weather and better light; it is a short taxi ride from downtown.

  2. 2

    Roman Theater

    A well-preserved 2nd-century Roman theater cut into the hillside, still used for performances. Its scale and setting make it a must-see for first-time visitors. Visit in the morning to avoid the midday heat; it is a short walk from downtown and the Citadel.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day trip to Jerash for Roman ruins; check bus and tour options on the day.
    • Swap an afternoon for Madaba and Mount Nebo, known for mosaics and views.
    • Float in the Dead Sea: a long half-day trip; check transport and prices on the day.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; the BRT bus network is the fast backbone, about 0.610 JOD a ride.
    • City buses are cheap but often Arabic-only; pay exact fare or swipe a Visa or MasterCard.
    • Yellow and grey taxis use meters; most trips in town stay under 2-5 JOD.
    • Uber and Careem both work well in Amman; core rides cost about 1-4 JOD.
    • Driving is common but traffic is heavy; car rental desks are at the airport and in town.
    • Walking is patchy: steep hills, missing sidewalks and few crossings, so plan some rides. No ferry service.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown (Al-Balad) · ≈ JOD 25–90 a night
      Walk to the Citadel, Roman Theatre and Hashemite Plaza. Cheapest base and best for street food.
    • Jabal Amman (1st Circle) · ≈ JOD 40–130 a night
      Quiet, leafy streets near Rainbow Street cafés. Good middle ground between old and new Amman.
    • Abdoun · ≈ JOD 70–200 a night
      Upscale, calm and close to restaurants and malls. Best if you want quiet nights and easy taxis.
    • Shmeisani · ≈ JOD 55–160 a night
      Modern business district with hotels and cafés. Central for the BRT and short rides to most sights.

    Food & drink

    • Start with falafel and hummus at a downtown shop; a full plate is often under 3 JOD.
    • Try mansaf, the national dish of lamb cooked in yogurt, for a bigger sit-down meal.
    • Rainbow Street and Jabal Amman have cafés and sweet shops; knafeh is the local dessert to order.
    • Cheap eats: shawarma, falafel and fuul sandwiches for about 1–3 JOD.
    • Book a popular restaurant for Friday lunch; many places are busy and some close early on Fridays.

    Tips

    1. Visit the Amman Citadel at sunset for spectacular views over the hilly city and Roman Theater
    2. Uber and Careem are the best and safest ways to navigate the difficult terrain
    3. Dress modestly in public spaces, covering shoulders and knees
    4. Eat falafel and hummus at the famous Hashem Restaurant in downtown
    5. Stairs connecting the various 'jabals' are common, so wear comfortable shoes
