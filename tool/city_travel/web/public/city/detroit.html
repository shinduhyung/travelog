Getting around

Drive or use ride apps for most trips; the QLine and People Mover help downtown and Midtown.

  • ✗MetroNo metro rail. Don't plan around a subway.
  • ✓TrainThe QLine streetcar runs along Woodward Ave from downtown to New Center; fare $1.50 for 3 hours. The People Mover loops downtown; fare $0.75 per ride.
  • ✓BusSMART and DDOT buses cover the metro; fare $2. Useful but slow for cross-town trips.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and Lyft are widely used; taxis also available. Handy for airport runs and late nights.
  • ✓Rental car ★Driving is easiest; freeways are modern but rush hour can be slow. Check parking at your hotel.
  • ✓WalkingWalking is good downtown; biking is growing with rental options.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cold, snowy winters, mild springs, warm humid summers and crisp autumns. Rain falls year-round.

  • ❄️Winter is cold (Dec–Feb)Highs around 1–4°C and lows near -6°C. Snow is likely. Bring a heavy coat, hat and gloves.
  • 🌸Spring is mild and wetHighs climb from 7°C in March to 20°C in May. April is the wettest month with about 14 rain days.
  • ☀️Summer is warm and humidHighs around 26–28°C from June to August. Not extreme, but muggy at times; mornings are best outdoors.
  • 🍂Autumn is crispHighs fall from 24°C in September to 9°C in November. A light jacket helps in the evenings.
  • 🌧️Rain any monthExpect roughly 9–14 rain days each month. Keep a compact umbrella handy.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for spring and autumn, a warm winter coat for Dec–Feb, and light clothes for summer. Comfortable shoes for walking.

Landmarks 7

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Detroit Institute of Arts

    One of America's great art museums, home to Diego Rivera's monumental Detroit Industry murals and a world-class collection spanning centuries. It is the city's top must-see for first-time visitors. Tip: closed Mondays; book timed tickets online and allow at least two hours.

  2. 2

    Motown Museum

    The original Hitsville U.S.A. house where the Motown sound was born, with studios and memorabilia that tell Detroit's music story. A pilgrimage for music fans. Tip: tours are guided and sell out, so reserve ahead; it is a short drive from downtown.

  3. 3

    The Henry Ford

    A vast museum complex in Dearborn covering American innovation, with the Rosa Parks bus and presidential limousines, plus Greenfield Village next door. Plan a full day. Tip: buy a combined ticket online and arrive early to beat crowds.

  4. 4

    Belle Isle Park

    A 982-acre island park in the Detroit River with a conservatory, aquarium, beach and skyline views. It is the city's green escape. Tip: drive or bike over; the park is free to enter and best on a sunny weekday.

  5. 5

    Detroit RiverWalk

    A revitalized waterfront promenade along the Detroit River with views of Canada and public art. Ideal for a stroll or bike ride. Tip: go at sunset for the best skyline photos; it is free and open daily.

  6. 6

    Eastern Market

    America's largest historic public market district, buzzing on Saturdays with local produce, food stalls and murals. A taste of Detroit life. Tip: come Saturday morning for the full market; bring cash for small vendors.

  7. 7

    Guardian Building

    A 1929 Art Deco skyscraper with a stunning painted lobby often called a cathedral of commerce. Quick but memorable. Tip: the lobby is free to visit on weekdays during business hours; dress casually but respectfully.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for Dearborn: the Henry Ford Museum and Greenfield Village show the auto and American history story.
    • Cross the river to Windsor, Canada for a different view of the skyline; bring your passport and check border rules.
    • Sports fans can swap a museum afternoon for a Tigers, Lions, Red Wings or Pistons game; check the schedule and prices on the day.

    Getting around

    • QLine streetcar runs along Woodward Ave from downtown to New Center; fare $1.50 for 3 hours.
    • People Mover loops downtown; fare $0.75 per ride.
    • SMART and DDOT buses cover the metro; fare $2.
    • Ride apps Uber and Lyft are widely used; taxis also available.
    • Driving is easiest; freeways are modern but rush hour can be slow.
    • Walking is good downtown; biking is growing with rental options.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown · ≈ USD 120–320 a night
      Walk to the riverfront, stadiums and the People Mover; easiest base without a car.
    • Midtown · ≈ USD 110–260 a night
      Next to the Detroit Institute of Arts and the Motown Museum; QLine streetcar to downtown.
    • Corktown · ≈ USD 100–240 a night
      Quiet, historic streets with bars and cafés; short ride to downtown and the riverfront.
    • Greektown · ≈ USD 110–280 a night
      Restaurants and nightlife on the doorstep; People Mover stop and walk to downtown sights.

    Food & drink

    • Try a Detroit-style square pizza: thick, crispy edges and cheese to the rim. Many local spots serve it.
    • Coney Island hot dogs are a Detroit staple; several long-running diners downtown and nearby.
    • Eastern Market on a Saturday morning: produce, food stalls and a lively local crowd.
    • Greektown is the place for Greek restaurants and late dinners near downtown.
    • Middle Eastern food in Dearborn, a short drive west, is a local favourite; check hours before you go.

    Tips

    1. Try Detroit-style pizza, known for its thick, crispy, square crust
    2. Visit the Motown Museum (Hitsville U.S.A.) where legendary artists recorded
    3. A car is almost entirely necessary to explore beyond the immediate downtown area
    4. The Detroit Institute of Arts is world-class and houses Diego Rivera's famous industry murals
    5. Cross the Ambassador Bridge or tunnel to briefly visit Canada, but bring a passport
