Getting around

Metro for most trips; walk the old centre.

  • ✓Metro ★13 lines, fast and cheap. Single Zone A fare €1.50–2, airport supplement €3. A Multi Card costs €2.50 and is reloadable; a ten-trip Metrobús ticket is about €12.20.
  • ✓BusBuses share the same tickets. Night buses run when the metro closes around 01:30.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are plentiful and metered. Uber, Cabify and Bolt operate in Madrid.
  • ✗Rental carNot needed in the city. Use the metro and trains for day trips.
  • ✓Walking ★Best way to see the centre. Bikes and e-scooters are widely rented.
  • ✓Tourist Travel CardUnlimited travel: 1 day €10, 2 days €17, 3 days €22.50, 5 days €32.50, 7 days €41. Worth it if you ride often.

Climate

Continental climate: hot, dry summers and cool winters, with the wettest spells in spring and autumn.

  • 🌡️Best months: May, June, SeptemberWarm days around 24–30°C and mostly dry. Great for walking the old centre.
  • 🥵Summer is very hot (July–August)Highs near 33–34°C with little rain. Do museums at midday and walk in the morning or evening.
  • 🌧️Wettest in spring and autumnMarch, April and October each see around 9 rain days. Pack a small umbrella.
  • ❄️Cool wintersJanuary highs near 10°C and lows around 1°C. A warm coat and layers are essential.
  • ☀️Sunny most of the yearEven wet months have plenty of bright days. Sunscreen matters from spring to autumn.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes and a hat for summer; a warm coat for winter; a rain layer for March, April and October; comfortable shoes for cobbled streets.

Landmarks 24

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Prado Museum

    Spain's premier art museum, home to masterpieces by Velázquez, Goya and El Greco. Book tickets online to skip the queue, and visit early morning or late afternoon for fewer crowds.

  2. 2

    Royal Palace of Madrid

    The official residence of the Spanish royal family, with over 3,000 opulent rooms. Arrive early to avoid long lines, and check for changing of the guard schedules.

  3. 3

    Plaza Mayor

    A grand 17th-century square surrounded by arcades and historic buildings. Best visited in the evening when the lights come on; it's a short walk from Puerta del Sol.

  4. 4

    Retiro Park

    Madrid's most beloved park, featuring a tranquil lake and the Crystal Palace. Rent a rowboat or simply stroll; it's easily reached by metro at Retiro station.

  5. 5

    Reina Sofia Museum

    Home to Picasso's Guernica and an outstanding collection of modern Spanish art. Free entry during certain hours; go early or late to avoid crowds.

  6. 6

    Puerta del Sol

    The bustling heart of Madrid, famous for its clock tower and the 'Oso y el Madroño' statue. Visit at night to see the illuminated neon signs and enjoy people-watching.

  7. 7

    Temple of Debod

    An ancient Egyptian temple gifted to Spain, set in a scenic park with stunning sunset views. Free to visit; go at dusk for the best photos.

  8. 8

    Gran Vía

    Madrid's most famous shopping street, lined with grand architecture and theaters. Best explored on foot; it's a short walk from Puerta del Sol.

    Swap in instead

    • Day trip to Toledo: train from Atocha in about 35 minutes; a walled old city full of history.
    • Day trip to Segovia: high-speed train in under 30 minutes for the Roman aqueduct and the Alcázar.
    • Art lovers: swap a city day for the Reina Sofía's Guernica and the Thyssen collection, or add El Escorial by train.

    Getting around

    • Metro: 13 lines, fast and cheap; single Zone A fare €1.50-2, airport supplement €3.
    • Multi Card costs €2.50 and is reloadable; ten-trip Metrobús ticket about €12.20.
    • Tourist Travel Cards: 1 day €10, 2 days €17, 3 days €22.50, 5 days €32.50, 7 days €41.
    • Buses share the same tickets; night buses run when the metro closes around 01:30.
    • Taxis are plentiful and metered; Uber, Cabify and Bolt operate in Madrid.
    • Walking is best in the centre; bikes and e-scooters are widely rented.

    Where to stay

    • Sol · ≈ EUR 90–200 a night
      Dead centre, walk to the Prado, Plaza Mayor and Royal Palace. Metro hub under the square. Noisy at night.
    • Gran Vía · ≈ EUR 100–230 a night
      Theatre strip with shops and big-name hotels. Walk to Sol and Chueca; lively late into the night.
    • La Latina · ≈ EUR 80–180 a night
      Old-town lanes, Sunday El Rastro market and the best tapas bars. Quiet mornings, busy weekends.
    • Salamanca · ≈ EUR 180–450 a night
      Elegant, calm and upscale. Good for shopping and quiet nights; a longer walk or short metro ride to the old centre.

    Food & drink

    • Do a tapas crawl: order one small plate and a drink per bar, then move on. Dinner starts late, often after 21:00.
    • Try local dishes: tortilla española, jamón ibérico, patatas bravas, cocido madrileño and bocadillo de calamares near Plaza Mayor.
    • Markets are good value: Mercado de San Miguel for snacks, San Antón and San Fernando for cheaper local stalls.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead, especially on weekends. Many close one day midweek; check before you go.
    • Late-night eats: churros con chocolate after midnight, and bars around Sol and La Latina stay open very late.

    Tips

    1. Locals eat late; lunch is typically 2-4 PM and dinner rarely starts before 9 PM
    2. Take advantage of free entry hours at major museums like the Prado and Reina Sofía
    3. Order a drink at traditional bars and it often comes with a free tapa
    4. Summers (July/August) are intensely hot; afternoon siestas are a practical way to avoid the heat
    5. A Multi Card (Tarjeta Multi) is required to load metro and bus tickets

    Souvenirs

    Violeta candies

    EUR 7–15100–200g · estimate

    Violet-flavored sugar candies from La Violeta, a Madrid shop that opened in 1915. They are a classic sweet of the city.

    Where: La Violeta, Plaza de Canalejas
    Buy a small bag first, since the box sizes are fixed and you can add more later.

    Vermouth

    EUR 10–18750ml–1L · estimate

    A fortified wine flavored with herbs, popular in Madrid as a pre-lunch drink. Zarro and Zecchini are local brands from the city.

    Where: Mariano Madrueño liquor shop
    Check that the bottle is sealed and packed well, since glass bottles can break in your luggage.

    Espadrilles

    EUR 15–351 pair · estimate

    Traditional Spanish canvas shoes with rope soles. This long-running Madrid shop is a good place to find them.

    Where: Casa Hernanz, Calle Toledo
    Try them on first, because sizing can run differently from shoes at home.
