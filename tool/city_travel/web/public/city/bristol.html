Getting around

Walk the centre and Harbourside; use buses or e-scooters for Clifton and longer hops.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram in Bristol. Don't plan around rail inside the city.
  • ✓Bus ★Buses cover the centre and suburbs. Contactless tap fares are around £2–3.
  • ✓TrainThe Severn Beach line links Temple Meads with Clifton Down and Avonmouth. Handy for a car-free day out.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are common; Uber and Bolt both operate in Bristol.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is slow in the centre. Use Park and Ride at Portway, Brislington or Long Ashton.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre and Harbourside are easy on foot. Temple Meads is about a 15 min walk.
  • ✓E-scooter / e-bikeDott e-scooters and e-bikes cost £0.99 to unlock plus about £0.16 per minute.

Climate

Temperate maritime climate: mild, often cloudy and rain in any month. Rarely very hot or very cold.

  • 🌤️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs of 16–21°C and long daylight. Best for walking the harbour and Clifton.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearExpect 10–15 rain days every month. Showers pass quickly; carry a small umbrella or waterproof.
  • ❄️Mild wintersJanuary and February highs near 8–9°C, lows near 2–4°C. Frost is possible but snow is rare.
  • 🧥Layers work bestDays can warm up then cool fast. A light jacket and a warm mid-layer cover most trips.
  • ☀️Summer is gentleJuly peaks near 21°C. No need for air-conditioning; evenings stay around 13°C.
  • 🧳What to packWaterproof jacket, comfortable walking shoes for hills and cobbles, and a warm layer for evenings.

Landmarks 18

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Clifton Suspension Bridge

    Bristol's most famous sight, a graceful Victorian span over the Avon Gorge with sweeping views. Walk across for free at any time; early morning or sunset gives the best light and fewer crowds.

  2. 2

    SS Great Britain

    Brunel's pioneering iron steamship, now a museum where you can go below the glass 'waterline' into the restored hull. Book tickets online in advance and allow a couple of hours; it sits in the harbour, a short walk from the city centre.

  3. 3

    Bristol Harbour

    The lively heart of the city, ringed by boats, markets and waterfront walks. Wander the harbourside in the afternoon and stay for dinner; it is an easy walk from Bristol Temple Meads station.

  4. 4

    M Shed

    A free museum on the harbourside telling Bristol's story through local voices and exhibits. Entry is free, so it is a good rainy-day stop; check opening days before you go.

  5. 5

    Bristol Museum & Art Gallery

    A free museum with natural history, art and archaeology, close to the university and Brandon Hill. Combine it with a climb up Cabot Tower nearby; admission is free but donations are welcome.

  6. 6

    Bristol Cathedral

    A striking medieval cathedral with a fine vaulted interior, near the harbourside. Entry is generally free; visit on a weekday morning for quiet and check service times before you go.

  7. 7

    Brandon Hill

    A green hill in the city centre topped by Cabot Tower, with free views over Bristol. Climb the tower on a clear day; it is a short walk from Park Street and the museum.

  8. 8

    We The Curious

    An interactive science centre on the harbourside, great for families and curious adults. Book tickets online and allow half a day; it is a short walk from M Shed.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for the Clifton Suspension Bridge and the Downs: walk or take a short bus, then tea in Clifton Village.
    • Take the train to Bath (about 15 min from Temple Meads) for Roman baths and Georgian streets. Check times on the day.
    • Head to the Severn Beach rail line for a half-day out to Avonmouth or the coast. Simple, cheap and car-free.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; the Severn Beach rail line links Temple Meads with Clifton Down and Avonmouth.
    • Buses cover the city centre and suburbs; contactless tap fares are around £2-3.
    • Taxis and private hire are common; Uber and Bolt both operate in Bristol.
    • Driving is slow in the centre; use Park and Ride sites at Portway, Brislington and Long Ashton.
    • The centre and Harbourside are easy on foot; Temple Meads is about a 15 min walk.
    • Dott e-scooters and e-bikes cost £0.99 to unlock plus about £0.16 per minute.

    Where to stay

    • Harbourside · ≈ GBP 90–200 a night
      Walk to the SS Great Britain, M Shed and the waterfront bars. Quiet at night but close to everything.
    • Old City · ≈ GBP 85–190 a night
      Central for St Nicholas Market, the harbour and Temple Meads. Best if you want to walk everywhere.
    • Clifton · ≈ GBP 100–240 a night
      Leafy streets, the Suspension Bridge and the university. Village feel, 20–30 min walk or a short bus to the centre.
    • Temple Meads · ≈ GBP 70–160 a night
      Cheapest and handiest for trains and coaches. 15 min walk to the centre; good for short stays.

    Food & drink

    • Try a West Country pasty and a proper cider. Ask for dry or sweet; cider here is stronger than you expect.
    • St Nicholas Market in the Old City has cheap global street food at lunch, roughly £6–12 a portion.
    • Stokes Croft and Gloucester Road are the independent café and brunch strips. Expect queues at weekends.
    • Book ahead for dinner on Friday and Saturday. Popular Harbourside and Clifton places fill up.
    • Look for local produce: Somerset cheese, Severn salmon and Bristol-made beer. Check menus for what's in season.

    Tips

    1. Take a walking tour to find original Banksy artworks hidden around his home city
    2. Walk across the Clifton Suspension Bridge for stunning views of the Avon Gorge
    3. Drink local West Country cider in a historic harbor pub
    4. Explore the SS Great Britain, an innovative 19th-century passenger ship
    5. The city has a fiercely independent spirit, best seen in the shops of Stokes Croft
