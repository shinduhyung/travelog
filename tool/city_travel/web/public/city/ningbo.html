Getting around

Metro for most trips; Didi or Amap for short hops and late nights.

  • ✓Metro ★Lines 1–5 cover the city. Fares are low and stations are signed in English.
  • ✓BusBuses cost ¥1, or ¥2 with air conditioning. Cheap but slower than the metro.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis start at ¥10 for the first 3 km plus ¥2 fuel tax. Didi and Amap work well; Uber does not operate in China.
  • ✓Rental carNot advised for short visits; traffic is dense downtown. Use metro and taxis instead.
  • ✓WalkingGood around Tianyi Square, the Old Bund and old streets. Use metro or a taxi for longer hops.
  • ✓Bike rentalBikes can be rented outside downtown; motorbikes are banned in the centre.

One night suits the Tianyi Pavilion, Moon Lake, and old Bund sightseeing in one day; skipping seafood meals and countryside trips keeps it brief.

Climate

Humid subtropical: hot, wet summers and cool, drier winters. Spring and autumn are the best times to visit.

  • 🌸Best time: April, May and OctoberHighs of 21–25°C with manageable rain. Good for walking the old streets and the Bund.
  • 🥵Hot, humid summer (July–August)Highs near 33°C and lows near 26°C. Do outdoor sights early or late; rest indoors at midday.
  • 🌧️Rain peaks in JuneAbout 19 rain days in June and 14 in May. Bring a compact umbrella and waterproof shoes.
  • 🧥Cool winters (December–February)Highs of 11–12°C and lows of 3–4°C. A warm coat and layers are needed, especially by the river.
  • 💧Humid most of the yearSummers feel sticky. Light, breathable clothes help; indoor air-conditioning can feel cold.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for spring and autumn, a rain jacket, sunscreen and a hat for summer, and a warm coat for winter.

Must-see

  1. 1

    Tianyi Pavilion

    China's oldest surviving private library and the pride of Ningbo, a quiet garden complex of wooden halls and stone courtyards. It is the one sight first-time visitors come for. Go on a weekday morning to avoid crowds, and check opening hours before you set out as they can change by season.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for Putuo or the nearby islands by ferry or bus; check schedules on the day.
    • Swap a city afternoon for a hill or lake walk outside downtown; use a taxi or Didi to get there.
    • Museum fans can spend a half-day at the Ningbo Museum and nearby parks.

    Getting around

    • Metro lines 1-5 cover the city; fares are low and stations are signed in English.
    • Buses cost ¥1, or ¥2 with air conditioning.
    • Taxis start at ¥10 for the first 3 km plus ¥2 fuel tax.
    • Ride apps Didi and Amap work well; Uber does not operate in China.
    • Driving is not advised for short visits; traffic is dense downtown.
    • Bikes can be rented outside downtown; motorbikes are banned in the centre.

    Where to stay

    • Tianyi Square · ≈ CNY 300–700 a night
      Central and walkable: near the old library, shopping and metro stops. Good first base.
    • Old Bund · ≈ CNY 350–800 a night
      Riverside walks, historic buildings and evening views. Quieter at night.
    • Yinzhou · ≈ CNY 250–600 a night
      Modern district with malls and metro links; good value rooms.
    • Haishu · ≈ CNY 280–650 a night
      Old streets and local food close by; easy metro access to the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Try Ningbo seafood: steamed fish, crab and salted shellfish. Prices vary by season, so ask before ordering.
    • Ningbo tangyuan (glutinous rice balls in sweet soup) are the city's signature snack; cheap at local shops.
    • Look for 'Ningbo cuisine' restaurants: fresh, lightly sweet and salty flavours, not heavy spice.
    • Old Bund and Tianyi Square have many mid-range restaurants; Haishu old streets are better for cheap local eats.
    • Seafood is sold by weight. Confirm the price per jin (500 g) before it is cooked.

    Tips

    1. Visit the Tianyi Pavilion, the oldest private library still existing in China
    2. Try local Ningbo seafood and the famous sweet sesame dumplings (Tangyuan)
    3. WeChat Pay and Alipay are absolutely essential for all payments
    4. The city is a major industrial hub with a very long maritime history
    5. High-speed trains make travel to Shanghai exceptionally convenient
