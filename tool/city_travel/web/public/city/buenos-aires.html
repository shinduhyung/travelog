Getting around

Subte for main sights; ride apps or taxis at night.

  • ✓Metro ★The Subte is small but reaches most sights. Pay with a SUBE card or contactless credit card.
  • ✓BusBuses are cheap and cover the whole city, but routes are confusing. Use a route app.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and Cabify work; taxis are plentiful and metered. Handy at night.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is stressful and parking is hard. Use transit or taxis instead.
  • ✓WalkingBest in Palermo, Recoleta and San Telmo. Ferries to Uruguay leave from Puerto Madero.
  • ✓FerryFerries to Uruguay leave from Puerto Madero. Bring your passport and check times on the day.

Climate

Humid subtropical climate: hot summers, cool-to-cold winters, and rain spread through the year.

  • ☀️Best time: March to May and September to NovemberMild highs of 17–25°C. Good for walking, parks and outdoor sights.
  • 🥵Summer is hot and humid (December to February)Highs near 27–28°C with muggy nights. Sightsee early or late; rest at midday.
  • 🧥Winter is cool (June to August)Highs of 14–16°C and lows near 8°C. Pack a warm coat and layers.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 6–9 rain days each month. Showers can be heavy but short; carry a small umbrella.
  • 🌬️Windy daysBreezy spells, especially near the river in Puerto Madero. A light windproof layer helps.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, comfortable walking shoes, a light rain jacket and sunscreen for summer.

Landmarks 39

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Teatro Colon

    One of the world's great opera houses, famous for its acoustics and gilded interior. Book a guided tour in advance, as slots sell out, and dress smartly for evening performances.

  2. 2

    Casa Rosada

    The pink presidential palace facing Plaza de Mayo, home to Argentina's executive branch. Visit on a weekday for the free guided tour, and see the balcony made famous by Eva Perón.

  3. 3

    Recoleta Cemetery

    An eerie, beautiful maze of mausoleums where Eva Perón rests. Go early morning to avoid crowds; entry is free and the gates are open daily.

  4. 4

    Plaza de Mayo

    The historic heart of the city, ringed by the Casa Rosada and the Cabildo. Come on a Thursday afternoon to see the Mothers of Plaza de Mayo march.

  5. 5

    Obelisco de Buenos Aires

    The city's iconic white obelisk on Avenida 9 de Julio, best seen at night when it is lit. It is a quick photo stop near the Teatro Colon.

  6. 6

    Caminito

    The brightly painted street in La Boca, alive with tango dancers and street art. Go on a sunny weekday morning and stay on the main streets for safety.

  7. 7

    El Ateneo Grand Splendid

    A stunning bookstore set inside a former theater, often ranked among the world's most beautiful. Entry is free; visit on a weekday to browse in peace.

  8. 8

    Puente de la Mujer

    A sleek white pedestrian bridge by Santiago Calatrava in the modern Puerto Madero district. Walk across at sunset and continue along the waterfront for dinner.

  9. 9

    MALBA

    The leading museum of Latin American art, with strong holdings of modern and contemporary work. Closed on Tuesdays; check for discounted late-afternoon tickets.

  10. 10

    Palacio Barolo

    A striking 1920s tower inspired by Dante's Divine Comedy. Book a guided tour to reach the lighthouse at the top for skyline views.

    Getting around

    • Subte (metro) is small but reaches most sights; pay with a SUBE card or contactless credit card.
    • Buses are cheap and cover the whole city, but routes are confusing; use a route app.
    • Buy a SUBE card at kioscos or Subte stations; one card can be shared by a group.
    • Ride apps such as Uber and Cabify work; taxis are plentiful and metered.
    • Driving is stressful; parking is hard, so use transit or taxis instead.
    • Walking is best in Palermo, Recoleta and San Telmo; ferries to Uruguay leave from Puerto Madero.

    Where to stay

    • Recoleta · ≈ ARS 70,000–180,000 a night
      Quiet, leafy and safe at night. Walk to the cemetery, museums and cafés; good for first-timers.
    • Palermo · ≈ ARS 65,000–200,000 a night
      Best for food and nightlife. Soho and Hollywood have bars, restaurants and boutiques on your doorstep.
    • San Telmo · ≈ ARS 50,000–130,000 a night
      Old cobbled streets, tango and the Sunday market. Livelier and cheaper; some streets are quiet at night.
    • Centro / Retiro · ≈ ARS 55,000–160,000 a night
      Central for the Obelisk, Plaza de Mayo and the train and bus stations. Busy by day, quieter after dark.

    Food & drink

    • Order bife de chorizo (sirloin) or ojo de bife at a parrilla. Portions are big; one steak can feed two light eaters.
    • Try empanadas, milanesa and provoleta. Lunch is the big meal; many places sell cheap set lunches (menú del día).
    • Drink local: Malbec and Torrontés wines, plus mate. Ask for agua sin gas if you want still water.
    • Dinner starts late. Locals sit down around 9–10pm; many restaurants are quiet before 8pm.
    • Book popular Palermo and Recoleta restaurants a few days ahead. Prices change fast with inflation, so check menus on the day.

    Tips

    1. Get a SUBE card to use the Subte and Colectivos
    2. Dinner happens very late — restaurants often don't fill up until 10 PM or later
    3. The parallel exchange rate (Blue Dollar) can make the city highly affordable for tourists bringing foreign currency
    4. Attending a Boca Juniors or River Plate football match is a legendary, intense experience
    5. Don't rush; local culture moves at a relaxed pace, especially in cafes
