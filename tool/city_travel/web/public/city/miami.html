Getting around

Walk in dense areas; use Uber or Lyft for longer trips and the beach.

  • ✓MetroMetrorail runs elevated trains to downtown, the airport and Dadeland; fare about $2.25. Metromover is free and links downtown and Brickell.
  • ✓BusMetrobus covers the county; route S runs downtown to Miami Beach.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis are metered and pricey; Uber and Lyft work well.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is common but traffic and parking are hard; walking is best in dense areas.
  • ✓Walking ★Best in dense areas like South Beach, Brickell and Coconut Grove.
  • ✓TrainTri-Rail commuter trains link Miami, Fort Lauderdale and West Palm Beach.

Climate

Subtropical Miami: warm most of the year, with a wet, humid summer and a drier, milder winter.

  • ☀️Best time: November to AprilHighs around 24–28°C with fewer rain days. Ideal for the beach, walking and outdoor sights.
  • 🌧️Wet season: June to SeptemberAbout 22–25 rain days a month. Expect short, heavy afternoon storms; mornings are often clearer.
  • 🥵Hot and humid summerHighs near 30–31°C and lows around 25–26°C. Muggy nights; plan indoor breaks midday.
  • 🌀Hurricane seasonRuns roughly June to November, peaking late summer and autumn. Check forecasts and travel insurance.
  • 🏖️Beach daysComfortable from spring to autumn, but summer sun is strong. Use shade and sunscreen.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, a rain jacket or umbrella for summer, sunscreen and a hat; a light layer for cooler winter evenings.

Landmarks 13

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    South Beach

    Miami's most famous stretch of sand, known for its turquoise water, lively boardwalk and people-watching. Go early in the morning for a quieter beach and cooler sun, and bring your own towel and water since rentals add up.

  2. 2

    Art Deco Historic District

    A compact stretch of pastel 1920s-30s buildings along Ocean Drive and Collins Avenue, best explored on foot. Late afternoon light is ideal for photos, and many buildings are best appreciated from the street.

  3. 3

    Wynwood Walls

    An open-air gallery of large-scale street murals that changes regularly and anchors the Wynwood arts neighborhood. Visit on a weekday morning to avoid crowds, and wear comfortable shoes for walking the surrounding blocks.

  4. 4

    Little Havana

    The heart of Miami's Cuban community, centered on Calle Ocho with cigar shops, cafes and street life. Go in the afternoon for the liveliest atmosphere and expect to pay in cash at smaller spots.

  5. 5

    Vizcaya Museum and Gardens

    A grand waterfront estate with formal gardens and richly decorated interiors, one of Miami's most photogenic sights. Book tickets online in advance and go at opening time to beat the heat and crowds.

  6. 6

    Everglades National Park

    A vast wetland wilderness just outside the city, home to alligators, wading birds and mangrove channels. Start early, bring insect repellent and sun protection, and check the park entrance and tour options before you go.

  7. 7

    Pérez Art Museum Miami

    A waterfront modern and contemporary art museum with strong holdings of Latin American and Caribbean work. It is a good indoor option on hot or rainy days; check opening days and ticket prices online.

  8. 8

    Miami Beach

    The barrier island that holds South Beach and much of the city's beach culture, with a long oceanfront promenade. Use the public beach access points and arrive before midday for parking and space.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a beach afternoon for the Everglades: an airboat tour from the Tamiami Trail side, about an hour's drive each way.
    • Art lovers can add a day in Wynwood and the Design District for murals and galleries.
    • Take a day trip to the Florida Keys or Key Biscayne beaches if you want quieter sand.

    Getting around

    • Metrorail runs elevated trains to downtown, the airport and Dadeland; fare about $2.25.
    • Metromover is free and links downtown and Brickell.
    • Metrobus covers the county; route S runs downtown to Miami Beach.
    • Taxis are metered and pricey; Uber and Lyft work well.
    • Driving is common but traffic and parking are hard; walking is best in dense areas.
    • Tri-Rail commuter trains link Miami, Fort Lauderdale and West Palm Beach.

    Where to stay

    • South Beach · ≈ USD 180–550 a night
      Walk to the beach, Ocean Drive and Lincoln Road. Best for first-timers who want sand and nightlife on foot.
    • Downtown/Brickell · ≈ USD 150–450 a night
      Free Metromover, good restaurants and bars, and quick links to the airport. Cheaper than the beach.
    • Coconut Grove · ≈ USD 160–400 a night
      Leafy, relaxed streets with cafés and marinas. Quieter base, a short drive from the beach.
    • Coral Gables · ≈ USD 170–480 a night
      Pretty Mediterranean-style streets, good dining and the Biltmore. Calm and walkable in the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Try Cuban food: a cubano sandwich, ropa vieja and strong cafecito. Little Havana on SW 8th Street (Calle Ocho) is the classic stop.
    • Stone crab claws are a Miami specialty, in season roughly October to May. Prices are high; check the day's market price.
    • Eat at a ventanita (walk-up window) for cheap coffee and snacks, often under $10.
    • Seafood and Latin dishes along the Miami River and in Brickell; book ahead on weekends.
    • Food halls and markets are good for groups with mixed tastes. Check opening hours on the day.

    Tips

    1. Spanish is spoken just as much, if not more, than English in many parts of the city
    2. Sunscreen and mosquito repellent are absolute necessities year-round
    3. Explore Little Havana for authentic Cuban coffee (cafecito), cigars, and domino games
    4. Hurricane season runs from June to November; keep an eye on weather forecasts
    5. South Beach is famous for its Art Deco architecture and nightlife, but food and drinks there are heavily overpriced
