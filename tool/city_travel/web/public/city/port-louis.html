Getting around

Metro Express for suburbs and taxis for short city hops; buses are cheap but slow.

  • ✓Metro ★Metro Express light rail links suburbs to the city, daily 6 AM to 10 PM, roughly every 12–15 minutes.
  • ✓BusTwo bus stations: Immigration Square for north and east routes, Victoria Square for south and west. Intercity tickets about MUR 15–30; express and AC buses cost more. City buses run until about 8 PM.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis within the city cost about MUR 10–50, a bit more at night. Ride apps are limited; arrange taxis by phone or at ranks. Avoid rush hours.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is chaotic, so park at the marina or hotel. Only worth it for trips outside the city.
  • ✓WalkingGood for the waterfront and city centre in the drier, cooler months. Use taxis or the metro for longer hops.

Climate

Tropical city: hot and humid most of the year, with a wet summer and a drier, cooler winter.

  • ☀️Best time: June to SeptemberHighs around 25–26°C and only 7–8 rain days. The most comfortable months for walking.
  • 🌧️Wet season: January to MarchAbout 21–24 rain days a month. Showers can be heavy; keep indoor plans ready.
  • 🥵Hot and humid summerHighs near 30°C with lows around 25°C. Go out early and rest at midday.
  • 🌂Carry rain gear in summerDecember to April brings frequent showers. A light rain jacket helps.
  • 🌬️Cooler evenings in winterJune to September lows drop to about 20°C. A light layer is useful at night.
  • 🧳What to packLight breathable clothes, sunscreen and a hat; a rain jacket for summer; a light layer for winter evenings.

Landmarks 13

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Aapravasi Ghat

    This UNESCO World Heritage site is the landing point where indentured labourers arrived in Mauritius, making it the island's most important historic landmark and a must-see for understanding its people. Visit in the cooler morning hours and wear comfortable shoes, as you'll walk over stone steps and ruins.

  2. 2

    Le Caudan Waterfront

    The lively harbour-side complex is Port Louis's social heart, with shops, restaurants and views over the water. Come in the late afternoon for cooler air and sunset views, and it's an easy walk from the city centre.

  3. 3

    Port Louis Central Market

    The bustling central market is the best place to feel the city's everyday life, with local produce, spices and crafts. Go early in the morning for the freshest atmosphere and fewer crowds, and bring small cash for purchases.

  4. 4

    Citadel Fort Adelaide

    This hilltop fort offers sweeping views over Port Louis and the harbour, making it a rewarding stop for history and photography. Visit in the morning or late afternoon to avoid the midday heat, and wear shoes suitable for walking uphill.

  5. 5

    Blue Penny Museum

    Home to rare stamps and Mauritian art, this museum is a compact cultural highlight in the Caudan area. Check opening hours before you go, as it closes on some days, and allow about an hour for the visit.

  6. 6

    Champ de Mars Racecourse

    One of the oldest racecourses in the southern hemisphere, it's a distinctive Port Louis experience with a colonial-era setting. Race days are the best time to visit; check the schedule in advance and dress smart-casual.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for a trip to Pamplemousses Botanical Garden, north of the city; check bus and taxi options on the day.
    • History fans can add Aapravasi Ghat and the Blue Penny Museum in one morning, then Fort Adelaide for the view.
    • Beach lovers can take a day trip to the north coast; arrange transport in advance as ride apps are limited.

    Getting around

    • Metro Express light rail links suburbs to the city, daily 6 AM to 10 PM, roughly every 12-15 minutes.
    • Two bus stations: Immigration Square for north and east routes, Victoria Square for south and west routes.
    • Buses run frequently with few schedules; first around 6 AM, intercity last around 6 PM, city buses until 8 PM.
    • Intercity bus tickets cost about MUR 15-30; express and AC buses cost more.
    • Taxis within the city cost about MUR 10-50, a bit more at night; avoid rush hours.
    • Ride apps are limited; arrange taxis by phone or at ranks. Driving is chaotic, so park at the marina or hotel.

    Where to stay

    • Waterfront · ≈ MUR 3,500–9,000 a night
      Walk to the market, Aapravasi Ghat and the Blue Penny Museum; safe to stroll in the evening.
    • City Centre · ≈ MUR 2,000–5,000 a night
      Closest to the bus stations and the market; good for early starts and cheap eats.
    • Champ de Mars · ≈ MUR 2,200–5,500 a night
      Quiet streets near the racecourse and Fort Adelaide; short taxi to the centre.
    • Caudan · ≈ MUR 3,000–7,500 a night
      Modern seafront quarter with restaurants and the light rail nearby.

    Food & drink

    • Try Mauritian street food: dholl puri, gateaux piments and alouda, usually under MUR 100.
    • The central market in Port Louis is good for fresh fruit, spices and cheap local snacks; go in the morning.
    • Creole and Indo-Mauritian dishes to look for: rougaille, fish vindaye and octopus curry.
    • Seafood is best near the waterfront; ask the price per weight before ordering.
    • Chinese-Mauritian noodles and dumplings are common and cheap in the city centre; check opening hours on the day.

    Tips

    1. Visit the Central Market (Bazaar) for fresh produce, spices, and local street food like Dholl Puri
    2. English is the official language, but French and Mauritian Creole are more widely spoken in daily life
    3. Drive on the left side of the road if renting a car
    4. Traffic getting in and out of the city during rush hours is notoriously heavy
    5. Explore the Caudan Waterfront for shopping, dining, and secure pedestrian walking
