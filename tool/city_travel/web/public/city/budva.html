Getting around

Walk within the centre and take a metered taxi or book through MonteGO for longer trips.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Don't plan around rail.
  • ✓BusLocal buses are limited. Intercity buses leave from the bus station, about a 20-minute walk from the Old Town. Herceg Novi buses run about every 30 minutes for around €6.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis cost around €5 for a ride within town. Use a metered taxi with a visible rate card, or book through MonteGO for upfront fare estimates.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is hard in summer. Traffic is heavy and Old Town parking is scarce and costly. Leave the car outside the centre.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is about 2 km², and the promenade links the coast. Walking is the easiest way to get around.
  • ✓Tourist boatTourist boats run to nearby beaches and Sveti Nikola island. They are not cheap, and there are no €1 water taxis.

Climate

Mediterranean climate: hot, dry summers and mild, wetter winters. The sea is warmest from July to September.

  • ☀️Best time: June to SeptemberWarm days of 21–31°C and very few rainy days in July and August. Best for beaches and nightlife.
  • 🥵Peak heat (July and August)Highs of 30–31°C. Swim early or late, and use shade in the Old Town.
  • 🌧️Rain mostly in winterBetween 11 and 14 rain days a month from October to May. Pack a light waterproof jacket.
  • 🌊Sea temperatureWarmest from late July to September. The water is cold for most of the year.
  • 🥶Cool wintersLows of 5–8°C in January and February. Many beach venues and some restaurants close.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes for summer, a warm layer for evenings, comfortable walking shoes for stone streets, and a swimsuit.

Landmarks 10

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Budva Old Town

    Budva's walled old town sits on the Adriatic and is the city's historic heart, with narrow stone streets, churches and sea walls to walk along. Go in the early morning or late afternoon to avoid the midday crowds and heat in summer.

  2. 2

    Sveti Stefan

    This former fishing village on a small islet joined to the mainland by a causeway is one of Montenegro's best-known views. It is mostly seen from outside, as access to the islet is restricted, so plan your visit around the viewpoints on the road.

  3. 3

    Sveti Stefan Beach

    This beach lies in front of the Sveti Stefan islet and is popular for swimming and sunbathing with a view of the village. Arrive early in peak summer for a good spot, and bring water shoes for the pebbly shoreline.

  4. 4

    Kotor Old Town

    Kotor's medieval walled town sits at the head of the Bay of Kotor and is a UNESCO-listed highlight within easy reach of Budva. Wear comfortable shoes, since the route up the city walls is steep, and visit mid-morning or after 4 pm when cruise groups are fewer.

  5. 5

    Perast

    Perast is a small town of Venetian-era palaces on the Bay of Kotor, famous for its waterfront and the islets offshore. It is best reached by bus or tour, and an unhurried lunch on the promenade makes a good half-day stop.

  6. 6

    Bay of Kotor Cruise

    A boat trip across the Bay of Kotor shows the mountains, towns and islands from the water, which is hard to see any other way. Book a morning departure for calmer water and clearer views, and check whether the cruise includes swimming stops.

  7. 7

    Skadar Lake National Park

    This large lake on the Montenegro-Albania border is a protected wetland known for birdlife, water lilies and small islands. Boat tours leave from Virpazar and are the best way to explore, so book ahead in summer.

  8. 8

    Mausoleum of Njegoš on Lovćen

    The mausoleum of the poet-prince Njegoš stands on Mount Lovćen above the Bay of Kotor, with wide views over the coast. The road up has many hairpin bends, so a guided tour or taxi is easier than driving yourself in poor weather.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Take a boat trip to Sveti Nikola island or a nearby beach. Tourist boats are not cheap, so compare prices at the marina first.
    • Spend a day in Herceg Novi. Intercity buses run about every 30 minutes for around €6 each way.
    • Visit Kotor or Perast for a historic day trip. Check bus or taxi times before you go, as schedules change by season.

    Getting around

    • Budva has no metro or tram. Local buses are limited, so most visitors walk or take taxis.
    • Intercity buses leave from the bus station, about a 20-minute walk from the old town. Herceg Novi buses run about every 30 minutes for around €6.
    • Taxis cost around €5 for a ride within town. Use a metered taxi with a visible rate card, or book through MonteGO, which gives upfront fare estimates.
    • The currency is the euro (€), not USD. Prices in this guide are in euros as listed on Wikivoyage.
    • Driving is hard in summer: traffic is heavy and old-town parking is scarce and costly. Leave the car outside the centre.
    • Walking is easiest. The centre is about 2 km², and the promenade links the coast; the seaside line is quieter. Tourist boats run to nearby beaches and Sveti Nikola island, but they are not cheap, and there are no €1 water taxis.

    Where to stay

    • Old Town · ≈ USD 120–400 a night
      Walled-town streets, the harbour and most restaurants are a short walk away. Quiet at night, but it has steep, narrow lanes and no parking.
    • Slovenska Plaža · ≈ USD 90–300 a night
      The main town beach, close to the centre and the promenade. Good for easy walks into town and for beach days.
    • Bečići · ≈ USD 110–350 a night
      A long sandy beach with many hotels and beach clubs. Best for beach-focused stays; it is a short taxi ride to the Old Town.
    • Rafailovići · ≈ USD 100–320 a night
      A quieter, small-bay area with rocky swimming spots and sea views. Good for a calm stay; expect taxi rides for nightlife.

    Food & drink

    • Try fresh grilled fish and seafood platters at harbour restaurants in the Old Town and along the marina.
    • Order local black risotto (crni rižoto) or grilled squid where the menu lists it, and ask for the catch of the day.
    • Eat at the Old Town cafés in the morning and the seafront restaurants at sunset; prices are higher right by the walls.
    • Look for local bakeries and burek or pljeskavica stands for a cheap lunch away from the main promenade.
    • Check menus for euro prices and whether a service charge is included before you order.
