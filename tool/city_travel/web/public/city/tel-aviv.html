Getting around

Walk and bike the center and promenade; use the light rail and buses for longer trips.

  • ✓Light rail ★Since 2023 it links Jaffa, Neve Tzedek, Rothschild Blvd, Sarona and Tel Aviv Center. Handy for the main sights.
  • ✓BusFrequent and cheap. Use a Rav-Kav smart card; no cash on buses.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsMetered taxis; Gett and Uber work in Tel Aviv. Good for late nights and the airport.
  • ✓Rental carPossible, but traffic is heavy and parking is hard to find. Skip it for city days.
  • ✓Walking ★Great along the beach promenade and in the center. The best way to see the city.
  • ✓BikeBiking is great along the promenade and in the center. Rent by the hour or day.
  • ✗FerryNo ferries to Tel Aviv. Private boats can dock at the marina.

Climate

Mediterranean climate: hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters. The beach season runs roughly May to October.

  • ☀️Best time: April to June and OctoberWarm days around 23–29°C with little rain. Ideal for the beach and walking the city.
  • 🥵Hot, humid summer (July to September)Highs of 31–32°C and warm nights. Swim early, rest in the shade at midday.
  • 🌧️Wet winter (December to February)Around 10–12 rain days a month and highs of 17–20°C. Pack a jacket and umbrella.
  • 🌂Rain is seasonalAlmost none from June to September. Most rain falls between November and March.
  • 🌊Sea and beachThe sea is warmest in late summer. Check flags and lifeguard hours on the day.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, hat and sunscreen for summer; a light jacket and rain layer for winter.

Landmarks 11

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Old Jaffa

    The ancient port quarter is Tel Aviv's most famous sight, with stone alleys, a flea market and sea views that draw every first-time visitor. Go early in the morning to beat the crowds and heat, and wear comfortable shoes for the cobblestones.

  2. 2

    Tel Aviv Beach

    The long Mediterranean beachfront is the heart of the city, with calm swimming and lively promenades. Late afternoon is best for swimming and sunset; the beaches are free and easy to reach on foot or by bus.

  3. 3

    Neve Tzedek

    One of the city's oldest neighborhoods, now a charming maze of restored houses, galleries and quiet lanes. Wander on a weekday morning when it is calm, and dress casually for walking.

  4. 4

    Carmel Market

    The bustling open-air market is the best place to taste local food and feel the city's everyday energy. Come before noon on Friday for the fullest stalls, and bring cash for snacks.

  5. 5

    Bauhaus/White City architecture

    Tel Aviv holds one of the world's great collections of Bauhaus and International Style buildings, a UNESCO-listed draw. A self-guided walk is free; start in the morning to avoid the midday sun.

  6. 6

    Rothschild Boulevard

    This leafy boulevard mixes historic buildings, cafes and the story of the city's founding. It is free and pleasant to stroll in the late afternoon or evening.

  7. 7

    HaYarkon Park

    The city's largest green space offers lawns, paths and river-side walks for a break from the streets. Early morning or late afternoon is coolest; entry is free.

  8. 8

    Tel Aviv Museum of Art

    A major museum with a strong modern and Israeli collection, worth a half day for art lovers. Check opening hours and ticket prices ahead, as some days are shorter.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a beach day for Old Jaffa: the port, the flea market and the alleys of the old town.
    • Add a day in Jerusalem. Buses and trains run often; check times on the day.
    • Take a half-day to Caesarea or the Carmel coast if you have a car or join a tour.

    Getting around

    • Light rail (since 2023) links Jaffa, Neve Tzedek, Rothschild Blvd, Sarona, and Tel Aviv Center.
    • Buses are frequent and cheap; use a Rav-Kav smart card (no cash on buses).
    • Taxis are metered; ride apps like Gett and Uber work in Tel Aviv.
    • Driving is possible but traffic is heavy and parking is hard to find.
    • Walking and biking are great along the beach promenade and in the center.
    • No ferries to Tel Aviv; private boats can dock at the marina.

    Where to stay

    • Tel Aviv Center · ≈ ILS 450–900 a night
      Walk to the beach, Rothschild Blvd and the light rail. Best base for first-timers.
    • Neve Tzedek · ≈ ILS 600–1,200 a night
      Quiet, pretty old streets near the beach and Jaffa. Good for couples.
    • Jaffa · ≈ ILS 400–850 a night
      Old port, flea market and history. Cheaper than the center; light rail links it.
    • Port area · ≈ ILS 550–1,100 a night
      Waterfront promenade, restaurants and parks. Calm and walkable.

    Food & drink

    • Try hummus, sabich (fried aubergine pita) and shakshuka. Cheap lunch spots charge about ILS 30–60.
    • Carmel Market: graze on fruit, cheese and street food. Busiest on Fridays; closed on Shabbat.
    • Falafel and shawarma are the fast, cheap option, about ILS 20–40 a portion.
    • Many restaurants are kosher and close from Friday afternoon to Saturday evening. Check opening hours.
    • Book dinner ahead for Thursday and Friday nights. Tipping is around 10–15%.

    Tips

    1. Public transport shuts down completely from Friday afternoon to Saturday evening for Shabbat
    2. It is consistently ranked as one of the most expensive cities in the world
    3. The culinary and vegan scenes are world-class; hummus and falafel are must-tries
    4. Explore the ancient, winding stone streets and flea markets of Jaffa just to the south
    5. Security checks at airports and malls are thorough and standard procedure
