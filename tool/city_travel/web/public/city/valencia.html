Getting around

Walk the flat centre; use metro or bus for the beach and the City of Arts and Sciences.

  • ✓Metro ★Metro València has 9 lines, some trams. Single zone fare about €1.50 plus €1 for the card.
  • ✓BusEMT buses reach most districts. Single ticket about €2, pay the driver by cash or card.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered; airport trips add a supplement. Uber, Bolt and Cabify operate.
  • ✓Rental carNot needed in the city. Useful only for trips out to the Albufera or nearby towns.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is flat and walkable. Bikes and the Turia park paths are popular.
  • ✓TrainRegional trains leave from Estació del Nord. No ferry service for visitors.

Climate

Mediterranean climate: mild winters, hot dry summers and a short rainy spell in spring and autumn.

  • ☀️Best time: May to June and September to OctoberWarm days around 24–28°C with few rain days. Good for both sightseeing and the beach.
  • 🥵July and August are hotHighs of 31°C and nights near 22°C. Sightsee early, rest at midday, swim in the evening.
  • 🌧️Rain peaks in March and SeptemberAbout 8 rain days each. Showers are often short; carry a light rain jacket.
  • 🌤️Mild wintersJanuary and December highs of 16–17°C with cool nights near 7–8°C. A coat is enough.
  • 🏖️Beach seasonSea and sand are best from June to September. May and October are fine for a walk, cooler for swimming.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes and sunscreen for summer, a warm layer for winter, and a small umbrella in spring and autumn.

Landmarks 13

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    City of Arts and Sciences

    Valencia's futuristic complex of white curves and glass is the city's modern icon and a must-see for any first-time visitor. Book tickets online for the aquarium and science museum to skip queues, and go early morning or at dusk for cooler weather and better photos.

  2. 2

    Valencia Cathedral

    This cathedral holds the claimed Holy Grail and mixes Romanesque, Gothic and Baroque styles in one building. Climb the tower for city views, and dress modestly since it is an active place of worship.

  3. 3

    Central Market of Valencia

    One of Europe's largest and most beautiful covered markets, a feast of fresh produce and local snacks under a stained-glass dome. Arrive before noon for the fullest stalls, and note it closes on Sundays.

  4. 4

    Turia Gardens (Jardín del Turia)

    A green ribbon running through the city in a former riverbed, perfect for walking or cycling between the old town and the City of Arts and Sciences. Go in the early morning or late afternoon to avoid the midday heat.

  5. 5

    La Lonja de la Seda

    A UNESCO-listed Gothic silk exchange with a stunning spiral-columned hall, a short walk from the Central Market. Visit on a weekday morning for quieter rooms and combine it with the market next door.

  6. 6

    Torres de Serranos

    These medieval gate towers once guarded the city and now offer a short climb with fine views over the old town. Go late afternoon for softer light and fewer crowds.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a beach afternoon for the Albufera lagoon south of the city: rice fields, boat trips and paella country. Check transport and tour options on the day.
    • Take a regional train from Estació del Nord to a nearby town for a half day. Buy tickets at the station.
    • Add a day for the City of Arts and Sciences: the science museum and oceanarium need several hours each.

    Getting around

    • Metro València has 9 lines (some trams); single zone fare about €1.50 plus €1 for the card.
    • EMT buses reach most districts; single ticket about €2, pay the driver by cash or card.
    • SUMA 10 card gives 10 journeys on metro and bus, about €8 plus card fee.
    • Taxis are metered; airport trips add a supplement. Ride apps like Uber, Bolt and Cabify operate.
    • The centre is flat and walkable; bikes and the Turia park paths are popular.
    • No ferry service for visitors; regional trains leave from Estació del Nord.

    Where to stay

    • Ciutat Vella · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      Walk to the cathedral, Central Market and Plaza de la Virgen. Busiest and noisiest at night.
    • El Carmen · ≈ EUR 70–170 a night
      Old quarter with street art and small bars. Lively and central, but can be loud late.
    • Ruzafa · ≈ EUR 85–200 a night
      Best food and café scene; flat 15–20 minute walk to the old town.
    • Eixample · ≈ EUR 80–190 a night
      Quieter, wide streets, good value; walkable to the centre and near the Turia park.

    Food & drink

    • Try paella at lunch, not dinner. Valencians eat it midday; book a table at a proper restaurant.
    • Central Market (Mercat Central) is the place for jamón, olives and fresh produce. Go in the morning; check opening hours on the day.
    • Tapas and raciones in Ruzafa and El Carmen. Expect small plates to share, roughly €3–8 each.
    • Horchata with fartons is the local sweet drink. Try it at a horchatería in the afternoon.
    • The city water is fine to drink. Many bars charge for bread or a terrace seat; ask first.

    Tips

    1. Valencia is the birthplace of Paella; order it at a traditional beachside restaurant
    2. Spend hours walking or cycling through the massive Turia Garden, which runs through the center of the city
    3. Visit the City of Arts and Sciences for stunning, futuristic architecture
    4. The city is much more relaxed and affordable than Barcelona or Madrid
    5. The beach is wide and easily accessible by tram or bike
