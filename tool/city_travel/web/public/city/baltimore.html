Getting around

Walk the harbor and Fells Point; use the free Circulator bus and light rail for the rest.

  • ✓MetroThe subway and light rail cost $2 per ride; a day pass is $4.60. The light rail runs from BWI Airport to the Inner Harbor in about 30 minutes for $2.
  • ✓Bus ★MTA buses cost $2 per ride, $4.60 for a day pass. The Charm City Circulator is free and links the harbor, Fells Point and Mount Vernon.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsUber and Lyft both operate widely. Taxis are metered but less common.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is optional. Parking downtown runs about $5 per hour near the harbor.
  • ✓Walking ★The Inner Harbor, Fells Point and Federal Hill are close together and easy on foot in mild weather.
  • ✓TrainMARC and Amtrak trains connect Penn Station to Washington, D.C. in about an hour.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cold winters, warm humid summers, and mild spring and fall. Rain falls year-round.

  • 🌸Best time: April, May, September and OctoberHighs of 18–26°C and comfortable evenings. Ideal for the harbor and Fort McHenry.
  • 🥵Summer is hot and humidJuly and August reach about 30°C with muggy air. Do outdoor sights early and stay in the shade.
  • 🧥Winter is coldJanuary highs near 6°C and lows below freezing. Bring a warm coat and gloves.
  • 🌧️Rain any monthExpect rain on 8–15 days each month. Summer is wettest; a small umbrella helps.
  • ☀️Long summer daysJune to August gives the most daylight for walking the waterfront in the evening.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes for cobbled streets. Add sunscreen and a hat for summer.

Landmarks 8

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    National Aquarium

    Baltimore's most famous attraction, with sharks, dolphins and a rainforest under one roof on the Inner Harbor. Book timed tickets online in advance, especially on weekends and school holidays, and arrive early to beat the crowds.

  2. 2

    Inner Harbor

    The city's waterfront heart, packed with shops, restaurants and views of the harbor. It is free to stroll; go in the morning or at sunset for the best light and fewer crowds, and it is an easy walk from downtown hotels.

  3. 3

    Fort McHenry

    The star-shaped fort that inspired 'The Star-Spangled Banner' during the War of 1812. Allow a couple of hours for the visitor center and ramparts; it is a short drive or water taxi ride from the Inner Harbor.

  4. 4

    American Visionary Art Museum

    A one-of-a-kind museum devoted to self-taught artists, with wildly creative exhibits and a giant whirligig outside. Check opening days before you go, as it closes early in the week, and leave time for the gift shop.

  5. 5

    George Peabody Library

    A breathtaking 19th-century library with cast-iron balconies and a soaring atrium, often called one of the most beautiful in the world. It is free to visit during open hours; go on a weekday morning for quiet photos.

  6. 6

    Maryland Science Center

    A hands-on science museum with an IMAX theater and a planetarium, great for families. Buy tickets online to skip lines and check showtimes for the planetarium before you arrive.

  7. 7

    Johns Hopkins University

    One of America's leading universities, with a handsome Homewood campus worth a walk. It is a short ride north of downtown; stroll the quads on a weekday when the campus is lively.

  8. 8

    Oriole Park at Camden Yards

    A beloved ballpark that helped spark the retro-stadium trend, right by downtown. Catch a game if the Orioles are in town, and buy tickets early for popular summer matchups.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for Fort McHenry and the Star-Spangled Banner story; check the ferry or bus times on the day.
    • Train to Washington, D.C. from Penn Station takes about an hour on MARC or Amtrak. Easy day trip.
    • Art lovers can swap a harbor afternoon for the Baltimore Museum of Art and the Walters Art Museum.

    Getting around

    • Light rail runs from BWI Airport to the Inner Harbor in about 30 minutes for $2.
    • MTA buses, light rail and subway cost $2 per ride; a day pass is $4.60.
    • The Charm City Circulator bus is free and links the harbor, Fells Point and Mount Vernon.
    • MARC and Amtrak trains connect Penn Station to Washington, D.C. in about an hour.
    • Uber and Lyft both operate widely; taxis are metered but less common.
    • Driving is optional; parking downtown runs about $5 per hour near the harbor.

    Where to stay

    • Inner Harbor · ≈ USD 150–320 a night
      Walk to the National Aquarium, the historic ships and Fort McHenry ferries. Easiest base for first-timers.
    • Fells Point · ≈ USD 130–280 a night
      Cobbled waterfront streets full of pubs and seafood. Quiet at night, close to the harbor.
    • Mount Vernon · ≈ USD 120–260 a night
      Museums, the Washington Monument and grand old streets. Good value and central.
    • Federal Hill · ≈ USD 130–270 a night
      Local restaurants and a hilltop view over the harbor. Short walk to the Inner Harbor.

    Food & drink

    • Order a Maryland crab cake: mostly lump crab, little filler. Prices vary by season, so check the menu.
    • Try steamed blue crabs in summer. They are messy and sold by the pound; ask for a table outside.
    • Fells Point has old waterfront taverns and seafood spots. Book ahead on weekend evenings.
    • Cheap eats: pit beef sandwiches and crab soup at local delis and markets, often under $15.
    • Lexington Market is a good lunch stop for local stalls. Check opening days before you go.

    Tips

    1. Try local blue crab, especially the famous Maryland crab cakes
    2. Walk the Inner Harbor and visit the National Aquarium
    3. The city is quite divided, so be aware of neighborhood boundaries
    4. Commuting to Washington D.C. by MARC train is cheap and efficient
    5. Visit the grave of Edgar Allan Poe, a historic city resident
