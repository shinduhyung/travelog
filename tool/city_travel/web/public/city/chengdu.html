Getting around

Metro for most trips; DiDi or taxis for evenings and airport runs.

  • ✓Metro ★Many lines with English ticket machines. WeChat/Alipay and most international cards accepted. Line 10 links Shuangliu Airport to the centre in about 30–40 min. Line 18 links Tianfu Airport to South Railway Station in about 50 min; ticket to Tianfu Square around ¥11.
  • ✓Bus¥2 air-conditioned, ¥1 older buses. Exact change or a transit card. Signs in Chinese only, so use a map app.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Metered taxis are cheap; add about ¥10 for the airport expressway toll when leaving town. DiDi works well.
  • ✗Rental carNot needed in the city; traffic and parking are a hassle. Use trains for day trips.
  • ✓WalkingThe flat centre is good on foot, and shared bikes are handy for short hops.

Climate

Humid subtropical: mild winters, warm summers and rain in every month, heaviest in July and August.

  • 🌸Best time: March to May and October to NovemberHighs of 19–27°C and manageable rain. Ideal for walking, pandas and day trips.
  • ☔Rain all yearEven the driest months see about 5 rain days. July and August are wettest with about 20. Always carry a small umbrella.
  • 🥵Hot, humid summer (June to August)Highs near 30–31°C with muggy air. Do outdoor sights early morning; rest indoors in the afternoon.
  • 🧥Cool, damp winter (December to February)Highs of 11–13°C and lows of 4–5°C. Homes and some rooms are lightly heated, so bring warm layers.
  • 🌫️Cloudy skies are commonChengdu is often overcast and hazy. Don't count on blue skies for photos.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a rain jacket or umbrella, comfortable walking shoes, and mosquito repellent for summer evenings.

Landmarks 10

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding

    The city's defining experience and the reason many people come to Chengdu: you can watch giant pandas up close, including playful cubs. Go early in the morning when the pandas are most active and feeding, and book tickets in advance, especially on weekends and holidays.

  2. 2

    Jinli Ancient Street

    A lively restored old street with traditional architecture, snacks and souvenir stalls, and it is the most famous stroll in the city. Visit in the late afternoon or evening when the lanterns come on, and expect crowds.

  3. 3

    Wuhou Shrine

    A historic shrine complex dedicated to Zhuge Liang and the Three Kingdoms era, set in peaceful gardens next to Jinli. It pairs naturally with a Jinli visit; go on a weekday morning for quieter paths.

  4. 4

    Du Fu Thatched Cottage

    The former residence of the great Tang poet Du Fu, now a serene museum and garden. It is a calm, green escape from the city center; allow an hour or two and go in the morning.

  5. 5

    Wenshu Monastery

    A working Buddhist monastery in the old city with incense-filled halls, tea houses and a vegetarian restaurant. It is easy to reach by metro and makes a relaxed half-day with nearby streets.

  6. 6

    People's Park

    The classic Chengdu park where locals drink tea, play mahjong and watch the world go by. Come in the afternoon, order a cup of tea at a teahouse and simply people-watch.

  7. 7

    Tianfu Square

    The central square and a handy orientation point in the modern city, with the Mao statue and metro interchange. It is a quick stop rather than a long visit; combine it with nearby shopping streets.

  8. 8

    Qingcheng Mountain

    A Taoist mountain outside the city with temples, forest trails and views, a popular day trip from Chengdu. Wear comfortable shoes and start early, as the full climb takes most of a day.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for the Leshan Giant Buddha by high-speed train, about 1 hour each way; check times on the day.
    • Spend a morning at the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding; go early, pandas are active before the heat.

    Getting around

    • Metro: many lines, English ticket machines, WeChat/Alipay and most international cards accepted.
    • Metro line 10 links Shuangliu Airport to the centre in about 30-40 min.
    • Metro line 18 links Tianfu Airport to South Railway Station in about 50 min; ticket to Tianfu Square around ¥11.
    • Buses cost ¥2 air-conditioned, ¥1 older; exact change or transit card; signs in Chinese only.
    • Taxis are metered and cheap; add about ¥10 for the airport expressway toll when leaving town.
    • Ride apps: DiDi works well; walking and shared bikes are good in the flat centre.

    Where to stay

    • Tianfu Square · ≈ CNY 250–700 a night
      Dead centre, on two metro lines; easy walk to museums and the river.
    • Chunxi Road · ≈ CNY 300–900 a night
      Shopping and food street on the doorstep; good metro links and many hotels.
    • Kuanzhai Alley · ≈ CNY 280–800 a night
      Old lanes, teahouses and snacks; quieter evenings, central but relaxed.
    • Wuhou Temple · ≈ CNY 200–600 a night
      Near the temple, Jinli Street and the panda-base bus routes; cheaper rooms.

    Food & drink

    • Hotpot is the must-do: a divided pot of mild and numbing-spicy broth, dipped in oil and garlic. Expect ¥80–150 per person with drinks.
    • Try mapo tofu, dan dan noodles and kung pao chicken at small local restaurants; a filling meal is often ¥20–50.
    • Snack streets: Jinli and Kuanzhai Alley for tourists, or local lanes for cheaper bites. Prices are higher at the famous streets.
    • Teahouses are part of the day: order jasmine tea, sit for an hour, and watch the city go by. Around ¥20–60 a pot.
    • Street stalls are fine but busy ones turn over food faster. Drink bottled or boiled water if your stomach is sensitive.

    Tips

    1. Sichuan hotpot is a staple, but be prepared for the intense numbing heat of the Sichuan peppercorn (málà)
    2. The local lifestyle is famously slow and relaxed compared to the hustle of Beijing or Shanghai
    3. Spend an afternoon drinking jasmine tea and playing Mahjong in People's Park
    4. Book tickets to the Giant Panda Breeding Base in advance and arrive early to see them active
    5. WeChat Pay and Alipay are absolutely mandatory for payments; cash is rarely accepted

      Souvenirs

      Pixian Doubanjiang (Chili Bean Paste)

      CNY 15–40250–500g · estimate

      This fermented chili bean paste comes from Pixian County, just outside Chengdu. It is the base of many Sichuan dishes, especially mapo tofu.

      Where: Large supermarkets or official stores that carry the Juancheng brand
      Check the sealed packaging and the aging period printed on the label before you buy.

      Shu Brocade Silk Pouch

      CNY 80–2501 small piece · estimate

      Shu brocade is a traditional silk weaving craft that has been practiced in Chengdu for centuries. Small pouches show the city's old textile skills in a portable form.

      Where: Gift shop at the Chengdu Shu Brocade Museum
      Look at the fabric and how the pattern is made, and ask whether the piece is hand-woven or machine-made.

      Sichuan Huajiao (Sichuan Peppercorn)

      CNY 20–6050–100g · estimate

      Sichuan peppercorns give the numbing sensation found in many local dishes. Chengdu cooking relies on them heavily.

      Where: Hongqi Chain or the spice section of large supermarkets
      Smell both red and green varieties before choosing, since their flavors differ.
