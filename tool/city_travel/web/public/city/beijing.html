Getting around

Metro for most trips; DiDi or Alipay taxis for late nights and places off the metro.

  • ✓Metro ★Extensive, cheap (¥3–10) and reaches most sights. Buy a Yikatong card or use Alipay/WeChat.
  • ✓BusFrequent but hard to navigate without Chinese; fares around ¥2.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Metered taxis, but drivers rarely speak English; show place names in Chinese characters. DiDi works and can be paid via Alipay or WeChat; Alipay also books taxis in English.
  • ✓Rental carNeeds a Chinese licence and a Beijing entry permit (进京证); most visitors skip driving.
  • ✓WalkingGood in hutongs and parks. Use Baidu or Amap maps, not Google.
  • ✓Shared bikeDockless shared bikes are great for last-mile trips; use Baidu or Amap maps.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cold, dry winters and hot, rainy summers. Spring and autumn are the best times to visit.

  • 🌸Best time: April, May, September, OctoberHighs around 19–28°C with low rain. Perfect for the Great Wall and long walks.
  • 🥶Winter is cold and dryDecember to February highs near 3–6°C and lows below freezing. Very little rain; pack a heavy coat.
  • 🥵Summer is hot and wetJune to August highs near 30–33°C, with the most rain in July. Plan indoor sights at midday.
  • 🌂Rain peaks in July and AugustAbout 12–14 rain days a month then. Other months are mostly dry.
  • 🌬️Spring can be windy and dustyMarch and April bring wind; air quality varies. Check the day's forecast before outdoor plans.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for spring and autumn, a heavy coat for winter, light breathable clothes and an umbrella for summer. Comfortable shoes for long walks.

Landmarks 33

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Forbidden City

    The world's largest palace complex and the heart of imperial China for five centuries, with 9,000 rooms of halls, courtyards and treasures. Book tickets online in advance (passport required) and go early on a weekday to avoid the biggest crowds.

  2. 2

    Great Wall of China

    The iconic ancient fortification winding over mountains north of Beijing, a bucket-list sight and UNESCO site. The Mutianyu section is the most visitor-friendly; take the cable car up and walk the restored ramparts, ideally in the morning.

  3. 3

    Summer Palace

    A vast imperial garden of lakes, pavilions and long corridors that was the Qing court's summer retreat. Allow half a day, arrive at opening, and wear comfortable shoes for the lakeside walks.

  4. 4

    Temple of Heaven

    A masterpiece of Ming architecture where emperors prayed for good harvests, set in a large park used by locals for tai chi. Visit early morning to see the park life, then enter the main halls with a combo ticket.

  5. 5

    Tiananmen Square

    One of the world's largest public squares, flanked by the Gate of Heavenly Peace and major museums. Security checks are strict, so carry your passport and avoid bringing large bags.

  6. 6

    Bird's Nest

    The striking National Stadium from the 2008 Olympics, a modern icon best seen lit up at night. Combine it with a stroll in the Olympic Park; the exterior is free to view.

  7. 7

    Ming Tombs

    The burial complex of thirteen Ming emperors in a mountain valley, with the grand Sacred Way and the excavated Dingling tomb. It is far from the city, so pair it with the Great Wall on a day trip.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a full day for the Great Wall: Mutianyu is popular and less crowded than Badaling; go early and check transport on the day.
    • Spend a morning at the Temple of Heaven and a park to watch locals dance and play cards.
    • Art and design fans can swap a shopping afternoon for the 798 art district.

    Getting around

    • Metro is extensive, cheap (¥3-10) and reaches most sights; buy a Yikatong card or use Alipay/WeChat.
    • Buses are frequent but hard to navigate without Chinese; fares around ¥2.
    • Taxis are metered but drivers rarely speak English; show place names in Chinese characters.
    • DiDi works and can be paid via Alipay or WeChat; Alipay also books taxis in English.
    • Renting a car needs a Chinese licence and a Beijing entry permit (进京证); most visitors skip driving.
    • Dockless shared bikes are great for last-mile trips; use Baidu or Amap maps, not Google.

    Where to stay

    • Dongcheng (Forbidden City) · ≈ CNY 350–1,200 a night
      Walk to Tiananmen and the Forbidden City; quiet hutongs nearby for dinner.
    • Chaoyang (Sanlitun) · ≈ CNY 400–1,500 a night
      Best for bars, restaurants and shopping; easy metro and taxi links.
    • Xicheng (Houhai) · ≈ CNY 300–1,000 a night
      Lakeside hutongs, close to the Drum Tower and Beihai Park.
    • Wangfujing · ≈ CNY 450–1,400 a night
      Central shopping street, one metro stop from the Forbidden City.

    Food & drink

    • Try Peking duck at a roast-duck restaurant; book ahead for dinner and expect roughly ¥150–350 per person.
    • Hutong snacks: jianbing (savoury crepe), baozi and noodles; small shops often cost ¥10–30 a dish.
    • Look for dumpling houses and noodle shops near your hotel; menus with pictures help if you don't read Chinese.
    • Street food is best in busy, well-trafficked spots; drink bottled water and skip lukewarm dishes.
    • Tea houses and courtyard cafés are good for a rest; check prices before ordering in tourist areas.

    Tips

    1. WeChat Pay and Alipay are absolutely essential for daily transactions
    2. Book tickets for the Forbidden City and other major attractions days or weeks in advance
    3. Visit the Mutianyu section of the Great Wall for fewer crowds and a toboggan ride down
    4. Air quality can occasionally be poor, so check AQI apps before planning outdoor activities
    5. Eat authentic Peking Duck at renowned restaurants like Quanjude or Da Dong
