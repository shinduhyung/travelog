Getting around

Metrorrey for downtown hops; Uber or DiDi for everything else.

  • ✓Metro ★Metrorrey is clean and cheap: about M$8.70 per ride. A rechargeable card costs about M$20.
  • ✓BusCity buses cost about M$15. Pay with a 'Me Muevo' card or the Urbani app; cash is being phased out. Ecovia BRT runs fast buses on Av. Ruiz Cortines, with free transfers to Metrorrey.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and DiDi both work well. Taxis are green-and-white or yellow-and-white; insist the driver uses the meter.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is possible but traffic and parking are tricky. Downtown hotels often offer free parking.
  • ✓WalkingGood around the Macroplaza and Barrio Antiguo. Use rides for longer hops, especially in summer heat.

Climate

Semi-arid climate with hot summers and mild winters. Rain is modest but peaks in May and September.

  • ☀️Best time: November to MarchMild days around 19–26°C and cool evenings. Good for walking the Macroplaza and parks.
  • 🥵Summer is hot (June to August)Highs around 33–34°C. Do outdoor sights early morning; use air-conditioned museums midday.
  • 🌧️Rain peaks in May and SeptemberAbout 11 rain days in each. Showers are often short; carry a light rain jacket.
  • 🌡️Big day–night swing in winterJanuary lows near 9°C and highs near 19°C. Layer up.
  • 💧Humidity in summerJune to September feel muggy. Drink water and take breaks.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes for summer, a warm layer for winter nights, sunscreen, and a light rain layer for May and September.

Landmarks 8

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    Macroplaza

    Monterrey's vast central plaza is the city's urban heart and the natural starting point for a first visit, linking the old town with the modern downtown. Go in the early morning or late afternoon to avoid the midday heat, and wear comfortable shoes for the long walk across it.

  2. 2

    Cerro de la Silla

    The saddle-shaped mountain is Monterrey's defining icon and frames the city from almost everywhere. The best views are on clear mornings; head to a viewpoint or the surrounding hills rather than attempting the summit without proper gear.

  3. 3

    Parque Fundidora

    A former steelworks turned sprawling park, it blends industrial heritage with green space and is a favorite for a relaxed half-day. Visit early on weekends to beat the crowds, and bring water and sun protection as shade is limited in parts.

  4. 4

    Barrio Antiguo

    The cobblestone old quarter is the most atmospheric place to wander, with colorful facades and a lively evening scene. Come in the late afternoon for photos, then stay for dinner; it is easily reached on foot from the Macroplaza.

  5. 5

    Museo de Historia Mexicana

    A well-regarded museum tracing Mexican history, located right by the Macroplaza and worth an air-conditioned break from the heat. Check opening days before you go, as many museums close on Mondays.

    Getting around

    • Metrorrey subway is clean and cheap: about M$8.70 per ride; a rechargeable card costs about M$20.
    • City buses cost about M$15; pay with a 'Me Muevo' card or the Urbani app, cash is being phased out.
    • Ecovia BRT runs fast articulated buses on Av. Ruiz Cortines; transfers to Metrorrey are free.
    • Uber and DiDi both work well in the city.
    • Taxis are green-and-white or yellow-and-white; insist the driver uses the meter.
    • Driving is possible but traffic and parking are tricky; downtown hotels often offer free parking.

    Where to stay

    • Macroplaza · ≈ MXN 900–2,200 a night
      Walk to the Macroplaza, museums and the cathedral. Central for first-time visitors and close to Metrorrey.
    • Barrio Antiguo · ≈ MXN 800–2,000 a night
      Old-town streets with bars and restaurants on the doorstep. Livelier at night, quieter in the day.
    • San Pedro Garza García · ≈ MXN 1,800–4,500 a night
      Upscale area with malls, restaurants and mountain views. Good for business trips; you'll need taxis or a car.
    • Parque Fundidora · ≈ MXN 1,100–2,600 a night
      Green park with museums and walking paths. Calm and family-friendly; a short ride from downtown.

    Food & drink

    • Try cabrito (roast kid goat) and agujas de res (beef ribs). These are the local specialties.
    • Breakfast is big here: look for machacado con huevo (dried beef with eggs) and barbacoa on weekends.
    • Carne asada is a social event. Many restaurants grill at the table; book ahead on weekends.
    • Snack on a 'torta' or street tacos; prices are usually modest, but check the menu first.
    • For a splurge, San Pedro Garza García has many well-known restaurants. Reserve in advance.

    Tips

    1. Eat 'Cabrito' (roast kid goat) and 'Machaca', staples of traditional Norteño cuisine
    2. Walk the Paseo Santa Lucía, a spectacular artificial river connecting the Macroplaza to Fundidora Park
    3. The summer heat is blistering; locals typically avoid outdoor activities during midday
    4. It is considered one of Mexico's most modern and wealthiest cities
    5. Hike up Chipinque or simply admire the iconic Saddle Mountain from the city center
