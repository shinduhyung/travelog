Getting around

Walk in the centre; use Metro Linea 1 and trams for longer hops.

  • ✓Metro ★Linea 1 runs driverless trains from Bengasi to Fermi, linking Porta Nuova and Porta Susa.
  • ✓BusBuses and trams run 06:00–00:30. Buy tickets before boarding from tobacconists or machines. A standard 'City + Suburban 100' ticket costs €2 and is valid for 100 minutes.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are available but ride apps like Uber are limited. Use FreeNow or local apps.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the centre is not recommended due to restricted streets and trams.
  • ✓Walking ★Walking is ideal in the centre. Bike sharing is available via Lime and RideMovi.

Climate

Continental climate with cold winters and warm summers. Rain is spread across the year, heaviest in spring and autumn.

  • 🌸Best time: April to June and September to OctoberMild days around 18–24°C. Good for walking and day trips.
  • ☀️Summer is hot but not extremeJuly and August reach about 30°C. Go to museums at midday and walk in the evening.
  • 🧥Winter is coldDecember to February see highs near 8–10°C and lows at or below freezing. Bring a warm coat.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearMay is wettest with about 16 rain days. Keep a small umbrella handy in spring and autumn.
  • 🌫️Po valley hazeWinter days can be grey and foggy. Plan indoor sights as backup.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes for cobblestones. Add a hat and sunscreen for summer.

Landmarks 13

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Mole Antonelliana

    Turin's unmistakable symbol, a soaring 19th-century tower that houses the National Museum of Cinema. Ride the glass lift to the top for panoramic city views; book tickets online to skip the queue, and go early or late for softer light.

  2. 2

    Egyptian Museum, Turin

    One of the world's great Egyptology collections, second only to Cairo, with mummies, statues and papyri. Allow at least two hours; buy timed tickets in advance and avoid Monday closures.

  3. 3

    Royal Palace of Turin

    The opulent former residence of the House of Savoy, a UNESCO site with lavish halls and armories. Visit in the morning when it's quieter, and note that some areas require a guided tour.

  4. 4

    La Venaria Reale

    A vast baroque palace and garden complex on the city's edge, often called Italy's Versailles. Take the train or bus from Turin, and set aside a half-day to explore the gardens and exhibitions.

  5. 5

    Basilica of Superga

    A hilltop basilica with sweeping views over Turin and the Alps, reached by a historic rack railway or bus. Dress respectfully, and check opening hours as the church closes at midday.

  6. 6

    Piazza Castello

    The elegant heart of baroque Turin, lined with palaces and arcades. It's a natural starting point for a walking tour; come in the late afternoon for a coffee under the porticoes.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for a trip to the Reggia di Venaria, a Savoy palace outside town. Check transport and opening hours on the day.
    • Take the train to Asti or Alba for wine country. Timetables and prices vary; check on the day.
    • Ride the historic tram or funicular up to Superga for city views. Check the schedule before you go.

    Getting around

    • Metro Linea 1 runs driverless trains from Bengasi to Fermi, linking Porta Nuova and Porta Susa.
    • Buses and trams run 06:00-00:30; buy tickets before boarding from tobacconists or machines.
    • A standard 'City + Suburban 100' ticket costs €2 and is valid for 100 minutes.
    • Taxis are available but ride apps like Uber are limited; use FreeNow or local apps.
    • Driving in the centre is not recommended due to restricted streets and trams.
    • Walking is ideal in the centre; bike sharing is available via Lime and RideMovi.

    Where to stay

    • Centro · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      Walk to Piazza Castello, the Egyptian Museum and the cafés. Best base for a first visit.
    • Porta Nuova · ≈ EUR 70–170 a night
      Next to the main station and Metro Linea 1. Easy arrival and good value hotels.
    • Porta Susa · ≈ EUR 65–160 a night
      High-speed train hub with metro links. Quieter streets, a short walk or ride to the centre.
    • Lingotto · ≈ EUR 60–150 a night
      The old Fiat factory with its rooftop track, plus a mall and art. Cheaper, but you ride the metro to the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Start the day with a bicerin, a hot coffee-chocolate drink, at a historic café in the centre.
    • Aperitivo is a Turin ritual: one drink, then a buffet of snacks, usually €8–12.
    • Try Piedmontese dishes: agnolotti pasta, vitello tonnato and tajarin. Book dinner ahead on weekends.
    • Buy gianduja chocolate and gianduiotti at old-school chocolate shops. Prices vary; check the counter.
    • Look for the fixed-price lunch menus (menù del giorno) at trattorias, often €12–18.

    Tips

    1. Known for its world-class chocolate and 'Bicerin' (a local coffee and chocolate drink)
    2. Explore the grand baroque plazas and wide, tree-lined boulevards
    3. The city is significantly more orderly and elegant than other Italian metropolises
    4. Take a trip to the nearby Alps for world-class mountain scenery
    5. The Egyptian Museum is one of the best of its kind in the world
