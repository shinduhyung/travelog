Getting around

Walk the centre; use buses and trams for Leith, the airport and longer hops.

  • ✓Tram ★Trams link the airport, Haymarket, Princes Street, Waverley and Leith. Buy tickets at stop machines.
  • ✓Bus ★Lothian buses cover the city. Pay with exact cash or contactless; day and multi-day tickets available.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsMetered taxis are plentiful. Uber and Bolt also operate in Edinburgh.
  • ✓Rental carNot worth it in the centre: many streets are closed and parking is scarce and pricey.
  • ✓WalkingThe centre is very walkable, though hilly with cobbles. Bikes and e-bikes are easy to hire, with some cycle lanes.
  • ✓Airport busAirlink 100 runs 24 hours to Waverley Bridge, about 20–30 minutes.

Climate

Cool and changeable: mild summers, cold winters and rain in every month. Rarely very hot or very cold.

  • 🌦️Rain any monthExpect rain on roughly 12–17 days each month. Showers pass quickly; carry a light waterproof.
  • 🌡️Mild summersJuly and August highs sit near 18°C, lows near 12°C. Rarely hot; layers help.
  • 🥶Cold wintersJanuary highs near 6°C, lows near 2°C. Wind off the sea makes it feel colder; bring a warm coat.
  • 🌬️Windy and exposedHills and the coast are breezier. A windproof layer matters more than an umbrella.
  • ☀️Long summer daysJune and July give very long daylight, so late walks are easy. Winter days are short.
  • 🧳What to packWaterproof jacket, layers, comfortable walking shoes for cobbles and hills, and a warm hat in winter.

Landmarks 15

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    Edinburgh Castle

    The city's defining sight, crowning Castle Rock with the Honours of Scotland and the Stone of Destiny. Book tickets online in advance and arrive at opening time to beat the crowds.

  2. 2

    Royal Mile

    The historic spine of the Old Town, linking the Castle to the Palace of Holyroodhouse past closes, kirks and street performers. Walk it early in the morning before the tour groups arrive.

  3. 3

    Palace of Holyroodhouse

    The monarch's official Scottish residence, full of royal history and Mary, Queen of Scots' chambers. Check opening days in advance as it closes for royal events.

  4. 4

    Calton Hill

    A short climb rewards you with the classic panorama of the city and the National Monument. Go at sunset for the best light; it is an easy walk from Princes Street.

  5. 5

    Forth Bridge

    A UNESCO-listed engineering icon spanning the Firth of Forth. View it from South Queensferry, reachable by train from Edinburgh in about 20 minutes.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for a climb up Arthur's Seat: a rough walk of about 1–1.5 hours up and down; wear grippy shoes.
    • Take a train or bus to South Queensferry to see the Forth Bridges, or a boat trip to Inchcolm Abbey; check sailings on the day.
    • History fans can add a day trip to Stirling Castle or Rosslyn Chapel; times and tickets vary, so check on the day.

    Getting around

    • Trams link the airport, Haymarket, Princes Street, Waverley and Leith; buy at stop machines.
    • Lothian buses cover the city; exact cash or contactless, day and multi-day tickets available.
    • Airlink 100 runs 24 hours to Waverley Bridge, about 20-30 min.
    • Taxis are metered and plentiful; Uber and Bolt operate in Edinburgh.
    • Driving is awkward: many central streets are closed and parking is scarce and pricey.
    • The centre is very walkable; bikes and e-bikes are easy to hire, with some cycle lanes.

    Where to stay

    • Old Town · ≈ GBP 90–260 a night
      Walk to the Castle, Royal Mile and Grassmarket. Lively and central, but hilly and noisy at night.
    • New Town · ≈ GBP 100–300 a night
      Georgian streets, Princes Street shops and gardens. Quieter, flatter and close to Waverley station.
    • Leith · ≈ GBP 70–200 a night
      Waterfront bars and restaurants, cheaper rooms. Trams and buses reach the centre in about 20 minutes.
    • Haymarket · ≈ GBP 75–210 a night
      Near the station, airport tram and bus links. Good value and a short walk to the West End.

    Food & drink

    • Try Scottish classics: haggis, neeps and tatties, Cullen skink soup and a full Scottish breakfast.
    • Look for a pub lunch in the Old Town or Stockbridge; expect roughly GBP 12–20 for a main.
    • Seafood is good in Leith: check the day's catch and book ahead for dinner.
    • Cheap eats: pies, baked potatoes and sandwiches for about GBP 5–9; many museums have cafés.
    • Book dinner ahead in August, when festival crowds fill central restaurants.

    Tips

    1. The Edinburgh Festival (August) makes the city incredibly busy; book accommodation months in advance
    2. Climb Arthur's Seat for an amazing panoramic view of the entire city and coastline
    3. The Old Town's cobblestones are beautiful but hard on the feet—wear sturdy shoes
    4. Scottish whisky tastings are a cultural staple
    5. Always carry a raincoat, as weather changes rapidly
