Getting around

MRT for most trips; EasyCard makes it cheaper and works on buses too.

  • ✓Metro ★Clean, safe and cheap: NT$20–65 per ride. Trains run about 06:00–00:00. EasyCard gives 20% off.
  • ✓BusFrequent city buses. Pay with EasyCard on boarding; transfers within an hour cost NT$7.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsMetered taxis are plentiful and Uber operates in Taipei. Good for late nights and luggage.
  • ✗Rental carNot advised for visitors. Parking is hard and scooters dominate the roads.
  • ✓WalkingSidewalks are good in central areas. YouBike share bikes are widely available for longer hops.

Climate

Subtropical and humid. Summers are hot and wet; winters are mild but grey and damp. Rain falls in every month.

  • 🌧️Rain all yearExpect 11–21 rain days each month. June is the wettest. Always carry a small umbrella.
  • 🥵Hot, humid summerJuly and August reach about 33°C with high humidity. Plan indoor sights at midday.
  • 🌤️Best months: October to AprilHighs of 19–27°C and less intense heat. Best for walking and day trips.
  • 🧥Mild winterJanuary and February sit around 14–20°C. A light jacket is enough; indoor spaces can feel cold.
  • ☂️Typhoon seasonLate summer and early autumn can bring storms. Check forecasts on the day and keep plans flexible.
  • 🧳What to packLight layers, a rain jacket or umbrella, and comfortable shoes. Add a warm layer for winter evenings.

Landmarks 31

Must-see · 9

  1. 1

    Taipei 101

    Taipei's defining skyscraper and a symbol of the city, with an observation deck offering sweeping views. Go on a clear day; tickets can be bought on the spot or online, and the best time is late afternoon to catch sunset and city lights.

  2. 2

    National Palace Museum

    Houses one of the world's largest collections of Chinese imperial artifacts, making it a must for history and art lovers. Visit on a weekday morning to avoid crowds; the museum is easily reached by bus or taxi from downtown.

  3. 3

    Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall

    A monumental landmark set in a large park, featuring the memorial hall, Liberty Square Arch, and the National Theater. The changing of the guard ceremony is a highlight; go early morning or late afternoon for cooler weather and fewer crowds.

  4. 4

    Shilin Night Market

    Taipei's most famous night market, offering a dizzying array of street food and local snacks. Come hungry and try classics like oyster omelets and giant fried chicken; it's busiest after 7 pm, so arrive earlier for a more comfortable experience.

  5. 5

    Longshan Temple

    A beautifully ornate Buddhist temple that remains an active place of worship, giving visitors a glimpse into local religious life. Dress respectfully and visit in the evening when the temple is beautifully lit and less crowded.

  6. 6

    Ximending

    A vibrant pedestrian shopping and entertainment district, often called Taipei's Harajuku. It's perfect for people-watching, street performances, and trendy shops; go in the evening when the area comes alive with neon lights.

  7. 7

    Dihua Street

    A historic street lined with traditional shops selling Chinese herbs, dried goods, and fabrics, especially lively during Lunar New Year. Wander the side alleys to discover old architecture and sample local snacks; it's a short walk from the MRT.

  8. 8

    Yehliu Geopark

    A unique coastal geological park famous for its mushroom-shaped rock formations, including the iconic Queen's Head. Bring sunscreen and water as there's little shade; it's about an hour's bus ride from Taipei, so plan for a half-day trip.

  9. 9

    Jiufen Old Street

    A charming hillside old street with teahouses, snack stalls, and stunning ocean views, often compared to Spirited Away. Visit on a weekday to avoid crowds, and stay for sunset when the lanterns light up the narrow alleys.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day trip to Jiufen and the coast by bus or train; check times on the day.
    • Take a hot-spring trip to Beitou on the MRT, about 30 minutes from the center.
    • Swap one city day for the National Palace Museum if you like art and history; allow half a day.

    Getting around

    • MRT is clean, safe and cheap: NT$20-65 per ride, trains about 06:00-00:00.
    • EasyCard gives 20% off MRT rides and NT$7 bus transfers within an hour.
    • City buses are frequent; pay with EasyCard on boarding.
    • Taxis are metered and plentiful; Uber operates in Taipei.
    • Driving is not advised for visitors; parking is hard and scooters dominate.
    • YouBike share bikes are widely available; sidewalks are good for walking.

    Where to stay

    • Ximending · ≈ TWD 1,200–3,500 a night
      Young, busy and central. Walk to the MRT, night markets and the old Wanhua temples. Good for first-timers who want nightlife.
    • Zhongshan · ≈ TWD 1,800–4,500 a night
      Quiet lanes, small shops and cafés. Two MRT lines and short rides to most sights.
    • Da'an · ≈ TWD 2,000–5,000 a night
      Leafy and calm, near the park and Yongkang Street food. A good base for couples and slower trips.
    • Taipei Main Station · ≈ TWD 1,400–3,800 a night
      Best for transport: MRT, trains and the airport line in one place. Practical for short stays and day trips.

    Food & drink

    • Eat at night markets: Shilin and Raohe are the big ones. Go hungry, share dishes, and carry small cash.
    • Try the classics: beef noodle soup, soup dumplings, oyster omelette, stinky tofu and shaved ice.
    • Yongkang Street in Da'an is good for a sit-down food walk with tea shops and dessert.
    • Convenience stores are cheap and open late. A quick meal or snack costs about NT$60–150.
    • Many small eateries are cash-first. Keep some TWD on you; card use is common but not everywhere.

    Tips

    1. Get an EasyCard for seamless travel on the MRT, buses, YouBikes, and purchases at convenience stores
    2. Night markets like Shilin and Raohe are the best places to experience local food culture
    3. Do not eat, drink, or chew gum on the MRT — the fines are strictly enforced
    4. Trash is not left on the street; you must hand it directly to musical garbage trucks or use station bins
    5. Carry an umbrella year-round for sudden rain showers and intense summer sun
