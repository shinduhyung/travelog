Getting around

Walk the centre; use trams for longer hops and ride apps for the airport.

  • ✗MetroNo metro in Bratislava. Don't plan around rail inside the city.
  • ✓BusBus 61 links the airport to the main train station; N61 runs at night. Buy tickets from machines before boarding.
  • ✓Tram / Trolleybus ★Trams and trolleybuses cover the centre. Buy tickets from machines before boarding.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsUber, Bolt, Hopin and Liftago work reliably. Airport taxis are pricey; use an app instead.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is easy, but parking in the old town is scarce and costly.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is very walkable. Bikes are fine along the Danube.
  • ✓FerryA ferry runs on the Danube to Devín and across to Austria in season.

Climate

Continental climate: cold winters, warm summers and rain spread through the year.

  • 🌡️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs from about 20°C to 28°C. Comfortable for walking the old town and castle.
  • ❄️Cold winters (December to February)Highs of 4–8°C and lows near or below freezing. Warm coat, hat and gloves needed.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 7–11 rain days every month. May and June are the wettest; March the driest.
  • ☀️Sunny summersJuly and August reach about 28°C with around 8 rain days. Long daylight hours.
  • 🍂Mild shoulder monthsApril and October sit around 16°C, good for sightseeing with fewer crowds.
  • 🧳What to packLayers and a waterproof jacket any month; a warm coat for winter; light clothes and sunscreen for summer.

Landmarks 10

Must-see · 4

  1. 1

    Bratislava Castle

    The city's most famous landmark, a massive white castle on a hill above the Danube with sweeping views over Bratislava and into Austria. It houses the Slovak National Museum and the grounds are free to wander. Go early morning or late afternoon to avoid crowds and catch the best light; the walk up from the Old Town takes about 15 minutes.

  2. 2

    St. Martin's Cathedral

    This Gothic cathedral is where Hungarian kings were crowned for centuries, making it a key piece of Bratislava's history. The interior is serene and the spire dominates the skyline. Dress modestly and check opening hours, as it closes for services; it's a short walk from Michael's Gate.

  3. 3

    Michael's Gate

    The only surviving medieval city gate, with a distinctive tower and a weapons museum inside. It marks the entrance to the charming Old Town. Climb the tower for a small fee for a close-up view of the old streets; it's easily reached on foot from anywhere in the center.

  4. 4

    Devín Castle

    These dramatic ruins sit on a cliff where the Morava River meets the Danube, offering a powerful sense of history and stunning views. It's a short bus ride from the city center. Bring comfortable shoes for the uneven paths and go on a clear day for the best scenery.

    Swap in instead

    • Take the Danube ferry to Devín castle in season for ruins and river views; check sailing times on the day.
    • Swap a city afternoon for a wine afternoon in the Small Carpathians; buses and trains reach the wine towns.
    • Cross to Austria by ferry in season for a half-day on the other bank; bring your passport.

    Getting around

    • Trams and trolleybuses cover the centre; buy tickets from machines before boarding.
    • Bus 61 links the airport to the main train station; N61 runs at night.
    • Ride apps Uber, Bolt, Hopin and Liftago work reliably; airport taxis are pricey.
    • Driving is easy but parking in the old town is scarce and costly.
    • The centre is very walkable; bikes are fine along the Danube.
    • A ferry runs on the Danube to Devín and across to Austria in season.

    Where to stay

    • Old Town (Staré Mesto) · ≈ EUR 70–180 a night
      Walk to the castle, the main square and most sights. Best for first-time visitors who want everything on foot.
    • Danube riverside · ≈ EUR 65–160 a night
      Quiet evenings by the water, close to the UFO bridge and the Devín ferry. A short walk or tram to the old town.
    • Petržalka · ≈ EUR 45–110 a night
      Cheapest beds and big apartment blocks south of the river. Good tram links; plain but practical.
    • Nové Mesto · ≈ EUR 50–120 a night
      Local, residential and near the main train station. Good value if you arrive by rail and don't mind a walk.

    Food & drink

    • Try Slovak classics: bryndzové halušky (potato dumplings with sheep cheese), kapustnica soup and lokše pancakes.
    • Pub lunches in the old town are good value; a main course often costs around EUR 8–14.
    • Look for a weekend 'street food' market or the old market hall for cheap local bites; check opening days on the day.
    • Wine bars serve Slovak and small-region wines; a glass is often a few euros. Ask for a local Frankovka or Veltlín.
    • Beer is cheaper than coffee in many places. A half-litre of local draft is often around EUR 2–3.

    Tips

    1. Vienna is less than an hour away by train or boat, making day trips incredibly easy
    2. Visit the UFO Observation Deck on the SNP Bridge for panoramic views
    3. Try traditional Bryndzové halušky (potato dumplings with sheep cheese)
    4. The Old Town is largely pedestrianized and very safe
    5. English is widely spoken by the younger generations
