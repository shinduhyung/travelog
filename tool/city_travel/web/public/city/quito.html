Climate

High-altitude equatorial climate: mild all year, cool nights, and rain possible in any month. July to September is driest.

  • 🌦️Rain in most monthsRain days range from about 12 in July to 29 in March. A light rain jacket beats an umbrella in the wind.
  • 🌡️Mild days, cool nightsHighs stay near 17–19°C all year; lows are 7–9°C. Bring a warm layer for evenings.
  • ☀️Best time: July to SeptemberFewer rain days and slightly warmer days. Best odds for volcano views and hikes.
  • ⛰️Altitude mattersQuito sits very high. Take the first day easy, drink water and go slow on stairs.
  • 🌤️Mornings are clearerClouds and showers often build in the afternoon. Do outdoor sights early.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a rain jacket, sturdy shoes and sunscreen. The sun is strong at altitude even when cloudy.

Landmarks 15

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    Historic Centre of Quito

    A UNESCO-listed colonial old town packed with churches, plazas and balconied streets, and the first thing any visitor should explore. Go early in the morning for quieter streets and better light; it is walkable from most central hotels and taxis are cheap.

  2. 2

    Middle of the World City

    The monument marking the equator, where you can stand with a foot in each hemisphere, is Quito's most famous photo stop. It is north of the city, so allow half a day and take a taxi or tour bus; go on a clear morning for views.

  3. 3

    Church of the Society of Jesus

    This Jesuit church is famous for its gold-leaf interior and is one of the most ornate sights in the old town. Dress respectfully and note that a small entry fee applies; visit early or late to avoid tour groups.

  4. 4

    Virgin of El Panecillo

    A huge winged statue on a hill with panoramic views over Quito and the surrounding volcanoes. Go on a clear day and take a taxi up rather than walking, especially after dark.

  5. 5

    Basilica of the National Vow

    A neo-Gothic basilica with towers you can climb for rooftop views over the old town. Wear comfortable shoes for the stairs and ladders; a small fee is charged to go up.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for the TelefériQo cable car and a short walk on the Pichincha slopes. Go early; clouds build by midday.
    • Swap a city afternoon for Mitad del Mundo and the Intiñan site on the equator, about an hour north.
    • Take a day trip to Cotopaxi National Park for volcano views and páramo walks. Book a tour; altitude is high.

    Getting around

    • Metro runs Quitumbe to El Labrador via Centro Histórico and La Mariscal in about 30 min; fares $0.45 general, $0.22 reduced.
    • Metro hours: 05:30-23:00 Mon-Fri, 07:00-23:00 Sat, 07:00-22:00 Sun and holidays.
    • El Trole, Metrobus and Ecovia buses cost about $0.35 per ride; pay at station entrances.
    • Taxis are yellow with door numbers; day minimum $1, night minimum $2, most city trips under $5.
    • Easy Taxi and Uber work in Quito; hotels can call a vetted taxi.
    • Old town is best on foot and car-free on Sundays; bikes allowed on Metro off-peak, first and last cars only.

    Where to stay

    • Centro Histórico · ≈ USD 35–160 a night
      Walk to the churches, plazas and museums. Quiet at night; car-free on Sundays. Many hostels and small hotels.
    • La Mariscal · ≈ USD 30–130 a night
      Backpacker and nightlife zone with the most restaurants and tour agencies. Metro stop nearby.
    • La Carolina · ≈ USD 60–220 a night
      Modern, safe and green next to the big park and malls. Good for families and business trips.
    • La Floresta · ≈ USD 45–150 a night
      Local, bohemian neighbourhood with cafés and food spots. Between the old town and the park.

    Food & drink

    • Try locro de papa (potato and cheese soup), hornado (roast pork) and llapingachos (cheese potato patties).
    • Set lunches (almuerzos) at small restaurants cost about $3–5: soup, main and juice. Great value.
    • Visit a market: Mercado Central or San Francisco for fruit, juices and cheap local plates.
    • Ceviche and seafood are good; highland trout is a common dish. Wash it down with canelazo (hot spiced drink).
    • Coffee is a local pride. Look for small specialty cafés in La Floresta and La Mariscal.

    Tips

    1. Altitude sickness is very common; hydrate well and take it easy on your first day
    2. Use Uber for safety and transparency
    3. Visit 'Mitad del Mundo' to stand on the equator line
    4. Take the TelefériQo cable car up the Pichincha volcano for incredible views
    5. The sun is very intense at this altitude; use strong SPF sunscreen daily
