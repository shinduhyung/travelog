Getting around

Trams for the centre and Ilidža, walking the old town, and taxis with a meter for late trips.

  • ✗MetroThere is no metro in Sarajevo. Use trams and trolleybuses instead.
  • ✓Tram / Trolleybus ★Trams and trolleybuses run the city. Tram 3 runs the full line from Ilidža to the centre. Tram 1 and 4 serve the central loop. A single ticket costs 2.20 KM and a day ticket costs 7.10 KM. Buy in the Javni prevoz KS app, from drivers or at GRAS kiosks.
  • ✓BusBuses cover routes beyond the trams. Buy tickets from the driver for 2.20 KM. Pre-bought tram tickets are not valid on buses.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsLegitimate taxis have a TAXI sign and plates with TA. Insist on the meter and ask for a receipt. Official firms include Paja 1522, Žuti 033 66 35 55 and Samir & Emir 1516. Taxis are the main scam risk, so avoid unmarked cars.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is not recommended in the Old Town, where streets are steep, narrow and one-way. Parking is very hard to find. A car is only useful for trips outside the city.
  • ✓Walking ★Walking is the best way to see the centre. Street signs are scarce, so use a map app.
  • ✓Bike rental (Nextbike)Nextbike has about 14 stations along the tram lines. You get 30 free minutes per day, then 1.50 KM after that. Separate bike lanes run along the river and main boulevard for over 5 km.
  • ✗FerryNo ferry service operates in the city.

Climate

Continental climate with cold, snowy winters and warm, fairly dry summers. Rain is spread through the year.

  • ☀️Best time: May to June and SeptemberHighs around 20–24°C with mild nights. Ideal for walking, cycling and the mountains.
  • 🥶Cold winters (December to February)Highs near 5–9°C and lows below freezing. Wear warm layers and waterproof shoes for icy streets.
  • 🔥Hot summers (July and August)Highs around 27°C. Walk early or late in the day and carry water.
  • 🌧️Rain year-roundBetween 9 and 16 rainy days a month. Carry a compact umbrella in every season.
  • 🏔️Mountain weather changes fastHigher ground is cooler than the city centre. Check the forecast before any hike or ride.
  • 🧳What to packWarm layers, a waterproof jacket, comfortable walking shoes and a small umbrella. Pack light clothing for summer days.

Landmarks 11

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Baščaršija

    The old Ottoman bazaar quarter is the heart of Sarajevo, with copper workshops, tea houses and narrow cobbled lanes. It is the best place to understand the city's layered history. Visit in the morning or early evening for quieter streets and better light for photos.

  2. 2

    Latin Bridge

    This small bridge is where Archduke Franz Ferdinand was assassinated in 1914, an event that helped trigger the First World War. A plaque and nearby exhibits mark the spot. It is a short walk from Baščaršija, so pair the two in one outing.

  3. 3

    Sebilj

    This wooden Ottoman-style fountain stands in the main square of Baščaršija and has become one of the city's best-known images. It is a quick stop on any walk through the old town. Go early in the day to photograph it without crowds.

  4. 4

    Sarajevo City Hall (Vijećnica)

    The Moorish Revival building was gutted by fire during the 1992-1995 siege and later restored, and it stands as a powerful symbol of the city's recovery. Its façade is one of the most striking in the centre. Check opening times in advance, as access to the interior can be limited.

  5. 5

    Gazi Husrev-bey Mosque

    Built in the 16th century, this is the largest and most important mosque in Sarajevo and a centrepiece of Ottoman-era architecture. Visitors should dress modestly and remove shoes before entering. Visit outside prayer times and avoid Friday midday if you want a calmer visit.

  6. 6

    Sarajevo Tunnel Museum (Tunnel of Hope)

    A section of the wartime tunnel that linked besieged Sarajevo to safe territory during the 1992-1995 siege is preserved here. The museum explains how it kept the city supplied and is a moving, essential visit for understanding recent history. Plan at least an hour, and consider a guided tour for context.

  7. 7

    Eternal Flame

    This memorial flame honours fallen soldiers and is a focal point for ceremonies and remembrance in the city. It is a simple but meaningful stop, and it sits close to other central sights. Visit during the day for the clearest view, and treat it as a place of remembrance.

    Getting around

    • Currency is the Bosnian convertible mark (KM), not USD. Prices below are in KM unless noted.
    • Trams and trolleybuses run the city. Tram 3 runs the full line from Ilidža to the centre. Tram 1 and 4 serve the central loop. There is no metro.
    • Single tram, trolleybus or bus ticket costs 2.20 KM. Day ticket is 7.10 KM. Buy in the Javni prevoz KS app, from drivers, or at GRAS kiosks. Bus tickets are bought from the driver, and pre-bought tram tickets are not valid on buses.
    • Taxis are the main scam risk. Legitimate taxis have a TAXI sign and plates with TA. Insist on the meter and ask for a receipt. Official firms include Paja 1522, Žuti 033 66 35 55 and Samir & Emir 1516.
    • Driving is not recommended in the Old Town, where streets are steep, narrow and one-way. Parking is very hard to find.
    • Walking is the best way to see the centre. Street signs are scarce, so use a map app. Nextbike rental has about 14 stations along the tram lines, with 30 free minutes per day and 1.50 KM after that. Separate bike lanes run along the river and main boulevard for over 5 km.
    • No ferry service operates in the city.

    Where to stay

    • Baščaršija · ≈ USD 60–180 a night
      Walk to the bazaar, Sebilj and most old-town sights. Quiet at night, and narrow streets mean you walk rather than drive.
    • Marijin Dvor · ≈ USD 70–200 a night
      Near the main boulevard and the river bike lanes. Trams 1, 3 and 4 serve the centre, so reaching the old town is easy.
    • Centar · ≈ USD 55–170 a night
      Central and walkable, with many cafés and shops. Good base for trams and the Nextbike stations along the tram lines.
    • Vratnik · ≈ USD 45–140 a night
      Quiet residential old-town edge below the fortress. Lovely views, but steep streets and no driving, so pack light.

    Food & drink

    • Try ćevapi (grilled minced meat rolls) in Baščaršija, usually served with raw onion and somun bread.
    • Order burek or sirnica (pastry with meat or cheese) for breakfast, then finish with baklava and Bosnian coffee in a copper pot.
    • Eat at the bazaar cafés and kebab grills around Baščaršija. Prices are in KM, so check the menu before ordering.
    • Sit in a traditional kafana for slow coffee and local dishes. Book ahead on weekends if you can.
    • Pay in KM. Carry some small notes and coins for kiosks and buses, since bus tickets are bought from the driver.
