Getting around

Contactless card on the Tube and buses; walk the centre.

  • ✓Metro ★The Underground, Elizabeth line and Overground take contactless cards and Oyster. Fares are capped per day.
  • ✓BusA flat £1.75 per bus, and a second bus within an hour is free (Hopper fare). Good for sightseeing above ground.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsBlack cabs are metered and can be hailed. Uber, Bolt and FREENOW are widely used.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the centre means the Congestion Charge and ULEZ fees. Visitors rarely need a car.
  • ✓Walking ★Central London is very walkable; many sights are 15–30 minutes apart on foot.
  • ✓FerryThames Clippers river boats link Westminster, the South Bank, the Tower and Greenwich.

Climate

Temperate and changeable: mild summers, cool winters, and rain in every month. Rarely extreme.

  • 🌦️Rain all yearAbout 10–13 rain days every month. Showers are often short; carry a small umbrella or jacket.
  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs of 17–23°C and long daylight. July is warmest; September is mild with fewer crowds.
  • 🧥Winter is cool, not freezingJanuary and February highs near 8–9°C and lows near 2–3°C. A warm coat and layers are enough.
  • 🌂March is the wettestAround 13 rain days. Plan indoor backups like museums and galleries.
  • 🕶️Summer days are longJune to August gives the most daylight for parks, walks and the river. Evenings stay light.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes for walking. Add sunglasses and a hat for summer.

Landmarks 78

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Big Ben

    The clock tower of the Houses of Parliament and London's best-known silhouette. Best seen from Westminster Bridge or Parliament Square, and from the South Bank when it is lit up at night.

  2. 2

    Tower of London

    A 1,000-year-old fortress that was palace, prison and execution site, now home of the Crown Jewels. Join a free Yeoman Warder (Beefeater) tour and go early to see the jewels before the queues build.

  3. 3

    Buckingham Palace

    The King's London residence. The Changing of the Guard runs on set mornings (check the schedule); the State Rooms open to visitors for about ten weeks each summer.

  4. 4

    Tower Bridge

    The Victorian bascule bridge next to the Tower of London. Walk across for free, or pay for the high walkways with glass floors 42 m above the Thames.

  5. 5

    Westminster Abbey

    Coronation church since 1066 and resting place of monarchs, Newton and Darwin. Paid entry for sightseeing; attend Evensong to hear the choir for free.

  6. 6

    British Museum

    Free world-history museum with the Rosetta Stone, the Parthenon sculptures and Egyptian mummies. Too big for one visit: pick three galleries and go at opening.

  7. 7

    London Eye

    The 135 m observation wheel on the South Bank; a 30-minute rotation gives views over Parliament and the city. Book a timed slot and go near sunset.

  8. 8

    St Paul's Cathedral

    Christopher Wren's domed masterpiece. Climb 528 steps through the Whispering Gallery to the Golden Gallery for one of the best views of the City.

  9. 9

    Natural History Museum

    Free museum in a cathedral-like building in South Kensington, with dinosaurs, a blue whale skeleton and the earthquake room. Good on rainy days and with kids.

  10. 10

    Windsor Castle

    The oldest occupied castle in the world, about 40 minutes by train from London. See the State Apartments and St George's Chapel; check closures for royal events before going.

    Swap in instead

    • Greenwich: take the Thames Clippers boat for the Cutty Sark, the market and the park.
    • Swap a museum day for a half-day trip to Hampton Court Palace or Kew Gardens by train.
    • Camden and Regent's Canal: walk the towpath from Camden Lock to Little Venice.

    Getting around

    • The Underground (Tube), Elizabeth line, Overground and buses all take contactless cards and Oyster; fares are capped per day.
    • Buses cost a flat £1.75 and a second bus within an hour is free (Hopper fare).
    • Black cabs are metered and can be hailed; Uber, Bolt and FREENOW are widely used.
    • Driving in the centre means the Congestion Charge and ULEZ fees; visitors rarely need a car.
    • Central London is very walkable; many sights are 15-30 minutes apart on foot.
    • Thames Clippers river boats link Westminster, the South Bank, the Tower and Greenwich.

    Where to stay

    • West End & Soho · ≈ GBP 150–400 a night
      Walk to theatres, Oxford Street and most central sights. Busy and noisy at night; book early.
    • South Kensington · ≈ GBP 140–380 a night
      Next to the Natural History and Science Museums and Hyde Park. Quiet streets, good for families.
    • South Bank & Southwark · ≈ GBP 120–350 a night
      Riverside walks, the Eye and the Tower within reach. Good Tube links from Waterloo and London Bridge.
    • King's Cross & Bloomsbury · ≈ GBP 110–300 a night
      The British Museum, the British Library and fast trains from King's Cross and St Pancras.

    Food & drink

    • Try a proper Sunday roast in a pub, and fish and chips from a chippy. Expect roughly £12–20 a main.
    • Markets are the best value: Borough Market for snacks, Brick Lane for curry, Camden for street food.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead, especially for dinner Thursday to Saturday.
    • Lunch is often cheaper than dinner at the same place; many pubs do good-value set lunches.
    • Tap water is safe and free to ask for. A pint of beer is roughly £5–7 in central pubs.

    Tips

    1. Oyster card or contactless payment covers all public transport — no need to buy individual tickets.
    2. Many world-class museums (British Museum, National Gallery, V&A) are free to enter.
    3. Pubs close around 11pm — plan evening drinks accordingly.
    4. Weather is unpredictable; always carry a light jacket even in summer.
    5. Book popular restaurants and West End shows well in advance.
