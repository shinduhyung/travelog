Getting around

Use the MRT and TransJakarta for main routes; Grab or Gojek for the rest.

  • ✓Metro ★Jakarta MRT runs Lebak Bulus to Bundaran HI, every 5–10 min, 05:00–midnight.
  • ✓Bus ★TransJakarta BRT has 14 mainlines on dedicated lanes; fares Rp2,000–20,000 with one card.
  • ✓TrainKAI Commuter trains link suburbs and run 04:30–midnight; Jabodebek and Jakarta LRT cover more areas.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsGrab and Gojek are the main ride apps; metered taxis exist but agree on price or use the meter.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is slow and congested; odd-even plate rules apply on some toll roads on weekdays.
  • ✗WalkingWalking long distances is impractical; use apps or transit between areas.

Climate

Tropical and hot all year, around 30–33°C by day and 23–24°C at night. Rain is common, heaviest from November to April.

  • 🌧️Wet season: November to AprilExpect 26–29 rain days a month. Showers are often heavy but short; carry an umbrella.
  • ☀️Driest months: July to SeptemberAbout 11–14 rain days. Best window for outdoor sights, though it stays hot.
  • 🥵Hot and humid all yearHighs of 30–33°C with high humidity. Plan outdoor time for early morning.
  • 🌂Pack for rainA compact umbrella or rain jacket and quick-dry shoes help in the wet months.
  • 💧Stay hydratedDrink bottled water often; tap water is not safe to drink.
  • 🧳What to packLight breathable clothes, sunscreen and a light layer for air-conditioned malls and trains.

Landmarks 15

Must-see · 3

  1. 1

    Monas

    Jakarta's most famous landmark, the National Monument, topped with a flame and set in a large central park. It is the symbol of the city and a must for first-time visitors. Go early in the morning to avoid heat and crowds, and note the monument is often closed on Mondays.

  2. 2

    Istiqlal Mosque

    Southeast Asia's largest mosque and a striking modern landmark right by Monas. Visitors can tour the prayer halls outside prayer times. Dress modestly and cover shoulders and legs; robes are often available at the entrance.

  3. 3

    Kota Tua Jakarta

    The old Dutch colonial quarter with historic buildings, squares and museums, best explored on foot. Come in the morning or late afternoon for cooler weather and better light. It is easily reached by train to Jakarta Kota station.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day trip to Bogor for the botanical gardens and cooler hills; check train times on the day.
    • History fans can spend a half day in Kota Tua: the old town square, museum and harbour area.
    • Families can swap a mall day for Ancol's waterfront parks and attractions.

    Getting around

    • Jakarta MRT runs Lebak Bulus to Bundaran HI, every 5-10 min, 05:00-midnight.
    • TransJakarta BRT has 14 mainlines on dedicated lanes; fares Rp2,000-20,000 with one card.
    • KAI Commuter trains link suburbs and run 04:30-midnight; Jabodebek and Jakarta LRT cover more areas.
    • Grab and Gojek are the main ride apps; metered taxis exist but agree on price or use the meter.
    • Driving is slow and congested; odd-even plate rules apply on some toll roads on weekdays.
    • Walking long distances is impractical; use apps or transit between areas.

    Where to stay

    • Central Jakarta (Thamrin) · ≈ IDR 400,000–1,500,000 a night
      Central for the MRT, big malls and the National Monument; easy to reach most sights.
    • South Jakarta (Kemang) · ≈ IDR 350,000–1,200,000 a night
      Cafés, restaurants and nightlife; quieter streets than the centre.
    • West Jakarta (Kota) · ≈ IDR 250,000–900,000 a night
      Next to Old Batavia (Kota Tua) and its museums; good for history-focused days.
    • North Jakarta (Ancol) · ≈ IDR 450,000–1,600,000 a night
      Waterfront parks and family attractions; cooler breeze but far from the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Try Jakarta staples: nasi goreng, sate ayam, gado-gado and kerak telor, an egg-and-rice street snack.
    • Eat at night markets and street warungs for cheap, fresh food; look for busy stalls with local queues.
    • Padang restaurants serve ready-made spicy dishes piled high; you pay for what you eat.
    • In malls, food courts are clean and cheap; sit-down restaurants cost more but are reliable.
    • Tap water is not safe to drink. Drink bottled or filtered water, and be careful with ice from street stalls.

    Tips

    1. Traffic jams (macet) are severe — rely on the MRT or TransJakarta BRT when possible
    2. Download Grab or Gojek for ride-hailing and food delivery; they are essential
    3. Street food (kaki lima) is a major part of the culture, but drink only bottled water
    4. Malls act as major social hubs due to air conditioning and lack of public parks
    5. Be prepared for heavy rain and localized flooding if traveling between November and March

      Batik is a traditional Indonesian method of dyeing cloth with wax patterns. It is one of the country's best-known crafts.

      Where: Alun Alun Indonesia, Grand Indonesia
      Check whether the pattern is hand-drawn batik cap or tulis, or just a printed copy. Hand-made versions cost more.

      Wayang kulit leather puppet

      IDR 150000–5000001 small piece · estimate

      Wayang kulit is a leather shadow puppet, tied to the Javanese shadow puppet theater. Jakarta is home to many Javanese craftspeople.

      Where: Sarinah craft shop
      Check that the leather is real and that the colors are hand-painted, not printed.
