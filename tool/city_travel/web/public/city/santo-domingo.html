Getting around

Walk the Zona Colonial and Malecón; use Uber or InDrive for longer trips.

  • ✓MetroTwo lines, RD$20 per ride with a smartcard (RD$60 card plus RD$20 minimum). Useful for some cross-city trips.
  • ✓BusCheap but confusing; fares around RD$20–40. Fine if you have time and local help.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis have no meters, so agree the fare before you get in. Uber and InDrive work and are often easier.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is chaotic; not recommended for short visits.
  • ✓Walking ★Best in the Zona Colonial and along the Malecón. Sidewalks are uneven, so watch your step.
  • ✓FerryFerry to Puerto Rico (Mayagüez or San Juan) is about US$200 roundtrip and takes around 12 hours.

Climate

Tropical climate: warm all year, with a wetter season from May to October and a drier, slightly cooler stretch from November to April.

  • ☀️Best time: December to MarchHighs around 28–29°C and fewer rain days. Ideal for walking the Colonial Zone.
  • 🌧️Wet season: May to OctoberAbout 20–23 rain days a month. Showers are often short and heavy; plan around them.
  • 🥵Hottest months: July and AugustHighs near 31°C with warm nights around 24°C. Go out early or after sunset.
  • 🌀Hurricane season: June to NovemberPeaks around September. Check forecasts and keep plans flexible.
  • 👕What to packLight, breathable clothes, a rain jacket or umbrella, sunscreen and a hat. A light layer for air-conditioned museums.
  • 🌂Rain timingDownpours often come in the afternoon. Do outdoor sights in the morning and keep a backup indoor plan.

Landmarks 10

Must-see · 4

  1. 1

    Ciudad Colonial

    The walled old town is the heart of the first European city in the Americas, packed with colonial streets, plazas and churches. Wander it on foot early in the morning before the heat and crowds build, and wear comfortable shoes for the cobblestones.

  2. 2

    Alcazar de Colon

    This palace built for Diego Columbus is the city's most striking colonial residence and a must for understanding its Spanish heritage. Check opening hours before you go, as they can vary, and go early to avoid tour groups.

  3. 3

    Ozama Fortress

    The oldest military fortress in the Americas guards the river mouth and offers some of the best views over the old town. Bring water and sun protection, as the grounds are largely open to the sun.

  4. 4

    Catedral Primada de América

    The first cathedral in the Americas anchors the old town and is a landmark of colonial architecture. Dress modestly, as it is an active church, and visit outside mass times.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a beach day: Boca Chica is about 30–40 minutes east by taxi or bus. Check conditions on the day.
    • History buffs can swap a museum for the Faro a Colón and the Columbus sites across the Ozama river.
    • Take a day trip to Los Tres Ojos caves and the Botanical Garden if you want nature close to the city.

    Getting around

    • Metro: two lines, RD$20 per ride with smartcard (RD$60 card + RD$20 minimum).
    • Buses: cheap but confusing; fares around RD$20-40.
    • Taxis: no meters; agree fare before; apps Uber and InDrive work.
    • Driving: chaotic; not recommended for short visits.
    • Walking: best in Zona Colonial and Malecón; sidewalks uneven.
    • Ferry: to Puerto Rico (Mayagüez/San Juan) about US$200 roundtrip, 12 hours.

    Where to stay

    • Zona Colonial · ≈ DOP 2,500–9,000 a night
      Walk to the cathedral, Alcázar and Plaza España. Best for first-timers who want history on foot.
    • Piantini · ≈ DOP 4,500–14,000 a night
      Modern business district with the best restaurants and malls. Quieter nights, short taxi rides to the old city.
    • Gazcue · ≈ DOP 2,500–7,000 a night
      Leafy, calm streets between the Colonial Zone and the Malecón. Good value and walkable to both.
    • Bella Vista · ≈ DOP 3,500–10,000 a night
      Residential area near the Malecón with apartments and mid-range hotels. Easy for longer stays.

    Food & drink

    • Start the day with mangú: mashed plantain with eggs, fried cheese and salami. Cheap and filling.
    • Try Dominican classics: la bandera (rice, beans, meat), sancocho stew and tostones.
    • Street snacks: empanadas, quipes and chimi burgers. Eat where locals queue; prices are low.
    • Seafood along the Malecón and in Boca Chica. Ask the price before ordering fish by weight.
    • Coffee is strong and local. Look for small cafés in the Zona Colonial; a cup is a few hundred DOP at most.

    Tips

    1. Spend most of your time exploring the Zona Colonial, which is highly walkable and packed with 16th-century history
    2. Use Uber to travel outside the Colonial Zone for safety and convenience
    3. Drink only bottled water and be cautious with street food
    4. Enjoy the vibrant nightlife with live Merengue and Bachata music
    5. Baseball is a religion here; catching a winter league game is an incredible experience
