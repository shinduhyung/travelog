Getting around

Ride apps or taxis for most trips; DART buses for cheap cross-city runs.

  • ✗MetroNo metro. DART is a bus rapid transit system, not rail.
  • ✓BusDART buses run on dedicated lanes; Phase 1 links Kimara to Kivukoni Ferry via Posta. Daladala minibuses are cheap (under TSh 1,000) but slow and crowded; look for POSTA signs.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis are everywhere; agree the fare first, as meters are rare. Bolt and Uber work in Dar and often beat street-taxi prices.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is on the left. Traffic is heavy, especially at evening rush hour.
  • ✓WalkingWalking suits the compact centre. Cycling is possible but risky on busy roads.
  • ✓FerryFerries from Kivukoni cross to Kigamboni in the south.

Climate

Tropical coastal climate: hot all year, with a long rainy season around April and short rains late in the year.

  • ☀️Best time: June to SeptemberDriest and coolest months, with highs near 28–29°C and few rain days. Ideal for beaches and walking.
  • 🌧️Long rains in AprilAbout 26 rain days, the wettest month. Expect heavy showers and plan indoor sights.
  • 🌦️Short rains in November and DecemberAround 20–21 rain days. Showers are often short but frequent; carry an umbrella.
  • 🥵Hot and humid all yearHighs stay around 28–31°C and lows 22–26°C. Drink water and take midday breaks.
  • 🌊Sea breezes helpCoastal spots like Oyster Bay and the islands feel cooler than the city centre.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, a hat, sunscreen and insect repellent. Add a rain jacket for April and November–December.

Landmarks 4

Must-see · 4

  1. 1

    National Museum of Tanzania

    The country's main museum, covering Tanzanian history, archaeology and the famous fossil finds of early humans. A good introduction before any safari or coastal trip. Go on a weekday morning to avoid crowds; it is a short taxi ride from the city centre.

  2. 2

    Kariakoo Market

    The city's bustling heart, a sprawling market where you can buy produce, fabrics and everyday goods alongside locals. Come in the morning when it is liveliest and coolest, and keep valuables close in the crowds.

  3. 3

    Askari Monument

    A central statue commemorating African soldiers who served in the World Wars, standing at a busy roundabout in the downtown area. It is a quick photo stop and a handy orientation point; visit by day and combine it with a walk through the surrounding streets.

  4. 4

    Coco Beach

    The city's best-known public beach, popular for sunset walks, local food stalls and a lively evening atmosphere. Go in the late afternoon for the sunset; swimming is not recommended at all times, so check with locals first.

    Swap in instead

    • Take the Kivukoni ferry to Kigamboni for a cheap half-day across the harbour.
    • Add a boat trip to Bongoyo or Mbudya island for a beach day; check boat times on the day.
    • History fans can swap a city afternoon for Bagamoyo's old town and slave-trade sites up the coast.

    Getting around

    • DART bus rapid transit runs on dedicated lanes; Phase 1 links Kimara to Kivukoni Ferry via Posta.
    • Daladala minibuses are cheap (under TSh 1,000) but slow and crowded; look for POSTA signs.
    • Taxis are everywhere; agree the fare first, as meters are rare.
    • Ride apps like Bolt and Uber work in Dar and often beat street-taxi prices.
    • Driving is on the left; traffic is heavy, especially at evening rush hour.
    • Walking suits the compact centre; cycling is possible but risky on busy roads.
    • Ferries from Kivukoni cross to Kigamboni in the south.

    Where to stay

    • City Centre (Posta) · ≈ TZS 120,000–350,000 a night
      Walk to markets, the ferry and DART buses. Best base for a short first visit.
    • Kivukoni · ≈ TZS 150,000–400,000 a night
      Quiet waterfront near the ferry to Kigamboni and the fish market. Good for early starts.
    • Msasani Peninsula · ≈ TZS 200,000–600,000 a night
      Restaurants, cafés and malls close by; a short ride to the beaches.
    • Oyster Bay · ≈ TZS 250,000–800,000 a night
      Seafront hotels and calm evenings. Popular with business travellers and couples.

    Food & drink

    • Try Swahili coastal dishes: pilau rice, urojo soup and grilled fish with coconut.
    • Mchikichini and Kariakoo markets are good for spices, fruit and cheap local meals.
    • Seafood is best near the water: Msasani and Oyster Bay have many fish restaurants.
    • Street food is cheap: mishkaki skewers and chips mayai often cost under TSh 5,000.
    • Tap water is not for drinking. Stick to bottled or filtered water, even in hotels.

    Tips

    1. Traffic is very heavy; plan significant buffer time for any road travel
    2. Use reputable ride-hailing apps like Uber or Bolt rather than navigating Daladalas
    3. Take a ferry to Zanzibar for world-class beaches and history
    4. Dress conservatively in town, though the coastal areas are more relaxed
    5. Eat local Swahili dishes like Pilau or various fresh seafood at the night markets
