Getting around

Metro for most trips; walk the old town and use taxis late at night.

  • ✓Metro ★Metro and FGC trains cover the city. Zone 1 tickets are cheap and contactless via T-mobilitat.
  • ✓BusCity and intercity buses fill the gaps. Night buses run when the metro closes.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered. Uber, Bolt and Cabify operate in Barcelona.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is slow and parking is costly. Leave the car outside the centre.
  • ✓Walking ★Walking is best in the old town. Bike lanes and Bicing-style bike hire are common.
  • ✓FerryFerries and cruise shuttles link the port to La Rambla and the Balearics.

Climate

Mediterranean climate: mild winters, hot dry summers and the most rain in autumn.

  • ☀️Best time: May to JulyWarm days around 21–29°C with little rain. Good for both sightseeing and the beach.
  • 🥵Hot summer (July and August)Highs near 29°C and lows around 21–22°C. Sightsee early morning or evening; rest at midday.
  • 🌧️Wettest in autumnSeptember has about 12 rain days and October about 10. Showers can be heavy; carry a small umbrella.
  • 🧥Mild but cool wintersJanuary highs near 14°C and lows around 5°C. A warm coat is needed, especially at night.
  • 🏖️Beach seasonComfortable swimming is roughly June to September. May and October are for walks, not long swims.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes and sunscreen for summer; a layer for cool evenings; a rain jacket for spring and autumn.

Landmarks 22

Must-see · 9

  1. 1

    Sagrada Familia

    Gaudí's unfinished basilica is the symbol of Barcelona and unlike any church you will ever see, with soaring columns and stained-glass light. Book timed tickets online well ahead, and go early morning or late afternoon for the best light and smaller crowds.

  2. 2

    Park Güell

    Gaudí's hillside garden of mosaics and playful pavilions offers sweeping views over the city. The monumental zone needs a timed ticket; arrive at opening or in the late afternoon to avoid the midday crush.

  3. 3

    La Rambla

    This tree-lined promenade is the city's most famous street, linking Plaça de Catalunya to the port. Walk it in the morning, keep valuables close, and detour into the side lanes of the Gothic Quarter.

  4. 4

    Casa Batlló

    Gaudí's fantastical townhouse on Passeig de Gràcia is a must for its undulating facade and dreamlike interiors. Buy skip-the-line tickets online and visit early to beat the tour groups.

  5. 5

    Gothic Quarter

    The medieval core is a maze of narrow lanes, hidden squares and Roman remnants best explored on foot. Come early or in the evening for quieter streets, and wear comfortable shoes for the cobblestones.

  6. 6

    Casa Milà

    Known as La Pedrera, this Gaudí apartment building has a wave-like stone facade and a surreal rooftop of chimneys. Rooftop visits are timed; book online and choose a late slot for softer light.

  7. 7

    Mercado de La Boqueria

    Barcelona's great market off La Rambla is a feast of jamón, seafood and fresh juice. Go before noon on a weekday for the full stalls without the worst crowds, and watch your belongings.

  8. 8

    Barcelona Cathedral

    The Gothic cathedral in the heart of the old town has a cloister with geese and a quiet rooftop. Dress modestly for entry, and check opening hours since visiting times change around services.

  9. 9

    Camp Nou

    The home of FC Barcelona is a pilgrimage for football fans, with a museum and stadium tour. Book tickets online in advance, and check whether the stadium is open during renovation works.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a beach afternoon for Montjuïc: cable car, gardens and city views, then the Miró foundation.
    • Take a half-day train to Montserrat for the mountain monastery; check times and ticket options on the day.
    • Add a day trip to Girona or Sitges by train if you want a change from the city.

    Getting around

    • Metro and FGC trains cover the city; zone 1 tickets are cheap and contactless via T-mobilitat.
    • City and intercity buses fill the gaps; night buses run when the metro closes.
    • Taxis are metered; ride apps like Uber, Bolt and Cabify operate in Barcelona.
    • Driving is slow and parking is costly; leave the car outside the centre.
    • Walking is best in the old town; bike lanes and Bicing-style bike hire are common.
    • Ferries and cruise shuttles link the port to La Rambla and the Balearics.

    Where to stay

    • Barri Gòtic · ≈ EUR 110–260 a night
      Sleep in the medieval centre. Walk to the cathedral, La Rambla and the port. Can be noisy at night.
    • Eixample · ≈ EUR 120–300 a night
      Grid streets with Modernista buildings. Good metro links and calmer nights than the old town.
    • El Born · ≈ EUR 130–320 a night
      Bars, boutiques and the Picasso Museum near the park. Lively but central and walkable.
    • Gràcia · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      Village feel with small squares and local restaurants. Quieter, a short metro ride from the sights.

    Food & drink

    • Try Catalan dishes: pa amb tomàquet (bread with tomato), escalivada and crema catalana.
    • La Boqueria market off La Rambla is busy and pricey; locals often shop at Santa Caterina or El Ninot instead.
    • Lunch is the big meal, often a fixed menú del dia around €12–20. Dinner starts late, often after 21:00.
    • Tapas bars in El Born and Gràcia are cheaper and calmer than the Rambla strip. Stand at the bar to pay less.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead. Many close one day a week and some shut in August; check on the day.

    Tips

    1. Pickpockets are highly skilled and active, especially on Las Ramblas and the metro
    2. Book tickets for the Sagrada Família and Park Güell weeks in advance
    3. Locals eat late; lunch is around 2 PM and dinner rarely starts before 9 PM
    4. Buy a T-Casual ticket for 10 journeys on public transport at a significant discount
    5. Catalan is the primary local language, though Spanish and English are widely understood

    Souvenirs

    Espadrilles

    EUR 25–551 pair · estimate

    Handmade traditional canvas shoes with rope soles. They have been made in Barcelona for generations, so they are a classic local buy.

    Where: La Manual Alpargatera, Carrer d'Avinyó
    Try them on before you buy to check the fit.

    Caganer figurine

    EUR 15–301 small piece · estimate

    A small figure of a man squatting, a Catalan Christmas tradition. It is placed in nativity scenes and reflects local folk humor.

    Where: Caganer.com, official Barcelona shop
    Check the recipient's taste first, since the figure can be seen as cheeky.

    Turrón

    EUR 8–18150 to 300 g · estimate

    A traditional nougat made with almonds and honey. It is a Catalan Christmas sweet, and Vicens is a well-known local maker.

    Where: Torrons Vicens, near La Rambla
    Buy a small box first if you are unsure which flavor the recipient likes.
