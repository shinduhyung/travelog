Getting around

Metro and walking for the centre; trams for the scenic hills.

  • ✓Metro ★Fast and cheap. The red line links the airport to the centre with one change.
  • ✓BusCarris buses cover the city. Buy a rechargeable Navegante card for about €0.50.
  • ✓TramTrams 15 and 28 are scenic but crowded; watch for pickpockets.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis use meters; insist on the meter and keep a receipt. Uber, Bolt and FreeNow work; look for the TVDE sign.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the centre is slow and parking is hard. Skip it for city days.
  • ✓Walking ★Walking works well on the flat Baixa; expect steep hills in Alfama and Bairro Alto.
Recommended stay★ 2 nights (3 days)

Two nights cover Baixa, Alfama, the castle viewpoints, and Belém's monastery and museums across two sightseeing days, without needing Sintra.

Climate

Mild Atlantic climate: warm dry summers, cool wet winters, and rain mostly from October to March.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberWarm and mostly dry, highs 23–28°C. Ideal for walking, trams and the riverfront.
  • 🌧️Wettest: October to MarchAround 8–10 rain days a month. Showers pass; pack a compact umbrella.
  • 🥵July and August are hotHighs near 28°C and very dry. Sightsee early morning and rest in the afternoon.
  • 🌬️Atlantic breezeThe river and ocean keep evenings cooler; a light layer helps after sunset.
  • 🌂Rain is rarely all dayWinter rain comes in spells. Plan indoor sights like museums as backup.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable shoes for cobbled hills, sunscreen and a hat in summer, a rain jacket in winter.

Landmarks 31

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Belém Tower

    A 16th-century fortress on the Tagus that is Lisbon's most recognizable symbol, with fine Manueline carvings and river views. Go early in the morning to avoid queues, and buy tickets online in advance.

  2. 2

    Jerónimos Monastery

    A vast, ornate monastery and UNESCO site that showcases Portugal's Age of Discovery. It is a short walk from Belém Tower; book a timed entry and dress respectfully.

  3. 3

    São Jorge Castle

    A hilltop Moorish castle with sweeping views over the city and river. Visit in the late afternoon for cooler weather and sunset light; wear comfortable shoes for the climb.

  4. 4

    Praça do Comércio

    Lisbon's grand riverside square, framed by yellow arcades and the triumphal arch. It is free to wander; come at dusk when the square is beautifully lit.

  5. 5

    Padrão dos Descobrimentos

    A monument shaped like a ship's prow, celebrating Portugal's explorers. Combine it with Belém Tower and the monastery; the riverside promenade is ideal for a stroll.

  6. 6

    Santa Justa Lift

    An ornate iron elevator linking the Baixa to Chiado, with a viewing platform at the top. Ride early or late to skip long lines, or climb the nearby streets for free views.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Sintra: train from Rossio, about 40 minutes, to palaces and gardens. Go early, it gets crowded.
    • Take a half-day trip to Cascais or Belém by train along the river for beaches and the tower.
    • Spend an evening in a fado house in Alfama or Mouraria; book ahead and expect a cover charge.

    Getting around

    • Metro is fast and cheap; red line links the airport to the centre with one change.
    • Carris buses and trams cover the city; buy a rechargeable Navegante card for about €0.50.
    • Trams 15 and 28 are scenic but crowded; watch for pickpockets.
    • Taxis use meters; insist on the meter and keep a receipt.
    • Uber, Bolt and FreeNow work in Lisbon; look for the TVDE sign on the car.
    • Driving in the centre is slow and parking is hard; walking and bikes work well on flat Baixa.

    Where to stay

    • Baixa · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      Flat, central grid near the river, tram 15 and metro. Easiest base for first-timers and walking.
    • Alfama · ≈ EUR 80–200 a night
      Oldest quarter, steep lanes and fado bars. Charming but hilly and noisy at night.
    • Chiado · ≈ EUR 120–280 a night
      Upscale shopping and cafés, walkable to Baixa and Bairro Alto. Good mix of comfort and location.
    • Bairro Alto · ≈ EUR 85–200 a night
      Nightlife heart; quiet by day, loud on weekend nights. Close to Chiado and Príncipe Real.

    Food & drink

    • Try a pastel de nata warm with cinnamon; the famous Belém bakery queues are long, many cafés are just as good.
    • Eat grilled sardines and bacalhau (salt cod) in a simple tasca; lunch menus often cost €8–15.
    • Order a bifana (pork sandwich) or a prego for a cheap snack, about €3–6.
    • Seafood is best near the river and in Cascais; check the price per kilo before ordering.
    • Vegetarian and vegan options are growing in Chiado and Príncipe Real; check menus on the day.

    Tips

    1. Wear extremely comfortable shoes with good grip — the cobblestone streets (calçada) are steep and slippery
    2. Eat a fresh Pastel de Nata (custard tart) sprinkled with cinnamon, preferably in Belém
    3. Ride Tram 28 early in the morning or late at night to avoid massive tourist crowds
    4. Dinner is traditionally eaten late, usually starting around 8:30 PM or 9:00 PM
    5. Listen to live Fado music in the Alfama or Bairro Alto districts for an authentic cultural experience

      Souvenirs

      Portuguese canned sardines

      EUR 12–243 cans, 120g each · estimate

      Canned sardines are a classic Portuguese food, and this Lisbon shop has been around for generations. They make a practical take-home gift.

      Where: Conserveira de Lisboa, Rua dos Bacalhoeiros
      Choose by fish type and oil preference rather than by the label design.

      Azulejo ceramic tile

      EUR 12–301 newly made tile · estimate

      Azulejos are hand-painted glazed tiles that decorate buildings and homes across Portugal. They are a defining part of Lisbon's look.

      Where: Sant'Anna workshop or Cortiço & Netos
      Avoid antique tiles of unclear origin, since some may come from demolished buildings.

      Portuguese cork wallet

      EUR 20–501 piece · estimate

      Cork crafting is a Portuguese tradition, and the material is soft, light, and water-resistant. Lisbon shops sell many cork goods.

      Where: Cork & Co, Bairro Alto
      Check the lining and the glue finish at the edges before buying.
