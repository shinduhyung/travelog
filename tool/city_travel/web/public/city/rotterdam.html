Getting around

Metro and trams for most trips; walk or bike in the flat centre.

  • ✓Metro ★Lines A/B/C run east-west; D/E run north-south via Beurs and Centraal. Services every 10 minutes by day.
  • ✓BusRET buses cover the city. Buy tickets in advance; no sales on board.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsUber and Bolt work here; taxis wait at Centraal and main squares.
  • ✓Rental carPossible, but parking and narrow streets make it a poor choice for a city visit.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is flat and compact. Walk between Markthal, Oude Haven and the river.
  • ✓BikeThe city is flat and bike-friendly. Rent a bike for longer rides along the river.
  • ✓FerryWaterBus ferries reach Kinderdijk and Dordrecht. Check times and fares on the day.
  • ✓TrainCentraal station links the city to the rest of the Netherlands. Pay with OV-Pay or an OV-chipkaart (about €8.50).

Climate

Temperate maritime climate: mild summers, cool winters and rain in every month. Pack layers and a rain jacket year-round.

  • 🌧️Rain all yearExpect 11–15 rain days every month. Showers pass quickly; a light rain jacket beats an umbrella in wind.
  • 🌡️Mild summersJuly and August peak near 22–23°C. Rarely hot; evenings stay cool, so bring a layer.
  • ❄️Cool wintersDecember to February highs sit around 7–8°C with lows near 2°C. Wind off the river feels colder.
  • 🌬️Windy cityIt is flat and open, so wind is common. A windproof jacket helps on bridges and boat trips.
  • 🌷Best months: May to SeptemberWarmest and brightest. May, June and September are the sweet spots for walking and cycling.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket, comfortable shoes for cobbles, and sunglasses for bright summer days.

Landmarks 20

Must-see · 4

  1. 1

    Erasmus Bridge

    Rotterdam's modern icon, a striking white cable-stayed bridge over the Maas that has become the symbol of the rebuilt city. Walk or cycle across for free views of the skyline, and come at sunset or after dark when it is lit up.

  2. 2

    Cube Houses

    A cluster of tilted, cube-shaped homes balanced on pillars, one of the city's most photographed modern sights. You can pay a small fee to visit the Show Cube museum inside; it is a short walk from Blaak station.

  3. 3

    Markthal

    A huge arched market hall with a colourful ceiling mural, packed with food stalls, bars and shops. It is busiest at lunchtime, so go early morning or late afternoon for a calmer browse; it sits right beside the Cube Houses.

  4. 4

    Euromast

    A tall observation tower offering the best panoramic view over Rotterdam and its port. Book tickets online to skip queues and go on a clear day; you can also take the rotating elevator up for an extra charge.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for Kinderdijk windmills: WaterBus ferry from the centre, check times and fares on the day.
    • Take the WaterBus to Dordrecht for a half-day of old streets and harbour views.
    • Rainy day? Spend it indoors at the Depot Boijmans Van Beuningen or the Maritime Museum.

    Getting around

    • RET runs metro, trams and buses; services come every 10 minutes by day.
    • Pay contactless with a bank card (OV-Pay) or an OV-chipkaart (about €8.50).
    • Buy tickets in advance; no sales on board buses, trams or metro.
    • Metro lines A/B/C run east-west; D/E run north-south via Beurs and Centraal.
    • Uber and Bolt work here; taxis wait at Centraal and main squares.
    • The city is flat and bike-friendly; WaterBus ferries reach Kinderdijk and Dordrecht.

    Where to stay

    • Centrum · ≈ EUR 90–200 a night
      Walk to Markthal, the old harbour and Centraal station. Best base for a first visit.
    • Kop van Zuid · ≈ EUR 100–220 a night
      Modern waterfront by the Erasmus Bridge; quick metro to the centre and good river views.
    • Oude Haven · ≈ EUR 95–210 a night
      Old harbour bars and cafés on the doorstep; central and lively in the evening.
    • Blijdorp · ≈ EUR 80–170 a night
      Quiet and green next to the zoo and Centraal; good for families and longer stays.

    Food & drink

    • Try a Dutch lunch: broodje haring (herring roll), kibbeling (fried fish) or a cheese sandwich.
    • Markthal: many stalls under one roof. Good for a cheap, quick meal; prices vary, check on the day.
    • Eat Indonesian rice tables (rijsttafel) or Surinamese roti; both are strong local food traditions.
    • Bars and cafés cluster around Oude Haven and Witte de Withstraat. Book dinner on weekends.
    • Supermarkets and takeaway shops keep everyday food costs down; a sit-down dinner costs more.

    Tips

    1. Visit the Markthal for incredible food and striking architecture
    2. Use an OV-chipkaart or contactless payment for all public transit
    3. Architecture lovers must see the Cube Houses (Kijk-Kubus) and the Erasmus Bridge
    4. Water taxis are thrilling and often faster than crossing the bridges by car
    5. Do not walk in the red bicycle lanes
