Getting around

Ride apps or the bus for most trips; walk downtown and around UT.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. A single commuter rail line (Red Line) runs north-south.
  • ✓Bus ★Capital Metro buses cover downtown, the Capitol and South Congress. Fares about $1.25 one way.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and Lyft work well. Taxis exist but are less common.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is common but traffic is heavy at rush hour; I-35 is often jammed.
  • ✓WalkingDowntown and UT are walkable. Summers are very hot, so plan shade and water.
  • ✓BikeBikes are popular and bike lanes exist on most major streets.

Climate

Subtropical: long hot summers, mild winters, and rain spread through the year.

  • ☀️Best time: March to May and October to NovemberMild to warm days around 21–29°C. Ideal for walking, parks and patios.
  • 🥵Summer is very hot (June to September)Highs of 32–36°C. Go outside early or after sunset and carry water.
  • 🌧️Rain any monthAbout 8–16 rain days a month, most in May. Showers are often short.
  • 🌡️Warm nights in summerLows stay near 22–25°C from June to September, so evenings stay hot.
  • 🧥Cool winter nightsDecember to February lows near 6–8°C. Bring a jacket for evenings.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes and sunscreen for summer; a light rain layer; a warm layer for winter nights.

Landmarks 8

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Texas State Capitol

    The seat of Texas government and the city's most recognizable historic building, with a dome taller than the US Capitol's. Free self-guided tours run daily; go early morning or late afternoon to avoid the heat and crowds, and note that the building is a short walk north of downtown.

  2. 2

    Lady Bird Lake

    A calm river-like lake in the heart of the city, lined with a popular hike-and-bike trail and skyline views. Rent a kayak or walk the trail at sunrise or sunset for the best light and cooler temperatures.

  3. 3

    Barton Springs Pool

    A spring-fed natural pool that stays cool year-round and is a beloved Austin ritual. Bring cash or check the city site for entry fees, arrive early on summer weekends, and expect a short walk from nearby parking.

  4. 4

    Zilker Park

    Austin's central green space, next to Barton Springs and Lady Bird Lake, great for picnics and skyline views. Visit on a weekday or early morning; it is easy to reach by bike or rideshare and gets busy during events.

  5. 5

    Bullock Texas State History Museum

    A well-run museum telling the story of Texas from early settlement to the modern era. Allow two to three hours, check ticket prices online, and go on a weekday for a quieter visit; it sits near the Capitol.

  6. 6

    University of Texas at Austin

    A large, lively campus that anchors the city's culture and is worth a stroll for its architecture and museums. Walk the main mall in the morning, and check whether visitor parking or guided campus tours are available in advance.

  7. 7

    COTALAND

    A motorsport-themed amusement park outside central Austin, best for families and thrill-seekers. Buy tickets online, check opening days before you go, and plan for a car or rideshare since it is not walkable from downtown.

    • Add a day trip to the Hill Country: small towns and swimming holes west of the city. A car helps.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; a single commuter rail line (Red Line) runs north-south.
    • Capital Metro buses cover downtown, the Capitol, and South Congress; fares are about $1.25 one way.
    • Ride apps Uber and Lyft work well; taxis are available but less common.
    • Driving is common but traffic is heavy at rush hour; I-35 is often jammed.
    • Downtown and UT are walkable; summers are very hot.
    • Bikes are popular; bike lanes exist on most major streets.

    • Try Texas BBQ brisket and ribs. Popular spots have long lines; go early or expect a wait.
    • Breakfast tacos are a local staple. Cheap, filling and sold at many taquerias and trucks.
    • Food trucks cluster on South Congress and in East Austin. Cash is handy at some.
    • Book ahead for dinner on weekends, especially downtown and East Austin.
    • Tap water is safe to drink. Many places refill bottles; summers are hot, so carry water.

    Tips

    1. Watch the massive Mexican free-tailed bat colony emerge from the Congress Avenue Bridge at dusk in summer
    2. Eat authentic Texas BBQ, but be prepared to wait in long lines for the most famous spots
    3. The 'Keep Austin Weird' slogan reflects the city's defense of local businesses against corporatization
    4. Beat the extreme summer heat by swimming in the natural, spring-fed Barton Springs Pool
    5. Traffic is notoriously awful, so plan extra time if driving across the city
