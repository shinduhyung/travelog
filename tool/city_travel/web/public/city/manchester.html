Getting around

Walk the compact centre; use the Metrolink tram for anything further.

  • ✓Metro ★Metrolink tram is quick and covers the centre and suburbs. Zone 1 is free with some train tickets.
  • ✓BusBee Network buses are yellow and franchised; single fares are around £2.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered. Uber, Bolt and local apps all work in the city.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the centre is slow and parking is costly. Use park and ride instead.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is compact and walkable. Starling Bikes offers bike share with e-bikes.
  • ✗FerryNo ferry in the city. The nearest cruise terminal is Liverpool.

Climate

Temperate and often wet: mild summers, cool winters, and rain possible in any month.

  • 🌧️Rain all yearExpect 12–19 rain days every month. A waterproof jacket beats an umbrella in the wind.
  • 🌡️Mild summersJuly and August peak near 20°C with lows around 12°C. Rarely hot.
  • 🧥Cool wintersJanuary and February highs near 7–8°C and lows around 2°C. Bring a warm coat.
  • 🌤️Best odds: April to JuneApril has the fewest rain days and May–June are mild and bright.
  • ☂️Wettest: July and DecemberJuly has about 19 rain days and December about 18. Plan indoor backups.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof shell and comfortable shoes for wet pavements.

Landmarks 11

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Old Trafford

    The home of Manchester United and one of the most famous football stadiums in the world, a pilgrimage for fans. Take a stadium tour to see the dressing rooms and tunnel; book tickets in advance, especially on match weekends.

  2. 2

    Science and Industry Museum

    Housed in the world's oldest surviving passenger railway station, it tells the story of Manchester's industrial revolution. Entry is free; go on a weekday morning to avoid school groups.

  3. 3

    Manchester Art Gallery

    A strong collection of fine art and decorative arts in a grand Victorian building. Free entry; combine with a stroll through the nearby Chinatown or Piccadilly Gardens.

  4. 4

    John Rylands Library

    A stunning neo-Gothic library that looks like a cathedral for books, home to rare manuscripts. Free to enter; it's a quiet spot, so visit early to avoid crowds.

  5. 5

    Chetham's Library

    The oldest public library in the English-speaking world, dating from 1653, and a hidden gem. Entry is by guided tour only; check times and book ahead.

  6. 6

    Manchester Cathedral

    A beautiful medieval church with a striking modern extension, right in the city centre. Free to enter; open daily, but check for service times if you want to look around quietly.

  7. 7

    Castlefield

    A historic area with Roman ruins and canal-side walks, now a trendy spot with bars and restaurants. Best explored on foot; start at the Roman gardens and follow the canal.

  8. 8

    Etihad Stadium

    Home of Manchester City, a modern football stadium with tours available. Take the tram to Etihad Campus; tours run most days but check availability online.

  9. 9

    Royal Exchange Theatre

    A unique theatre-in-the-round inside the historic Royal Exchange building. Catch a show if you can; otherwise, the building is worth a look for its architecture.

  10. 10

    University of Manchester

    A leading university with a notable campus and museums like the Manchester Museum. Wander the campus and visit the free Manchester Museum; it's a short bus ride from the centre.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for a tram or train trip out to a nearby town or the countryside; check times on the day.
    • Football fans can add a stadium tour; book ahead as slots sell out.
    • Add a live music night at a smaller venue instead of a second museum.

    Getting around

    • Metrolink tram is quick and covers the centre and suburbs; Zone 1 is free with some train tickets.
    • Bee Network buses are yellow and franchised; single fares are around £2.
    • Taxis are metered; Uber, Bolt and local apps all work in the city.
    • Driving in the centre is slow and parking is costly; use park and ride instead.
    • The centre is compact and walkable; Starling Bikes offers bike share with e-bikes.
    • No ferry in the city; the nearest cruise terminal is Liverpool.

    Where to stay

    • Piccadilly · ≈ GBP 70–160 a night
      Next to the main train station and tram stops; easiest base for a short first visit.
    • Northern Quarter · ≈ GBP 80–180 a night
      Independent bars, cafés and record shops on your doorstep; walkable to the centre.
    • Deansgate · ≈ GBP 90–200 a night
      Central and close to the canal, museums and big shopping streets.
    • Ancoats · ≈ GBP 75–170 a night
      Quiet former mill district with good food and drink; about 15 minutes' walk to Piccadilly.

    Food & drink

    • Try a proper Northern Sunday roast or a pie in a traditional pub; prices vary, so check the menu.
    • Curry Mile on Wilmslow Road is the classic spot for South Asian food; many places are cheap and casual.
    • The Northern Quarter has the densest cluster of independent cafés and brunch spots.
    • Look for food halls and markets for a mix of stalls at mid-range prices; check opening days on the day.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead for Friday and Saturday nights, especially in Ancoats and the Northern Quarter.

    Tips

    1. Always carry an umbrella or a rain jacket; the weather is famously wet and unpredictable
    2. Football fans must tour Old Trafford (United) or the Etihad Stadium (City)
    3. Explore the Northern Quarter for independent record stores, vintage shops, and street art
    4. Use the contactless Metrolink trams to easily travel to MediaCityUK or the suburbs
    5. Locals (Mancunians) are known for their approachability, dry humor, and fierce civic pride
