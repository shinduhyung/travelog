Getting around

Grab or taxi for most trips; city buses if you are saving money and have time.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Do not plan around rail in the city.
  • ✓Bus ★19 new, air-conditioned local bus lines with a flat 8,000 ₫ fare inside city limits. Use the DanaBus app or danangbus.vn for routes. Avoid the look-alike danabus.vn site, which has outdated prices.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Mai Linh, Vinasun and Song Han are reliable taxi firms. Grab is widely used and shows prices upfront.
  • ✓Motorbike taxi (xe ôm)Cheap for short hops. Helmets are required by law, so insist the driver gives you one.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is chaotic, so many visitors hire a car with a driver instead of driving themselves.
  • ✓Scooter or bike rentalRent a scooter or 4-speed bike near Tran Cao Van. Electric bicycles need no licence and suit city travel.
  • ✓WalkingWorks well along the beach and in central streets.
  • ✗FerryNo ferry serves the city.

Climate

Tropical coastal climate: hot, humid and sunny from spring to summer, with a wetter season from autumn to early winter.

  • ☀️Best time: March to AugustMostly dry days at 24–34°C with fewer rain days. Good for beaches, sea swims and motorbike trips.
  • 🌧️Wet season: September to DecemberRain falls on 22–26 days a month. Showers come and go, so plan indoor sights and keep rain gear in your bag.
  • 🥵Hot afternoons (May to August)Highs reach 32–34°C. Do outdoor sights before 11:00 and rest in the afternoon.
  • 💧Humidity year-roundAir feels heavy, especially near the sea. Carry water and wear light, breathable clothes.
  • 🌊Sea conditions changeWaves can be rough in the wet months. Check local warnings and follow lifeguard flags at the beach.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, a hat, strong sunscreen, a compact rain jacket and a light layer for air-conditioned rooms. Bring modest clothes for temples.

Landmarks 18

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Ba Na Hills Cable Car

    Asia's longest cable car climbs to the Ba Na Hills, a mountain resort with French-inspired architecture and themed gardens. The ride itself offers wide views over the forest and coast. Book tickets in advance and go early in the day to avoid queues at the top.

  2. 2

    Golden Bridge

    A pedestrian bridge held up by giant stone hands, perched on the Ba Na Hills, and one of the most photographed sights in Vietnam. It is best seen early morning, before the crowds and haze build up. Wear comfortable shoes, as the walk includes steps and uneven stone.

  3. 3

    Sun World Ba Na Hills

    The amusement park at the top of the hills, known as Fantasy Park, offers many rides, attractions and themed areas. It is the main reason many visitors stay for a full day. Combine it with the cable car ticket to save time and money.

  4. 4

    Marble Mountains

    A cluster of marble hills with caves, tunnels and Buddhist shrines, with climbs offering views over the coast. It is a short trip from the city centre. Wear sturdy shoes and cover your shoulders when entering temples.

  5. 5

    Lady Buddha

    A huge white statue of the Goddess of Mercy on the Son Tra Peninsula, overlooking the sea and the city. It is one of the most recognisable landmarks in Da Nang. Visit in the morning or late afternoon for softer light and fewer visitors, and dress modestly.

  6. 6

    My Khe Beach

    A long sandy beach with calm water, often cited as one of the best in Vietnam. It is ideal for a relaxed swim or sunset walk. Go in the early morning or late afternoon, as the midday sun is strong.

  7. 7

    Dragon Bridge, Da Nang

    A steel bridge shaped like a dragon that spans the Han River, famous for fire and water displays on weekend nights. It is a lively spot in the evening. Arrive before dark to find a viewing spot along the riverbank.

  8. 8

    Hai Van Pass

    A winding mountain road with dramatic views over the coastline, linking Da Nang with Hue. It is best experienced by motorbike, car or the scenic train. Go on a clear day for the best views, as cloud often covers the summit.

    Swap in instead

    • Ba Na Hills: a mountain resort with a long cable car ride. Check prices and opening times on the day.
    • Hoi An: an old trading town about 45 minutes away by car or taxi, depending on traffic. Good for a half-day or evening.
    • Marble Mountains: stone caves and small temples close to the beach. Easy to add to a half-day, with a taxi or bike.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram. The city has 19 local bus lines, all new and air-conditioned, with a flat 8,000 ₫ fare inside city limits.
    • Use the DanaBus app or danangbus.vn for routes. Avoid the look-alike danabus.vn site, which has outdated prices.
    • Taxis are reasonable. Mai Linh, Vinasun and Song Han are reliable. Grab is widely used and shows prices upfront.
    • Motorbike taxis (xe ôm) are cheap for short hops. Helmets are required by law, so insist the driver provides one.
    • Rent a scooter or 4-speed bike near Tran Cao Van. Driving is chaotic, so many visitors hire a car with a driver.
    • Electric bicycles need no licence and suit city travel. Walking works well along the beach and in central streets. No ferry serves the city.

    Where to stay

    • My Khe Beach · ≈ VND 1,000,000–3,500,000 a night
      Closest to the sand and the seafront walk. Many resorts and mid-range hotels. Good base for beach days.
    • Hai Chau (District 1) · ≈ VND 600,000–2,500,000 a night
      The city centre, near the Dragon Bridge, Han Market and most restaurants. Easy bus and taxi links.
    • Son Tra Peninsula · ≈ VND 1,500,000–6,000,000 a night
      Quiet, green and near the sea. Few shops nearby, so plan on taxis or Grab for dinner. Best for resorts.
    • Tran Cao Van (Thanh Khe) · ≈ VND 300,000–1,200,000 a night
      Budget guesthouses and cafés. Popular with backpackers and the place to rent a scooter or bike.

    Food & drink

    • Try mì Quảng, the local turmeric noodle dish with a little broth, and bánh xèo, the crispy pancake.
    • Han Market is the easiest place to sample fresh seafood and local snacks in one visit.
    • Eat at busy local stalls. Pick the places with a queue of locals and plastic stools.
    • Seafood restaurants along the beach road are good for dinner. Check prices on the menu before ordering.
    • Ask for water and ice carefully. Stick to bottled water if your stomach is sensitive.
