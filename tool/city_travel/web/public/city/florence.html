Getting around

Walk the centre; use the tram from the airport and Park & Ride sites, and buses for longer trips.

  • ✗MetroFlorence has no metro. Use trams, buses or walking inside the city.
  • ✓Tram ★Tram T2 links the airport to Santa Maria Novella, about 20 minutes, running until about 00:30 on weekdays and 02:00 on weekends. Tram 1 runs from Villa Constanza Park & Ride through the western suburbs to Santa Maria Novella, about 25 minutes, every 3 to 10 minutes.
  • ✓BusAutolineeToscane buses serve the city. Tap a contactless Visa, Mastercard or Amex card, or buy a €1.70 paper ticket before boarding. A 90-minute hop-on, hop-off window applies.
  • ✓Taxi / Uber / FreeNowTaxis have few stands and are hard to hail on the street. Book by phone or use the stand at Santa Maria Novella, and carry cash. Check locally whether Uber or FreeNow is available.
  • ✓Rental carDriving inside the historic centre is restricted by camera and fines are high. Park at Park & Ride sites and walk or take the bus in.
  • ✓Walking ★Most of the centre is flat and walkable in about 30 minutes end to end. Shared city bikes are available, and cycle paths help you bypass the old streets.

Climate

Mild Mediterranean-style climate: warm summers, cool winters and rain on about 10 days a month most of the year.

  • 🌤️Best time: April to June and September to OctoberHighs around 19–28°C with fewer extremes. Good for walking the centre and sitting outdoors.
  • 🔥Hot summers (July and August)Highs near 32°C and lows near 19°C. Visit galleries in the morning and walk in the shade.
  • 🧥Cool winters (December to February)Highs around 11–13°C and lows near 2–3°C. Pack a warm coat for evenings.
  • 🌧️Rain on about 10 days a monthRain is spread through the year, with fewer wet days in July and August. Carry a small umbrella or jacket.
  • 🚶Walk early or lateMost sights are close together. Start before 9:00 in summer to beat heat and crowds.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable walking shoes, layers for cool evenings, sun hat in summer and modest clothing for churches.

Landmarks 18

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Florence Cathedral

    Santa Maria del Fiore is the city's defining landmark, crowned by Brunelleschi's red-tiled dome. Climbing the dome gives close views of the painted interior and the rooftops below. Dress with shoulders and knees covered, and book a dome-climb time slot in advance, since entry is timed and limited.

  2. 2

    Uffizi Gallery

    One of the world's great art museums, it holds Botticelli's The Birth of Venus and Primavera along with major Renaissance paintings. The collection is large, so plan two to three hours. Book a timed entry ticket online, as same-day queues can be long.

  3. 3

    Galleria dell'Accademia

    This is the home of Michelangelo's David, the marble statue that draws most visitors to Florence. Seeing it up close is a highlight of any trip. Reserve a timed-entry ticket ahead, because the museum often sells out.

  4. 4

    Ponte Vecchio

    This medieval stone bridge over the Arno River is lined with jewelry shops and is one of the most photographed spots in Italy. It is best seen at sunrise or in the evening, when the crowds thin. It is an easy walk from the Uffizi and the Pitti Palace.

  5. 5

    Piazza della Signoria

    This open square is the civic heart of Florence, with sculptures and the grand facade of the town hall around it. It is a good place to start a walking tour of the historic centre. Visit early in the morning for fewer people.

  6. 6

    Palazzo Vecchio

    The city's medieval town hall dominates Piazza della Signoria with its tall tower and has served as the seat of Florentine government for centuries. Its interior rooms are rich with Renaissance decoration. Buy a ticket on site or online, and allow about an hour.

  7. 7

    Piazzale Michelangelo

    This hilltop square on the south bank of the Arno offers the classic panoramic view over Florence and its cathedral dome. It is especially popular at sunset. You can walk up from the river or take a bus, then descend on foot.

    Getting around

    • Tram T2 links the airport to Santa Maria Novella station and runs until about 00:30 on weekdays and 02:00 on weekends; the route takes about 20 minutes.
    • Tram 1 runs from Villa Constanza Park & Ride through the western suburbs to Santa Maria Novella, about 25 minutes, every 3 to 10 minutes.
    • AutolineeToscane buses serve the city. Tap a contactless Visa, Mastercard or Amex card, or buy a €1.70 paper ticket before boarding; a 90-minute hop-on, hop-off window applies.
    • Taxis have few stands and are hard to hail on the street. Book by phone or use the stand at Santa Maria Novella, and carry cash. Check locally whether Uber or FreeNow is available.
    • Driving inside the historic centre is restricted by camera and fines are high. Park at Park & Ride sites and walk or take the bus in.
    • Most of the centre is flat and walkable in about 30 minutes end to end. Shared city bikes are available, and cycle paths help you bypass the old streets.

    Where to stay

    • Santa Maria Novella · ≈ EUR 120–300 a night
      Near the main station, the Duomo area and the tram links to the airport and Park & Ride sites.
    • San Lorenzo · ≈ EUR 130–320 a night
      Close to the Medici Chapels and the central market; busy streets with many restaurants.
    • Santa Croce · ≈ EUR 120–300 a night
      Lively piazza, leather shops and easy walks to the Duomo and the Arno; good for food.
    • Oltrarno · ≈ EUR 110–280 a night
      Quieter artisan streets across the Arno, with Palazzo Pitti and Santo Spirito square nearby.

    Food & drink

    • Eat at a trattoria with a handwritten menu; Florentine dishes like ribollita and bistecca are best shared.
    • Visit the Mercato Centrale for a casual lunch; the San Lorenzo market stalls are good for snacks.
    • Try a schiacciata sandwich or a gelato from a shop with covered tubs and no piled-high colours.
    • Book dinner ahead on weekends and in high season; many places close one day a week, so check first.
    • Aperitivo is common in the late afternoon; order a spritz and small snacks at a bar.
    • Tap water is safe to drink; ask for a bottle of local Chianti at a wine bar for a fair price.
