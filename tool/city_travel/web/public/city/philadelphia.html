Getting around

Walk in Center City and use SEPTA for longer hops; ride apps for late nights.

  • ✓MetroSEPTA runs the subway and trolleys; fares are about $2–2.50 per ride. Pay with a SEPTA Key card or contactless.
  • ✓BusBuses cover the whole city. SEPTA Key card or contactless payment works.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered. Uber and Lyft both operate widely.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is possible but parking is costly and traffic is heavy in Center City.
  • ✓Walking ★Easy in Center City; most sights are within 30 minutes on foot.
  • ✓BikeBikes are popular; the Schuylkill River Trail is a favorite.
  • ✓FerryThe RiverLink ferry runs to Camden in summer.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cold winters, warm humid summers, and the best weather in spring and fall.

  • 🌸Best time: April to June and September to OctoberHighs around 17–26°C with moderate rain. Ideal for walking Old City and the parks.
  • 🥵Summer is hot and humid (July–August)Highs near 30–31°C. Plan indoor sights at midday and carry water.
  • 🧥Winter is cold (December–February)Highs around 5–8°C and lows near or below freezing. Bring a warm coat, hat and gloves.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 8–14 rain days a month, most in July. A small umbrella is worth packing any month.
  • 🍂Fall is crisp and clearOctober and November are cooler with fewer rain days — good for long walks.
  • 🧳What to packLayers and comfortable walking shoes. Add a rain jacket, and a heavy coat for winter visits.

Landmarks 20

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Independence Hall

    The birthplace of the United States, where the Declaration of Independence and Constitution were debated and signed. It is the essential first stop for any visitor. Tickets are free but timed, so reserve online in advance and arrive at the security line early.

  2. 2

    Liberty Bell

    The iconic cracked bell and symbol of American freedom sits in a free exhibit right across from Independence Hall. Entry is free and lines move quickly; go early morning to avoid crowds.

  3. 3

    Philadelphia Museum of Art

    A world-class collection topped by the famous Rocky Steps and statue outside. Allow several hours for the galleries. Book tickets online and wear comfortable shoes for the climb and the large building.

  4. 4

    Reading Terminal Market

    A historic indoor market packed with local food stalls, from cheesesteaks to Amish baked goods. It is the best single place to eat like a local. Come hungry on a weekday to avoid weekend crowds.

  5. 5

    Eastern State Penitentiary

    A hauntingly preserved 19th-century prison that shaped modern incarceration, with Al Capone's cell on view. The audio tour is excellent. It is a short walk from the art museum; book ahead in autumn for the seasonal events.

  6. 6

    Elfreth's Alley

    The oldest continuously inhabited residential street in the country, lined with brick colonial homes. It is a quick, free photo stop in Old City. Visit on a weekday since residents still live here.

  7. 7

    National Constitution Center

    An interactive museum devoted to the US Constitution, with a dramatic theater presentation. It complements Independence Hall nearby. Check opening hours and combine it with the Old City sights.

  8. 8

    Philadelphia Zoo

    America's first zoo, with a strong collection and family-friendly layout. It is a good half-day outing. Buy tickets online and go on a weekday morning for cooler, quieter visits.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for the Philadelphia Museum of Art and the Rocky Steps, then walk the Schuylkill River Trail.
    • Eastern State Penitentiary and the Barnes Foundation make a good half-day in Fairmount.
    • In summer, take the RiverLink ferry to Camden for the waterfront and aquarium.

    Getting around

    • SEPTA runs the subway, trolleys, and Regional Rail; fares are about $2-2.50 per ride.
    • Buses cover the whole city; SEPTA Key card or contactless payment works.
    • Taxis are metered; Uber and Lyft both operate widely.
    • Driving is possible but parking is costly and traffic is heavy in Center City.
    • Walking is easy in Center City; most sights are within 30 minutes on foot.
    • Bikes are popular; the Schuylkill River Trail is a favorite. RiverLink ferry runs to Camden in summer.

    Where to stay

    • Center City · ≈ USD 150–350 a night
      Most central. Walk to City Hall, Reading Terminal Market and the subway; short walk or ride to Old City.
    • Old City · ≈ USD 140–320 a night
      Steps from Independence Hall, the Liberty Bell and Elfreth's Alley. Quieter at night.
    • Rittenhouse Square · ≈ USD 180–420 a night
      Leafy, upscale and full of restaurants and shops. Good base for food and shopping.
    • University City · ≈ USD 120–280 a night
      Near Penn, Drexel and the museums along the Schuylkill. Cheaper rooms, short ride to Center City.

    Food & drink

    • Order a cheesesteak at a classic stand; ask for 'wit' or 'witout' onions. Expect about $10–15.
    • Reading Terminal Market: one big hall of local food stalls. Go hungry and early; it is busy at lunch.
    • Try a soft pretzel and water ice (Italian ice) — two cheap Philadelphia classics.
    • Many small restaurants are BYOB; bring your own wine and save on the bill.
    • Book ahead for popular dinner spots on weekends. Prices range from cheap eats to fine dining.

    Tips

    1. Eat a classic Philly cheesesteak, but skip the tourist traps and ask a local for their favorite neighborhood spot
    2. Center City is highly walkable, making it easy to stroll from historical sites to modern dining
    3. SEPTA is convenient, but stay aware of your surroundings, particularly on the subway late at night
    4. Run up the iconic steps of the Philadelphia Museum of Art, famously featured in the movie 'Rocky'
    5. Visit Independence Hall and the Liberty Bell, but book tickets well in advance during summer
