Getting around

The MRT Line 6 metro for cross-city trips; ride apps or CNG auto-rickshaws for the rest.

  • ✓Metro ★MRT Line 6 runs Uttara to Motijheel. Cheap and fast; a good way to skip traffic.
  • ✓BusCity buses are crowded, noisy and signed only in Bengali. Hard for first-time visitors.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and Pathao work in Dhaka. CNG auto-rickshaws are cheapest for longer rides; meters are rarely used, so agree the fare.
  • ✗Rental carDriving yourself is not recommended; traffic is chaotic. Hire a car with a driver instead.
  • ✓WalkingOften fastest for short hops; watch pollution and traffic.
  • ✓RickshawCycle rickshaws are quick for short hops. Agree the fare first.
  • ✓FerryFerries and rocket steamers leave from Sadarghat river port.

Climate

Tropical monsoon climate: a cool dry winter, a hot sticky spring, and a long, very wet summer.

  • ☀️Best time: November to FebruaryDry, mild days around 24–29°C and cooler evenings. Ideal for walking and the old city.
  • 🥵Hot season: March to MayHighs reach 33°C with rising humidity. Go out early and rest indoors at midday.
  • 🌧️Monsoon: June to SeptemberRain on 25–28 days a month. Expect flooded streets and traffic delays; carry an umbrella.
  • 💧Humid most of the yearLows stay near 24–26°C in the wet months. Light, breathable clothes help.
  • 🌂Pack for rain in summerA compact umbrella and quick-dry shoes are useful from May to October.
  • 🧳What to packLight cotton clothes, a hat and sunscreen; a light layer for December–January evenings; modest dress for mosques.

Landmarks 18

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    Jatiyo Sangsad Bhaban

    Louis Kahn's monumental parliament complex is Dhaka's defining modern landmark and one of the most striking buildings in South Asia. It is a working government site, so access to the interior is limited; view it from the surrounding grounds and check locally whether visitor entry is open before you go.

  2. 2

    Lalbagh Fort

    A 17th-century Mughal fort complex with gardens, a mosque and the tomb of Pari Bibi, and the most atmospheric historic site in the old city. Go in the cooler morning hours and dress modestly, as it is a heritage and religious-adjacent site.

  3. 3

    Ahsan Manzil

    The pink riverside palace of the Nawabs is Dhaka's most photographed heritage building, now a museum of period rooms. It sits near Sadarghat, so combine it with a walk along the Buriganga; mornings are cooler and less crowded.

  4. 4

    Shaheed Minar

    The iconic monument to the language movement martyrs and a symbol of Bengali identity, central to any understanding of the city. It is an open public memorial, free to visit; go early morning or evening and dress respectfully.

  5. 5

    Dhakeshwari Temple

    Dhaka's principal Hindu temple and an important site of worship in the old city. Remove shoes before entering, dress modestly, and avoid photographing worshippers without permission.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a morning at Ahsan Manzil and the Sadarghat riverfront, then a short boat ride on the Buriganga.
    • Day trip to Sonargaon folk-art museum and Panam City, roughly 1–2 hours by road; check traffic before you go.
    • Spend an afternoon at the Liberation War Museum and the National Museum for the 1971 history.

    Getting around

    • MRT Line 6 metro runs Uttara to Motijheel; cheap and fast.
    • City buses are crowded, noisy and signed only in Bengali.
    • Cycle rickshaws are quick for short hops; agree the fare first.
    • CNG auto-rickshaws are cheapest for longer rides; meters rarely used.
    • Uber and Pathao ride apps work in Dhaka.
    • Driving yourself is not recommended; traffic is chaotic.
    • Walking is often fastest; watch pollution and traffic.
    • Ferries and rocket steamers leave from Sadarghat river port.

    Where to stay

    • Gulshan · ≈ BDT 6,000–18,000 a night
      Quiet, green and full of good restaurants and cafés. Easy for ride apps and the airport road.
    • Banani · ≈ BDT 5,000–15,000 a night
      Next to Gulshan, with malls, bakeries and many mid-range hotels. Calmer than the old city.
    • Motijheel · ≈ BDT 2,500–8,000 a night
      The business heart and a stop on the MRT Line 6 metro. Good value and central for the new city.
    • Old Dhaka · ≈ BDT 2,000–7,000 a night
      Steps from Lalbagh Fort, Ahsan Manzil and the food streets. Lively and loud; basic rooms for less.

    Food & drink

    • Start with kacchi biryani, Dhaka's signature rice dish. Old Dhaka spots are famous; go early for lunch.
    • Try kebabs and borhani, a salty yogurt drink that cools the spice. Cheap and filling.
    • Street sweets: roshogolla, sandesh and mishti doi. Buy from busy shops with high turnover.
    • Old Dhaka food walks along Nazira Bazar and Chawkbazar are best in the evening; go with a local guide.
    • Drink only bottled or filtered water. Skip ice and raw salads at street stalls if your stomach is new to the city.

    Tips

    1. Dhaka is the rickshaw capital of the world; taking a ride is a chaotic but essential experience
    2. Traffic gridlock is extreme, so plan ample travel time and use the Metro Rail whenever possible
    3. Drink only sealed bottled water and be cautious with street food to avoid stomach illnesses
    4. Dress modestly to respect local cultural and religious norms
    5. Visit Ahsan Manzil (The Pink Palace) and Lalbagh Fort for a glimpse of Mughal and colonial history
