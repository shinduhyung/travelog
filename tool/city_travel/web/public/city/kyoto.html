Climate

Four clear seasons: cold, dry winters, a hot humid summer and a rainy spell in June–July. Spring and autumn are the best times to walk.

  • 🌸Best time: spring and autumnApril and November bring mild days around 16–22°C and the famous blossoms and leaves. Book early.
  • 🥵Hot, humid summerJuly and August reach 30–32°C with many rain days. Sightsee at sunrise and rest in the afternoon.
  • 🌧️Rainy season in June–JulyAround 15–18 rain days. Showers are often short; a small umbrella is enough.
  • ❄️Cold, dry winterJanuary lows near -1°C. Days are clear and quiet; temples are far less crowded.
  • 🍂Autumn is the busiestNovember has only about 7 rain days but huge crowds at maple spots. Go early morning.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable walking shoes, a light rain jacket, and layers for cool mornings. Add a warm coat for December–February.

Landmarks 30

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Fushimi Inari Taisha

    Thousands of vermilion torii gates wind up the mountain, making this Japan's most photographed shrine. It is free and open 24 hours; go before 8am or after 5pm to avoid the crowds.

  2. 2

    Kinkaku-ji

    The gold-leaf-covered pavilion reflected in a mirror pond is Kyoto's postcard image. Open 9am-5pm; arrive at opening time for the calmest visit.

  3. 3

    Kiyomizu-dera

    A vast wooden stage juts over the hillside, offering sweeping views of the city. The temple is open from 6am, so early morning is the quietest; it's a 20-minute walk uphill from the bus stop.

  4. 4

    Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

    Walking through towering green bamboo is a quintessential Kyoto experience. It is free and always open; visit before 8am to enjoy the path without crowds.

  5. 5

    Gion District

    Kyoto's historic geisha quarter is lined with wooden machiya houses and teahouses. Wander the side streets in the evening for the best atmosphere, and remember that private lanes are off-limits to photography.

  6. 6

    Nijo Castle

    A UNESCO-listed castle famous for its 'nightingale' floors that squeak underfoot. Open 8:45am-5pm (closed some Tuesdays in winter); allow two hours to explore the palace and gardens.

  7. 7

    Ginkaku-ji

    The Silver Pavilion, set in a tranquil moss garden, is a masterpiece of Zen aesthetics. Open 8:30am-5pm; combine with a walk on the nearby Philosopher's Path.

  8. 8

    Ryoan-ji

    Japan's most famous dry landscape garden, with 15 rocks arranged on raked white gravel. Open 8am-5pm; sit on the veranda and try to see all 15 rocks at once.

  9. 9

    Sanjusangendo

    A long hall housing 1,001 gilded statues of Kannon, the goddess of mercy. Open 8:30am-5pm; photography is not allowed inside, so take your time to absorb the sight.

  10. 10

    Tenryu-ji

    A major Zen temple with a stunning pond garden backed by the Arashiyama hills. Open 8:30am-5pm; the garden is especially beautiful in autumn.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a temple morning for a day trip to Nara: about 45 minutes by train, with deer park and the Great Buddha.
    • Add Uji for green tea and the Byodo-in temple, roughly 30 minutes by train from Kyoto Station.
    • Fushimi Inari's full hill walk takes 2–3 hours. Go early morning to beat the crowds.

    Getting around

    • Two subway lines (Karasuma and Tozai) cover central Kyoto; fares ¥220-330.
    • City buses reach most sights; flat fare ¥230, day pass ¥700.
    • Taxis are metered; ride-hailing apps like GO and Uber operate.
    • Driving is not recommended; parking is scarce and expensive.
    • Walking and cycling are ideal for temples and backstreets; bike rentals from ¥1000/day.
    • No ferry service in Kyoto.

    Where to stay

    • Kyoto Station · ≈ JPY 8,000–25,000 a night
      Best base for first-timers: bullet trains, buses and the subway all start here. Easy day trips to Nara and Osaka.
    • Downtown (Shijo-Kawaramachi) · ≈ JPY 9,000–30,000 a night
      Central shopping and dining. Walk to Gion, Pontocho and Nishiki Market; good bus links.
    • Gion · ≈ JPY 12,000–45,000 a night
      Old wooden streets and lantern-lit lanes. Quiet at night, close to Kiyomizu-dera and Yasaka Shrine.
    • Arashiyama · ≈ JPY 10,000–35,000 a night
      Bamboo grove and river views on your doorstep. Calm and green, but a train ride from the center.

    Food & drink

    • Try Kyoto home cooking (obanzai) and yudofu, a simple hot-pot tofu dish served at temple restaurants.
    • Nishiki Market is a covered food street: graze on pickles, tamagoyaki and skewers. Many stalls close by late afternoon.
    • Kaiseki is the city's fine-dining tradition. Lunch sets cost far less than dinner; book ahead.
    • Matcha everything: tea, sweets and soft serve in Uji and around the temples.
    • Standing noodle bars and ramen shops near Kyoto Station are cheap and fast, often under ¥1000 a bowl.

    Tips

    1. Temples like Kinkaku-ji and Kiyomizu-dera are crowded; visit early in the morning
    2. Respect local customs and do not photograph geisha without permission in Gion
    3. Bicycle rental is the most local and convenient way to see the city's many hidden spots
    4. Try 'Kaiseki', a traditional multi-course haute cuisine unique to Kyoto
    5. Purchase a bus pass for unlimited travel, as buses serve most tourist destinations
