Getting around

TransMilenio for cheap main routes; taxi or app for evenings and hills.

  • ✗MetroNo metro yet. Don't plan around rail inside the city.
  • ✓Bus ★TransMilenio BRT and green SITP buses use a rechargeable Tullave card. Fares about COP$3,600 (TransMilenio) and COP$3,200 (SITP); the card costs about COP$8,000. Crowded at rush hour.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber, Cabify, DiDi and InDrive work but are officially unregulated; drivers may ask you to sit in front. Call taxis by dispatch or app, never hail on the street; check the meter starts and lock doors.
  • ✓Cable carTransMiCable gondola links Ciudad Bolívar to TransMilenio; a separate tourist cable car climbs Monserrate.
  • ✓TrainNo intercity trains; Turistren steam trains run to Zipaquirá several times a week.
  • ✗Rental carNot recommended: heavy traffic, restricted plates and confusing parking. Use buses, taxis and apps instead.
  • ✓WalkingGood in La Candelaria, Chapinero and Zona Rosa by day. Hills and uneven pavements; avoid unlit streets at night.

Climate

High-altitude tropical city: mild all year, cool nights, and rain possible in any month. September is driest, March and November wettest.

  • 🌡️Mild days, cool nightsHighs stay near 18–20°C all year; lows around 8–10°C. Bring a jacket for evenings.
  • 🌧️Rain in every monthExpect 8–24 rain days per month. Showers are often short and heavy in the afternoon.
  • ☀️Driest: August and SeptemberAbout 8–10 rain days. Best odds for Monserrate views and day trips.
  • ☔Wettest: March and NovemberAbout 22–24 rain days. Plan museums and cafés, and carry an umbrella.
  • ⛰️Altitude about 2,600 mTake the first day easy. Drink water and skip heavy exercise until you adjust.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket, comfortable shoes for hills and cobblestones, and sunscreen for sunny spells.

Landmarks 17

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Gold Museum

    Houses one of the world's largest collections of pre-Columbian gold, a true must-see for understanding Colombia's indigenous heritage. Go early on a weekday to avoid crowds; tickets are affordable and the museum is in La Candelaria.

  2. 2

    Monserrate Sanctuary

    A mountaintop church with sweeping views over Bogotá, reached by funicular or cable car. Visit on a clear morning for the best panorama; dress warmly as it's cooler up there.

  3. 3

    La Candelaria

    The historic old town with colorful colonial streets, museums, and lively plazas. Wander on foot during the day; it's central and safe for tourists, with many cafes and shops.

  4. 4

    Botero Museum

    Displays works by Fernando Botero and other renowned artists in a beautiful colonial building. Admission is free; combine with a stroll through La Candelaria.

  5. 5

    Plaza de Bolivar

    The city's main square, flanked by the cathedral and government buildings, rich in history. Visit during daylight hours; it's a short walk from La Candelaria.

  6. 6

    Cerro de Guadalupe

    A prominent mountain offering panoramic views and a pilgrimage site. Best visited on a clear day; take a taxi or bus to the base and be prepared for a steep walk.

  7. 7

    Usaquén

    A charming old town area with cobblestone streets, great for dining and shopping. Visit on weekends for the flea market; it's a short taxi ride from the center.

  8. 8

    Teatro Colon, Bogota

    A historic theater with stunning architecture and regular performances. Check the schedule for concerts or tours; located near La Candelaria.

    ★★★★★ · Half day · 41 km from Bogotá

    Best way: The direct bus takes about 1h 17m from Portal del Norte, faster and cheaper than a car or taxi for this distance, with a short taxi or local bus for the last stretch to the Salt Cathedral.

    Take a direct bus from Bogotá's Portal del Norte terminal to Zipaquirá (about 1 hour), then a short taxi or local bus to the Salt Cathedral.

    Swap in instead

    • Ride the TransMiCable to Ciudad Bolívar for views and murals — go with a local guide.
    • Day trip to Zipaquirá salt cathedral, about 1.5 hours by road; the Turistren steam train runs several times a week.
    • Hike or take the cable car up Monserrate on a clear morning; check the day's weather first.

    Getting around

    • TransMilenio BRT and green SITP buses use a rechargeable Tullave card.
    • TransMilenio fare is about COP$3,600 and SITP about COP$3,200; the card costs about COP$8,000.
    • TransMiCable gondola links Ciudad Bolívar to TransMilenio; a tourist cable car climbs Monserrate.
    • Uber, Cabify, DiDi and InDrive work but are officially unregulated; drivers may ask you to sit in front.
    • Call taxis by dispatch or app, never hail on the street; check the meter starts and lock doors.
    • No intercity trains; Turistren steam trains run to Zipaquirá several times a week.

    Where to stay

    • La Candelaria · ≈ COP 120,000–350,000 a night
      Walk to the Gold Museum, Plaza de Bolívar and Botero Museum. Lively by day, quiet and a bit rough-edged at night.
    • Zona Rosa · ≈ COP 250,000–700,000 a night
      Best for restaurants, bars and nightlife; safe to walk in the evening. Far from the historic sights.
    • Chapinero · ≈ COP 150,000–450,000 a night
      Central, good cafés and cheaper than Zona Rosa. A short ride to both the old town and the north.
    • Usaquén · ≈ COP 200,000–600,000 a night
      Calm, leafy and close to the airport side of town. Sunday flea market and good food. Farthest from La Candelaria.

    Food & drink

    • Try ajiaco (potato and chicken soup), bandeja paisa and arepas. Lunch menus (almuerzo) cost about COP$15,000–30,000.
    • Fruit is the highlight: try lulo, feijoa, granadilla and maracuyá at a market like Paloquemao or La Perseverancia.
    • Book a food tour in La Candelaria or Chapinero to taste safely and learn the names.
    • Coffee: order tinto for a small black coffee. Specialty cafés in Chapinero and Zona Rosa serve single-origin beans.
    • Street food is cheap but pick busy stalls. Drink bottled or filtered water; tap water is not for everyone.

    Tips

    1. Altitude sickness is common on the first day; rest, stay hydrated, and limit alcohol
    2. On Sundays, the 'Ciclovía' closes major avenues to cars, opening them for cyclists and pedestrians
    3. The weather changes constantly; carrying an umbrella and wearing layers is essential
    4. Be highly vigilant with your smartphone in public and especially on crowded TransMilenio buses
    5. Take the funicular or cable car up Monserrate for breathtaking panoramic views of the sprawling city
