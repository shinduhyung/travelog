Getting around

Metro, trams and walking cover the centre; use the 100E bus for the airport.

  • ✓Metro ★Lines 1–4 plus trams and trolleybuses cover the centre. Buy tickets or passes at machines and validate every ticket.
  • ✓Bus ★Buses and trams are frequent. Bus 100E runs 24 hours between Deák Ferenc tér and the airport. Validate every ticket or risk a fine.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis have fixed citywide rates. Főtaxi is the licensed airport operator, and Bolt works well.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is slow and parking is hard. Skip it for city sightseeing; walking and cycling suit the flat Pest side.
  • ✓WalkingThe flat Pest side is easy on foot. Cross the river for Castle Hill, which involves some climbing.
  • ✓FerryA river ferry and hop-on boats link stops along the Danube.

Climate

Continental climate: cold winters, warm summers, and rain spread through the year.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberWarm days from about 21°C to 28°C. Ideal for walking, the river and outdoor bars.
  • 🥶Cold winters (December to February)Highs of 4–8°C and lows near or below freezing. Wear a warm coat, hat and gloves.
  • 🌂Rain any monthExpect roughly 8–12 rainy days each month. Carry a small umbrella year-round.
  • 🌡️Summer heat is mildJuly and August peak near 28°C, not extreme. Mornings and evenings are the best times out.
  • 🍂Spring and autumn are sweet spotsApril–May and September–October give mild temperatures and fewer crowds.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable walking shoes, layers, a rain jacket, and swimwear for the thermal baths.

Landmarks 29

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Hungarian Parliament Building

    This riverside neo-Gothic palace is Budapest's defining silhouette and home to the Holy Crown. Book a guided interior tour in advance, and view the facade best from the Danube bank at dusk.

  2. 2

    Buda Castle

    The hilltop palace complex dominates the skyline and houses the Hungarian National Gallery. Take the funicular or a short walk up, and go early to avoid crowds.

  3. 3

    Fisherman's Bastion

    A fairy-tale terrace with the best panorama over the Danube and Parliament. Visit at sunrise or after dark for fewer people and dramatic light.

  4. 4

    Széchenyi Chain Bridge

    The iconic suspension bridge links Buda and Pest and is a symbol of the city. Walk across at night when it is illuminated, and combine it with a stroll to the Castle.

  5. 5

    St. Stephen's Basilica

    Budapest's largest church, with a dome you can climb for sweeping views. Dress modestly, and time your visit to hear the organ or see the relic.

  6. 6

    Heroes' Square

    A grand monument to Hungarian history at the end of Andrássy Avenue. Pair it with a visit to the City Park and Vajdahunyad Castle behind it.

  7. 7

    Széchenyi Thermal Baths

    One of Europe's largest thermal bath complexes, with outdoor pools and stunning architecture. Bring a swim cap and towel, and go on a weekday morning to avoid queues.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for Szentendre: a short suburban train ride up the Danube to a pretty art town.
    • Bath lovers can add a second thermal bath; the Széchenyi and Gellért baths are the classic picks.
    • History fans can take a half-day trip to the Memento Park for Soviet statues, or to Esztergom for the basilica.

    Getting around

    • Metro lines 1-4 plus trams and trolleybuses cover the centre; buy tickets or passes at machines.
    • Bus 100E runs 24 hours between Deák Ferenc tér and the airport.
    • Buses and trams are frequent; validate every ticket or risk a fine.
    • Taxis have fixed citywide rates; Főtaxi is the licensed airport operator, and Bolt works well.
    • Driving is slow and parking is hard; walking and cycling suit the flat Pest side.
    • A river ferry and hop-on boats link stops along the Danube.

    Where to stay

    • Belváros · ≈ HUF 22,000–55,000 a night
      The flat inner city: walk to the Danube, Váci utca and the Parliament. Best for first-timers.
    • Castle District · ≈ HUF 28,000–70,000 a night
      Quiet cobbled streets on the Buda side, with the palace and Fisherman's Bastion on your doorstep.
    • Jewish Quarter · ≈ HUF 18,000–45,000 a night
      Ruin bars, cheap eats and the Great Synagogue; lively and central on the Pest side.
    • Palace District · ≈ HUF 20,000–50,000 a night
      Grand 19th-century streets near the Parliament and Nyugati station; good value and calm at night.

    Food & drink

    • Try gulyás (goulash soup), pörkölt stew and lángos flatbread; a filling lunch is often 2500–4500 HUF.
    • The Great Market Hall (Nagy Vásárcsarnok) is good for paprika, sausages and cheap upstairs eats.
    • Ruin bars in the Jewish Quarter: Szimpla Kert is the famous one; expect busy crowds after dark.
    • Book a table for dinner on Friday and Saturday; popular places fill up. A mid-range dinner is about 6000–12000 HUF per person.
    • Budapest is famous for cakes: try a slice of dobos torta or somlói galuska in a traditional café.

    Tips

    1. Ticket inspectors are notoriously strict; always validate your paper ticket immediately upon boarding
    2. Spending an afternoon in a thermal bath (Széchenyi or Gellért) is an essential local experience
    3. District VII is famous for its 'Ruin Bars' built into abandoned pre-war buildings
    4. Due to a 19th-century historical legend, it is considered bad luck to clink beer glasses together
    5. Walking across the Chain Bridge at night offers spectacular views of the illuminated Parliament

    Souvenirs

    Hungarian sweet paprika

    HUF 1500–3500100–150g · estimate

    Ground red paprika is the country's signature spice and a staple of Hungarian goulash. Szeged and Kalocsa are its best-known growing regions.

    Where: Great Market Hall spice stalls or Vásárcsarnok grocery shops
    Check the origin label for Szeged or Kalocsa, and confirm whether it is sweet or hot before you buy.

    Tokaji Aszú dessert wine

    HUF 7000–15000500ml · shop prices

    A honeyed dessert wine made from botrytis-affected grapes in the Tokaj region. Hungary has made it for centuries.

    Where: Bortársaság, central Budapest shop
    Check the grade and vintage on the label, since they set the sweetness and price.

    Kalocsa embroidery piece

    HUF 4000–120001 small table mat · estimate

    Colorful flower embroidery from the Kalocsa region, a traditional folk craft shown on Hungarian clothing and linens.

    Where: Folkart Kézművesház, Régiposta Street
    Ask whether the piece is hand-embroidered, since machine-made versions are common.
