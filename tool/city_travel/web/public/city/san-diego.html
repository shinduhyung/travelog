Getting around

Ride apps or a rental car for most trips; the trolley and #992 bus cover downtown and the airport.

  • ✓MetroMTS Trolley light rail links downtown, Old Town, Mission Valley and San Ysidro. Useful for those areas; not a full city network.
  • ✓BusMTS buses cover the city. The #992 Flyer runs between SAN terminals and Santa Fe Depot downtown.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis are metered; Uber and Lyft operate widely and are usually cheaper.
  • ✓Rental car ★A car is the most efficient way to see the sprawling county. Expect rush-hour congestion.
  • ✓WalkingDowntown and the bayfront are walkable. Bikes and scooters are common near the beaches.
  • ✓TrainThe Coaster commuter train and Amtrak Pacific Surfliner serve the coast north to Oceanside and Los Angeles.

Climate

Mild Mediterranean-style coast: warm dry summers, cool winters and little rain outside winter and spring.

  • ☀️Best time: July to OctoberWarmest and driest, highs 24–26°C. Best for beaches and outdoor days.
  • 🌫️May grey and June gloomCoastal fog and cloud in late spring and early summer. It often clears by midday; inland is sunnier.
  • 🌧️Rain is light and winter-basedMost rain falls December to March, peaking in March at about 8 rain days. Summer is almost dry.
  • 🧥Cool evenings year-roundLows drop to 9–19°C depending on month. Bring a light layer even in summer.
  • 🌊Ocean stays coolWater is chilly much of the year. Wetsuits are common for surfing; check conditions on the day.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a light jacket, sunscreen and comfortable shoes. Add a rain shell for winter and spring visits.

Landmarks 14

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    San Diego Zoo

    One of the world's most celebrated zoos, home to pandas, elephants and a huge collection set in Balboa Park's canyons. Buy tickets online in advance for a discount, and arrive at opening to beat the crowds and heat.

  2. 2

    Balboa Park

    A sprawling cultural park with museums, gardens and Spanish colonial architecture, and the zoo sits within it. Go on a weekday morning, wear comfortable shoes, and use the free tram to get around.

  3. 3

    USS Midway Museum

    Tour a decommissioned aircraft carrier docked downtown, with planes on deck and crew quarters below. Book online, allow at least three hours, and go early to avoid the midday sun on the flight deck.

  4. 4

    La Jolla Cove

    A scenic cove with sea lions, tide pools and clear water for snorkeling and kayaking. Visit early morning for fewer crowds and better wildlife viewing, and keep a respectful distance from the sea lions.

  5. 5

    Coronado Beach

    A wide, golden-sand beach with the historic Hotel del Coronado as its backdrop. Take the ferry or drive over the bridge, and go in the late afternoon for softer light and fewer people.

  6. 6

    Cabrillo National Monument

    A clifftop monument with sweeping views over the harbor, the city and the Pacific, plus a historic lighthouse. Drive or take a rideshare to Point Loma, and come on a clear day for the best views.

  7. 7

    Old Town San Diego

    The historic birthplace of the city, with adobe buildings, museums and Mexican restaurants. It is easy to reach by trolley; go on a weekday to avoid weekend crowds.

  8. 8

    San Diego Harbor Cruise

    A boat tour of the harbor showing the skyline, naval ships and the Coronado Bridge from the water. Book a late-afternoon departure for cooler air and good light, and bring a light jacket for the breeze.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a beach afternoon for a day trip north on the Coaster or Pacific Surfliner to Oceanside or Encinitas.
    • Add a morning at Torrey Pines State Reserve: short cliff trails and ocean views; parking fills early, check on the day.
    • Cross to Coronado by ferry for the beach, the Hotel del Coronado and a slow lunch.

    Getting around

    • MTS Trolley (light rail) links downtown, Old Town, Mission Valley and San Ysidro.
    • MTS buses cover the city; the #992 Flyer runs between SAN terminals and Santa Fe Depot.
    • Taxis are metered; Uber and Lyft operate widely and are usually cheaper.
    • A car is the most efficient way to see the sprawling county; expect rush-hour congestion.
    • Downtown and the bayfront are walkable; bikes and scooters are common near beaches.
    • The Coaster commuter train and Amtrak Pacific Surfliner serve the coast north to Oceanside and Los Angeles.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown / Gaslamp Quarter · ≈ USD 150–350 a night
      Walk to the bayfront, Petco Park and the Gaslamp restaurants. Trolley and #992 airport bus start here.
    • Harbor Island / Airport area · ≈ USD 140–300 a night
      Quiet bayfront hotels, minutes from SAN airport and downtown. Good for early flights and families.
    • La Jolla · ≈ USD 220–600 a night
      Walk to the cove, sea lions and snorkel spots. Upscale and calm; 20–30 minutes from downtown by car.
    • Mission Valley · ≈ USD 120–260 a night
      Cheaper chain hotels near the trolley and shopping. Central for driving to the Zoo and beaches.

    Food & drink

    • Try a fish taco: grilled or fried fish, cabbage slaw and crema. Cheap stands and sit-down spots both do it well.
    • California burrito: carne asada, fries, cheese and salsa wrapped in a tortilla. A full meal for one.
    • Little Italy on India Street for pasta, pizza and a lively evening scene; book ahead on weekends.
    • Seafood by the water in Point Loma and the Embarcadero; check the day's catch and prices on the board.
    • Craft beer is big here. Many tasting rooms in North Park and Miramar; expect about $8–10 a pint.

    Tips

    1. The craft beer scene is world-class; Miramar and North Park are packed with famous breweries
    2. Rent a car if you want to explore the extensive coastline and inland neighborhoods
    3. You can take the MTS Trolley directly to the San Ysidro border crossing to walk into Tijuana, Mexico
    4. Fish tacos and California burritos (stuffed with french fries) are absolute must-tries
    5. Balboa Park offers incredible museums, gardens, and the famous San Diego Zoo
