Getting around

Walk central Boston and use the T for longer hops; the Silver Line is free from the airport.

  • ✓Metro ★The T subway has four colour-coded lines; the Blue Line links the airport area to downtown. Use a CharlieCard and tap in.
  • ✓BusBuses use the same CharlieCard. The Silver Line bus runs free from Logan to South Station and connects to the Red Line.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are mostly white and cost $25–50 from Logan to downtown. Uber and Lyft pick up and drop off at Logan; check the current pickup zone.
  • ✓Rental carNot needed for central Boston, which is compact and walkable. Consider one only for trips outside the city.
  • ✓Walking ★Central Boston is compact and walkable. Wear comfortable shoes for the Freedom Trail.
  • ✓FerryFerries serve the harbour and are a good way to see the waterfront. Check times on the day.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cold, sometimes snowy winters and warm, humid summers. Spring and autumn are the easiest months for walking.

  • 🌸Best time: May, June, September and OctoberHighs around 17–25°C and lows around 9–15°C. Comfortable for the Freedom Trail and the waterfront.
  • 🥶Winter is cold (December to February)Highs near 3–6°C and lows near -6 to -2°C. Snow and ice are possible; wear warm, waterproof boots.
  • 🥵Summer is warm and humid (July and August)Highs near 28°C with lows near 18–19°C. Take indoor breaks and carry water.
  • 🌧️Rain in every monthAbout 8–12 wet days a month. A small umbrella or rain jacket is worth packing year-round.
  • 🍂Autumn cools quicklyOctober highs near 17°C drop to about 11°C in November. Bring layers for evenings.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable walking shoes, layers, and a rain layer. Add a warm coat, hat and gloves from December to March.

Landmarks 33

Must-see · 10

  1. 1

    Faneuil Hall Marketplace

    A historic market and meeting hall that has been a gathering place since the 1740s, now filled with shops and food stalls. It is a must-see for its role in the American Revolution and lively atmosphere. Tip: visit on a weekday morning to avoid crowds and enjoy the street performers.

  2. 2

    USS Constitution

    The world's oldest commissioned naval vessel still afloat, launched in 1797 and famous for its role in the War of 1812. Tours are led by Navy sailors and include the story of 'Old Ironsides'. Tip: the ship is free to visit but requires a timed ticket from the Charlestown Navy Yard visitor center; go early as lines form quickly.

  3. 3

    Boston Common

    America's oldest public park, dating back to 1634, and the starting point of the Freedom Trail. It offers a green escape in the heart of the city and hosts seasonal events. Tip: combine with a walk through the adjacent Public Garden for the best experience.

  4. 4

    Freedom Trail

    A 2.5-mile red-brick path connecting 16 significant historical sites, from Boston Common to Bunker Hill. It is the best way to see the city's revolutionary history in one go. Tip: wear comfortable shoes and start early; guided tours are available but the trail is self-guided and free.

  5. 5

    Fenway Park

    Home of the Boston Red Sox, this is one of the most iconic ballparks in America, opened in 1912. Even non-baseball fans enjoy the historic atmosphere and the Green Monster. Tip: take a guided tour on non-game days to see the press box and dugout.

  6. 6

    Museum of Fine Arts

    One of the largest art museums in the United States, with a collection spanning ancient to contemporary art, including works by Rembrandt, Monet, and Van Gogh. It is a must for art lovers. Tip: visit on a weekday afternoon for fewer crowds; the museum is easily reached by the Green Line E train.

  7. 7

    New England Aquarium

    A leading aquarium featuring a massive central tank with sharks, sea turtles, and thousands of fish, plus penguin exhibits and a IMAX theater. It is a favorite for families. Tip: buy tickets online in advance to skip the line, and go early in the day.

  8. 8

    Harvard Square

    The vibrant heart of Cambridge, home to Harvard University and a mix of historic buildings, bookstores, and cafes. It offers a glimpse of college life and intellectual history. Tip: take the Red Line to Harvard station and wander the yard; free student-led tours are often available.

  9. 9

    Old North Church

    Boston's oldest church, famous for the lanterns that signaled Paul Revere's midnight ride in 1775. It is a key stop on the Freedom Trail. Tip: check opening hours as they vary seasonally; a small donation is suggested for entry.

  10. 10

    Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum

    An interactive museum where you can throw tea overboard and learn about the pivotal event leading to the American Revolution. It is engaging for all ages. Tip: book tickets online for a specific time slot, as tours are timed.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Cambridge: walk or take the Red Line to Harvard Square and the MIT area.
    • Take a harbour ferry or water taxi for a cheap view of the skyline and the waterfront.
    • Add a day trip by train to Salem or Providence; check timetables on the day.

    Getting around

    • The T subway has four colour-coded lines; the Blue Line links the airport area to downtown.
    • The Silver Line bus runs free from Logan to South Station and connects to the Red Line.
    • Buses and the subway use a CharlieCard; tap or buy at stations.
    • Taxis are mostly white and cost $25-50 from Logan to downtown.
    • Uber and Lyft pick up and drop off at Logan; check the current pickup zone.
    • Central Boston is compact and walkable; bikes and ferries serve the harbour.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown and Waterfront · ≈ USD 180–420 a night
      Walk to the Freedom Trail, Faneuil Hall and the aquarium. Best base for a first visit.
    • Back Bay · ≈ USD 200–480 a night
      Pretty streets, shopping on Newbury Street and easy T rides to the centre.
    • Beacon Hill · ≈ USD 190–450 a night
      Quiet brick lanes next to Boston Common and the State House; small inns and guesthouses.
    • Seaport · ≈ USD 220–520 a night
      New waterfront hotels, harbour views and the ICA museum; 15–20 minutes' walk to downtown.

    Food & drink

    • Try a lobster roll and New England clam chowder; prices vary a lot, so compare menus before you sit down.
    • Walk the North End for Italian pastries and pasta; it is busy at weekends, so go early or on a weekday.
    • Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market have many food stalls — handy for groups with different tastes.
    • For cheap eats, look around Chinatown and the student areas near universities.
    • Book popular dinner spots ahead, especially in summer and on game nights near Fenway Park.

    Tips

    1. Get a CharlieCard for discounted fares on the T and buses
    2. Walking the Freedom Trail (2.5 miles) is the best way to see historical sites
    3. Prepare for unpredictable weather and strong winds off the harbor, especially in winter
    4. Seafood (lobster rolls, New England clam chowder) is a local specialty you must try
    5. Driving in the city center is notoriously difficult and parking is very expensive
