Getting around

Walk the heritage core and use Grab or Rapid Penang buses for longer trips.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram on Penang Island. Don't plan around rail.
  • ✓Bus ★Rapid Penang buses fan out from Weld Quay via KOMTAR; fares RM1.40–4. Free CAT buses loop the heritage core and modern centre.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Grab is the main ride app. Taxis use a coupon system or meter; agree the price first.
  • ✓Rental carPossible but traffic and parking in the heritage core are hard. Most visitors skip it.
  • ✓WalkingThe heritage core is compact and walkable. Link Bike day pass is RM6.
  • ✓FerryRapid Ferry links Butterworth rail station to Weld Quay, RM1.20.

Climate

Tropical and hot all year, around 29–31°C by day and 25–26°C at night. Rain is common in every month, heaviest from September to November.

  • 🌡️Hot all yearHighs stay near 29–31°C and lows near 25–26°C. No cool season; pack light clothes.
  • 🌧️Rain in every monthExpect 15–30 rain days a month. Showers are often short and heavy, not all-day.
  • ☂️Driest window: January to February15–19 rain days. The best odds for dry sightseeing and outdoor walks.
  • 🌊Wettest: September to November27–30 rain days. Keep indoor backups and check forecasts before hill or beach trips.
  • 🥵Humidity and heatMuggy most days. Walk early morning or evening and rest in air-conditioned cafés at midday.
  • 🧳What to packLight breathable clothes, a compact umbrella or rain jacket, sandals, and sunscreen.

Landmarks 12

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    George Town UNESCO Zone

    The historic core of shophouses, temples and colonial buildings is the reason most visitors come, and it is best explored on foot. Go early in the morning to beat the heat and the crowds.

  2. 2

    Fort Cornwallis

    The star-shaped colonial fort on the waterfront is George Town's most famous landmark and a good starting point for a walk through the old town. Visit in the morning for cooler weather and fewer visitors.

  3. 3

    Khoo Kongsi

    This ornate clan house is one of the most impressive Chinese heritage buildings in the city, with richly decorated halls and carvings. It is a short walk from the old town center; check opening hours before you go.

  4. 4

    Penang Hill

    A funicular ride takes you up for cooler air and sweeping views over the island and city. Go late afternoon to catch the sunset, and expect queues on weekends.

  5. 5

    Kek Lok Si Temple

    A large hillside Buddhist temple complex with a towering statue and pagoda, one of Penang's signature sights. Dress modestly and allow a couple of hours to wander the terraces.

  6. 6

    Clan Jetties

    Traditional stilt villages on the waterfront show a way of life unique to Penang. Walk quietly since people still live here, and go in the morning for softer light.

  7. 7

    Armenian Street

    A charming old-town street lined with heritage shophouses and street art nearby. It is best on foot and early in the day before the tour groups arrive.

  8. 8

    Pinang Peranakan Mansion

    A beautifully restored mansion filled with Peranakan antiques and furnishings, giving a vivid sense of Straits Chinese life. It is an indoor stop, handy during the midday heat.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Penang Hill: take the funicular for cooler air and views. Go early to beat queues.
    • Kek Lok Si temple in Air Itam is a half-day trip; combine with a laksa lunch nearby.
    • Beach time at Batu Ferringhi, or a day trip to the national park and beaches on the north coast.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram on Penang Island; buses do the work.
    • Rapid Penang buses fan out from Weld Quay via KOMTAR; fares RM1.40-4.
    • Free CAT buses loop the heritage core and modern centre.
    • Grab is the main ride app; taxis use coupon or meter.
    • Rapid Ferry links Butterworth rail station to Weld Quay, RM1.20.
    • The heritage core is compact and walkable; Link Bike day pass RM6.

    Where to stay

    • UNESCO Heritage Site · ≈ MYR 120–450 a night
      Walk to the murals, clan houses and hawker food. Best for first-timers without a car.
    • Gurney Drive · ≈ MYR 180–500 a night
      Seafront promenade and the famous hawker centre; short ride to the heritage core.
    • Tanjung Tokong · ≈ MYR 160–420 a night
      Quieter local area with cafés and malls; between the city and the beaches.
    • Batu Ferringhi · ≈ MYR 200–700 a night
      Beach resorts and night market; far from town, so plan on buses or Grab.

    Food & drink

    • Eat at hawker centres: char kway teow, laksa, and cendol. Expect roughly RM5–15 a dish; check prices on the day.
    • Kopitiam breakfast: kaya toast, half-boiled eggs and local coffee. Cheap and quick.
    • Peranakan (Nyonya) dishes are the local signature: try a set meal in a heritage shophouse.
    • Gurney Drive hawker centre is busy at night; go early for a seat.
    • Little India and Chinatown lanes are good for cheap eats. Many stalls close on one weekday; check on the day.

    Tips

    1. Widely considered the best food city in Malaysia; sample Char Kway Teow and Assam Laksa
    2. Wander the narrow streets of the historic core to see vibrant street art and shophouses
    3. Take the funicular train up Penang Hill for cool air and panoramic views
    4. Grab is very cheap and the most convenient way to explore the island
    5. The culture is a unique blend of Malay, Chinese, and Indian influences

      Souvenirs

      Nyonya Beaded Slippers

      MYR 250–6001 pair, adult size · estimate

      These slippers show off Peranakan beadwork, a craft that blends Chinese and Malay styles. It is a classic keepsake from George Town's Peranakan heritage.

      Where: Nyonya beaded shoe workshops around Armenian Street
      Check the size before you pay, since the beadwork is handmade and fit can vary.

      Tau Sar Piah

      MYR 12–30Box of 12 to 16 · shop prices

      These are Penang's signature mung bean pastries, sold in boxes as a favorite gift to take home. Locals have been buying them for generations.

      Where: Ghee Hiang or Him Heang main shops
      Check the ingredient list, since some versions use lard, which matters for some travelers.

      Nutmeg Candy

      MYR 8–20100 to 250 g · estimate

      Penang is known for nutmeg, and this candy is a simple way to taste that local spice. The sealed packs make it easy to carry.

      Where: Specialty shops at Chowrasta Market
      Choose sealed packaging so the candy travels well in your luggage.
