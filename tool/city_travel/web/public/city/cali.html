Getting around

Taxis and ride apps for most trips; MIO buses if you're saving money.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or train in the city. Don't plan around rail.
  • ✓BusMIO buses run in exclusive lanes with air-conditioned cars and smart-card payment. The MIO card costs about COP$6,000; a single ride is about COP$3,500. About 23 other urban bus companies also cover the city, about COP$3,500 a trip.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis are fast and affordable; call one and check the meter is running. Uber and DiDi operate in Cali; use official taxis at night.
  • ✓Rental carPossible, but city traffic and parking are a hassle. Better for out-of-town day trips; check licence rules.
  • ✓WalkingWalking works in Granada and San Antonio. Use taxis after dark.

Climate

Cali is mild all year, around 25°C by day and 18–19°C at night. Rain is common in every month, so plan around showers.

  • 🌡️Mild all yearHighs stay near 25–27°C and lows near 18–19°C. No extreme heat or cold; light layers work.
  • 🌧️Rain in every monthExpect rain on 22–28 days a month. Showers are often short; carry a compact rain jacket.
  • ☔Wettest months: March, October, NovemberRain on about 27–28 days. Keep indoor options and a taxi budget ready.
  • 🌤️Drier months: February, July, AugustRain on about 22 days. Still pack rain gear, but outdoor plans are easier.
  • 🌅Plan mornings outdoorsRain often builds later in the day. Do nature trips and walking early, then move indoors.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, a rain jacket or umbrella, and comfortable shoes for hilly, wet streets. A light layer for cool evenings.

Landmarks 6

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Cristo Rey

    The towering hilltop statue of Christ is Cali's most recognizable sight, offering sweeping views over the city and the Cauca valley. It is a short drive or hike up from the west side of town; go in the late afternoon for cooler air and sunset light, and bring water if you walk up.

  2. 2

    Cerro de las Tres Cruces

    Three crosses crown this hill and mark one of Cali's favorite viewpoints and exercise spots. Locals hike up early in the morning for safety and cooler temperatures; go with others and avoid carrying valuables.

  3. 3

    San Antonio

    This historic old-town neighborhood is known for its colonial charm, bohemian cafés and a small hilltop church with city views. Wander the steep streets in the morning or early evening, and note that the area is best explored on foot.

  4. 4

    Museo del Oro

    Cali's gold museum displays pre-Columbian metalwork and archaeological pieces from the region. It is a compact, affordable indoor stop, ideal for a rainy afternoon; check opening hours before you go.

  5. 5

    La Merced

    A historic religious complex with colonial architecture and a quiet atmosphere in the city center. It pairs well with a walk through the surrounding old streets; dress modestly if you enter the church.

  6. 6

    Zoológico de Cali

    One of Colombia's best-known zoos, home to a wide range of Andean and tropical species. It is a good family half-day out; go early to avoid the midday heat and allow a few hours.

    Swap in instead

    • Salsa fans: swap a sightseeing afternoon for a beginner dance class in Granada. Prices vary; book a day ahead.
    • Birdwatchers: a guided early-morning trip to a nearby cloud-forest reserve is worth a half day. Check conditions on the day.
    • History buffs: add a half day for the gold museum and the colonial churches in Centro, with a taxi back before dark.

    Getting around

    • MIO buses run in exclusive lanes with air-conditioned cars and smart-card payment.
    • MIO card costs about COP$6,000; a single ride is about COP$3,500.
    • About 23 urban bus companies also cover the city; a regular trip is about COP$3,500.
    • Taxis are fast and affordable; call one and check the meter is running.
    • Ride apps like Uber and DiDi operate in Cali; use official taxis at night.
    • Walking works in Granada and San Antonio; use taxis after dark.

    Where to stay

    • Granada · ≈ COP 180,000–420,000 a night
      Leafy, walkable and full of restaurants and cafés. Good base for first-time visitors.
    • El Peñón · ≈ COP 150,000–350,000 a night
      Quiet, central and close to the river; a short taxi ride to Granada and San Antonio.
    • San Antonio · ≈ COP 140,000–320,000 a night
      Colonial hilltop lanes with cafés and sunset views. Charming but hilly; take taxis after dark.
    • Centro · ≈ COP 90,000–220,000 a night
      Cheapest and most historic, near the gold museum and markets. Busy and rough-edged at night.

    Food & drink

    • Try local snacks: pandebono (cheese bread), aborrajados (fried plantain with cheese) and cholado, a fruit-and-ice cup.
    • Lunch at a market or a set-menu 'corrientazo' spot: soup, rice, beans and meat for a low price. Check the day's price on the board.
    • Look for Pacific-coast cooking: fish and shellfish in coconut sauce, plus fried plantain. Ask what is fresh that day.
    • Granada and San Antonio have the widest choice of cafés and restaurants; book ahead on weekend evenings.
    • Coffee is excellent. Order tinto (black) or a latte at a local café rather than a chain.

    Tips

    1. Known as the 'Capital of Salsa'; take a dance class or visit a local club
    2. Cali has a very distinctive, high-energy culture
    3. Stay in safe tourist areas and use Uber for travel at night
    4. The city is famous for its mild evenings despite the tropical heat
    5. Try local sugar cane-based juices and snacks
