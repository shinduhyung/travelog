Getting around

A rental car or ride apps for most trips; DART rail for downtown and suburbs.

  • ✓Metro ★DART light rail covers downtown and many suburbs; best for commuters. Tap a contactless card or phone on DART readers; you pay the cheapest valid fare.
  • ✓BusDART buses reach most places but are slow and infrequent off-peak.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis hail at stands and hotels; Uber and Lyft both work widely.
  • ✓Rental car ★A car is the simplest way around; rush hour on I-35 and US-75 is brutal.
  • ✓WalkingDowntown is walkable; bikes and scooters suit short hops, not sprawl.

Climate

Humid subtropical: long hot summers, mild winters, and rain spread through the year.

  • ☀️Best time: March to May and October to NovemberHighs of 19–28°C make walking and outdoor sights comfortable.
  • 🥵Summer is very hot (June to September)Highs of 32–35°C with warm nights near 22–25°C. Do outdoor plans early or after dark.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 7–14 rain days a month. May is wettest. Carry a small umbrella.
  • ❄️Winter is mild but chilly at nightJanuary highs near 13°C and lows near 3°C. Bring a warm jacket.
  • ⛈️Spring stormsSpring can bring thunderstorms. Check the forecast on the day.
  • 🧳What to packLight layers, a hat and sunscreen for summer; a warm layer for winter nights; comfortable shoes for the sprawl.

Landmarks 14

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    Dealey Plaza

    The site of the 1963 assassination of President Kennedy and the most visited historic spot in Dallas. Come early in the morning to walk the plaza and the grassy knoll before crowds arrive; it is free and open to the public.

  2. 2

    Sixth Floor Museum

    Housed in the former Texas School Book Depository, this museum tells the story of the Kennedy assassination with artifacts and the sniper's window view. Book timed tickets online in advance, as entry slots sell out on weekends.

  3. 3

    Reunion Tower

    Dallas's most recognizable skyline landmark, with an observation deck offering sweeping views over the city. Go near sunset for the best light and check the weather before buying tickets, since views depend on clear skies.

  4. 4

    The Dallas Museum of Art

    A major art museum in the Arts District with a wide collection spanning centuries and cultures. General admission is free, making it an easy must-see; check opening days before you go.

  5. 5

    The Dallas Arboretum and Botanical Garden

    Lovely lakeside gardens known for seasonal flower displays. Visit in spring or fall for peak blooms and allow a couple of hours to walk the grounds.

    Best way: The TRE commuter train runs direct from Dallas to central Fort Worth in about an hour with no changes.

    Grapevine · 40 min by bus

    ★★★☆☆ · Half day · 36 km from Dallas

    Best way: A 40-minute bus ride beats the train-plus-walk combo and drops you near the historic downtown.

    Take the Trinity Railway Express from Dallas to the Grapevine-Main Street station, then a short walk or local shuttle into the historic downtown; no changes needed on the train.

    • Bus ★40 min · from $3-$5
    • Car28 min · taxi ≈ $80

    What to see

    • Historic downtown Main Street
    • Grapevine Vintage Railroad
    • Wine tasting rooms
    • Grapevine Mills area

    Good to know

    • Historic downtown and winery tasting rooms are the highlights
    • Weekend services can be limited, so check times ahead
    • Autumn's GrapeFest is the busiest time to visit

    Denton · 1 h 25 by bus

    ★★☆☆☆ · Half day · 63 km from Dallas

    Best way: The direct regional bus is faster than the two-leg A-train route and needs no connection.

    Take the DCTA A-train from Dallas via a connection at Trinity Mills (DART Green Line) to downtown Denton, or a direct regional bus; the A-train terminates at Downtown Denton station.

    • Train1 h 53 · from $4-$9
    • Bus ★1 h 25 · from $7-$11
    • Car49 min · taxi ≈ $130

    What to see

    • Denton Courthouse Square
    • University of North Texas campus
    • Local breweries and music venues
    • Bayless-Selby House Museum

    Good to know

    • The downtown square and university campus are the main sights
    • A-train connections via Trinity Mills add time
    • Check return times, as evening services are sparse

    Swap in instead

    • Add a half-day at the Dallas Arboretum and Botanical Garden, or the Perot Museum of Nature and Science for families.
    • Sports fans can swap a museum for a Cowboys or Mavericks game; check the schedule and ticket prices on the day.
    • Drive 30–40 minutes to Fort Worth for the Stockyards and its twice-daily cattle drive.

    Getting around

    • DART light rail covers downtown and many suburbs; best for commuters.
    • DART buses reach most places but are slow and infrequent off-peak.
    • Tap a contactless card or phone on DART readers; you pay the cheapest valid fare.
    • Taxis hail at stands and hotels; Uber and Lyft both work widely.
    • A car is the simplest way around; rush hour on I-35 and US-75 is brutal.
    • Downtown is walkable; bikes and scooters suit short hops, not sprawl.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown · ≈ USD 130–320 a night
      Walk to the Sixth Floor Museum, the Arts District and the convention center. DART rail lines meet here.
    • Uptown · ≈ USD 150–380 a night
      Restaurants and bars along McKinney Avenue, a short walk or trolley ride from downtown.
    • Deep Ellum · ≈ USD 120–300 a night
      Live music, murals and late-night food. Lively at night, quieter by day.
    • Oak Lawn · ≈ USD 110–280 a night
      Good-value hotels and a short drive to Uptown and downtown. Cedar Springs nightlife nearby.

    Food & drink

    • Try Texas BBQ brisket and ribs; popular spots sell out by late afternoon, so go early or check on the day.
    • Tex-Mex is the local staple: fajitas, enchiladas and queso. Expect USD 12–25 a plate.
    • Breakfast tacos are a cheap Dallas ritual, often USD 3–5 each. Grab them from a taqueria or food truck.
    • Deep Ellum and Uptown have the densest restaurant rows. Book ahead for weekend dinner.
    • Tipping is expected: about 20% at sit-down restaurants and bars.

    Tips

    1. A rental car is virtually essential to navigate the vast distances of the Metroplex
    2. Summer heat is oppressive; limit outdoor activities to the early morning and rely on air conditioning
    3. Tex-Mex and smoked BBQ brisket are the undisputed kings of the local culinary scene
    4. Visit the Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza for a sobering look at JFK's life and assassination
    5. If driving, get a TollTag or be prepared for mail-in bills, as many major highways are tolled
