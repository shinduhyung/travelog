Climate

Four clear seasons: cold, fairly dry winters and hot, humid, rainy summers.

  • 🌸Best time: spring and autumnApril, May and October bring mild highs around 19–23°C, ideal for walking and sightseeing.
  • 🥵Hot, humid summerJuly and August reach highs near 30–32°C with many rain days. Take indoor breaks and carry water.
  • 🌧️Rainy spellsJune and September have the most rain days. Keep an umbrella and a backup indoor plan.
  • ❄️Cold, drier winterJanuary and February highs near 9–10°C, lows near 1°C. Few rain days; dress warmly.
  • 🌂Pack an umbrellaRain days range from about 7 to 16 a month, so a compact umbrella helps year-round.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for spring and autumn, light breathable clothes for summer, and a warm coat for winter.

Landmarks 19

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Osaka Castle

    The city's defining landmark, with a reconstructed keep, moats and parkland that tell Osaka's story from the 16th century. Go early in the morning to beat the crowds, and note the castle museum charges an admission fee.

  2. 2

    Dotonbori

    Osaka's neon-lit entertainment hub, famous for its canal-side signs and street-food stalls. Come after dark for the full effect, and arrive by metro to Namba station.

  3. 3

    Umeda Sky Building

    A striking twin-tower complex with a floating garden observatory offering sweeping city views. Visit near sunset for the best light; there is a ticket for the observatory.

  4. 4

    Tsutenkaku

    The retro tower that anchors the Shinsekai district, a beloved symbol of old Osaka. Combine it with a wander through Shinsekai's streets, and buy a ticket for the observation deck.

  5. 5

    Shitenno-ji

    One of Japan's oldest Buddhist temples, with a peaceful precinct and a five-story pagoda. It is easily reached by metro and is calmest in the early morning.

  6. 6

    Sumiyoshi Taisha

    A major shrine with distinctive architecture and a serene, wooded setting. Go in the morning for quiet, and check the shrine's own access from the nearby tram or train station.

  7. 7

    Universal Studios Japan

    A major theme park and a big draw for first-time visitors, especially families. Book tickets in advance and arrive at opening to make the most of the day.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for a day trip to Kyoto or Nara by train; check times and fares on the day.
    • Add a half-day at Universal Studios Japan if you like theme parks; buy tickets ahead.
    • Spend an evening in Shinsekai for kushikatsu and the retro atmosphere.

    Getting around

    • Osaka Metro and JR trains cover the city; use an IC card like ICOCA.
    • Buses are useful but less frequent; taxis are easy to hail.
    • Ride apps: Uber and GO work in Osaka.
    • Driving is not recommended for visitors; parking is expensive.
    • Walking and cycling are great in central areas; rent bikes from stations.
    • Ferries connect to Kansai Airport and nearby islands.

    Where to stay

    • Namba · ≈ JPY 9,000–22,000 a night
      Steps from Dotonbori nightlife and easy train links to the airport and Kyoto.
    • Umeda · ≈ JPY 10,000–25,000 a night
      Main rail hub for day trips; big malls and food halls under the station.
    • Shinsaibashi · ≈ JPY 9,500–23,000 a night
      Shopping arcade on your doorstep, walkable to Namba and Dotonbori.
    • Tennoji · ≈ JPY 7,000–17,000 a night
      Quieter and often cheaper; good rail links and park nearby.

    Food & drink

    • Try takoyaki and okonomiyaki; Dotonbori has plenty of stalls and small shops.
    • Kuromon Ichiba market is good for grilled seafood and skewers; prices vary, check before you buy.
    • Kushikatsu (fried skewers) is a local specialty; the Shinsekai area is known for it.
    • Convenience stores and standing noodle bars are cheap and quick, often under ¥1000 a meal.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead or go early; many small places are cash-friendly.

    Tips

    1. Osaka is known as the 'Kitchen of Japan'; prioritize street food like Takoyaki and Okonomiyaki
    2. People in Osaka are known for being more outgoing and humorous than in Tokyo
    3. Get an ICOCA or Suica card for seamless travel on all trains and buses
    4. Dotonbori is incredibly crowded at night but a must-see for the neon lights and food
    5. Universal Studios Japan (Super Nintendo World) is a massive draw just outside the city
