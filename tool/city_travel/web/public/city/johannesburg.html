Getting around

Uber or Bolt for most trips; Gautrain for the airport and Sandton.

  • ✓Train ★Gautrain is the fast, safe rail link between OR Tambo, Sandton, Rosebank and Pretoria.
  • ✓MetroMetrorail commuter trains are cheap but not advised for tourists.
  • ✓BusCity and Putco buses are limited; minibus taxis are cheap but risky for visitors.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and Bolt work well; use comfort or black options where possible.
  • ✓Rental carRenting a car gives the most flexibility; traffic peaks 06:30-09:00 and 15:30-18:30.
  • ✓WalkingWalking is fine in malls and some suburbs; avoid walking between distant areas.

Climate

High-altitude city with warm, wet summers and dry, cold winters. Rain falls mostly as afternoon thunderstorms.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberDry, sunny days with few rain days. Cool to cold nights, so pack layers.
  • 🌧️Wet summer (November to February)Around 11–16 rain days a month, usually short afternoon storms. Mornings are often clear.
  • ❄️Cold winter nightsJune and July drop to about 4°C at night. Days stay near 17°C and sunny.
  • 🌤️Mild shoulder monthsApril, May, September and October are comfortable, with highs of 20–26°C.
  • ⛈️Afternoon thunderstormsSummer storms can be sudden. Avoid open high ground late in the day.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for cool mornings and warm afternoons, a rain jacket in summer, and a warm jacket for winter nights.

Landmarks 19

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Apartheid Museum

    The essential introduction to South Africa's 20th-century history, with powerful exhibits on segregation and the struggle for freedom. Book tickets online in advance and allow at least two hours; it's a short drive from central Johannesburg.

  2. 2

    Soweto

    The sprawling township is a living monument to South Africa's past and present, best explored with a local guide. Visit on a weekday morning for a calmer experience and to see daily life in action.

  3. 3

    Mandela House

    Nelson Mandela's former home in Soweto offers an intimate look at his early life and the anti-apartheid movement. Combine it with a broader Soweto tour; tickets are available on site.

  4. 4

    Constitution Hill

    A former prison turned constitutional court and museum, telling stories of justice and human rights. Guided tours run regularly; wear comfortable shoes for the uneven grounds.

  5. 5

    Gold Reef City

    A theme park and casino complex built around a historic gold mine, offering rides and underground tours. Check ride schedules in advance and book tickets online to skip queues.

  6. 6

    Carlton Centre

    Once Africa's tallest building, its top-floor observation deck gives sweeping views over the city. Go on a clear day; the viewing area has limited hours, so check before you visit.

  7. 7

    Ponte City

    This iconic cylindrical tower is a symbol of Johannesburg's urban renewal. While not generally open to the public, it's best viewed from nearby neighbourhoods or on a guided city tour.

  8. 8

    Maboneng Precinct

    A revitalised urban hub full of street art, independent shops and cafes, perfect for a stroll. Visit on a weekend when the area is liveliest, and keep an eye on your belongings.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for Soweto: Vilakazi Street, the Hector Pieterson Museum and a shisa nyama lunch.
    • History fans can swap a museum for the Cradle of Humankind and Sterkfontein Caves, about an hour's drive.
    • Add a half-day at the Apartheid Museum and Constitution Hill if you have a third day.

    Getting around

    • Gautrain is the fast, safe rail link between OR Tambo, Sandton, Rosebank and Pretoria.
    • Metrorail commuter trains are cheap but not advised for tourists.
    • City and Putco buses are limited; minibus taxis are cheap but risky for visitors.
    • Uber and Bolt work well; use comfort or black options where possible.
    • Renting a car gives the most flexibility; traffic peaks 06:30-09:00 and 15:30-18:30.
    • Walking is fine in malls and some suburbs; avoid walking between distant areas.

    Where to stay

    • Sandton · ≈ ZAR 900–2,600 a night
      Safest base for first-timers. Gautrain to the airport, malls and restaurants on foot, easy Ubers to the sights.
    • Rosebank · ≈ ZAR 850–2,400 a night
      Gautrain stop, good hotels and the Sunday rooftop market. A calmer, walkable suburb close to the museums.
    • Maboneng · ≈ ZAR 600–1,600 a night
      Downtown arts district with galleries, cafés and street art. Lively by day; take an Uber after dark.
    • Melville · ≈ ZAR 550–1,400 a night
      Leafy student neighbourhood with bars and cheap eats on 7th Street. Good value, short drive to town.

    Food & drink

    • Try a braai (South African barbecue) and bunny chow, a curry served in a hollowed bread loaf.
    • Maboneng and Braamfontein have the best café and street-food scene; go at lunchtime and take an Uber back.
    • Soweto tours often include a lunch stop; ask for a local shisa nyama (grill) spot.
    • Cheap eats: kota sandwiches, vetkoek and boerewors rolls at markets for around ZAR 40–90.
    • Book dinner in Sandton or Rosebank. Many kitchens close early; check hours on the day.

    Tips

    1. Use Uber or Bolt for getting around, especially at night, for safety and convenience
    2. The Apartheid Museum is a must-visit for understanding the country's complex history
    3. Be vigilant with your belongings and avoid flashing valuables in the street
    4. The high altitude might cause slight fatigue or shortness of breath on your first day
    5. Visit the vibrant neighborhoods of Maboneng or Braamfontein for arts, cafes, and nightlife
