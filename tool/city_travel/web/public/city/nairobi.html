Getting around

Uber or Bolt for most trips; taxis where apps are slow.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Don't plan around rail in the city.
  • ✓BusMatatus (minibuses) are the main public transport. Cheap but crowded and can be risky; use them with caution.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Uber and Bolt operate widely; airport to centre costs about Ksh 900–1,500. Regular taxis take cash only, so agree the fare first.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is on the left. Traffic is heavy, especially at rush hour. Only worth it if you're heading out of the city.
  • ✗WalkingPossible in some areas by day, but not recommended at night. No bike share.

Climate

Nairobi sits high, so days are mild year-round and nights are cool. Rain comes in two seasons: long rains in April–May and short rains in November.

  • ☀️Best time: January to March and June to SeptemberWarm days around 23–27°C with the fewest rain days. Good for parks, markets and day trips.
  • 🌧️Long rains in April and MayApril has about 26 rain days. Expect heavy afternoon showers, muddy trails and slower traffic.
  • 🌦️Short rains in NovemberAbout 23 rain days, usually afternoon downpours. Mornings are often clear.
  • 🧥Cool nights all yearLows sit around 12–16°C. Bring a light jacket or fleece for evenings and early game drives.
  • 🌤️July and August are cool and dryHighs near 23°C with only 5–7 rain days. Pack layers; mornings can feel chilly.
  • 🧳What to packLight layers, a rain jacket, comfortable shoes and sunscreen. Add a warm layer for nights and a hat for the sun.

Landmarks 18

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Nairobi National Park

    The only national park inside a capital city, where you can see lions, rhinos and giraffes against a skyline backdrop. Go early morning for the best wildlife sightings and cooler temperatures; arrange a guided game drive or self-drive with a 4x4.

  2. 2

    Giraffe Centre

    A conservation centre where you can hand-feed endangered Rothschild's giraffes from a raised platform. Visit on weekdays to avoid crowds; tickets are available at the gate and include a short educational talk.

  3. 3

    Karen Blixen Museum

    The former home of the author of 'Out of Africa', preserved with original furnishings and lush gardens. Take a guided tour for context; it's a short drive from the Giraffe Centre, so combine both in one trip.

  4. 4

    Nairobi National Museum

    Kenya's flagship museum, covering human origins, ethnography and natural history, including the famous Turkana Boy fossil. Allow at least two hours; it's centrally located and easily reached by taxi.

  5. 5

    Kenyatta International Convention Centre

    An iconic tower with a rooftop helipad offering panoramic views of Nairobi. Go on a clear day for the best photos; the observation deck is open to visitors for a small fee.

  6. 6

    Karura Forest

    A serene urban forest with walking and cycling trails, waterfalls and caves. Rent a bike at the entrance or join a guided walk; go early to avoid the heat and bring water.

  7. 7

    Bomas of Kenya

    A cultural village showcasing traditional homesteads and dance performances from Kenya's ethnic groups. Check the performance schedule in advance; it's a short drive from the city centre.

  8. 8

    Ngong Hills

    A scenic ridge offering hiking trails and sweeping views over the Rift Valley. Arrange a guided hike for safety; start early and bring sun protection and water.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day trip to Nairobi National Park at dawn; go with a guide and check park fees on the day.
    • Swap a city afternoon for the David Sheldrick elephant orphanage visit, which runs for about an hour in the morning.
    • Take a half-day to Karura Forest for walking and bike trails; go with a friend and in daylight.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; matatus (minibuses) are the main public transport.
    • Matatus are cheap but crowded and can be risky; use them with caution.
    • Taxis are available but negotiate fare before; cash only.
    • Uber and Bolt operate widely; rides from airport to centre cost Ksh 900-1,500.
    • Driving is on the left; traffic is heavy, especially at rush hour.
    • Walking is possible in some areas but not recommended at night; no bike share.

    Where to stay

    • Westlands · ≈ KES 6,000–18,000 a night
      Central for restaurants, bars and malls. Easy pickup point for tours and taxis to most sights.
    • Karen · ≈ KES 9,000–30,000 a night
      Green and quiet, close to the Giraffe Centre, the elephant orphanage and Karen Blixen Museum.
    • Gigiri · ≈ KES 10,000–28,000 a night
      Leafy and calm near the UN and Village Market; good for families and longer stays.
    • City Centre · ≈ KES 4,000–14,000 a night
      Cheapest base and walkable to the market and museums by day. Quieter at night; take taxis after dark.

    Food & drink

    • Try nyama choma (grilled meat) with kachumbari salad and ugali; order by weight and share.
    • Eat Kenyan-Indian food: samosas, bhajias and chapati are cheap and everywhere.
    • Carnivore near Langata is the famous meat restaurant; book ahead and check current prices.
    • Cheap eats: street stalls and small cafeterias serve a plate of rice, beans and greens for a few hundred KES.
    • Drink only bottled or filtered water; peel fruit yourself and go easy on raw salads at street stalls.

    Tips

    1. Nairobi National Park borders the city, offering wildlife viewing against a skyscraper skyline
    2. Use Uber or Bolt instead of hailing street taxis or navigating Matatus
    3. Avoid walking at night in unfamiliar areas for safety reasons
    4. The high altitude means evenings can get surprisingly chilly, so bring a light jacket
    5. M-Pesa (mobile money) is used everywhere, though cash and cards are still accepted in tourist spots
