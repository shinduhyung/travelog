Getting around

Metro and walking for the centre; tap a bank card and go.

  • ✓Metro ★Lines A–D plus two funiculars run about 05:00–00:00. Fastest way between the centre, the station and Fourvière.
  • ✓TramFive tram lines link the centre, campuses and suburbs.
  • ✓BusBuses cover the city, but some stop after 21:00. Check the last bus on the day.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis and apps like Uber and Bolt work. Taxis wait at stations and the airport.
  • ✗Rental carNot needed in the centre. Parking is tight; use transit instead.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is walkable. Vélo'v bikes and the Navigône ferry help on nice days.
  • ✓FerryThe Navigône river ferry crosses the Saône on nice days. Check times on the day.

Climate

Semi-continental climate: warm to hot summers, cold winters, and rain spread across the year.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberWarm days around 20–28°C. July and August are hottest and driest.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 7–13 rain days every month. May and January are wettest; August is driest.
  • ❄️Cold wintersDecember to February highs near 7–8°C and lows near 1–2°C. Bring a warm coat.
  • 🌡️Hot summer afternoonsJuly and August reach about 28°C. Sightsee in the morning and rest in the shade at midday.
  • 🍂Mild spring and autumnApril, May, September and October are comfortable for walking, with highs of 17–23°C.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes for cobbles. Add a hat and sunscreen for summer.

Landmarks 9

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière

    Lyon's most famous sight, a hilltop basilica with sweeping views over the city and the Saône. It is a working religious site, so dress respectfully and keep quiet inside. Go early morning or late afternoon for softer light and fewer crowds; you can walk up from Vieux Lyon or take the funicular.

  2. 2

    Vieux Lyon

    One of Europe's largest Renaissance old towns, a maze of cobbled lanes and hidden passageways worth wandering for an afternoon. It sits at the foot of Fourvière, so pair the two in one outing. Come on a weekday to avoid the heaviest crowds.

  3. 3

    Traboules of Croix-Rousse

    These covered passageways were used by silk workers and let you cut through buildings between streets. They are a distinctive Lyon experience and best explored on a guided walk, since some are private and closed to visitors. Wear comfortable shoes for the hills.

  4. 4

    Parc de la Tête d'Or

    A large central park with a lake, gardens and a free botanical and zoo area, ideal for a relaxed break. It is free to enter and easy to reach by metro or bus. Visit in the morning for calm and cooler weather.

  5. 5

    Place Bellecour

    A vast open square in the heart of the city, a natural meeting point between the two rivers and the old town. It is free and open at all hours. Use it as a landmark to orient yourself before heading to Vieux Lyon or the shops.

  6. 6

    Cathédrale Saint-Jean-Baptiste

    A Gothic cathedral in Vieux Lyon with fine stained glass and an astronomical clock. Entry to the cathedral is generally free, though dress modestly. It is a short walk from the Saône and fits easily into an old-town stroll.

  7. 7

    Musée des Confluences

    A striking modern museum at the meeting of the Rhône and Saône, covering natural history and world cultures. Its architecture alone is worth the trip. Check opening days and buy tickets online to skip queues.

  8. 8

    Saône River Cruise

    A boat trip on the Saône gives a different view of the old town and the hills. It is a good way to rest your feet after walking. Book a daytime departure and arrive a little early for a good seat.

    Swap in instead

    Getting around

    • Metro lines A-D plus two funiculars run about 05:00-00:00.
    • Five tram lines link the centre, campuses and suburbs.
    • Tap any contactless bank card: about €2.10 per 1-hr journey, capped near €6.30/day.
    • Buses cover the city; some stop after 21:00.
    • Taxis and ride apps like Uber and Bolt work; taxis wait at stations and the airport.
    • The centre is walkable; Vélo'v bikes and the Navigône ferry help on nice days.

    Where to stay

    • Presqu'île · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      Between the two rivers. Walk to Vieux Lyon, the shops and most restaurants. Best all-round base.
    • Vieux Lyon · ≈ EUR 100–260 a night
      Cobbled lanes, traboules and the funicular up to Fourvière. Quiet at night, touristy by day.
    • Croix-Rousse · ≈ EUR 70–170 a night
      The old silk-workers' hill. Village feel, markets and views. Steep walks down to the centre.
    • Part-Dieu · ≈ EUR 80–190 a night
      Business district by the main train station and airport bus. Handy for day trips, less charming.

    Food & drink

    • Try a bouchon: small Lyonnais restaurants serving quenelles, andouillette and salade lyonnaise. Book ahead for dinner.
    • Les Halles de Lyon Paul Bocuse is the covered food market. Go hungry, taste cheese and charcuterie, and eat lunch at a counter.
    • Order local dishes: pike quenelle, rosette de Lyon sausage, and praline tart for dessert.
    • Cheap eats: a sandwich or kebab in Presqu'île, or a slice of pizza near the university, roughly €8–15.
    • Lunch menus (formule) are much cheaper than dinner. Expect about €18–30 at a mid-range bouchon.

    Tips

    1. Eat at a traditional 'Bouchon' to experience rich, authentic Lyonnaise cuisine
    2. Explore the 'traboules' — hidden Renaissance passageways cutting through buildings in Vieux Lyon and Croix-Rousse
    3. Take the funicular up to the Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière for spectacular views
    4. A Lyon City Card is a great value for unlimited transit and access to major museums
    5. It offers a much more relaxed, intimate pace compared to the crowds of Paris
