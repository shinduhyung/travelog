Getting around

Walk the centre and use trams for longer hops; kiosk or app tickets are cheapest.

  • ✗MetroNo metro. Trams are the main public transport.
  • ✓Tram ★Trams run 24 hours: 15 day lines and 4 night lines. Buy tickets at kiosks or the MOJ ZET app, cheaper than from the driver. A short 30-minute ticket costs about €0.53 at a kiosk.
  • ✓BusZET buses cover the suburbs and use the same tickets as trams.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis, Bolt and Uber all work. Airport rides cost about €14–20.
  • ✗Rental carNot needed in the centre; walking and trams cover the sights. Consider only for trips outside the city.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is best walked. Public bikes cost about €0.66 per 30 minutes.

Climate

Continental climate: cold winters, warm summers, and rain spread through the year, wettest in May.

  • 🌤️Best time: May to SeptemberWarm days from 21°C to 28°C. June and September are the sweet spots; July and August are hottest.
  • 🌧️May is the wettest monthAbout 15 rain days. Pack a rain jacket and keep indoor plans ready.
  • ❄️Winter is coldJanuary highs near 5°C and lows around -2°C. December and February are only a little milder.
  • 🧥Layers all yearEvenings cool down in summer. Bring a light layer in warm months and a warm coat from November to March.
  • ☀️Summer heat is manageableJuly and August peak near 28°C with mild nights around 18°C. Sightsee early and rest at midday.
  • 🎒What to packComfortable walking shoes, a rain jacket, sunglasses, and warm layers for winter. An umbrella helps in spring and autumn.

Landmarks 10

Must-see · 9

  1. 1

    Zagreb Cathedral

    Croatia's tallest church and the city's defining spire, with a richly restored interior and a treasury. Go early morning to have the square in front mostly to yourself, and dress modestly since it is an active place of worship.

  2. 2

    St. Mark's Church

    Famous for its brightly tiled roof bearing the city and national coats of arms, sitting on the cobbled heart of Gornji Grad. It is a short walk up from Ban Jelačić Square; check opening hours before going as interior access is limited.

  3. 3

    Gornji Grad

    The old upper town of lanes, squares and historic buildings that gives Zagreb its character. Wander it on foot in the late afternoon, combining it with St. Mark's Church and the Stone Gate nearby.

  4. 4

    Ban Jelačić Square

    The central square and meeting point of the city, ringed by cafés and the starting point for most walks. Come in the evening when it is liveliest, and use it as your orientation point for the lower town.

  5. 5

    Dolac Market

    The colourful open-air market where locals buy produce, cheese and flowers. Arrive before noon for the fullest stalls, and bring small cash for the vendors.

  6. 6

    Stone Gate

    A medieval gate that survived a fire and now shelters a shrine with a revered icon, still visited by pilgrims. It sits on the walk between the square and Gornji Grad, so it is easy to include on a walking route.

  7. 7

    Lotrščak Tower

    A historic tower with a daily cannon firing at noon and views over the old town. Climb it around midday to catch the cannon, and note the small entry fee.

  8. 8

    Museum of Broken Relationships

    A quirky, moving museum of objects donated after breakups, one of the city's most talked-about sights. It is compact, so allow about an hour; buy tickets on arrival.

  9. 9

    Mirogoj Cemetery

    A grand park-like cemetery with arcades and domes, often described as an open-air gallery. It is a short bus or tram ride from the centre; go in daylight and dress for the weather.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for the Plitvice Lakes: long day trip, book transport and tickets ahead.
    • Take the short trip to Samobor for cream cakes and easy hill walks.
    • Ride the cable car to Sljeme on Medvednica for views and a walk, if the weather is clear.

    Getting around

    • Trams run 24 hours: 15 day lines and 4 night lines.
    • ZET buses cover the suburbs and use the same tickets as trams.
    • Buy tickets at kiosks or the MOJ ZET app; cheaper than from the driver.
    • A short 30-minute ticket costs about €0.53 at a kiosk.
    • Taxis, Bolt and Uber all work; airport rides cost about €14-20.
    • The centre is best walked; public bikes cost about €0.66 per 30 min.

    Where to stay

    • Donji Grad · ≈ EUR 70–150 a night
      Flat, central grid of parks and museums. Walk to everything; good tram links.
    • Gornji Grad · ≈ EUR 80–170 a night
      The old Upper Town: quiet lanes, St Mark's Church and the Stone Gate on your doorstep.
    • Lower Town centre · ≈ EUR 75–160 a night
      Around Ban Jelačić Square and the Dolac market. Busiest spot for food and nightlife.
    • near Glavni Kolodvor · ≈ EUR 65–140 a night
      By the main train and bus stations. Easy airport bus and day trips; 10–15 minutes' walk to the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Try štrukli (baked cheese pastry), sarma and punjene paprike at a konoba (tavern).
    • Dolac market, just above the main square: fruit, cheese and honey in the morning; go early.
    • Grab a cheap lunch of burek or čevapi from a bakery or grill; expect roughly €5–10.
    • Coffee is a ritual: sit at a terrace for a small espresso or a 'bijela kava'.
    • For nightlife food, look around Tkalčićeva street and the Lower Town; prices vary, check menus.

    Tips

    1. Cafe culture is a serious local pastime; locals spend hours drinking espresso on the terraces of Tkalčićeva street
    2. Visit the unique and highly acclaimed Museum of Broken Relationships in the Upper Town
    3. The Grič cannon fires every day exactly at noon from the Lotrščak Tower — do not be startled
    4. Zagreb's Christmas Market (Advent) is consistently voted one of the best in Europe
    5. Validate your paper tram ticket at the yellow machines upon entering to avoid fines

    Souvenirs

    Licitar heart

    EUR 5–121 small piece · estimate

    A painted gingerbread heart decorated with bright glaze and mirror-like shapes. It is a traditional Croatian craft that is closely linked to Zagreb and its folk culture.

    Where: Dolac Market craft stalls or Licitar workshop shops
    These hearts are for decoration only and are not meant to be eaten.

    Croatian necktie

    EUR 50–1501 piece · estimate

    The cravat is the traditional Croatian neckwear that gave the word 'cravat' to the world. Zagreb is the city most often linked to its origin.

    Where: Croata, Ilica 5 Oktogon
    Check whether the tie is silk and where it was made before you buy.
