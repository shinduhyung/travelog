Getting around

Walk in the centre; use U-Bahn and trams for longer hops and always validate your ticket.

  • ✓Metro ★Rheinbahn runs U-Bahn, trams and buses. Single ticket €3.80, 24-hour pass €7.60 (2026). Validate before boarding; fines €60 for unstamped tickets.
  • ✓BusRheinbahn buses fill the gaps where trams don't run. Same tickets as U-Bahn and trams; validate before boarding.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsUber, Bolt and Free Now work in Düsseldorf. Handy for late nights and airport runs.
  • ✓Rental carThe city centre is an environmental zone; cars need a pollution sticker. Parking is limited, so a car is best for day trips out of town.
  • ✓Walking ★Walking is easy in the compact centre. Bike hire from about €9 a day is a good alternative.
  • ✗FerryNo ferry across the Rhine. Use the bridges to cross between the centre and Oberkassel.

Climate

Temperate and changeable: mild summers, cool winters, and rain in every month. Pack layers and an umbrella year-round.

  • 🌧️Rain all yearExpect 11–17 rain days every month. Showers pass quickly; a small umbrella and a waterproof jacket help.
  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs around 19–24°C and long evenings. Ideal for the Rhine promenade, Altstadt terraces and parks.
  • 🧥Cool wintersDecember to February highs are 6–8°C with lows near 1–3°C. A warm coat, scarf and gloves are worth packing.
  • 🌡️Rarely very hotSummer highs top out near 24°C. Heatwaves are short; most days are comfortable for walking.
  • 🍂Good shoulder monthsApril, May, September and October are mild and less crowded. Rain is still likely, so keep plans flexible.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket, an umbrella and comfortable walking shoes. Add a warm coat and gloves for November to March.

Landmarks 6

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    Rheinturm

    Düsseldorf's most famous landmark, a slender concrete television tower on the Rhine with an observation deck and restaurant offering sweeping views over the city and river. Go near sunset for the best light, and check opening hours before you climb since the deck can close early in winter.

  2. 2

    Altstadt, Düsseldorf

    The historic old town, known as the 'longest bar in the world' for its dense cluster of pubs and breweries, is the heart of the city and a must for a first visit. Wander it on foot in the late afternoon, then settle in for an evening of Altbier.

  3. 3

    Königsallee

    Düsseldorf's grand boulevard, split by a wide canal lined with trees and high-end shops, is the city's elegant showcase. It is free to stroll; come on a weekday morning for a quieter walk and good photos along the water.

  4. 4

    Schloss Benrath

    A pink rococo palace set in parkland on the southern edge of the city, one of Düsseldorf's finest historic sights. Reach it by tram or S-Bahn, and book a guided interior tour in advance as access is by tour only.

  5. 5

    St. Lambertus Basilica

    The old town's landmark church with its distinctive twisted spire, a short walk from the Rhine promenade. Entry is free and quiet; visit in the morning to avoid crowds and dress respectfully.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city afternoon for Schloss Benrath: a palace and gardens south of town, reachable by tram or S-Bahn. Check opening days.
    • Take the train to Cologne for the cathedral and old town, roughly 25–35 minutes each way. Check times and fares on the day.
    • On a rainy day, swap a walk for the Kunstpalast or the Film Museum. Check current exhibitions and prices.

    Getting around

    • Rheinbahn operates U-Bahn, trams, and buses; single ticket €3.80, 24-hour pass €7.60 (2026).
    • Validate tickets before boarding; fines €60 for unstamped tickets.
    • Ride apps Uber, Bolt, and Free Now work in Düsseldorf.
    • Driving: city centre is an environmental zone; cars need a pollution sticker.
    • Walking is easy in the compact centre; bike hire from ~€9/day.
    • No ferry across the Rhine; use bridges for crossing.

    Where to stay

    • Altstadt · ≈ EUR 110–220 a night

    • Oberkassel · ≈ EUR 120–240 a night

    Food & drink

    • Try Rheinish dishes: Himmel und Erde (black pudding with apple and potato), Sauerbraten and Reibekuchen (potato fritters).
    • Drink Altbier in the Altstadt brewpubs. It comes in small glasses and is refilled without asking; say when to stop.
    • Go to the Carlsplatz market for snacks, cheese and coffee. Check opening hours on the day.
    • Rheinish mustard and Printen gingerbread make easy gifts. Look in the Altstadt shops.
    • Book dinner ahead on weekends. Many kitchens close earlier on Sundays; check on the day.

    Tips

    1. Try Altbier (traditional dark beer) in the historic breweries of the Altstadt
    2. The Altstadt is dubbed the 'longest bar in the world' due to its hundreds of interconnected pubs
    3. Königsallee (the Kö) is one of Europe's premier luxury shopping streets, complete with a scenic canal
    4. The city has the largest Japanese community in Germany; don't miss the authentic ramen in the Little Tokyo district
    5. Rivalry with neighboring Cologne is a running joke; avoid ordering Kölsch (Cologne beer) in Düsseldorf
