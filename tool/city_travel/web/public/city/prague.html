Getting around

Walk the centre; use metro and trams for longer hops.

  • ✓Metro ★Three lines (A, B, C) run roughly 05:00–24:00. Fast for crossing the city; not needed inside the old centre.
  • ✓Tram ★Trams cover the centre well. Buy tickets at yellow machines or in apps. A 90-minute ticket costs about 50 CZK; a 24-hour ticket about 150 CZK.
  • ✓BusBuses mostly serve the outskirts and the airport. Use metro and trams for sightseeing.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsUber and Bolt work in Prague. Use them to avoid taxi touts.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the centre is slow and parking is restricted. Use P+R lots and take transit in.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is walkable. Charles Bridge and Old Town are best on foot.

Climate

Four clear seasons: cold winters, warm summers and mild spring and autumn. Rain is spread through the year.

  • 🌡️Best time: May, June and SeptemberHighs around 19–25°C with long days. Comfortable for walking the castle and old town.
  • ❄️Winter is cold (December to February)Highs of 4–7°C and lows near or below freezing. Pack a warm coat, hat and gloves.
  • ☔Rain all yearAbout 8–12 rain days every month. A small umbrella or rain jacket is always useful.
  • 🥾Cobblestones need good shoesThe old centre is paved with uneven stones. Wear comfortable, grippy shoes, especially when wet.
  • 🌤️Summer is warm, not extremeJuly and August peak near 26°C. Start sightseeing early and take a midday break.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for changeable days, a waterproof layer, walking shoes and a warm coat for winter.

Landmarks 26

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Charles Bridge

    Prague's most iconic sight, a 14th-century stone bridge lined with baroque statues and framed by the castle skyline. Go at sunrise to enjoy it before the crowds arrive, and note it is pedestrian-only.

  2. 2

    Prague Castle

    The vast hilltop complex is the historic seat of Czech rulers and dominates the city's skyline. Book castle circuit tickets online and allow at least half a day; it is a steep uphill walk or a short tram ride from Lesser Town.

  3. 3

    Old Town Square

    The heart of the old city, ringed by colorful facades and home to the famous astronomical clock. Come a few minutes before the hour to see the clock's apostles perform, but expect big crowds.

  4. 4

    St. Vitus Cathedral

    A soaring Gothic cathedral inside Prague Castle with stunning stained glass and royal tombs. Dress respectfully and arrive early, as security lines at the castle grow quickly after mid-morning.

  5. 5

    Prague Astronomical Clock

    A medieval mechanical marvel on the Old Town Hall that draws a crowd every hour for its moving figures. Stand to the side for a better view, and consider climbing the tower for rooftop views over the square.

  6. 6

    Petřín Lookout Tower

    A miniature Eiffel-style tower on a green hill offering the best panorama of the city. Walk up through the park or take the funicular, then climb the stairs for the view on a clear day.

  7. 7

    Church of Our Lady before Týn

    The twin Gothic spires rising over Old Town Square are one of Prague's defining images. The interior is free to enter and quieter than the square outside; check opening hours before visiting.

  8. 8

    Dancing House

    A playful modern landmark by the river designed by Gehry and Milunić. View it from the riverside embankment, and book ahead if you want to visit the rooftop bar for views.

    Swap in instead

    • Day trip to Kutná Hora: train from the main station, about an hour each way, to see the bone church and old town.
    • Swap one museum for a half-day at Vyšehrad: fortress walls, a cemetery of famous Czechs and river views.
    • Take a tram to Letná or Petřín for a park walk and a beer garden with a city view.

    Getting around

    • Metro has 3 lines (A, B, C) and runs roughly 05:00–24:00.
    • Trams cover the centre well; buy tickets at yellow machines or in apps.
    • A 90-minute ticket costs about 50 CZK; a 24-hour ticket about 150 CZK.
    • Ride apps Uber and Bolt work in Prague; use them to avoid taxi touts.
    • Driving in the centre is slow and parking is restricted; use P+R lots.
    • The centre is walkable; Charles Bridge and Old Town are best on foot.

    Where to stay

    • Staré Město · ≈ CZK 1,800–4,500 a night
      Steps from Old Town Square and Charles Bridge. Walk to most sights; noisier at night.
    • Malá Strana · ≈ CZK 1,600–4,000 a night
      Quiet cobbled lanes under the castle. Romantic and close to the bridge; fewer trams.
    • Vinohrady · ≈ CZK 1,200–2,800 a night
      Leafy, local and full of cafés and restaurants. Good value; 15–20 minutes by tram or metro to the centre.
    • Nové Město · ≈ CZK 1,400–3,200 a night
      Central and practical around Wenceslas Square. Main train station and metro nearby; busy streets.

    Food & drink

    • Try Czech classics: svíčková (beef in cream sauce), goulash with bread dumplings, and fried cheese.
    • Prague is famous for beer. A half-litre of local lager is often cheaper than a coffee; prices vary by pub.
    • Snack on a trdelník (chimney cake) from a street stall, or grab a chlebíček open sandwich from a deli.
    • Lunch menus (polední menu) at local pubs are the best value: a soup and main for a modest price.
    • Book popular dinner spots ahead for weekends. Many kitchens close early on Sunday.

    Tips

    1. Validate your transit ticket in the yellow machines before your first ride, or face steep fines
    2. Czech beer (Pilsner) is world-famous and often cheaper than bottled water in restaurants
    3. Avoid exchanging money at street kiosks in the tourist center to avoid terrible exchange rates; use ATMs
    4. The Astronomical Clock chimes every hour, but beware of pickpockets in the gathered crowd
    5. Wear comfortable, flat shoes; the beautiful cobblestone streets will destroy high heels

    Souvenirs

    Bohemian crystal glass

    CZK 600–15001–2 pieces · shop prices

    Czech cut glass, famous for its clear, sparkling finish. Prague has made fine crystal for centuries, so it is a classic local keepsake.

    Where: Rückl official store or Prague City Tourism shop
    Moser's high-end pieces cost far more than most budgets, so choose a simpler Bohemian glass instead.

    Marionette

    CZK 1900–35001 small piece · shop prices

    A hand-carved wooden puppet. Prague has a long puppetry tradition, and marionettes are a familiar sight in the city's puppet shops and theaters.

    Where: Truhlář Marionety, Malá Strana
    Look for small wooden puppets from Truhlář, which are the usual size for a souvenir.

    Becherovka

    CZK 350–600700 ml bottle · estimate

    A herbal liqueur with cinnamon and other spices, made in the nearby spa town of Karlovy Vary. It is a popular Czech drink sold across Prague.

    Where: Becherovka Museum shop
