Getting around

Walk the centre; use the metro for longer hops and ride apps for late nights.

  • ✓Metro ★Two lines cover the centre and main sights. Buy a Metromoney card at stations.
  • ✓BusBuses and marshrutkas reach most of the city. Pay with the card or contactless.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Bolt and Yandex Go work well and are usually cheaper than street taxis. Street taxis often quote high — agree the price first or use an app.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is chaotic. Most visitors skip renting a car in town.
  • ✓WalkingThe centre is walkable, but pavements are uneven, so wear good shoes.

Climate

Tbilisi has four clear seasons: cold winters, mild springs, hot dry-ish summers and a warm, pleasant autumn.

  • 🌡️Best months: May, September and OctoberMild to warm days around 17–25°C. September is warm and drier than spring.
  • ☀️Hot summer (July–August)Highs near 30°C. Sightsee in the morning, then rest or stay indoors in the afternoon.
  • 🌧️Wettest in springMarch, April and May have the most rain days (12–14). Carry a small umbrella.
  • ❄️Cold winter (December–February)Highs of 6–8°C and lows near or below freezing. A warm coat and shoes are essential.
  • 🍂Autumn is a sweet spotSeptember and October are warm and drier, with 7–9 rain days. Great for walking and day trips.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for spring and autumn, a warm coat for winter, and light clothes plus sun protection for summer. Good walking shoes for uneven pavements.

Landmarks 17

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Narikala Fortress

    The ancient hilltop citadel is Tbilisi's defining sight, with sweeping views over the Old Town and river. Go in late afternoon for cooler air and sunset light; walk up from the Old Town or take the cable car.

  2. 2

    Old Tbilisi

    The maze of lanes, balconies and bathhouses is the city's historic heart and best explored on foot. Start early to avoid crowds and wear comfortable shoes for the cobbled, hilly streets.

  3. 3

    Holy Trinity Cathedral of Tbilisi

    One of the largest Orthodox cathedrals in the world, it dominates the skyline and is a must for its scale and frescoes. Dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees) and visit outside service times for quiet viewing.

  4. 4

    Bridge of Peace

    The glass-and-steel pedestrian bridge is a modern icon linking the Old Town with Rike Park. Visit at dusk when the LED lights switch on; it is a short walk from the sulfur baths.

  5. 5

    Chronicles of Georgia

    This monumental colonnade of carved pillars tells Georgia's history and offers wide views over the city and sea. It is a bit out of the centre, so take a taxi and go in the morning for soft light.

  6. 6

    Tbilisi Sulfur Baths

    The domed bathhouses in Abanotubani are where the city's name comes from and a classic Tbilisi experience. Book a private room in advance and bring a towel; evenings are busiest.

  7. 7

    Metekhi Church

    Perched on a cliff above the Kura River, this historic church frames one of the city's most photographed views. Combine it with a walk through the Old Town and cross the nearby Bridge of Peace.

  8. 8

    Mtatsminda Park

    A hilltop amusement park reached by a funicular, with panoramic views and a giant Ferris wheel. Ride up in the late afternoon and stay for sunset over the city.

    Build my plan

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Mtskheta, the old capital: about 30 minutes by marshrutka or taxi, with the Jvari church viewpoint.
    • Add a day trip to the Kakheti wine region (Sighnaghi, Telavi). Go with a driver or tour so you can taste wine safely.
    • Take the cable car up to Narikala and walk down through the Old Town; finish at the sulphur baths in Abanotubani.

    Getting around

    • Metro has two lines; buy a Metromoney card at stations.
    • Buses and marshrutkas cover most of the city; card or contactless payment.
    • Bolt and Yandex Go work well and are usually cheaper than street taxis.
    • Street taxis often quote high; agree the price first or use an app.
    • Driving is chaotic; most visitors skip renting a car in town.
    • The centre is walkable; pavements are uneven, so wear good shoes.

    Where to stay

    • Old Town (Abanotubani) · ≈ GEL 80–300 a night
      Walk to the sulphur baths, Narikala and the main lanes. Lively but can be noisy at night.
    • Rustaveli & Freedom Square · ≈ GEL 120–450 a night
      Central and well connected: metro, museums and the main avenue on your doorstep.
    • Vera & Chugureti · ≈ GEL 70–250 a night
      Quieter, local feel with cafés and bars; a short walk or cheap ride to the centre.
    • Sololaki · ≈ GEL 90–320 a night
      Historic streets with old balconies, between Freedom Square and the Old Town.

    Food & drink

    • Try khinkali (dumplings) and khachapuri (cheese bread). Eat khinkali by hand, hold the top knot and sip the juice first.
    • Book a long dinner: Georgian tables fill with plates to share. Expect GEL 30–70 per person without much wine.
    • Wine is the local pride. Ask for qvevri (clay-jar) wines by the glass; a good bottle is often GEL 25–60 in a restaurant.
    • Cheap eats: bakeries sell fresh khachapuri and lobiani for a few GEL. Street food and small cafeterias keep meals under GEL 15.
    • Vegetarians do well: lobio (beans), badrijani (aubergine rolls) and pkhali (walnut vegetable pâtés) are everywhere.

    Tips

    1. Relax in the ancient sulfur baths in the Abanotubani district
    2. Georgia is one of the world's oldest wine regions; explore local varieties like Saperavi
    3. Take the cable car to Narikala Fortress for incredible views of the city
    4. The historic center is a maze of charming balconies and steep alleys
    5. Download Bolt for cheap and transparent taxi services

      Souvenirs

      Minankari enamel earrings

      GEL 60–1801 pair · estimate

      Minankari is a Georgian enamel art style with fine colored patterns on metal. It is a traditional craft closely linked to Tbilisi's artisans.

      Where: Dry Bridge Market or Gallery 27
      Check that the silver is real and ask whether the enamel is hand-made.

      Kvevri Georgian wine

      GEL 25–60750 ml · estimate

      Kvevri wine is made by fermenting grapes in large clay jars buried in the ground. Georgia has used this method for thousands of years.

      Where: 8000 Vintages, Tbilisi
      Check the grape variety and whether the bottle is an amber wine, which has skin contact.

      Churchkhela

      GEL 10–202 to 3 pieces · estimate

      Churchkhela is a candy made from nuts strung on a thread and dipped in thickened grape juice. It is a classic Georgian snack sold across the country.

      Where: Badagi official shop or Dezerter Bazaar
      Check the production date and look for no added coloring.
