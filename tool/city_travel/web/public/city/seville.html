Getting around

Walk the old town, since the main sights are compact. Use buses or taxis for longer trips.

  • ✓MetroOne line runs south-west to south-east and misses the old town. Tickets cost €1.30 for one zone.
  • ✓BusCity buses are frequent and cover most of the city. A single fare is €1.40, or €0.35–0.38 with a rechargeable bonobus card.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis are easy to find. Cabify, Uber, Bolt and FreeNow all operate, and Cabify is the local favourite. Check the fare before you ride.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the old city is tricky. Park in a hotel garage, and never leave anything in the car because break-ins are common.
  • ✓Walking ★Walking is the best way to see the sights, since the main points are compact.
  • ✓TramTrams run about 2 km from Prado de San Sebastián to Plaza Nueva, past the cathedral. Most visitors walk this stretch instead.
  • ✓Bike share and scootersSevici bikes and Lime scooters are available around the city.
  • ✗FerryThere is no ferry.

Climate

Hot, dry summers and mild, sunny winters. Spring and autumn are the best times to walk the old city.

  • ☀️Best time: March to May and October to NovemberMild days, roughly 16–27°C, and comfortable evenings. Best for walking and outdoor tapas.
  • 🥵Summer is extreme (June to August)Highs reach about 33–37°C with almost no rain. Visit sights early, rest midday and go out again after dark.
  • 🌧️Rain is mostly in winter and springRainy days cluster from October to March, with the most in March. Showers are usually short.
  • 🌙Cool winter eveningsDecember to February can dip to about 6–8°C at night. Bring a jacket for evening walks.
  • 🧴Strong sun, even in springSun is strong from April. Use sunscreen, a hat and water, and seek shade in the middle of the day.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes for summer, layers for winter, comfortable walking shoes for cobbled streets, and a small umbrella for spring and autumn.

Landmarks 17

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Seville Cathedral

    One of the largest Gothic cathedrals in the world, it holds the tomb of Christopher Columbus and a richly decorated interior. Its scale alone makes it a must-see for first-time visitors. Go early in the morning or late in the afternoon to avoid the heaviest crowds, and buy tickets online in advance.

  2. 2

    Alcázar of Seville

    A working royal palace with Mudéjar palace halls, tiled courtyards and formal gardens that rank among Spain's finest. It is one of the most atmospheric places in the city. Book a timed entry ticket ahead, as queues can build up, and dress for walking outdoors.

  3. 3

    Giralda

    The bell tower of the cathedral, originally built as a minaret, and the city's most recognisable landmark. Climbing it rewards you with wide views over the rooftops. Visit with the cathedral ticket, and wear comfortable shoes since the ascent is by ramp.

  4. 4

    Plaza de España, Seville

    A grand half-circle of brick and tile built for the 1929 Ibero-American Exhibition, with canal-side bridges and painted alcoves for each province. It is especially photogenic in the golden hour. Reach it by walking through Parque de María Luisa, which is a short stroll away.

  5. 5

    Barrio de Santa Cruz

    The old Jewish quarter, a maze of narrow lanes, orange-tree squares and whitewashed houses. It is the best area to wander and eat tapas. Visit in the cooler morning or evening, as the lanes get crowded at midday.

  6. 6

    Casa de Pilatos

    A 16th-century palace blending Mudéjar and Renaissance styles, with azulejo-tiled patios and a lovely garden. It is a quieter alternative to the Alcázar. The ground floor entry ticket is inexpensive and can be bought online.

  7. 7

    Torre del Oro

    A 13th-century riverside watchtower on the Guadalquivir, once used to guard the port and chain across the river. It is a short walk from the Triana Bridge and makes a good stop on a riverside walk. Go in the late afternoon for pleasant light on the water.

    Swap in instead

    • Take a day trip to Córdoba by train to see the Mezquita and its historic old town.
    • Visit Itálica, the Roman ruins just outside Seville, for a quieter half-day.
    • Spend an afternoon at the Plaza de España and Parque de María Luisa for gardens and riverside walks.

    Getting around

    • Metro has one line that runs south-west to south-east and misses the old town. Tickets cost €1.30 for one zone.
    • Trams run about 2 km from Prado de San Sebastián to Plaza Nueva, past the cathedral. Most visitors walk this stretch instead.
    • City buses are frequent and cover most of the city. A single fare is €1.40, or €0.35–0.38 with a rechargeable bonobus card.
    • Taxis are easy to find. Cabify, Uber, Bolt and FreeNow all operate, and Cabify is the local favourite. Check the fare before you ride.
    • Driving in the old city is tricky. Park in a hotel garage, and never leave anything in the car because break-ins are common.
    • Walking is the best way to see the sights, since the main points are compact. Sevici bikes and Lime scooters are also available. There is no ferry.

    Where to stay

    • Santa Cruz · ≈ EUR 110–260 a night
      Walk to the Alcázar, the cathedral and the narrow lanes; very atmospheric, but streets are tight and quiet at night.
    • Triana · ≈ EUR 80–200 a night
      Across the river from the old town, with tapas bars and flamenco tablaos; a short walk to the centre.
    • El Arenal · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      Near the bullring and the river, a short walk to Santa Cruz and the cathedral; busy and convenient.
    • Macarena · ≈ EUR 70–170 a night
      Local neighbourhood north of the old town, with lower prices; a longer walk or bus ride to the main sights.

    Food & drink

    • Eat tapas in small bars: order a few plates to share, such as jamón, croquetas and pimientos de padrón.
    • Try the bars in Triana along Calle Betis and across the river for lively, local-style evenings.
    • Eat late: many locals dine after 21:00, so kitchens may be quieter before then.
    • Pick a sit-down café or bar for breakfast with toast and coffee, usually cheaper than a full meal (check prices on the day).
    • Ask for the menu del día at lunch for a set meal at a good price (check on the day).
