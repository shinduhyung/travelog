Getting around

Walk the old quarters, and use city buses for the hills and the Alhambra.

  • ✓MetroOne metro line runs in Granada. A single ticket costs about €1.65 (Nov 2024). A rechargeable green travel card costs €1.50 at tobacco shops and needs at least €5 of credit.
  • ✓Bus ★Alhambra Buses cover most sights: C30 to the Alhambra, C31 to Albayzin and C34 to Sacromonte. Each trip costs €1.60 (Nov 2024). Multi-trip cards cost €5, €10 or €20.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are easy to find. Ride apps such as Uber or Cabify are available; check locally.
  • ✓Rental carDriving in the centre is restricted, with camera-enforced limits. Park outside the historic core or use a garage such as Parking HH Maristas.
  • ✓Walking ★Walking is the best way to see the old quarters, but streets are steep, narrow and uneven. Buses help on the hills.
  • ✗FerryNo ferry service applies to Granada.

Climate

Continental climate with hot summers, cold winters and a clear shoulder season in spring and autumn.

  • 🌸Best time: April to May and OctoberHighs around 20–25°C with fewer crowds. Ideal for the Alhambra and long walks in the old quarters.
  • 🥵Hot summer (June to August)Highs around 30–35°C and lows near 16–21°C. Visit sights early or late and rest at midday.
  • 🥶Cold winter (December to February)Highs around 13–15°C and lows near 3–5°C. Bring a warm jacket for evenings and early mornings.
  • 🌧️Rain is most likely in March and NovemberAround 8–12 rainy days in those months. Pack a light waterproof and check the forecast before day trips.
  • ☀️Sunny most of the yearRain falls on only 2–4 days in June to August. Sunscreen and a hat matter from late spring onward.
  • 🧳What to packComfortable shoes with good grip for steep, uneven streets. Layers for cool evenings, plus sun protection in summer.

Landmarks 10

Must-see · 7

  1. 1

    Alhambra

    The Nasrid palace complex on a hill above the city is Granada's defining sight, with intricate Islamic stucco, tiled courtyards and the Nasrid Palaces. Timed-entry tickets sell out, so book well ahead, especially for evening visits. Wear comfortable shoes, because the site is large and sloped.

  2. 2

    Generalife

    The summer palace and gardens sit next to the Alhambra and are known for their water channels, terraces and cypress-lined paths. It is best seen in late afternoon, when the light is soft. Entry is usually bundled with Alhambra tickets, so check your booking.

  3. 3

    Albayzín

    This hillside quarter of whitewashed lanes and cave-style houses has some of the best views of the Alhambra, especially from its miradors. Walk it in the late afternoon or at sunset. Wear sturdy shoes, as the streets are steep and cobbled.

  4. 4

    Sacromonte

    Sacromonte is known for its hillside caves, historic Romani community and flamenco. Many cave venues host evening shows. Go by taxi or a guided tour after dark, since the lanes are steep and poorly lit.

  5. 5

    Granada Cathedral

    The Renaissance cathedral in the city centre holds a richly decorated interior and a notable chapel. It is close to the Royal Chapel, so the two make an easy pairing. Visit in the morning, when the crowds are thinner.

  6. 6

    Royal Chapel of Granada

    The Gothic-Renaissance chapel holds the tombs of Ferdinand and Isabella and the Catholic Monarchs. It is a short walk from the cathedral in the historic centre. Buy tickets at the entrance and allow about an hour.

  7. 7

    Corral del Carbón

    This 14th-century former granary with a Moorish horseshoe arch is one of the few remaining Islamic-era buildings in the city centre. It is a quick stop on a walk through the old bazaar area. It is free to enter and easy to reach on foot.

    Getting around

    • One metro line runs in Granada. A single ticket costs about €1.65 (Nov 2024). A rechargeable green travel card costs €1.50 at tobacco shops and needs at least €5 of credit.
    • Local buses cover most sights. Alhambra Buses (C30 to the Alhambra, C31 to Albayzin, C34 to Sacromonte) cost €1.60 per trip (Nov 2024). Multi-trip cards cost €5, €10 or €20.
    • Taxis are easy to find. Ride apps such as Uber or Cabify: check locally.
    • Driving in the centre is restricted. Camera-enforced limits apply, so park outside the historic core or use a garage such as Parking HH Maristas.
    • Walking is the best way to see the old quarters, but streets are steep, narrow and uneven. Buses help on the hills.
    • No ferry service applies to Granada.

    Where to stay

    • Centro · ≈ EUR 70–180 a night
      Central base near Plaza Nueva and the Cathedral. Easy walks, many restaurants, but busy and noisy at night.
    • Albayzin · ≈ EUR 80–220 a night
      Historic hillside quarter facing the Alhambra. Great views and romantic streets, but steep and uneven. Bring comfortable shoes.
    • Realejo · ≈ EUR 60–160 a night
      Calm former Jewish quarter with local bars and a relaxed feel. Quieter than Centro and close to the historic core.
    • Sacromonte · ≈ EUR 60–150 a night
      Cave-house neighbourhood known for flamenco. Atmospheric, but far from the centre and best reached by bus or taxi at night.

    Food & drink

    • Tapas often come free with a drink in many bars. Order a drink and ask what comes with it; check on the day.
    • Try the local dish of habas con jamón (broad beans with ham) and piononos, a small rolled sponge cake.
    • Eat early or book ahead for dinner. Many kitchens open around 20:00 to 21:00 and fill quickly in high season.
    • Visit a covered market such as Mercado San Agustín for fresh produce and cheap snacks.
    • Plan a tapas crawl in Centro or Realejo, where bars are close together and walkable.
