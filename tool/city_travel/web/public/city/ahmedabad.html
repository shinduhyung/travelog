Getting around

Auto rickshaw or Ola/Uber for most trips; walk the Old City on foot.

  • ✗MetroNo metro yet. Don't plan around rail inside the city.
  • ✓BusAMTS city buses and BRTS (Janmarg) buses run roughly 6AM–11PM. AMTS fares about ₹3–25; a ₹45 day pass exists but is rarely worth it. BRTS bus 1000 links the airport and Karnavati Club, max fare ₹50, every 30 min.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Ola and Uber work, plus local taxi apps. Auto rickshaws are cheap; agree the fare first or use the prepaid booth at the airport.
  • ✓Rental carPossible, but driving is hectic. Most visitors do better with autos and app cabs.
  • ✓Walking ★Best way to explore the Old City lanes. Use cycle rickshaws for short hops.

Climate

Hot and mostly dry, with a short, heavy monsoon from June to September. November to February is the comfortable window.

  • ☀️Best time: November to FebruaryHighs of 27–32°C and cool nights. Ideal for walking the Old City and day trips.
  • 🥵Very hot April to JuneHighs of 38–41°C. Do outdoor sights at sunrise or after dark and rest indoors at midday.
  • 🌧️Monsoon July to SeptemberUp to 23 rain days in July. Short heavy downpours; keep plans flexible and carry an umbrella.
  • 💧Humid in the rainsSticky from June to September. Light, quick-dry clothes help.
  • 🌡️Big day-to-night swing in winterJanuary lows near 14°C. A light jacket is useful in the evening.
  • 🧳What to packLight cotton clothes, a hat and sunscreen; an umbrella for monsoon months; a light layer for winter nights.

Landmarks 6

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Sabarmati Ashram

    This riverside ashram was Mahatma Gandhi's home and base during India's freedom struggle, making it the city's most meaningful pilgrimage for visitors. Go early morning to avoid heat and crowds; entry is free, and the museum is closed on some public holidays, so check ahead.

  2. 2

    Adalaj Stepwell

    A stunning five-storey stepwell carved with intricate Hindu and Islamic motifs, it is one of Gujarat's finest architectural treasures and a photographer's dream. It lies about 18 km north of the city; hire a taxi or auto for the half-day trip, and visit in the cooler morning hours.

  3. 3

    Jama Masjid, Ahmedabad

    Built in 1424, this mosque in the old city is famed for its yellow sandstone arches and the shaking minarets nearby, and it anchors the bustling heritage quarter. Dress modestly, remove shoes, and avoid prayer times; it is a short walk from Bhadra Fort.

  4. 4

    Bhadra Fort

    The 15th-century fort founded by Ahmed Shah marks the origin of Ahmedabad and now hosts a lively evening market and cultural events. Come after 5 pm when the stalls open and the heat fades; the fort itself is free to enter.

  5. 5

    Sarkhej Roza

    A serene complex of tombs and mosques often called the 'Acropolis of Ahmedabad', it blends Islamic and Hindu architectural styles in a peaceful lakeside setting. It is about 8 km southwest of the centre; go at sunset for the best light and fewer crowds.

  6. 6

    Kankaria Lake

    A circular 15th-century lake turned family recreation hub, with a zoo, toy train and evening fountain shows, it is the city's favourite outdoor escape. Visit in the late afternoon; the lakefront promenade is free, though individual attractions charge small fees.

    • Craft lovers can swap an afternoon for the Calico Museum of Textiles (check opening days and book ahead) or the Auto World Vintage Car Museum.

    • No metro yet; AMTS city buses and BRTS (Janmarg) buses run roughly 6AM-11PM.
    • AMTS fares run about ₹3-25; a ₹45 day pass exists but is rarely worth it.
    • BRTS bus 1000 links the airport and Karnavati Club, max fare ₹50, every 30 min.
    • Auto rickshaws are cheap; agree the fare first or use the prepaid booth at the airport.
    • Ride apps Ola and Uber work in Ahmedabad; local taxi apps too.
    • Driving is hectic; walking the Old City is best, and cycle rickshaws suit short hops.

    Where to stay

    • Old City (Kalupur) · ≈ INR 900–3,500 a night
      Walk to the pol lanes, Jama Masjid and the Kalupur market. Lively and central, but noisy and few mid-range rooms.
    • Ellisbridge · ≈ INR 2,000–6,500 a night
      Quiet, leafy and close to the Sabarmati Ashram and Law Garden. Good mix of mid-range and business hotels.
    • Navrangpura · ≈ INR 2,500–8,000 a night
      Central for restaurants, shops and CG Road. Easy pick-up point for taxis and buses to most sights.
    • Satellite · ≈ INR 3,000–9,000 a night
      Newer hotels and malls, good for families and longer stays. Further from the Old City, so plan on taxis.

    Food & drink

    • Start with a Gujarati thali: unlimited rotli, dal, shaak, rice and sweets, roughly ₹250–600 a person at mid-range places.
    • Street snacks to try: dhokla, khaman, fafda with jalebi (a Sunday morning favourite) and dabeli.
    • Old City food walks near Manek Chowk are famous; the night market there turns into street food after dark.
    • Ahmedabad is largely vegetarian and many places are dry (no alcohol). Check before you book a dinner.
    • Cheap and filling: a plate of snacks or a simple thali for under ₹150 at local cafeterias. Drink bottled or filtered water.

    Tips

    1. Gujarat is a 'dry state,' meaning alcohol is strictly prohibited for locals, though foreigners can obtain a permit
    2. The street food scene at Manek Chowk (which turns into a night market) is legendary
    3. The city is overwhelmingly vegetarian, offering incredible Gujarati thalis
    4. Visit the Sabarmati Ashram for a deeply moving look into Mahatma Gandhi's life and work
    5. Summers (April-June) are brutally hot, often exceeding 42°C (108°F)
