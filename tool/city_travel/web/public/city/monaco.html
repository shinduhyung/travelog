Getting around

Walk the central area, use the train to Nice or Menton, and take the Bateau Bus across the harbour.

  • ✗MetroMonaco has no metro or tram. Plan around the train and buses instead.
  • ✓BusFive urban routes (1, 2, 4, 5, 6) run from about 06:00 to 21:00. A single ticket is €2 on board, or €1.50 from a machine at the stop. A day pass is about €5.50 (2016 price, check locally). A night bus runs on a circular route from 22:00 to 04:00.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis cannot be hailed on the street. Use the stands on Avenue de Monte Carlo or at the station, or ask your hotel. Agree the fare first or make sure the meter runs. Uber and Bolt cannot pick up in Monaco.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is hard and street parking is scarce. Use a garage and check the Monapass app. Renting a scooter or car in Nice is an option if you prefer to drive in.
  • ✓Walking ★The best way to get around. Seven public escalators and lifts are free. Bicycles and e-bikes suit fit riders, given the steep hills.
  • ✓Train ★The station connects to Nice, Menton and Cap d'Ail on the coastal line, with 2-4 services per hour.
  • ✓Bateau Bus (ferry)Crosses Port Hercule for €2 in daylight. A quick way between the harbour areas.

Climate

Mild Mediterranean climate: warm, sunny summers, cool wet winters, and the sea keeps temperatures steady.

  • ☀️Best time: May to June and September to OctoberWarm days around 19–25°C and fewer crowds than midsummer. Good for walks and sea views.
  • 🔥Hot summers (July and August)Highs near 28–29°C and the busiest, most expensive months. Swim early and rest in the middle of the day.
  • 🌧️Rain is spread through the yearAbout 7–9 rainy days from October to May. Showers are often brief, so carry a small umbrella.
  • 🧥Cool winter eveningsLows of 5–7°C from December to February. Pack a warm layer for the harbour at night.
  • 🚶Steep streetsThe old town and hills have many steps and slopes all year. Comfortable shoes matter more than the weather.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for the sea breeze, comfortable walking shoes, sunscreen and a light jacket. Smart-casual clothes for the casino.

Landmarks 9

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Casino de Monte-Carlo

    This 19th-century casino is the glamorous heart of Monaco, set in the Monte-Carlo quarter with a grand facade and ornate interiors. Even if you do not gamble, the building and its gardens are worth a look. Dress smartly for the gaming rooms and check opening times and entry rules before you go; the main room is typically the one you pay to enter.

  2. 2

    Prince's Palace of Monaco

    The residence of the Grimaldi princes sits on the Rock of Monaco and gives the best views over the harbour. Visitors can see the State Apartments and the changing of the guard ceremony at noon. Arrive early in the day to avoid queues, and allow time for the walk up through the old town.

  3. 3

    Monaco Grand Prix Circuit

    The street circuit that hosts the famous Formula One race winds through the city's streets, past the harbour and the casino. Outside race weekend you can walk or drive parts of the track and see the famous corners. Plan around the race calendar, since the streets are closed and crowded during the event.

  4. 4

    Oceanographic Museum

    Founded by Prince Albert I, this museum combines marine science exhibits with an aquarium and a striking clifftop building. It is a good choice for families and rainy days. Buy tickets online ahead of peak season to skip the line.

  5. 5

    Saint Nicholas Cathedral

    This Byzantine-style cathedral near the palace is the resting place of several Grimaldi princes, including Prince Rainier III and Princess Grace. Its striking mosaics and quiet interior offer a break from the busy streets. Modest dress is expected when visiting.

  6. 6

    Port Hercule

    Monaco's main harbour is packed with superyachts and is a pleasant place to watch the boats and the Rock behind them. Walk the quay in the early evening for the best light. Boat cruises depart from around the harbour area.

    Swap in instead

    • Take the train to Menton (2-4 services per hour) for a quieter old town and lemon-tree gardens. Check the schedule on the day.
    • Swap a day for Nice by train for its old town, the Promenade des Anglais and the flower market.
    • Add a short trip to Eze village by bus or coach from Nice, with views over the sea and a hilltop garden. Check times locally.

    Getting around

    • Monaco has no metro or tram. The train station connects to Nice, Menton and Cap d'Ail on the coastal line, with 2-4 services per hour.
    • Urban buses run on five routes (1, 2, 4, 5, 6) from about 06:00 to 21:00. A single ticket is €2 on board, or €1.50 from a machine at the stop. A day pass is about €5.50 (2016 price, check locally). A night bus runs on a circular route from 22:00 to 04:00.
    • Taxis cannot be hailed on the street. Use the stands on Avenue de Monte Carlo or at the station, or ask your hotel. Agree the fare first or make sure the meter runs.
    • Uber and Bolt are banned for pickups in Monaco. You can be dropped off in Monaco, but to leave you must walk to France or order a ride starting in France.
    • Driving is hard. Street parking is scarce, so use a garage and check the Monapass app. Rent a scooter or car in Nice if you prefer to drive in.
    • Walking is the best option. Seven public escalators and lifts are free. The Bateau Bus across Port Hercule costs €2 in daylight. Bicycles and e-bikes suit fit riders, given the steep hills.

    Where to stay

    • Monte Carlo · ≈ USD 450–1,500 a night
      Near the Casino, Opera and main hotels; the most glamour and the highest prices. Good for a romantic trip.
    • Monaco-Ville · ≈ USD 300–900 a night
      The old walled town on the Rock, with the Palace, cathedral and museums. Quiet at night, but many steep steps.
    • La Condamine · ≈ USD 200–600 a night
      Flat, central and close to the train station, the harbour and the bus routes. Often the best value.
    • Fontvieille · ≈ USD 250–700 a night
      Modern and calm, next to the Stade Louis II and the Princess Grace Rose Garden. Good for a longer stay.

    Food & drink

    • Try Monegasque dishes such as barbagiuan (small fried pastries with greens or cheese) and stocafi (dried cod stew). Ask at local bistros.
    • Eat lunch at the Condamine market hall for fresh produce and quick local plates. Prices are lower than the Monte Carlo cafés.
    • For a harbour dinner, pick a terrace at Port Hercule or La Condamine. Book ahead in summer and during Grand Prix week.
    • Monaco is small and expensive. Cafés and bakeries near the station are a good budget choice for breakfast and snacks.
    • Restaurant prices include service charges in most places. Check the bill and menu prices before you order wine.
