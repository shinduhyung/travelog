Getting around

Ride apps or a rental car for most trips; Metro rail for downtown, Hollywood and Santa Monica.

  • ✓Metro ★Subway and light rail link Downtown, Hollywood, Santa Monica and Long Beach. Fares about $1.75 per ride.
  • ✓BusMetro buses cover the whole sprawl. Use a TAP card or contactless tap on rail and bus.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis are metered and costlier than apps. Uber and Lyft operate widely and are the usual choice.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is fastest off-peak, but freeways jam roughly 7–10am and 3–7pm.
  • ✓WalkingPleasant in beach towns and Hollywood. Bikes and e-scooters are common near the coast.
  • ✓FerryNo ferries serve the city centre. Harbour boats run from San Pedro to Catalina Island.

Climate

Warm and mostly dry, with mild winters and hot, sunny summers. Coastal areas stay cooler than inland.

  • ☀️Best time: April to June and September to OctoberMild to warm days with little rain. Good for both sightseeing and the beach.
  • 🥵Hot late summerJuly and August reach about 31–32°C inland. Beaches are cooler; carry water and seek shade.
  • 🌫️Coastal fogMornings near Santa Monica and Venice can be grey in late spring and early summer; it often clears later.
  • 🌧️Rain is lightMost rain falls between November and March, usually a few days a month. An umbrella is handy in winter.
  • 🧥Cool winter nightsJanuary and December nights drop to about 8°C. Pack a warm layer for evenings.
  • 🧳What to packLight layers, sunglasses and sunscreen; a jacket for winter nights and coastal fog.

Landmarks 31

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Universal Studios Hollywood

    The world's first Universal park and a must for movie fans, combining a working studio tour with major rides. Buy tickets online in advance and arrive at opening to beat the queues; it is in Universal City, reachable by Metro B Line.

  2. 2

    Hollywood Sign

    The most recognizable symbol of the film industry, best seen from Griffith Park or the Lake Hollywood area. Go early in the morning for clear light and fewer crowds; there is no parking at the sign itself, so hike or view from a distance.

  3. 3

    Griffith Observatory

    A free public observatory with sweeping views over Los Angeles and the Hollywood Sign. Entry is free but planetarium shows are ticketed; visit at sunset for the best light and check opening days, as it is closed on some weekdays.

  4. 4

    Santa Monica Pier

    A classic seaside pier with an amusement park, aquarium and ocean views at the end of Route 66. It is free to walk on; ride the Ferris wheel at sunset and take the Metro E Line to Downtown Santa Monica.

  5. 5

    Hollywood Walk of Fame

    The famous sidewalk of celebrity stars along Hollywood Boulevard, free to stroll. Come early or on a weekday to avoid crowds, and combine it with the TCL Chinese Theatre next door.

  6. 6

    The Getty Center

    A hilltop art museum with world-class collections, gardens and panoramic city views. Admission is free; pay only for parking, and take the tram up from the garage.

  7. 7

    Venice Beach

    A lively boardwalk with street performers, muscle beach and canals nearby. Rent a bike to ride to Santa Monica; weekends are busiest, so go on a weekday morning.

  8. 8

    TCL Chinese Theatre

    Historic movie palace famous for its forecourt of celebrity handprints and footprints. The forecourt is free to visit; book a movie ticket to see the ornate interior.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for the Getty Center or the Getty Villa; both are free or low cost, check parking fees.
    • Swap a beach afternoon for a studio tour in Burbank or Hollywood; book ahead.
    • Take a harbour boat from San Pedro to Catalina Island if you have a full extra day.

    Getting around

    • Metro Rail subway and light rail link Downtown, Hollywood, Santa Monica and Long Beach; fares about $1.75 per ride.
    • Metro buses cover the whole sprawl; a TAP card or contactless tap works on rail and bus.
    • Taxis are metered and costlier than apps; Uber and Lyft operate widely and are the usual choice.
    • Driving is fastest off-peak but freeways jam from roughly 7-10am and 3-7pm.
    • Walking is pleasant in beach towns and Hollywood; bikes and e-scooters are common near the coast.
    • No ferries serve the city centre; harbour boats run from San Pedro to Catalina Island.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown · ≈ USD 150–350 a night
      Central for Metro rail, museums and food halls; good value on weekends.
    • Hollywood · ≈ USD 140–320 a night
      Walk to the Walk of Fame and the studios; easy subway rides downtown.
    • Santa Monica · ≈ USD 220–550 a night
      Beach and pier on the doorstep; light rail to downtown LA.
    • West Hollywood · ≈ USD 200–480 a night
      Nightlife, restaurants and walkable streets between Hollywood and the beaches.

    Food & drink

    • Try tacos and burritos at taquerias and food trucks; a meal often costs $10–15.
    • Koreatown is great for late-night Korean BBQ and noodles; expect $25–40 a person.
    • Grand Central Market downtown has many stalls under one roof; budget $10–20.
    • Book popular dinner spots ahead, especially on weekends in West Hollywood.
    • Beach towns have casual seafood and burger shacks; check hours, some close early.

    Tips

    1. A car is practically essential — public transit is limited outside downtown.
    2. Traffic on the 405 and 101 freeways is brutal during rush hours (7–10am, 4–7pm).
    3. In-N-Out Burger is a local institution — order 'animal style' for the full experience.
    4. Many popular beaches (Venice, Santa Monica, Malibu) are free; arrive early for parking.
    5. Smog is real — air quality apps are useful before outdoor activity days.
