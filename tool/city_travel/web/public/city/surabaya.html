Getting around

Ride-hailing motorbike taxis (Gojek, Grab) for most trips; buses if you're saving money.

  • ✗MetroNo metro or tram. Commuter trains are very limited; don't plan around rail.
  • ✓BusSuroboyo Bus and Trans Semanggi are air-conditioned, Rp 5,000, cashless only (QRIS or e-money card). Clean but waits run 15–30 min; track them on the GOBIS app.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Gojek, Grab and Maxim work well; use GoRide or GrabBike to beat traffic. Taxis are plentiful — check the meter (argo) is on. Green SM runs an electric fleet.
  • ✓Rental carPossible, but traffic is heavy and parking is hard. Most visitors do better with ride apps.
  • ✗WalkingPatchy. Sidewalks are inconsistent, so use ride apps for anything beyond a few blocks.
  • ✓BecakCycle rickshaws are common. Agree a price first, around Rp 5,000–20,000.
  • ✓FerryFerries leave from Tanjung Perak harbour. Check schedules on the day.

Climate

Tropical and hot all year, around 30–33°C by day. Rain is heavy from November to April and light from June to September.

  • ☀️Best time: June to SeptemberDriest months, with only about 2–10 rain days. Good for walking and day trips.
  • 🌧️Wet season: November to AprilAbout 24–29 rain days a month. Showers are often short but heavy; plan indoor backups.
  • 🥵Hot every monthHighs stay near 30–33°C and lows near 24–25°C. October is the hottest month.
  • 💧Humid all yearExpect muggy air, especially near the coast. Light, breathable clothes help.
  • ⛈️Afternoon stormsIn the wet season, storms often hit in the afternoon. Do outdoor sights in the morning.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes, a rain jacket or umbrella, sandals, sunscreen and mosquito repellent.

Landmarks 6

Must-see · 5

  1. 1

    Ampel Mosque

    Surabaya's most famous landmark and the heart of the old Arab quarter, a pilgrimage site tied to the spread of Islam in Java. Visit outside prayer times and dress modestly; the surrounding Ampel market is lively in the morning.

  2. 2

    House of Sampoerna

    A beautifully preserved colonial-era cigarette factory and museum that tells the story of one of Indonesia's best-known companies. It is a must-see for its architecture and free guided tours; go in the morning to watch the hand-rolling floor at work.

  3. 3

    Suramadu Bridge

    Indonesia's long bridge linking Surabaya to Madura and one of the city's defining modern sights. Drive or take a taxi across for views, ideally in the late afternoon or at sunset.

  4. 4

    Surabaya Zoo

    A large, long-established city zoo and a familiar family outing in Surabaya. Go early in the day when it is cooler and animals are more active.

  5. 5

    Cheng Hoo Mosque

    A distinctive mosque blending Chinese and Islamic architecture, honoring the admiral Cheng Hoo. It is a short stop worth combining with a visit to the old town; dress modestly.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day for Malang: cooler hill town with colonial streets, about 2 hours by train or car.
    • Bromo sunrise trips leave at night from Surabaya; expect a long, cold, bumpy drive. Book ahead.
    • Swap a city afternoon for a mangrove and fishing-village stop on the east coast; check transport on the day.

    Getting around

    • No metro or tram; commuter trains are very limited.
    • Suroboyo Bus and Trans Semanggi are air-conditioned; fare Rp 5,000, cashless only (QRIS or e-money card).
    • Buses are clean but waits run 15-30 min; track them on the GOBIS app.
    • Gojek, Grab and Maxim work well; use GoRide or GrabBike to beat traffic.
    • Taxis are plentiful; check the meter (argo) is on. Green SM runs an electric fleet.
    • Becak (cycle rickshaws) are common; agree a price first, around Rp 5,000-20,000.
    • Walking is patchy; sidewalks are inconsistent. Ferries leave from Tanjung Perak harbour.

    Where to stay

    • Tunjungan · ≈ IDR 350,000–900,000 a night
      Central. Walk to Tunjungan Plaza mall and the old colonial streets; easy for taxis and buses.
    • Gubeng · ≈ IDR 300,000–800,000 a night
      Next to Gubeng train station and the airport bus; good for early departures to Malang or Bromo.
    • Genteng · ≈ IDR 250,000–700,000 a night
      Old city core near the Arab quarter, Chinatown and the bridge; budget hotels and street food.
    • Darmo · ≈ IDR 400,000–1,100,000 a night
      Quieter, greener south side with malls and family hotels; a short ride from the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Try rawon (dark beef soup with keluak nut), served with rice and bean sprouts. A local favourite.
    • Sate kelapa and lontong balap are Surabaya classics. Look for busy warungs (small eateries) at lunch.
    • The Arab quarter near Ampel: try nasi kebuli and fresh bread from the bakeries.
    • Cheap eats: street stalls and food courts, roughly Rp 15,000–40,000 a plate. Check prices first.
    • Drink bottled water. Ice from busy cafés is usually fine; skip it at quiet roadside stalls if unsure.

    Tips

    1. Use Grab or Gojek for safe and transparent taxi/motorbike rides
    2. The weather is consistently hot; plan activities for early mornings or late afternoons
    3. Visit the House of Sampoerna for a look at local history and tobacco production
    4. Traffic is chaotic and intimidating; rely on ride-hailing services for navigation
    5. Indonesian is the primary language, but English is understood in business settings
