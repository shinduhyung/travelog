Getting around

Ride-hailing or metered taxis for most trips; buses are cheap but slow.

  • ✗MetroNo metro in the city. Don't plan around rail inside Haikou.
  • ✓TrainCommuter trains on the Hainan high-speed line link the airport and main stations.
  • ✓BusCity buses are cheap and cover most routes; fares are a few yuan.
  • ✓Taxi / ride apps ★Taxis meter from about ¥10; insist on the meter (dabiao). DiDi and Amap work well; Uber does not operate.
  • ✓Bikes / scootersBikes and electric scooters are easy to rent; riverside paths are good.
  • ✓FerryFerries cross to Xuwen and Beihai from New Port and Xiuying Port.
  • ✗Rental carNot needed for a city visit; use taxis, buses and trains instead.
  • ✓WalkingGood around Bo'ai Old Street and the waterfront in the cooler months; use rides in summer heat.

Climate

Tropical and humid: warm all year, with a hot, very wet summer and a mild, drier winter.

  • ☀️Best time: November to MarchHighs of 21–27°C and the fewest rain days. Ideal for walking the old streets and the coast.
  • 🌧️Wet season May to OctoberAbout 19–23 rain days a month, often short heavy showers. Carry a light rain jacket.
  • 🥵Hot summerJune peaks near 33°C with warm nights around 27°C. Do outdoor sights early morning.
  • 🌀Typhoon seasonLate summer and autumn can bring storms. Check forecasts and keep travel plans flexible.
  • 💧Humid year-roundSea air keeps it muggy, especially May to September. Light, quick-dry clothes help.
  • 🧳What to packLight breathable clothes, sunscreen and a hat; a rain jacket in summer; a light layer for winter evenings.

Landmarks 3

Must-see · 3

  1. 1

    Qilou Old Street

    Haikou's most famous sight is a long arcade of early-20th-century shophouses with ornate facades, best explored on foot in the late afternoon when the light softens and the arcades fill with local life. Go early morning or after sunset to avoid the midday heat, and wear comfortable shoes since the street stretches for well over a kilometre.

  2. 2

    Haikou Century Bridge

    This cable-stayed bridge over the mouth of the Haikou River is the city's modern icon and a fine spot for sunset views and an evening stroll. Come around dusk for the best light and cooler air; it is free to walk across.

  3. 3

    Qiongzhou Strait

    The strait separating Hainan from the mainland defines Haikou's setting, and the waterfront is the place to feel the sea breeze and watch ferries cross. Visit in the early morning or evening for comfortable temperatures, and bring sun protection during the day.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a day trip to the volcanic crater park and lava villages north of the city; check opening hours on the day.
    • Take the high-speed train to Sanya for beaches and resort life, roughly 1.5–2 hours each way.
    • Slow down on the West Coast: a sunset walk, a beach café and an evening seafood dinner.

    Getting around

    • No metro; commuter trains on the Hainan high-speed line link the airport and main stations.
    • City buses are cheap and cover most routes; fares are a few yuan.
    • Taxis meter from about ¥10; insist on the meter (dabiao).
    • Ride-hailing apps DiDi and Amap work well; Uber does not operate.
    • Bikes and electric scooters are easy to rent; riverside paths are good.
    • Ferries cross to Xuwen and Beihai from New Port and Xiuying Port.

    Where to stay

    • Bo'ai Old Street · ≈ CNY 200–500 a night
      Walk to the arcaded old streets, snack stalls and small temples. Central for buses and taxis.
    • Haixiu Avenue · ≈ CNY 250–600 a night
      Downtown shopping and food, close to the museum and easy taxi pickups.
    • West Coast · ≈ CNY 350–900 a night
      Beach resorts and long seaside walks; quieter nights, farther from the old town.
    • Meilan District · ≈ CNY 200–550 a night
      Closest to the airport and the high-speed train station; handy for early flights.

    Food & drink

    • Try Hainan staples: Wenchang chicken, coconut rice, and a bowl of Hainan-style noodles for breakfast.
    • Seafood: pick fish and shellfish at a market or tank restaurant and have it cooked; agree the price per weight first.
    • Street snacks around Bo'ai Old Street: coconut water, grilled squid and sweet rice cakes, mostly ¥5–20.
    • Cool down with qingbuliang, a sweet coconut-milk dessert soup with beans and fruit.
    • Big night food streets and open-air seafood places get busy after 7pm; go early or expect a wait.

    Tips

    1. Enjoy the local Hainanese chicken rice and fresh coconut water
    2. Explore the Qilou Old Street for unique Southeast Asian-influenced arcade architecture
    3. Visit the Haikou Volcanic Cluster Global Geopark to hike around dormant craters
    4. Typhoon season peaks between July and September, so monitor weather reports
    5. WeChat Pay and Alipay are essential, as with all of China
