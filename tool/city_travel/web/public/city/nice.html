Getting around

Tram line 2 to the airport and centre, then walk the Old Town and seafront.

  • ✗MetroNo metro in Nice. Use tram line 2 and buses instead.
  • ✓BusLignes d'Azur buses charge €1.70 a ride, with a transfer allowed within 74 minutes. Buy a top-up card or pay with contactless. Always validate.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are pricey and some overcharge. Uber and Bolt both operate, with a ride-share pick-up zone at the airport.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is hard in the centre: one-way streets and scarce metered parking. Use an underground car park, roughly €8-10 a day.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is walkable. Old Town and the seafront are close to each other.
  • ✓Tram ★Tram line 2 links the airport with the centre (Jean Médecin). It runs about every 8 minutes on weekdays, roughly 04:30 to after midnight.
  • ✓FerryFerries run from Nice port to Corsica. Check local schedules before you go.
  • ✓Shared bikes / e-scootersShared bikes and e-scooters are available in parts of the city.

Climate

Mild Mediterranean climate: warm, sunny summers and mild winters, with rain spread across the year.

  • ☀️Best time: May to June and SeptemberHighs of about 21-25°C and fewer rain days than autumn. Good for walking and the beach.
  • 🥵Hot summers (July and August)Highs of 28-29°C and the fewest rain days, around 3-4. Go to the beach early and rest in the afternoon.
  • 🌧️Wettest in May and OctoberAbout 9 rain days a month. Keep a compact umbrella and plan museum visits for showery days.
  • 🧥Cool wintersLows of about 5-7°C from December to February. Bring a warm jacket for evenings by the sea.
  • 🌅Shoulder seasonsApril and October are mild, with highs of 18-21°C. Good for sightseeing with smaller crowds.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for changing temperatures, comfortable walking shoes for the hills, sun hat and sunscreen for summer, and a light rain jacket for autumn.

Landmarks 16

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Promenade des Anglais

    This long seafront promenade is the city's most famous walk, lined with palm trees, belle époque hotels and the pebble beach. It is best at sunset when the Baie des Anges glows. Walk it early morning or late afternoon to avoid the midday heat, and bring water.

  2. 2

    Vieux Nice

    The Old Town is a maze of ochre and pastel buildings, narrow lanes, churches and small squares full of cafés. Its lively atmosphere and local food make it the heart of Nice. Wander without a map, and go in the evening when the lanes are busiest.

  3. 3

    Castle Hill (Colline du Château)

    This hilltop park offers the best panoramic views over the bay, the Old Town and the harbour. It also has waterfalls, shaded paths and ruins of an old castle. Reach it by the lift or stairs from the Old Town, and visit at sunset for the views.

  4. 4

    Cours Saleya Market

    This open-air market sells flowers, fresh produce, olives and local specialities, and is set among restaurants in the Old Town. It is busiest in the morning and is a good place to see daily Niçois life. Go before noon, as stalls thin out by early afternoon.

  5. 5

    Place Masséna

    This grand, sunny square, with its red and ochre facades and fountains, is the gateway between the Old Town and the modern city. It is a popular meeting point and a good spot for a coffee break. It is easy to reach on foot or by tram.

  6. 6

    Musée Matisse

    This museum in the Cimiez hills holds a large collection of works by Henri Matisse, who lived and worked in Nice for decades. The villa and gardens are as appealing as the art. Check opening hours before going, as the museum is closed on some days, and reach it by bus.

  7. 7

    Musée Marc Chagall

    This museum sits in the Cimiez hills and houses the largest public collection of Marc Chagall's Biblical Message paintings. The setting among pine trees makes it a calm visit. Pair it with the Matisse museum nearby, and check the schedule ahead of time.

  8. 8

    Èze Village

    This medieval hilltop village above the sea is one of the Riviera's best-known sights, with stone lanes, galleries and views down to the coast. It is a short trip from Nice and is usually visited by bus or guided tour. Go early to avoid the day-tripper crowds, and wear comfortable shoes for the steep paths.

    Swap in instead

    • Take a bus trip to Monaco or Eze for a half day. Lignes d'Azur buses cost €1.70 a ride, so validate each trip.
    • Spend a day in Cannes or Antibes. Check bus or train times on the day, as schedules change.

    Getting around

    • Tram line 2 links the airport with the centre (Jean Médecin). It runs about every 8 minutes on weekdays, roughly 04:30 to after midnight.
    • Lignes d'Azur buses charge €1.70 a ride, with a transfer allowed within 74 minutes. Buy a top-up card or pay with contactless. Always validate.
    • Taxis are pricey and some overcharge. Uber and Bolt both operate, with a ride-share pick-up zone at the airport.
    • Driving is hard in the centre: one-way streets and scarce metered parking. Use an underground car park, roughly €8-10 a day.
    • The centre is walkable. Old Town and the seafront are close. Shared bikes and e-scooters are available in parts of the city.
    • Ferries run from Nice port to Corsica. Check local schedules before you go.

    Where to stay

    • Vieux Nice · ≈ EUR 110–260 a night
      Walk to the Old Town lanes, Cours Saleya market and the seafront; best for food lovers, but streets are narrow and lively at night.
    • Promenade des Anglais · ≈ EUR 150–400 a night
      Sea views and the beach are right outside; a short walk to the Old Town and tram line 2 at the airport route.
    • Carré d'Or · ≈ EUR 120–300 a night
      Quiet shopping streets between the station and the sea; good value and an easy tram ride to the airport.
    • Cimiez · ≈ EUR 130–320 a night
      Leafy hill district with the Matisse and Chagall museums; calmer nights but a steeper walk or a bus or taxi to the sea.

    Food & drink

    • Try socca, a thin chickpea flour pancake, at a counter in the Old Town. Eat it hot and sold by the slice.
    • Visit the Cours Saleya market in the morning for fruit, cheese and olives. Check the day, as the market hours change in winter.
    • Order pissaladière (onion tart) and pan bagnat (tuna roll) for a cheap lunch. Expect about €6-12 at a simple bakery.
    • Book seafront or Old Town dinners ahead in summer. Prices vary a lot, so check the menu before you sit down.
    • Stay away from restaurants with staff outside calling you in. Walk a few streets off the main square for better food.
