Getting around

Metro and tram for the centre; walk Le Panier and the port.

  • ✓Metro ★Two lines, running 05:00–00:30. Single ticket €1.70, or €15 for ten.
  • ✓Tram ★Three lines until 00:30, same tickets as the metro. Handy along the main boulevards.
  • ✓BusGood coverage, but most routes stop after 21:00. Night buses are limited, so plan evening trips around that.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis exist but watch for rogue drivers. Uber and similar apps work and are often easier.
  • ✗Rental carNot recommended in the centre: narrow streets and parking problems. Useful only for day trips out of town.
  • ✓WalkingBest way to see the Vieux-Port and Le Panier, though the old town is steep in places.
  • ✓BikeLe vélo scheme costs €1 a week with the first 30 minutes free. Few cycle paths, so ride carefully.
  • ✓FerryBoat across the Vieux-Port and to Frioul island, about €10 return. Check sailings on the day if windy.

Climate

Mediterranean climate: mild winters, hot dry summers and a windy, sometimes wet spring and autumn.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs of 21–28°C and little rain. Ideal for the port, beaches and calanques.
  • 🥵July and August are hotAround 28°C by day and 20–21°C at night. Sightsee in the morning; rest in the afternoon.
  • 🌧️Wettest in autumnOctober and November bring the most rain days (about eight to nine). Showers can be heavy but short.
  • 💨The mistral windA strong, cold north wind can blow any season. It clears the sky but makes the sea rough and boat trips uncertain.
  • 🌊Sea swimmingComfortable roughly June to September. Outside that, beaches are for walking, not swimming.
  • 🧳What to packLight clothes and sunscreen for summer, a windproof layer and rain jacket for spring and autumn, warm layers for winter.

Landmarks 8

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Notre-Dame de la Garde

    Marseille's most famous sight, a hilltop basilica crowned by a golden statue of the Virgin that watches over the whole city and the sea. The climb rewards you with sweeping views over the harbor and the Calanques. Go early in the morning or near sunset for the best light and fewer crowds; you can walk up or take the bus.

  2. 2

    Calanques National Park

    A dramatic stretch of limestone coves and turquoise water right at the city's edge, the reason many people fall in love with Marseille. Hiking the coastal trails or taking a boat trip is the classic way to see it. In summer some calanques close on hot, fire-risk days, so check access before you go and bring water.

  3. 3

    MuCEM

    A striking modern museum on the waterfront, linked to the old Fort Saint-Jean by footbridges, devoted to Mediterranean cultures. Even if you skip the exhibitions, the building and rooftop views are worth the trip. The outdoor areas and fort are free to wander; go late afternoon for cooler air and harbor light.

  4. 4

    Cathédrale de la Major

    A vast, striped Romanesque-Byzantine cathedral near the port, far bigger and more ornate than most visitors expect. It sits beside the MuCEM and the Joliette district, so it fits easily into a waterfront walk. Entry is free; dress respectfully and avoid visiting during services.

  5. 5

    Palais Longchamp

    A grand 19th-century palace built around a monumental water fountain, with gardens and museums on either side. It is a favorite spot for a relaxed stroll away from the port crowds. Come in the morning for quiet gardens, and check museum opening days before planning a visit.

  6. 6

    Orange Velodrome

    Marseille's iconic football stadium, home of Olympique de Marseille and famous for its roaring atmosphere on match days. Even outside games, it is a landmark of the city's sporting identity. Tickets for matches sell fast, so book well ahead and arrive early for the walk from the metro.

    Swap in instead

    • Take the boat to the Frioul islands (about €10 return) for a half-day of rocky coves and sea views.
    • Swap a city afternoon for Cassis by train: short ride, then a boat to the calanques if the sea is calm.
    • If you have a car-free day, ride bus and cable car up to Notre-Dame de la Garde for the best view; check opening hours on the day.

    Getting around

    • Metro: 2 lines, runs 05:00-00:30; tickets €1.70 single, €15 for 10.
    • Tram: 3 lines, runs until 00:30; same tickets as metro.
    • Buses: extensive network, but most stop after 21:00; night buses limited.
    • Taxis: available but beware of rogue drivers; ride apps like Uber work.
    • Driving: not recommended in centre; narrow streets and parking issues.
    • Bikes: le vélo scheme, €1/week, first 30 min free; few cycle paths.
    • Ferry: boat across Vieux-Port and to Frioul; €10 return.

    Where to stay

    • Vieux-Port · ≈ EUR 110–260 a night
      The harbour is the city's front door: ferries, metro and most sights within walking distance.
    • Le Panier · ≈ EUR 90–220 a night
      Old-town lanes above the port, close to MuCEM and the cathedral; quiet at night, steep streets.
    • La Canebière · ≈ EUR 80–190 a night
      Central and cheaper, on the main boulevard between the port and the stations; busier and noisier.
    • Prado · ≈ EUR 100–230 a night
      Beachside strip south of the centre; good for swimming and long walks, further from the old town.

    Food & drink

    • Try bouillabaisse once, but it is a proper sit-down meal: expect roughly €35–70 per person, and book ahead.
    • Cheaper and just as local: panisse (chickpea fries), navettes biscuits and a pastis at a port café.
    • Go to the fish market on the Quai des Belges at the Vieux-Port in the morning; it closes early.
    • Noailles market area is the place for cheap North African and Middle Eastern food, roughly €8–15 a meal.
    • Aperitif hour is a local ritual: many bars do cheaper drinks and small plates in the early evening.

    Tips

    1. Eat authentic Bouillabaisse (traditional fish stew) in the Old Port area
    2. Take a boat tour to the Calanques National Park for breathtaking limestone cliffs and blue water
    3. Explore the historic Le Panier neighborhood for street art and narrow alleys
    4. Be cautious of belongings in crowded areas and around the port
    5. Enjoy the vibrant, multi-ethnic atmosphere that makes it distinct from other French cities

    Souvenirs

    Marseille Soap

    EUR 10–182 cubes, 300g each · estimate

    A traditional olive-oil-based soap made in Marseille for centuries. It is known for its simple, pure formula.

    Where: La Corvette or Fer à Cheval flagship stores
    Check that the label says 72% vegetable oils, and confirm the maker before you buy.

    Santon Figurine

    EUR 15–351 small figure · estimate

    A small handmade clay figure from Provençal Christmas nativity scenes. Santons are a local craft tradition of Provence.

    Where: Santons Marcel Carbonel, Rue Neuve Sainte-Catherine
    Look for pieces that are handmade and hand-painted, not mass-produced.

    Navette Biscuits

    EUR 7–15200 to 300g · estimate

    Boat-shaped cookies flavored with orange blossom. They are a famous old-fashioned sweet from Marseille bakeries.

    Where: Four des Navettes, Rue Sainte
    Buy them at this long-established bakery, which is a well-known local spot.
