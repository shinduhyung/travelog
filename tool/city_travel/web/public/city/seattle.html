Getting around

Link light rail for the airport, Downtown and Capitol Hill; walk or ride apps for short hops.

  • ✓Train ★Link light rail 1 Line links Sea-Tac, Downtown, Capitol Hill and the University of Washington. Easy for airport trips.
  • ✓BusKing County Metro buses cover the city. Pay with an ORCA card or tap your phone.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered. Uber and Lyft both operate widely, handy for late nights and airport runs.
  • ✓Rental carEasy to arrange, but Downtown traffic is heavy at most hours. Consider skipping it in the city.
  • ✓Walking ★Good Downtown and on the waterfront. Bikes and e-scooters are common for longer hops.
  • ✓FerryWashington State Ferries leave Downtown for Bainbridge Island and Bremerton. Check times and fares on the day.

Climate

Mild, wet winters and dry, sunny summers. Rain is common from October to March; July and August are the driest.

  • ☀️Best time: July and AugustHighs near 25°C and only about 3–5 rain days. The driest, sunniest stretch for outdoor plans.
  • 🌧️Wet season: November to JanuaryAbout 18–19 rain days a month and highs of 7–10°C. Bring a waterproof jacket, not just an umbrella.
  • 🌦️Spring is showeryMarch to May warms from about 11°C to 18°C, but expect around 10–15 rain days a month.
  • 🧥Layers beat a heavy coatDays are rarely very cold or very hot. A light waterproof shell over layers works most of the year.
  • 🌥️Grey skies are normalCloud cover is common outside summer. Plan one indoor sight per day as a backup.
  • 🧳What to packWaterproof jacket, comfortable walking shoes and layers. Add sunglasses and a hat for July and August.

Landmarks 18

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Space Needle

    Seattle's most recognizable icon, offering sweeping views of the city, Puget Sound and Mount Rainier on clear days. Book tickets online in advance and go early morning or near sunset for the best light and smaller crowds.

  2. 2

    Pike Place Market

    A bustling historic market famous for fish throwing, local produce, crafts and the original Starbucks. Arrive before 10am to avoid the biggest crowds, and wear comfortable shoes for the steep streets.

  3. 3

    Chihuly Garden and Glass

    A stunning museum showcasing Dale Chihuly's colorful glass sculptures, with both indoor galleries and an outdoor garden. It sits right next to the Space Needle, so combine both in one visit; tickets are timed.

  4. 4

    Museum of Pop Culture

    A must for music and pop-culture fans, with immersive exhibits on Nirvana, Jimi Hendrix, science fiction and more. The building itself is a landmark; allow at least two hours and check for special exhibitions.

  5. 5

    Kerry Park

    The classic postcard viewpoint of Seattle, with the Space Needle framed against Mount Rainier and the skyline. It's free and especially magical at sunset or on a clear day; go early for parking.

  6. 6

    Seattle Aquarium

    A family-friendly aquarium on the waterfront with local marine life, touch pools and a new Ocean Pavilion. Buy tickets online to skip lines and visit on weekday mornings for a quieter experience.

  7. 7

    Gas Works Park

    A unique park built on a former gasification plant, with industrial relics and great views of Lake Union and the skyline. It's a relaxed spot for a picnic or kite flying; best on sunny afternoons.

  8. 8

    Fremont Troll

    A quirky, giant sculpture under the Aurora Bridge that has become a beloved photo stop. It's free and quick to visit; combine it with a stroll through the Fremont neighborhood's shops and cafes.

    Swap in instead

    • Take a Washington State Ferries trip from Downtown to Bainbridge Island: a short crossing with skyline views. Check fares and times on the day.
    • Swap a city afternoon for a day trip toward the mountains or water; ask your hotel about current options and conditions.

    Getting around

    • Link light rail 1 Line links Sea-Tac, Downtown, Capitol Hill, and the University of Washington.
    • King County Metro buses cover the city; ORCA card or phone tap pays fares.
    • Taxis are metered; Uber and Lyft both operate widely.
    • Driving is easy to arrange but Downtown traffic is heavy at most hours.
    • Walking is good Downtown and on the waterfront; bikes and e-scooters are common.
    • Washington State Ferries leave Downtown for Bainbridge Island and Bremerton.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown · ≈ USD 180–420 a night
      Walk to Pike Place, the waterfront and the Space Needle; Link light rail to the airport and University of Washington.
    • Capitol Hill · ≈ USD 150–380 a night

    • Belltown · ≈ USD 160–400 a night

    • South Lake Union · ≈ USD 170–400 a night
      Quieter and modern, near the Space Needle and Lake Union; good for longer stays, less nightlife.

    Food & drink

    • Start at Pike Place Market: walk the stalls, watch the fish throwers, and grab a cheap pastry or chowder.
    • Seattle is a coffee city. Try a local roaster's café rather than a chain; expect about $4–6 for a latte.
    • Seafood is the local specialty: salmon, crab and oysters. Waterfront and market restaurants cost more; check menus first.
    • Budget eats: teriyaki, pho and food trucks around Capitol Hill and the University District, often $10–15 a meal.
    • Book popular dinner spots ahead, especially on weekends. Many kitchens close earlier than in bigger cities.

    Tips

    1. Locals famously do not use umbrellas; bring a good quality rain jacket with a hood
    2. Pike Place Market is best visited early in the morning before the massive cruise ship crowds arrive
    3. Get an ORCA card to easily tap on and off all public transit systems
    4. The terrain is extremely steep; be prepared for steep inclines when walking from the waterfront to downtown
    5. Visit the Ballard Locks to watch boats transition between the lakes and Puget Sound, and see the salmon ladder
