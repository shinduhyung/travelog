Getting around

Use the metro and local trains for speed; Uber or Ola for door-to-door trips.

  • ✓Metro ★Modern and fast. The Aqua Line links the airport to BKC and Colaba.
  • ✓Train ★Suburban 'local' trains are the city's artery. Buy a ₹275 Tourist Ticket for first class all day.
  • ✓BusCheap and everywhere. Use the m-Indicator app to plan routes.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsMetered black-and-yellow taxis and blue cabs are common; agree the fare or insist on the meter. Uber and Ola work well.
  • ✓FerryFerries from Ferry Wharf reach Elephanta Island and nearby beaches.
  • ✗Rental carNot practical for most visitors; traffic is heavy and parking is hard. Use taxis and trains instead.
  • ✓WalkingGood in Colaba and Fort for sights. Use transport for longer trips, especially in heat or rain.

Climate

Tropical monsoon climate: hot and humid most of the year, with a long, very wet monsoon from June to September.

  • ☀️Best time: November to MarchDry days around 29–32°C with cooler evenings. Ideal for walking, ferries and street food.
  • 🌧️Monsoon: June to SeptemberRain on most days, heaviest in July and August. Expect wet streets and possible travel delays.
  • 🥵Hot and humid April to MayHighs near 33°C with rising humidity. Sightsee early morning and rest in the afternoon.
  • 🌂Carry rain gear in monsoonAn umbrella or light raincoat is essential from June to September.
  • 💧Humidity all yearIt feels hotter than the numbers. Light, breathable clothes help.
  • 🧳What to packLight cotton clothes, comfortable walking shoes, sunscreen and a hat; add rain gear for the monsoon months.

Landmarks 15

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Gateway of India

    Mumbai's most famous monument, a basalt arch on the harborfront built to commemorate a royal visit. Go early morning to avoid crowds and heat; it's free and a short walk from Colaba Causeway.

  2. 2

    Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus

    A UNESCO-listed Victorian Gothic railway station that is still a working commuter hub. Visit outside rush hours (mid-morning or afternoon) to admire the facade and interior without the crush; it's free to enter.

  3. 3

    Elephanta Caves

    Ancient rock-cut cave temples on an island in Mumbai Harbour, reached by a one-hour ferry from the Gateway of India. Ferries run roughly 9am–2pm and are closed on Mondays; wear comfortable shoes for the climb.

  4. 4

    Taj Mahal Palace Hotel

    Mumbai's iconic heritage hotel facing the Gateway of India, famed for its architecture and history. Non-guests can visit the lobby and some restaurants; dress smart-casual and expect security checks.

  5. 5

    Marine Drive

    The sweeping seaside promenade known as the Queen's Necklace, best at sunset when the lights come on. It's free and ideal for an evening stroll; avoid midday heat.

  6. 6

    Haji Ali Dargah

    A mosque and tomb on an islet linked by a causeway, a striking sight at high tide. Dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees); it's free and busiest on Fridays.

  7. 7

    Siddhivinayak Temple

    One of Mumbai's most visited Hindu temples, dedicated to Ganesha. Remove shoes and dress respectfully; early morning or late evening is less crowded, and it's free to enter.

  8. 8

    Dhobi Ghat

    A vast open-air laundry where dhobis wash clothes by hand, a unique slice of Mumbai life. View from the Mahalaxmi bridge for free; be respectful and ask before photographing workers.

    Lonavla (Lonavala) · 2 h 00 by train

    ★★★★☆ · Full day · 82 km from Mumbai

    Best way: Direct trains from Dadar reach Lonavala in 2 hours, faster than the bus and right by the town.

    Direct suburban and express trains run from Mumbai's CSMT and Dadar stations to Lonavala, with local taxis for the hill viewpoints.

    • Train ★2 h 00 · from $1-$12
    • Bus2 h 40 · from $4-$30
    • Car1 h 05 · taxi ≈ 1,510 INR

    What to see

    • Bhushi Dam
    • Karla Caves
    • Tiger Point
    • Rajmachi viewpoint

    Good to know

    • Book express train seats ahead, especially for weekends
    • The monsoon (June-September) is the classic season for the hill views and waterfalls
    • Local taxis are needed to reach the viewpoints from the station

    Alībāg (Alibaug) · 2 h 59 by bus

    ★★★☆☆ · Full day · 94 km from Mumbai

    Best way: The bus runs in under 3 hours and drops you in Alibaug town, while the ferry route needs a road transfer at the far end.

    Take a ferry from the Gateway of India or a bus/train via Panvel, then a short road transfer into Alibaug town.

    • Bus ★2 h 59 · from $1-$5
    • Car1 h 13 · taxi ≈ 1,720 INR

    What to see

    • Alibaug Beach
    • Kolaba Fort
    • Murud-Janjira area coastline
    • Local seafood lunch

    Good to know

    • Ferries from the Gateway of India are the scenic alternative but depend on sea conditions
    • Weekends get crowded, so go on a weekday if you can
    • The beaches and forts are spread out, so expect local transport once there

    Swap in instead

    • Take a ferry from Ferry Wharf to Elephanta Island for the cave temples; check sailing times on the day.
    • Spend a half day in Bandra: cafés, street art and the sea link views.
    • Add a day trip to a nearby beach or hill area; ask your hotel about current transport options.

    Getting around

    • Mumbai Metro is modern and fast; Aqua Line links the airport to BKC and Colaba.
    • Suburban 'local' trains are the city's artery; buy a ₹275 Tourist Ticket for first class all day.
    • Buses are cheap and everywhere; use the m-Indicator app to plan routes.
    • Metered black-and-yellow taxis and blue cabs are common; agree the fare or insist on the meter.
    • Uber and Ola ride apps work well across the city.
    • Ferries from Ferry Wharf reach Elephanta Island and nearby beaches.

    Where to stay

    • Colaba · ≈ INR 2,500–12,000 a night
      Walk to the Gateway of India, museums and the ferry wharf. Best first-time base for sights and food.
    • Fort · ≈ INR 3,000–14,000 a night
      Central and close to Colaba sights, with metro and train links. Good mix of heritage hotels and business stays.
    • Bandra · ≈ INR 3,500–15,000 a night
      Cafés, nightlife and the Bandra-Worli Sea Link. Near the domestic airport and BKC business district.
    • Juhu · ≈ INR 3,000–13,000 a night
      Beach-side stays near the airport. Quieter evenings and easy access to the western suburbs.

    Food & drink

    • Try Mumbai street snacks: vada pav, pav bhaji, bhel puri and pani puri. Eat where locals queue; prices are low, often ₹20–100.
    • Have a meal at an old Irani café: bun maska, chai and keema. Cheap and full of character.
    • Seafood is a highlight: try a thali or fish curry at a local restaurant. Ask about spice level.
    • Vegetarian food is easy to find everywhere, from thali places to upscale restaurants.
    • Drink bottled or filtered water. Go easy on very spicy food on your first day.

    Tips

    1. Avoid the local trains during extreme rush hours (8-11 AM, 5-8 PM) unless you are prepared for severe crowding
    2. Vada Pav and Pav Bhaji are quintessential local street foods you must try
    3. Meters in taxis and auto-rickshaws are generally reliable, but ride-hailing apps like Uber and Ola are easier
    4. Monsoon season can cause severe waterlogging; plan travel carefully if visiting in July or August
    5. Carry small denominations of cash (rupees) for street vendors and small shops
