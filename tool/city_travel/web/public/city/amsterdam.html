Getting around

Walk or bike in the centre; use trams for longer hops.

  • ✓Tram ★The main way around, run by GVB. Most routes run about 06:00 to midnight. Tap a contactless bank card (OVpay) or use an OV-chipkaart.
  • ✓MetroFills the gaps where trams don't go. Same GVB tickets and OVpay tap-in.
  • ✓BusCovers areas beyond the tram lines. A few night buses run after midnight.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsUber and Bolt work in Amsterdam. Taxis are metered and pricey; use them for late nights or heavy bags.
  • ✗Rental carNot worth it in the compact centre. Parking is limited and costly; stick to trams, bikes and walking.
  • ✓Walking ★The centre is flat and compact, so walking is often fastest. Watch for bike lanes.
  • ✓BikeLocals' favourite. Rent for a half day and follow the rules: use bike lanes and park only in marked spots.
  • ✓FerryFree ferries cross the IJ behind Centraal station. A cheap way to see Amsterdam Noord.

Climate

Temperate and changeable: mild summers, cool winters, and rain in every month. Always carry a light waterproof.

  • 🌧️Rain all yearExpect 12–15 rain days every month. Showers pass quickly, so a light rain jacket beats an umbrella in wind.
  • 🌡️Mild summersJuly and August peak around 22°C with lows near 14–15°C. Rarely hot; evenings stay cool.
  • 🧥Cool wintersDecember to February highs sit near 7–8°C and lows near 2–4°C. A warm coat, hat and gloves help.
  • 🌸Best months: May to SeptemberWarmest and brightest, around 17–22°C. Also the busiest, so book museums and stays early.
  • 💨Wind and changeable skiesWeather shifts within a day. Check the forecast each morning and keep plans flexible.
  • 🧳What to packLayers, a waterproof jacket, comfortable walking shoes and a small day bag. Add a warm layer for winter.

Landmarks 29

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Anne Frank House

    The secret annex where Anne Frank wrote her diary is a deeply moving must-see. Book tickets online well in advance; they sell out and are timed-entry only.

  2. 2

    Van Gogh Museum

    Houses the world's largest collection of Van Gogh's paintings, including Almond Blossom and Wheatfield with Crows. Reserve a timed ticket online and go early to avoid crowds.

  3. 3

    Rijksmuseum

    The Dutch national museum, home to The Night Watch and The Milkmaid. Book online, and don't miss the stunning Rijksmuseum Library inside.

  4. 4

    Canal Ring

    The UNESCO-listed ring of canals is the city's iconic backdrop, best explored on foot or by boat. Early morning or evening gives the best light and fewer crowds.

  5. 5

    Dam Square

    The historic heart of Amsterdam, flanked by the Royal Palace and close to the Red Light District. It's a central meeting point and easy to reach on foot from Centraal Station.

  6. 6

    Red Light District

    A unique urban hub and one of the city's oldest quarters, known for its narrow alleys and window brothels. Visit respectfully, keep photos to yourself, and go with a guide if you want context.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Haarlem: a short train ride, with a pretty centre and the Frans Hals museum. Check times and fares on the day.
    • Take the free ferry behind Centraal station to Amsterdam Noord for a different, quieter side of the city.
    • Rent a bike for a half day and ride the canals and the Vondelpark. Follow local rules and park only in marked spots.

    Getting around

    • Trams are the main way around; metro and buses fill the gaps, run by GVB.
    • Most routes run about 06:00 to midnight, with a few night buses.
    • Pay by tapping a contactless bank card (OVpay) or an OV-chipkaart.
    • Uber and Bolt work in Amsterdam; taxis are metered and pricey.
    • The centre is flat and compact, so walking and bikes are often fastest.
    • Free ferries cross the IJ behind Centraal station.

    Where to stay

    • Centrum · ≈ EUR 120–320 a night
      Walk to Dam Square, the Anne Frank House and Centraal station. Busiest and priciest, but you save on transit.
    • Jordaan · ≈ EUR 110–280 a night
      Quiet canals, small galleries and cafés. Ten minutes on foot to the centre and the Anne Frank House.
    • De Pijp · ≈ EUR 90–240 a night
      Local feel, Albert Cuyp market and lots of food. Near the Museumplein for the big art museums.
    • Oud-West · ≈ EUR 80–210 a night
      Good value and a short tram ride to the centre. Popular with locals and repeat visitors.

    Food & drink

    • Try Dutch snacks: bitterballen, raw herring from a stall, and stroopwafels fresh off the iron.
    • Albert Cuyp market in De Pijp is good for cheap eats; check opening hours on the day.
    • Indonesian rijsttafel is a Dutch classic; book ahead for dinner.
    • Cheap lunch: broodjes (sandwiches) and soup in cafés, roughly EUR 8–15.
    • Tap water is safe to drink. Carry a bottle and refill it.

    Tips

    1. OV-chipkaart or contactless payment covers all public transport
    2. Watch out for bicycles when walking and crossing the street — they have the right of way
    3. Renting a bike is the most authentic way to explore, but lock it securely
    4. Museums like the Rijksmuseum and Van Gogh Museum require booking well in advance
    5. Most shops and supermarkets close relatively early compared to other global cities

    Souvenirs

    Stroopwafels

    EUR 5–158 to 12 pieces · shop prices

    These thin waffles are filled with caramel syrup. They are a classic Dutch treat sold all over Amsterdam.

    Where: Lanskroon or Rudi's at Albert Cuyp
    Check the best-before date on freshly made ones, since they do not last long.

    Tony's Chocolonely chocolate

    EUR 9–162 to 3 bars, 180 g each · estimate

    This is a Dutch chocolate brand known for its bold, uneven bars and its focus on fair cocoa sourcing.

    Where: Tony's Chocolonely Super Store, Oudebrugsteeg
    Buy the store-only flavors first, because they are hard to find elsewhere.

    Delft Blue pottery tile

    EUR 10–301 tile · estimate

    Delft Blue is a hand-painted blue and white pottery style from the Dutch town of Delft. It is a well-known symbol of Dutch craft.

    Where: Heinen Delfts Blauw or the Rijksmuseum shop
    Look for the official Delft mark, because many copies are sold.
