Climate

Humid continental climate: long cold winters, warm humid summers, and rain in every month.

  • ☀️Best time: June to SeptemberWarm days around 20–25°C. Ideal for walking, parks and festivals.
  • 🥶Winter is long and coldDecember to March highs sit between -6°C and 2°C, with nights well below freezing. Pack a heavy coat, hat and gloves.
  • 🌂Rain all yearExpect roughly 9–14 wet days every month. A compact umbrella is worth carrying.
  • 💧Humid summersJuly and August feel muggy, highs near 24–25°C. Plan outdoor time for mornings.
  • 🍂Shoulder seasons are mildMay and September–October are comfortable, around 13–20°C, with fewer crowds.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for changing weather, a waterproof jacket, comfortable walking shoes, and warm winter gear if visiting December to March.

Landmarks 14

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Notre-Dame Basilica of Montreal

    A stunning Gothic Revival basilica with a breathtaking blue-and-gold interior, it is Montreal's most iconic sight. Book tickets online in advance and visit early morning or late afternoon to avoid crowds; modest dress is recommended.

  2. 2

    Old Montreal

    The historic heart of the city, with cobblestone streets, 18th-century architecture, and a lively waterfront. Wander on foot and stop at Place Jacques-Cartier; best explored in the morning before tour groups arrive.

  3. 3

    Saint Joseph's Oratory

    Canada's largest church, perched on Mount Royal, draws pilgrims and visitors for its impressive dome and city views. Entry to the basilica is free; take the metro to Côte-des-Neiges and wear comfortable shoes for the climb.

  4. 4

    Mount Royal Park

    Designed by Frederick Law Olmsted, this sprawling park offers the best skyline views from the Kondiaronk Belvedere. Go at sunset for a spectacular panorama; it's a short walk from downtown or a quick bus ride.

  5. 5

    Place Jacques-Cartier

    A lively public square in Old Montreal, lined with street performers, cafés, and historic buildings. It's perfect for people-watching; visit in summer when the square is buzzing with activity.

  6. 6

    Montreal Museum of Fine Arts

    One of Canada's most prestigious art museums, with an encyclopedic collection spanning centuries. Check for free admission to the permanent collection on the first Sunday of each month; the museum is easily reached by metro at Guy-Concordia.

  7. 7

    Jean-Talon Market

    One of North America's largest open-air markets, bursting with fresh produce, local cheeses, and artisanal goods. Go hungry and sample the stalls; it's a short walk from Jean-Talon metro station and best visited on weekday mornings.

  8. 8

    Olympic Park and Biodome

    Built for the 1976 Olympics, this complex includes the iconic inclined tower and the Biodome's immersive ecosystems. Buy a combined ticket for the Biodome and tower; take metro line 1 to Pie-IX and allow at least half a day.

    Swap in instead

    • Add a half-day on Mount Royal: walk up from the Plateau for city views, or take bus 11 to the lookout.
    • Cross to the south shore for a different view: check river shuttles in summer, or take the metro to Parc Jean-Drapeau.
    • Rainy day? Swap a walk for the Museum of Fine Arts or the McCord Stewart Museum; check opening hours on the day.

    Getting around

    • Metro: 4 lines, frequent and fast; fares $3.50 per ride or day pass $10.
    • Buses: extensive network; use same tickets as metro.
    • Taxis: available but pricier; Uber and Lyft operate.
    • Driving: traffic and parking are challenging; not recommended for short visits.
    • Walking/bikes: BIXI bike-share $5 for 24h access; many bike lanes.
    • Ferry: none for tourists, but river shuttles exist in summer.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown · ≈ CAD 180–420 a night
      Central for shopping, museums and the main metro lines; easy walk to the Plateau and Old Montreal.
    • Old Montreal · ≈ CAD 220–550 a night
      Cobbled streets, the basilica and the waterfront on your doorstep; prettiest at night.
    • Plateau Mont-Royal · ≈ CAD 130–320 a night
      Cheaper, lively and full of cafés and BYOB restaurants; walk up to Mount Royal park.
    • Quartier des Spectacles · ≈ CAD 170–400 a night
      Next to the main festival venues and a short walk from both Downtown and Old Montreal.

    Food & drink

    • Try the local classics: a Montreal bagel (chewier and sweeter than New York's), smoked meat sandwiches, and poutine.
    • Jean-Talon Market in Little Italy is great for cheese, produce and cheap snacks; Atwater Market is closer to Downtown.
    • Many small restaurants in the Plateau are BYOB — bring your own wine from a corner store (dépanneur) to save money.
    • Brunch is a local ritual; expect queues on weekend mornings, so go early or book ahead.
    • Prices vary widely: a casual lunch is roughly $12–20, dinner mains around $20–35. Check menus on the day.

    Tips

    1. French is the official language; greeting people with a simple 'Bonjour' is polite before switching to English
    2. Poutine and Montreal-style smoked meat sandwiches are essential culinary experiences
    3. Winter is bitterly cold; utilize the massive Underground City to stay warm while shopping and commuting
    4. Buy an OPUS card for easy access to the metro and bus network
    5. During summer, the city comes alive with massive street fairs and the International Jazz Festival
