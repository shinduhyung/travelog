Getting around

Walk downtown and use the TTC subway and streetcars; tap a PRESTO or contactless card.

  • ✓Metro ★TTC subway Lines 1 and 2 cover downtown well. Tap a PRESTO card or contactless card to ride.
  • ✓BusBuses and streetcars run frequently. The King Street streetcar is transit-only through the core.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsTaxis are metered. Uber and Lyft both operate widely.
  • ✓Rental carDriving is grid-easy, but downtown parking is costly and rush hour is heavy.
  • ✓Walking ★Best way to see downtown. Bike Share Toronto is handy along the waterfront.
  • ✓FerryA short free ferry and pedestrian tunnel link Billy Bishop Airport to the mainland.

Climate

Continental climate with cold winters and warm, humid summers. Rain falls year-round, spread over many days.

  • ☀️Best time: May to SeptemberHighs from 18°C to 26°C and mild evenings. Best for walking, the islands and patios.
  • 🥶Winter is cold (December to February)Highs near 0°C to 3°C and lows down to -8°C. Bring a heavy coat, hat and gloves.
  • 🌧️Rain in every monthAbout 9–13 rain days a month. April and October are the wettest; pack a light rain jacket.
  • 🌡️Summer can be humidJuly and August highs near 26°C with muggy spells. Carry water and plan indoor breaks.
  • 🍂Spring and fall are mildMay and September are comfortable, around 18–22°C. Good shoulder-season months.
  • 🧳What to packLayers for cool evenings, a waterproof jacket, and warm boots in winter. Comfortable shoes for walking.

Landmarks 22

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    CN Tower

    Toronto's defining icon and one of the tallest freestanding structures in the world, with sweeping views over the city and Lake Ontario. Book tickets online in advance to skip lines, and go near sunset for the best light.

  2. 2

    Royal Ontario Museum

    Canada's largest museum, mixing natural history, world cultures and dinosaurs in a striking crystal-clad building. Allow at least half a day and check for discounted evening hours.

  3. 3

    Distillery District

    A pedestrian-only historic district of Victorian industrial buildings now filled with galleries, shops and cafes. It is a pleasant stroll any time; visit on a weekday morning to avoid crowds.

  4. 4

    Ripley's Aquarium of Canada

    A large aquarium beside the CN Tower with walk-through tunnels and sharks overhead, great for families. Buy timed tickets online and go early on weekends.

  5. 5

    St. Lawrence Market

    A beloved historic market with food stalls, local produce and the famous peameal bacon sandwich. Come hungry on a Saturday morning when it is liveliest.

  6. 6

    Casa Loma

    A grand early-20th-century castle with towers, gardens and secret passages to explore. Take the audio guide and allow a couple of hours.

  7. 7

    Art Gallery of Ontario

    A major art museum with a celebrated Frank Gehry-designed facade and a strong Canadian and international collection. Check for free or reduced admission nights.

  8. 8

    Toronto Islands

    Car-free islands just offshore with beaches, bike paths and skyline views back at the city. Take the ferry from the harbour and bring a picnic.

    Swap in instead

    • Take the short ferry to the Toronto Islands for skyline views and a picnic; check the day's schedule.
    • Ride the subway to the Royal Ontario Museum and the AGO in one day if museums are your thing.
    • Day trip to Niagara Falls by train or bus; it is a long day, so start early.

    Getting around

    • TTC subway (Line 1 and 2) and streetcars cover downtown well; tap a PRESTO card or contactless card.
    • Buses and streetcars run frequently; King Street streetcar is transit-only through the core.
    • Taxis are metered; Uber and Lyft both operate widely.
    • Driving is grid-easy but downtown parking is costly and rush hour is heavy.
    • Walking is best downtown; bike-share (Bike Share Toronto) is handy along the waterfront.
    • A short free ferry and pedestrian tunnel link Billy Bishop Airport to the mainland.

    Where to stay

    • Downtown/Union · ≈ CAD 200–450 a night
      Walk to the CN Tower, the waterfront and the main subway lines. Easiest base for a first visit.
    • Yorkville · ≈ CAD 280–650 a night
      Quiet, upscale blocks near the ROM and good restaurants. Two subway stops from downtown.
    • Entertainment District · ≈ CAD 180–420 a night
      Next to King West bars, theatres and the Rogers Centre. Lively at night, sometimes noisy.
    • Distillery District · ≈ CAD 220–500 a night
      Cobbled, car-free lanes with galleries and cafés. A short streetcar ride east of the core.

    Food & drink

    • Try a peameal bacon sandwich at St. Lawrence Market; go early, many stalls close by mid-afternoon.
    • Chinatown and Kensington Market are close together: dim sum, dumplings and cheap tacos in one walk.
    • Poutine, butter tarts and a Toronto-style brunch are the local staples; expect CAD 15–30 for a main.
    • Book dinner ahead on weekends in King West and Yorkville; walk-ins are hard after 7pm.
    • Tipping is expected: about 15–20% on the bill, and it is often added to larger groups.

    Tips

    1. PRESTO card or contactless payment is essential for all TTC and GO Transit rides
    2. The PATH is a massive underground pedestrian network — extremely useful in winter
    3. Winters can be bitterly cold; dress in heavy layers and invest in good boots
    4. Visit the St. Lawrence Market or Kensington Market for great local food
    5. Stand on the right, walk on the left on subway escalators
