Metro for most trips; use Didi or taxis for late nights and airport runs.

Climate

Subtropical and humid: mild dry winters, hot wet summers, and a long rainy season from spring to autumn.

  • ☀️Best time: November to MarchMild, mostly dry days around 20–25°C. Ideal for parks, walking and sightseeing.
  • 🥵Hot, humid summerJune to September brings 30–31°C highs and heavy humidity. Move slowly and use air-conditioned breaks.
  • 🌧️Long rainy seasonMay to September has 21–25 rain days a month. Carry an umbrella and keep plans flexible.
  • 🌀Typhoon seasonStorms are possible roughly summer to early autumn. Check forecasts and transport alerts before travel.
  • 🌂Pack for rain and sunLight layers, a compact umbrella, quick-dry shoes and sunscreen. A light jacket helps in winter.
  • 🧳Indoor backupsMalls, museums and food halls are good rainy-day options; save outdoor trails for dry days.

Landmarks 16

Must-see · 6

  1. 1

    Window of the World

    Shenzhen's most famous attraction, a theme park packed with miniature replicas of world landmarks from the Eiffel Tower to the Pyramids. It is a classic first-timer stop and great for families. Go on a weekday morning to avoid crowds, and bring water since the park is large and sunny.

  2. 2

    Ping An International Finance Centre

    One of the tallest skyscrapers in China and the defining tower of Shenzhen's skyline. The observation deck gives sweeping views over the city and toward Hong Kong on clear days. Visit near sunset for the best light, and book tickets online in advance.

  3. 3

    Lianhuashan Park

    A large hilltop park with lawns, a famous statue and panoramic views of the Futian skyline. It is a favorite spot for locals and a relaxed break from the city. Come in the late afternoon for cooler weather and skyline photos.

  4. 4

    Shenzhen Museum

    The best place to understand Shenzhen's rapid rise from fishing village to megacity. Exhibits cover local history and culture, and entry is typically free. It is a good rainy-day or midday option; check opening hours before you go.

  5. 5

    OCT-LOFT Creative Culture Park

    A converted factory district full of galleries, design shops and cafes, and the heart of Shenzhen's creative scene. It is ideal for a relaxed afternoon of browsing and photos. Go on a weekday for a quieter visit.

  6. 6

    Splendid China Folk Village

    A large cultural theme park with miniature versions of famous Chinese sites and performances of ethnic minority culture. It suits visitors who want a compact overview of China's landmarks and traditions. Plan a half day and check show times on arrival.

    • Cross to Hong Kong by metro or ferry for a day if your visa allows; check entry rules before you go.

    Getting around

    • Metro is fast, cheap and covers most sights; fares from about ¥2.
    • Buses fill the gaps; use a transit card or phone app to pay.
    • Taxis and ride apps like Didi work well; airport to Futian about ¥100.
    • Driving yourself is not advised for short visits; traffic is heavy.
    • Walking is fine in central districts; shared bikes are everywhere.
    • Ferries link Shekou and Fuyong to Hong Kong, Macau and Zhuhai.

    Where to stay

    • Futian · ≈ CNY 350–900 a night
      Central and well connected by metro; good base for parks, malls and day trips across the city.
    • Luohu · ≈ CNY 250–700 a night
      Old downtown next to the Hong Kong border crossing; busy shopping streets and easy metro links.
    • Nanshan · ≈ CNY 400–1,000 a night
      Tech district with theme parks and coastal walks; handy for Shenzhen Bay and the western sights.
    • Shekou · ≈ CNY 300–850 a night
      Waterfront area with the ferry terminal to Hong Kong, Macau and Zhuhai; relaxed expat feel.

    Food & drink

    • Start with Cantonese dim sum for breakfast or lunch; order a few baskets to share and expect queues at popular spots.
    • Try street snacks in older districts: rice rolls, fish balls and grilled skewers; prices are low, often under ¥30 a portion.
    • Seafood is a local favourite; pick your fish and agree the price per weight before cooking.
    • Night markets and food streets are busiest after 8pm; go hungry and carry small cash as a backup.
    • Book popular restaurants ahead on weekends; many city malls have good mid-range chains with English menus.

    Tips

    1. WeChat Pay and Alipay are essential as physical cash is almost completely obsolete
    2. Visit Huaqiangbei to experience the largest electronics market in the world
    3. The metro is cheap and the absolute best way to get around the sprawling city
    4. VPNs are required to access Western websites and apps (Google, WhatsApp, etc) while on local networks
    5. Many locals are migrants from other provinces, so Mandarin is spoken much more than Cantonese
