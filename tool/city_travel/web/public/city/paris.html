Getting around

Walk in the centre and use the Métro for longer hops.

  • ✓Metro ★The Métro and RER cover the whole city. A single ticket is about €2.50; weekly Navigo passes are good value.
  • ✓BusBuses use the same tickets. Slower than the Métro but scenic.
  • ✓Taxi / ride appsMetered taxis with flat fares from the airports. Uber, Bolt and G7 apps work everywhere.
  • ✗Rental carDriving and parking in Paris are hard. A car is only useful for day trips.
  • ✓Walking ★Central Paris is compact and very walkable. Vélib' bikes are everywhere.
  • ✓TrainRER C goes to Versailles; Disneyland is on RER A.

Climate

Mild, changeable weather with rain in every month. Summers are warm, not hot; winters are cold and grey.

  • 🌤️Best months: May to SeptemberHighs around 19–26°C. Long daylight and the best odds of dry, pleasant days.
  • 🌧️Rain all yearAbout 9–13 rain days every month. Showers are often short; carry a small umbrella.
  • ❄️Cold wintersDecember to February highs of 7–10°C and lows near 2°C. Warm coat, scarf and layers.
  • ☀️Warm, not hot, summersJuly peaks near 26°C. Comfortable for walking, but book popular sights ahead in peak season.
  • 🧥Pack layersMornings and evenings are cooler than midday. A light jacket works most of the year.
  • 👟Wear good shoesYou will walk a lot on cobbles and stone. Waterproof shoes help on wet days.

Landmarks 104

Must-see · 8

  1. 1

    Eiffel Tower

    The symbol of Paris. Book summit or 2nd-floor tickets online ahead; for photos go to Trocadéro, and see it sparkle for five minutes on the hour after dark.

  2. 2

    Louvre Museum

    The world's most visited museum: Mona Lisa, Venus de Milo, Winged Victory. Timed tickets are required; enter via the Carrousel entrance and give it at least half a day.

  3. 3

    Notre-Dame de Paris

    The Gothic cathedral reopened in December 2024 after the 2019 fire. Entry is free with a reservable time slot; the towers have a separate paid climb.

  4. 4

    Arc de Triomphe

    Napoleon's triumphal arch at the top of the Champs-Élysées. Climb to the roof for the 12 avenues radiating out; use the underground passage, never cross the roundabout.

  5. 5

    Sacré-Cœur Basilica

    White basilica on the hill of Montmartre with a free church and a panoramic view over Paris. Combine with the painters' square Place du Tertre.

  6. 6

    Palace of Versailles

    Louis XIV's palace with the Hall of Mirrors and huge gardens, 40 minutes from Paris by RER C. Closed Mondays; book a timed slot and arrive at opening.

  7. 7

    Musée d'Orsay

    Impressionist masterpieces (Monet, Van Gogh, Renoir) inside a former railway station. Smaller than the Louvre and doable in 2-3 hours; closed Mondays.

  8. 8

    Champs-Élysées

    The grand avenue from Concorde to the Arc de Triomphe, lined with flagship stores. Walk it once, ideally at night; cafés here are pricey.

    Swap in instead

    • Swap a city day for Versailles: take RER C, then walk the palace and gardens. Allow a full day.
    • Travelling with kids? Swap a museum morning for Disneyland on RER A. Book tickets ahead.
    • Want a slower day? Ride a Vélib' bike along the Seine and stop at a market for lunch.

    Getting around

    • The Métro and RER cover the whole city; a single ticket is about €2.50 and weekly Navigo passes are good value.
    • Buses use the same tickets and are slower but scenic.
    • Taxis are metered with flat fares from the airports; Uber, Bolt and G7 apps work everywhere.
    • Driving and parking in Paris are hard; a car is only useful for day trips.
    • Central Paris is compact and very walkable; Vélib' bikes are everywhere.
    • RER C goes to Versailles; Disneyland is on RER A.

    Where to stay

    • Le Marais · ≈ EUR 150–320 a night
      Central and walkable. Close to Notre-Dame, the Pompidou and good cheap food. Lively at night.
    • Saint-Germain & Latin Quarter · ≈ EUR 180–400 a night
      Classic Paris feel by the river. Good for cafés, bookshops and the Luxembourg Gardens.
    • Louvre & Opéra · ≈ EUR 200–450 a night
      Closest to the Louvre, the Tuileries and big department stores. Best metro links for short trips.
    • Montmartre · ≈ EUR 110–260 a night
      Quieter and often cheaper. Village feel around Sacré-Cœur, but longer rides to the centre.

    Food & drink

    • Try a basic bistro lunch: many places do a fixed 'formule' of two or three courses for about €18–28.
    • Buy picnic food at a street market or bakery: baguette, cheese and fruit for under €10.
    • Order the plat du jour at lunch. It is usually cheaper than the same dish at dinner.
    • Tap water is free and safe. Ask for 'une carafe d'eau' instead of bottled water.
    • Book popular dinner spots a few days ahead. Many small places are closed one or two weekdays; check on the day.

    Tips

    1. Buy a carnet (multi-trip ticket book) or Navigo pass for metro and bus travel.
    2. Most major museums are free the first Sunday of each month.
    3. Lunch menus (formule midi) at sit-down restaurants offer 3 courses for 12–18€ — best value for fine dining.
    4. Learn a few French phrases — even a simple 'Bonjour' and 'Merci' is greatly appreciated.
    5. Pickpockets are active around the Eiffel Tower and Montmartre; use a money belt in crowds.
